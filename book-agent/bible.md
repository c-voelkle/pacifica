# Story Bible: The Grand Confederation of Pacifica

## 1. Character Voice Profiles
- **The Chronicler (Narrator):** Modeled after Herodotus and Thucydides. The tone is academic, slightly detached, but prone to bouts of awe. Uses words like 'inevitable,' 'grandeur,' and 'providence.'
- **Elias Thorne (The American Caesar):** A voice of quiet, gravelly authority. He speaks in short, punchy sentences. He is a man of the frontier who learned to speak like a king. He values loyalty above all else.
- **General Marcus Vance:** Pragmatic, weary, and deeply militaristic. His voice is defined by logistics and the harsh reality of the battlefield.
- **Lady Kaiulani (Diplomat):** Elegant, sharp-witted, and protective of her people. Her voice represents the fusion of indigenous royalty and imperial politics.

## 2. World Rules
- **The Pacific Pivot:** Geography is the primary driver of history. The Atlantic is a backwater; the Pacific is the 'Great Arena.'
- **Technological Acceleration:** The fusion of American industry and Asian resources leads to an earlier, steam-heavy industrial revolution (steampunk elements but grounded in reality).
- **Imperial Syncretism:** The Empire does not just conquer; it absorbs. Shinto-Christianity, American-Samoan architecture, and Anglo-Japanese naval traditions are the norm.
- **The Fall of the East:** The U.S. East Coast is a fractured, de-industrialized zone of 'Free States' under Thorne's thumb.

## 3. Sensory Palette
- **Colors:** Imperial Blue, Sunset Gold, Coal Black, and the deep green of the jungle.
- **Smells:** Salt spray, ozone from steam engines, sandalwood incense, and gunpowder.
- **Sounds:** The rhythmic clanking of shipyard cranes, the roar of the Pacific surf, the brassy fanfares of the Imperial Anthem.

## 4. Themes
- **The Cycle of Empire:** Rise, consolidation, hubris, and decay.
- **Individual vs. History:** Can one man’s vision truly redirect the course of humanity?
- **The Cost of Order:** Security and prosperity bought at the price of traditional liberty.

## 5. Stylistic Negative Constraints
- **No Modernisms:** Avoid 21st-century slang or perspectives on social justice; characters must remain rooted in their 19th/early 20th-century worldviews.
- **No Magic:** The 'miracles' of the Empire are technological and logistical, not supernatural.
- **No Simple Heroism:** Thorne is neither a pure hero nor a pure villain; avoid 'good vs. evil' tropes.

## 6. Confederate character values

Use [Confederate character values for Pacifica](references/confederate_value_schema.md) and the four `confederate_value_profile` entries in [the character bible](character_bible.json) for Civil War characterization. These profiles refine the existing voice fields; they do not establish additional plot events.

Separate the reason a man entered Confederate service from what keeps him serving and what could justify refusal, surrender, or departure. Let political independence, household protection, honor, comradeship, professional duty, material fairness, and religious obligation compete through specific decisions. The Confederacy's commitment to slavery remains part of this world: concern for family or criticism of command does not establish opposition to slavery. Give each character both a costly virtue and a harm he permits or explains away.

- **Jefferson Davis:** His central concern is whether a government accepting Thorne's help can retain legitimate authority and political independence. He can understand Pacifica's superiority while rejecting its claim to jurisdiction. Preserve his investment in the slaveholding order. Pain, anger, and pride may interrupt a coherent argument; they should not replace it. Make the human cost of refusing terms concrete.
- **Judah Benjamin:** Give his negotiations a positive purpose: keeping institutions able to protect civilians, feed armies, recover prisoners, and negotiate another day. Let each concession preserve something identifiable and surrender something precise. His measured legal voice distinguishes temporary arrangements from permanent transfers of authority. He understands Davis's objection even when he reaches another judgment. His Jewish identity does not explain financial calculation or moral detachment; pragmatism does not establish antislavery convictions.
- **Major Julian St. Clair:** His grievance is that Thorne converts pledged solidarity into a financial account whose repayment extinguishes obligation. Honor must govern his own treatment of subordinates as well as his demands on equals. Let him recognize military realities and make reasoned objections despite his wound. His personal generosity coexists with his stake in an exclusionary slaveholding society.
- **Colonel Armistead:** Give the move from Confederate service to Pacifica's Atlantic Auxiliary a defensible personal purpose, then test that purpose in later imperial service. Preserving survivors, securing provisions or arrears, and supporting households are possible motives to develop, not events already established. His practical Virginia voice attends to terrain, orders, and particular men. Ask when professional continuity becomes complicity in conduct he once condemned. Do not identify this fictional character with historical Lewis Armistead.

For Western scenes, bring commanders' credibility, family proximity, civilian losses, food, transport, and uncertain information into the foreground. Select a named voice from the source register rather than treating one account as representative of an entire army or theater. The guide distinguishes wartime letters and diaries, official reports, retrospective memoirs, later interpretation, and fictional applications. Events after the alternate-history divergence remain comparative evidence, not automatic events in Pacifica.
