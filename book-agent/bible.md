# Story Bible: The Grand Confederation of Pacifica\n\n## 1. Character Voice Profiles\n- **The Chronicler (Narrator):** Modeled after Herodotus and Thucydides. The tone is academic, slightly detached, but prone to bouts of awe. Uses words like 'inevitable,' 'grandeur,' and 'providence.'\n- **Elias Thorne (The American Caesar):** A voice of quiet, gravelly authority. He speaks in short, punchy sentences. He is a man of the frontier who learned to speak like a king. He values loyalty above all else.\n- **General Marcus Vance:** Pragmatic, weary, and deeply militaristic. His voice is defined by logistics and the harsh reality of the battlefield.\n- **Lady Kaiulani (Diplomat):** Elegant, sharp-witted, and protective of her people. Her voice represents the fusion of indigenous royalty and imperial politics.\n\n## 2. World Rules\n- **The Pacific Pivot:** Geography is the primary driver of history. The Atlantic is a backwater; the Pacific is the 'Great Arena.'\n- **Technological Acceleration:** The fusion of American industry and Asian resources leads to an earlier, steam-heavy industrial revolution (steampunk elements but grounded in reality).\n- **Imperial Syncretism:** The Empire does not just conquer; it absorbs. Shinto-Christianity, American-Samoan architecture, and Anglo-Japanese naval traditions are the norm.\n- **The Fall of the East:** The U.S. East Coast is a fractured, de-industrialized zone of 'Free States' under Thorne's thumb.\n\n## 3. Sensory Palette\n- **Colors:** Imperial Blue, Sunset Gold, Coal Black, and the deep green of the jungle.\n- **Smells:** Salt spray, ozone from steam engines, sandalwood incense, and gunpowder.\n- **Sounds:** The rhythmic clanking of shipyard cranes, the roar of the Pacific surf, the brassy fanfares of the Imperial Anthem.\n\n## 4. Themes\n- **The Cycle of Empire:** Rise, consolidation, hubris, and decay.\n- **Individual vs. History:** Can one man’s vision truly redirect the course of humanity?\n- **The Cost of Order:** Security and prosperity bought at the price of traditional liberty.\n\n## 5. Stylistic Negative Constraints\n- **No Modernisms:** Avoid 21st-century slang or perspectives on social justice; characters must remain rooted in their 19th/early 20th-century worldviews.\n- **No Magic:** The 'miracles' of the Empire are technological and logistical, not supernatural.\n- **No Simple Heroism:** Thorne is neither a pure hero nor a pure villain; avoid 'good vs. evil' tropes.
