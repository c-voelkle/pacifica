"The Grand Confederation of Pacifica"

Introduction: The Chronicler’s Perspective

    Prologue:
        Reflections on the unprecedented rise of the Pacific Empire.
        Tone: Awe mixed with academic objectivity, modeled after Herodotus.
        Acknowledgment of the difficulties of chronicling events spanning continents, decades, and diverse peoples.
    Setting the Stage:
        Brief overview of the current world and its many differences to the early 19th century.
        Introduction of the chronicler's main sources (letters, oral traditions, imperial decrees, etc.).

Part I: The Rise of the American Caesar (1850s–1890s)
1. Early Life of the Protagonist:

    A bold youth from Kentucky who left home at an early age to settle on the Frontier.
    Early adventures: clashes with Comanche, KBD, Mexican bandits
    Vision for the Pacific Arc emerges during KBD deployment

2. Campaign for Power:

    Builds a loyalist faction among KGC, settlers, soldiers, and businessmen.
    Clash with the U.S. government: Initially framed as rebellion.

3. The War of Northern Aggression and the Weakening of the Union (1861-1866)
    Union defeats lead to desperate guerilla tactics, loss of legitimacy
    

4. The Providentorship and the Closing of the American Interregnum (1866-1867)
    The Battle of Washington (fictional): A military confrontation leading to the fall of the U.S. federal government and his election as Providentor of the American Free States.
    Characteristics and Policies of the Providentorship
    The Salutary Effects of Freedom on the Post-War AFS
    Restructuring of the Military and Pivot to the West

5. Pacific Ambitions (1867-1888)
    Purchase of Department of Alaska, Discovery of Yukon Gold
    Pacific Island Acquisitions, Expansion to Hawaii, Guam, Samoa (1870-1878)
    Spanish-American War (1879)
    The Question of Japan
    The Pacific Rim, Changing Relations with Trade Cities in China and Siberia (1870-1882)
    Campaigns in Japan and China (1882-1886): 
        Details of battles, alliances, betrayals, and cultural assimilation.
        Comparison of the American Caesar to Alexander the Great.
    Bridging the Bering Strait: The Russo-American War and its Consequences (1887)
    Expansion into Southeast Asia, Indonesia, and Australia, First Contest with Britain (1888-1890):
        Challenges of distance and diverse climates.
        Local resistance and American technological advantages.

Part II: The Consolidation of the Grand Confederation of Pacifica (1890s–1910s)
1. Administrative Reforms:
    Division of Confederacy into 124 States plus three administered territories
    Creation of a pan-Pacific bureaucracy: Integration of diverse peoples into the empire.
    Division of Powers at the State Level
        

2. Cultural Transformations:

    Fusion of American, Asian, and Indigenous traditions.
    Description of various key cities (e.g., Pacifica in Australia, Capital at San Francisco).
    Conclusion of the Panama Canal and the port city of Panama (1900)
    Religious shifts: Syncretism between Christianity and local belief systems.
    Industrialisation of the Pacific

3. Struggles for Succession (1912-1914):

    The Caesar’s death sparks a power struggle among his generals and heirs.
    Detailed accounts of key battles, coups, and assassinations.
    Second Contest with Britain and France, Great Indian War, Treaty of Rangoon (1912-1913)
    The rise of a new dynasty: One of the Providentor’s generals consolidates power through both diplomacy and military strength.
    Establishment of Imperial Capital at Thalassia, Hawaii (1914)

Part III: The Pacific Empire and the World (1910s–1940s)
1. Effects on Other Regions:

    Europe:
        Europe after the Treaty of Rangoon: Britain humbled, Bonapartism returns to France
        Alliance between Germany and Russia
        The European Civil War (1918-1919)
        Establishment of the European Confederation (1919)
    Latin America:
        Influx of Pacific imperialists seeking to control trade routes through the Panama Canal.
    Central Asia:
        Rising tensions as the empire pushes westward, Afghan War (1921-1925), Alliance with Persia (1926)

2. Internal Threats:

    Uprisings in Siberia and Southeast Asia.
    Conflicts between the central government and local governors.

3. Technological and Social Change:

    Industrial revolution accelerated by Pacific Empire innovations.
    Social stratification: A new elite of "imperial citizens" emerges, sidelining traditional American pioneers.
