## Authority and mechanism

Japan remains wealthy and retains its accumulated fiscal cash. Bank shareholders first absorb recognized losses; Japan funds capital reconstruction; the BOJ supplies secured domestic liquidity; San Francisco provides a separately tested foreign-settlement facility. Severe loss of confidence, simultaneous short-term nonrenewal and capital flight make the external crisis consequential.

The mechanism and Concord continuity are approved. All numerical stocks, haircuts, rates, withdrawal assumptions, reserve buffers and amounts are MODEL scenarios. The six-week import buffer is an operating target, not a gold-cover statute and not a substitute for the separate zero-reserve payment test.

## Contract and accounting

The $600m commitment allocates $200m to Pacifica and $400m to private underwriters. Drawings are one-third public and two-thirds private. At the working draw these are $165.62m and $331.24m. Private subscriptions are an allocation of existing planned overseas investment, not an extra source of saving.

A Japanese stabilization agency borrows under a Japanese sovereign guarantee and on-lends the same amount to the reconstructed banks. Its coupon receipts and principal recoveries meet the foreign loan's service. Japan's fiscal accounts show the borrowing, matching asset, on-lending advances and recoveries. Pacifica's public and private loan claims match those obligations.

The coupon is 5.5%, accruing from actual draw months and paid in December 1927. Equal principal installments run from 1929 to 1933. The schedule includes 1933 expressly, so the novel's ending does not erase the last obligation. Debt service survives a lapse of the political arrangement; future credit availability is a distinct question.

The domestic BOJ facility has a $400m authorization, $700m eligible collateral and a 30% haircut. It draws $295m; 7% collection of the surviving $5bn loan book covers its repayment in 1928. These are MODEL repayment-capacity assumptions. Bank capital is restored to 8% of surviving loans, a planning convention rather than a historical legal standard.

Pacifica tests a 50% bullion call on the rescue drawing and retains an additional callable settlement balance for the rest through 1932. A separate 100%-bullion-call test also passes the Story gold-cover check. No loan commitment is treated as cash, no Japanese yen reserve is counted as gold, and no receipts from unused commitments are invented.

## Political and narrative continuity

The Senate authorizes the capped credit subscription under the ordinary budget but rejects the Concord treaty. Julian's subsequent provisional application remains illegal in substance. The emergency loan does not supply treaty-making authority. Kaiulani's 1928 consent and indemnity remain necessary.

Hirohito and Saionji choose common financial and strategic support over payment restrictions, depreciation or a more destructive liquidation. The Concord follows an existing friendship and naval partnership, with a continuing settlement undertaking. It does not arise from a claim that Japan has no public wealth. Its 65/35 common-cost quota is separate from the rescue's 1/3 public underwriting share.

## Tests and limits

Independent formula reconstruction and the workbook engine reproduce the active Story figures. The original $300m facility leaves a $196.85m operating-buffer shortfall. With no San Francisco credit the true payment deficit is $121.85m and the buffer shortfall $496.85m.

The no-rescue counterfactual still grants the later normal FX receipts: this is favorable to Japan and avoids claiming a permanent deficit from a temporary shock. December receipts cannot settle June bills. The commitment is agreed before the May reopening even though the working drawing occurs in June.

The severe sudden stop and capital flight are essential premises. With only 50% of short external funding not renewed, or with no capital flight, the modeled external need disappears. The prose therefore shows a systemic panic, not ordinary bank weakness. The Stress case remains unfunded and is explicitly flagged; later figures in that case are conditional obligations.

History supports the bad-loan, moratorium and bank-restructuring mechanisms. It does not establish the alternate scenario's numerical stocks. Historical Japan returned to gold in January 1930; the Concord's monetary arrangements are explicit fictional divergences.
