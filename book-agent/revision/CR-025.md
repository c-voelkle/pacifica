**CR-025 integration:** [Wartime credit, Japan's boom and who pays](CR-025.md) explains how the Entente loans of 1914–18 build Japanese factories and shipyards on short foreign money, how peace and the 1923 earthquake leave those debts unresolved, and who finances the champions' growth abroad. Its amounts are MODEL estimates.

The champions grow first on their own profits, and the savings bank lends them the nation's deposits. The Crown's shares, taken in exchange for charters and patents, make the state an owner without building anything. Abroad the champions build with partners: Japanese and Chinese merchant houses and Gulf and Mexican investors take about a quarter of each venture. Before 1914 the champions sell bonds in London, Paris and Amsterdam, and in the 1920s on Wall Street and in Tokyo. Like the Union before 1914, Pacifica becomes a creditor through its Treasury and partly a debtor through its companies, and foreigners draw a growing income from its success. **[MODEL — CR-025]** By 1929 foreign holders own about $3.8bn of claims on the champions and draw about $280m a year from them.

San Francisco syndicates gather the Treasury's money, Pacifican savings and foreign subscriptions into the Entente loans. Investors in the neutral Union buy through Pacifican correspondents even while Washington competes with Thalassia; they keep their own claims and their own risk. Washington discourages loans to belligerents at first, so in 1914 Pacifica's own savers carry the loans alone. When the Union's banks grow wary of unsecured Entente paper at the end of 1916, loans secured on the Allies' requisitioned foreign securities, many of them Pacifican bonds, help carry the last two years of the war, and Pacifican savers take up more than before. Much of the money comes back as orders, and Japanese shipyards, mills and trading houses take a large share of them. **[PROPOSED — CR-030]** Russia's share comes back as locomotives and freight cars. Pacifican works build Russian-gauge engines on Entente credit and ship them through Vladivostok, and most of them go to work on the Siberian and Manchurian lines.

**[MODEL — CR-025]** The advances total $8.5bn: $1.7bn from the Treasury, $2.85bn from Pacifican savers and $3.95bn from abroad, about half of it from Union investors and the rest from collateral loans, Japan, the neutrals, and the Gulf and Latin America. About $1.66bn becomes Japanese orders. Japanese firms keep about $124m of their profits for a $1.33bn expansion and borrow the other $1.2bn from their banks.
