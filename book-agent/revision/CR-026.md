## Source paragraphs

**CR-026 integration:** [Kiyoshi's bid for the crown](CR-026.md), revision 6. With Silas dead, Kiyoshi claims the crown in 1913 and renounces it in 1914 in exchange for the Kuroda queen; the Act of Settlement names him as Elias's son. He founds his own Kuroda branch and is made a count in 1907; Kiyotaka's son Kiyonaka keeps the main house.

Kiyoshi has entered Kuroda Kiyotaka’s house in 1886 as an adopted son-in-law, marrying into it and taking the name Kuroda Kiyoshi. Japan gives him the name Elias withheld. His family position binds the naval party to a recognized Japanese house. Lady Chiyo belongs to another branch of that house; **[APPROVED — CR-026]** Kiyotaka's own son Kiyonaka remains heir of the main house. Kiyoshi's bride is Kiyotaka's adopted daughter Hyaku. After the victory of 1905 Kiyoshi founds his own branch house and is made Count Kuroda in 1907; his son will inherit it.

**[APPROVED — CR-026]** With Silas dead, Kuroda Kiyoshi claims the crown the Act is to create. Early in 1913 he petitions the Regent as the founder's eldest surviving son and asks that the crown follow the founder's blood. He offers to reign only for his lifetime, with Julian after him; Victoria sees that an eldest-son rule would carry the crown to his Kuroda son. He needs only to deny Julian two-thirds and outlast a Regent of seventy-five. In Tokyo his mother's cousin Uehara Yūsaku has just brought down a cabinet by resigning the army ministry; Kiyoshi lets it be known that if the Act passes over him, no officer of the naval party will accept the western squadron's command. In October the squadron anchors off Kāneʻohe Bay on a courtesy visit, in sight of Thalassia, and he gives no order.

Succession passes to the eldest son, then eldest daughter, otherwise to an heir adopted with Senate consent. Noe's Maui chiefly descent strengthens Julian's claim alongside his adoption by a Kamehameha queen. Loyalists dispute whether a hānai son may continue the Kamehameha line. The Act heals much politically, but does not reverse the Supreme Court's ignored judgment of 1876. **[APPROVED — CR-026]** The Act recites Kiyoshi's renunciation and names him "Kuroda Kiyoshi, son of Elias Thorne", the only public document that ever does. He never uses the name.

Julian marries Lady Chiyo, a Japanese woman of the kazoku and another branch of the Kuroda house. Kiyoshi arranges the match, binding the naval party to his half-brother’s throne. **[APPROVED — CR-026]** The match is the price of his renunciation: his house supplies the queen, and the heir after Julian is of Kuroda blood. Chiyo’s Japanese standing protects the imperial class from appearing to have abandoned Japan. Their son is named Kahekili for Noe’s Maui line, with the Japanese name 雷, Ikazuchi, given by meaning through Chiyo’s family; **[PROPOSED]** he is born about 1916. Exact wedding and birth dates remain qualified. The heir will reign in Hawaii as Kamehameha VII; he is distinct from Elias and Victoria’s lost Kalanimoku. Japan’s succession law excludes him, so the shared dynasty does not merge the crowns.

**[PROPOSED]** In her final months as regent, Victoria blocks the worst of Japan’s Twenty-One Demands on China. Tokyo retreats and concludes that access to Thalassia’s institutions offers more than direct defiance. **[PROPOSED — CR-026]** Kiyoshi backs her, the first service he renders the settled crown. The episode tests the limits of a partnership in which Japan has learned Pacifica’s methods and grown strong enough to demand a share in their direction.

Restoration officers strike against Inukai's government in May 1932 to stop the Concord's first renewal. Their manifesto names Chiyo and Kahekili's betrothal and attacks the Thalassia bakufu and the merchant houses that share in its champions. Emperor Shōwa and the navy stand against them, and the coup fails. Japanese-crewed carriers in the western squadron make the danger to Oʻahu concrete; Kiyoshi's son holds its Japanese units loyal. **[APPROVED — CR-026]** He learned in 1913 that a fleet cannot make a sovereign.
