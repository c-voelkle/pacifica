## Summary

1. **The historical Victoria Kamāmalu replaces the fictional Kaiulani.** Born in 1838, sister of Kamehameha IV and V, Kuhina Nui as Kaʻahumanu IV from 1855. Every canon role of Kaiulani passes to her; references to Kaiulani in CR-016 to CR-026 refer to her.
2. **She chooses Elias.** He hears her chant at the Confederation ceremony of 1856 and her voice haunts him for life. In 1857 she refuses Lunalilo and Kalākaua out of love for him, and her brothers accept the match for reasons of rank, property and party. They marry in November 1858. Kiyoshi is conceived that spring, before the wedding.
3. **She survives 1866 and becomes Queen in 1872.** Monterey's physicians save her after her kahuna's failure; she succeeds Lot under his 1864 constitution and appoints Ruth Kuhina Nui.
4. **1874 is a lease, then a creep.** She ends the barracks mutiny of 1873 herself and keeps the Queen's Guard, which convinces the admirals they need Pearl Harbor. After Lunalilo's death she refuses a rising in her name, then signs a lawful fifty-year lease of Puʻuloa. The confederal ordinances of 1874–1876 and the Territorial Council that stretch it into rule are illegal; her Supreme Court says so, and she lets the ruling go. No massacre, no abeyance.
5. **Kalākaua is her mythmaker and envoy; the Five run the machine.** The heads of the chartered estates govern the technocratic state under an unwritten arrangement: the Crown does not touch the estates, and the estates never move against the Crown.
6. **From 1887 she is Regent of Pacifica and still Queen of Hawaii.** The Act of Settlement of 1914 joins her crown to the Crown of Pacifica; in 1915 she lays down both and Julian accedes.

## B. The design

### B1. Who she is

The portrait rests on the sources, read against their biases. Sophia Cracroft (1861), an Anglican guest of Wyllie and Emma, found her stately, imposing and of unpleasant countenance, and recorded the "very inconvenient influence" of the missionaries over her acts as Kuhina Nui. Hawaiian tradition held her Lunalilo's equal "in looks, intelligence, manners, and volatility"; Commissioner Gregg found her at fifteen well educated and more engaging than most girls her age. She was the pro-American Kamehameha, refused to leave the Congregational church when her family turned Anglican, led the Kawaiahaʻo choir, chanted and composed in the old style, and in her last historical years turned back toward Hawaiian tradition. Twice the American party tried to make her its candidate (1854 by Wyllie's account; 1863, when missionaries argued that the regency was hers), and twice she gave way to her family: in 1863 she proclaimed Lot herself, letting the constitution's form go for an orderly succession.

Her pattern: others mean to use her; she yields on form and keeps the substance. Her constant is her people's survival. What changes over the novel is the price she will pay for it.

### B2. The courtship and the marriage

Elias, driven by freedom, finds a woman with everything except freedom: rank, land, office, two brothers and a church deciding for her. She chooses him in a song; the man who cannot be given anything is chosen by the woman who cannot be given away. She carries the deepest genealogy in the Pacific, the one conquest money cannot buy, and the origin of the House of Thorne-Kamehameha. At seventeen she presides over the Privy Council; he has never met a partner who can sign a state into being. Their marriage is two households and two seats by design, which is why a restless man can commit to it and why it can die without ending.

### B3. 1863–1872

The Society, the choir, the kahuna, the illness of 1866 and the doctors from Monterey. She leaves 1866 trusting no power, Hawaiian or haole, that cannot show results, while keeping the chants, the language, Kalama's friendship and her faith.

### B4. Queen, the mutiny and the Guard

Kuykendall's account of September 1873 is the model: about sixty Household Troops against their drillmaster Jajczay and Adjutant General Judd; Lunalilo, sick at Waikīkī, vacillated for days, wrote to "my loving people" and disbanded the army, a humiliation that left the kingdom without force. Victoria does the reverse on every point. The cost is personal: Judd is her own schoolmate and her own party's man. The result is political: the kingdom keeps a guard loyal to its Queen alone, and that is what the admirals cannot accept.

Historical anchors: Jajczay was born in Buda, conscripted in 1850, imprisoned at Olmütz, rode with the hussars who escorted Maximilian to Mexico, reached Honolulu by 1872 and killed himself in Costa Rica in 1874. Judd (1835–1890), son of Gerrit P. Judd, Royal School classmate of Kalākaua, became Kalākaua's chamberlain and went with him on the 1881 tour. Moehonua succeeded Jajczay.

### B6. The Five and the unwritten arrangement

The constitutional basis is canon: the Polytechnic's chartered estate (examinations, audit, General Staff, telegraph), the Office of Public Measures, the Crown's shares in the champions and the Development Fund's claim on every surplus. The people at their head hold force, money, communications and the numbers; the Crown holds legitimacy and the land under its capital.

The terms, never written: the Crown does not touch the estates' charters, appointments or the Development Fund; the estates never take sides against the Crown and never let a faction capture them. Tests: Donner (1887) holds; Kiyoshi's bid (1913) is decided by the Five's neutrality; Julian's Senate quarrel (1915–1917) strains it; the Altai report (1921) breaks it, the priests lying at the Crown's request; their successors decide 1932.

### B7. The arc

The hardening comes between 1874 and 1887, not in 1874. She spends thirteen years defending a bargain everyone calls betrayal and finds that defending it requires more and more of what she disliked in Elias. By 1887 the Regent must make the bargain look justified, which is why she measures everything. The reader cannot tell whether her measures come from love of the people, guilt or rivalry with Noe.

### B8. Where the canon changes

- §6: no fictional cousin; the historical appointment of January 1855; the 1857 refusals and songs; the November 1858 wedding.
- §12: 30 November 1863; Lot keeps the office; the 1866 survival.
- §17: the accession, the mutiny, the February rising, the lease and the ordinances replace Lunalilo's reign, the election, the abeyance and the massacre.
- §19: Kalākaua; §24: the Five; §27: the Act joins the crowns rather than restoring an abeyant throne.

### B9. Mythic structure

**Backbone myths.**
1. **The Kumulipo:** night to day, and a genealogy that justifies a line. The Chronicler's history is the Kumulipo of the House of Thorne-Kamehameha.
2. **Lono and Kaikilani:** a queen regnant and her hot-headed, "instinctively generous" husband; when he wrongs her the chiefs rise in her name and she refuses their revolt. Victoria in February 1874. The echo stays political, not literal.
3. **The triple marriage of Laʻamaikahiki:** a voyaging hānai son, three wives, three founding lines. Elias's three women and the three sons of the Truce.
4. **Māui fishing up the islands:** the line that breaks when a brother looks back. The joined Pacific, and 1932.
