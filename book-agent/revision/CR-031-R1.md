# CR-031-R1 — Japanese construction and labour organisation

**Status:** APPROVED — direct author correction, 23 October 2026. **Parent:** [CR-031](CR-031.md). **Scope:** The industrial premise and its Kamchatka/Kolyma consequences. Other CR-031 territorial, corporate, diplomatic and OPEN/PROPOSED/MODEL decisions retain their existing scopes.

## Author decision

> One issue with cr-031 - it seems to suggest that Japan would be using forced labor in the tako beya style. However in this timeline Japan has been working together with Pacifica for decades, not just learning modern project management and assembly line techniques but actively competing with them to come up with better and more efficient ways of working. This would effectively move the Japanese construction miracle into the 19th century (as the model shows) and rule out any margin eating reliance on labor syndicates and local gang bosses.

## Operative correction

Japan and Pacifica are reciprocal industrial innovators and competitors over decades. By the later nineteenth century, Japan's construction industry already combines professional project organisation, coordinated and repeated work, machinery and skilled crews. The timing is an author-approved feature of this alternate history.

Contractors retain recruitment, training, payment and production control within their professional enterprises. Their advantage lies in completed work, fewer delays, skilled-worker retention and efficient use of plant and materials. Reliance on coercive labour syndicates or local gang bosses would surrender margins and control. The correction concerns that intermediary system; it establishes no ban on ordinary worker association or collective bargaining.

The proposed Kamchatka Company uses this industrial system. The bonded-gang / tako beya premise and Pacifican complicity through knowingly approving those camps are removed. Korean participation remains paid contractual employment within the otherwise proposed migration arrangements. The Company’s proposed founding, ownership fraction and cash stake are not newly approved by this correction.

Kolyma remains Siberian and employs free, well-paid workers with Pacifican dredges. The assertion that forced labour would necessarily be faster and cheaper is removed. Equipment, logistics, skills and retention explain operating performance; no unsupported wage discount or replacement productivity figure is inserted.

The original source snapshot remains available in Git history. The original CR-031 reading record retains clearly labelled pre-correction excerpts for provenance, with this decision taking precedence. No new atrocity, scandal or character knowledge is substituted for the deleted premise. The existing Altai arc remains independently qualified.
