## Original supplied-master excerpts — before CR-031-R1

**CR-031 integration:** [The Treaty of Harbin: the North, the victors' terms and China's articles](CR-031.md), revision 4. The Siberian Republic is a federation of graded autonomy: provinces, an autonomous Sakha land with a port at Okhotsk, and a North-East Territory from Ola Bay to Nikolayevsk with Chukotka. Kamchatka and the Koryak coast become a republic under Japanese protection; Transbaikalia is a protectorate under a Transbaikal Council and a civilian Resident after the Cossack Host turns Semenov out in 1919. China recovers Russia's privileges, Amur navigation to the sea and suzerainty over Mongolia. Pacifica takes the Commander Islands, Arctic leases and a charter, insists that Siberian ships pass the straits, and is complicit in the Kamchatka Company's forced labour. The fortification question is **[OPEN-031-11]**.

**[PROPOSED]** In 1918 Pacifica pays and supplies the Czechoslovak Legion. **[PROPOSED — CR-030]** The Legion sails from Vladivostok in 1920, once Siberian and Japanese troops can take over its stretch of the line, and the wagons it has lived in go back to carrying freight. The coalition moves west from Japanese Vladivostok through Chinese-owned Manchuria under a single command. Its local partner is the Siberian Regionalist government, initially under Pyotr Vologodsky, while Zhang Zuolin supplies the Chinese cooperation. A Pacifican railway board runs the line west of Chita. **[PROPOSED — CR-030]** Trained before the war, the board takes over the engines Pacifican works built for Russia and pays the railwaymen and the coal miners on time in sound money. It feeds their families from its own stores so that nobody leaves the line to look for bread. New loops, larger water mains and strengthened bridges on the single-track branch from Manchuria double its trains within a year. Three paths in ten stay with Siberian grain, coal and passengers: the railway half of the land-and-grain guarantee. **[PROPOSED — CR-031]** The board's worst enemy in 1919 is not the Red Army but the ataman at the junction, who holds its trains for payment and shoots whoever he calls a Bolshevik. Tokyo stops his subsidy, and that summer the Transbaikal Cossack circle meets at Chita with a Japanese diplomat in the hall. A frontline officer with his own St George's Cross moves that the Host recognise the Siberian government and put its regiments under the coalition. The ataman's officers walk out, the vote carries, and he leaves for Dairen on a Japanese pension. The Cossacks of the Onon and the Argun ride west to the Ob. Chinese ownership, Japanese running rights and common wartime administration keep the supply route usable without giving its territory to Japan.

The coalition fights for an independent Siberia, leaving Kolchak’s all-Russian ambition to overreach. **[PROPOSED]** As Omsk falls in late 1919 it takes its planned position behind the Ob at Novonikolayevsk. In the winter of 1919–1920 it defeats the Red Army’s attack across the frozen river. **[PROPOSED — CR-030]** The battle is prepared the summer before. While the line is free and the rivers are open, the depots behind the Ob are filled, barges come up the Amur and the Shilka to Sretensk, and the first Kara Sea convoy carries stores up the Ob and the Yenisei. In the battle the trains bring only what the depots cannot hold. The victory is settled; tactics and strengths remain proposed. A peasant land-and-grain guarantee supplies a political basis for holding the railway country. **[PROPOSED — CR-031]** The guarantee travels down the Lena. Yakutia keeps its own committees under Vasily Nikiforov, who founded the Union of Yakuts in 1906 and went to prison for it, and elects an assembly in 1919, and when Kolchak's officials are overthrown on the Okhotsk coast, at Anadyr and in Kamchatka that winter, relief ships and an amnesty answer them instead of a punitive column. Pacifica freezes the debt books of its own San Francisco traders in Chukotka until a commission can write them down. German clients in the west and Chinese cooperation prevent Moscow from concentrating its full strength in the east.

Moscow signs the Treaty of Harbin in 1921. Its name and year are settled; the detailed settlement remains **[PROPOSED]**. The Siberian Republic holds the country from the Ob to Lake Baikal, governed from Irkutsk with its Regional Duma at Tomsk. Japan already holds the Ussuri region, Vladivostok and Sakhalin; it gains a client sphere in Transbaikalia around Chita. **[PROPOSED — CR-031]** The sphere is the whole province from Baikal to the Argun: a council of Buryats, Cossacks and townsmen under a civilian Japanese Resident, with Japanese settlers, the Chernovsky coal and the Nerchinsk mines, and the junction of the line to Manchuria. Japan renounces the pan-Mongol schemes it once backed. The Cossack Host that turned out its ataman in 1919 sits in the council, and he stays in Dairen on his pension. **[APPROVED — CR-031]** The Siberian Republic extends to the Okhotsk coast west of Ola Bay and to Nikolayevsk. **[PROPOSED — CR-031]** It also holds Yakutia, Chukotka and the Arctic coast, with the islands Russia claimed in 1916 east of the Kara Sea. It is a federation more than a state: its constitution of 1922 makes Yakutia an autonomous Sakha land with its own assembly, language, communal land and a port at Okhotsk, at the same moment as Moscow's rival offer, and it governs the empty coast and Chukotka as a territory with a commissioner and a council of the districts and clans. The Ob becomes a shared river, and the straits into the Kara Sea stay open to merchant ships. **[APPROVED — CR-031]** Kamchatka and the Koryak coast as far as Ola Bay become a republic under Japanese protection. **[PROPOSED — CR-031]** Siberia never held them: every ship that reached Petropavlovsk sailed from Vladivostok, and in some summers the Japanese fishermen on the peninsula outnumbered everyone who lived there. When the Petropavlovsk council rose against Kolchak's governor it had no grain, and the relief ships that came were Japanese. Tokyo remembers Nikolayevsk, where partisans killed its garrison and residents in the spring of 1920 and burned the town. The army demanded the Amur coast in reprisal; the treaty gives satisfaction there instead, with a Siberian trial of the partisan leaders and an indemnity. The same massacre sends Kamchatka's frightened Russians to ask for Japanese protection, and Harbin recognises their republic and its Resident. On the Siberian coast a twenty-five-year convention gives Japanese boats the majority of the lots. Out of fairness, Pacifica insists that Siberian ships may pass the straits of a sea that is now Japanese on three sides. China secures the Amur and gains a sphere in Outer Mongolia and Tuva, with Siberian trade and settlement rights. **[PROPOSED — CR-031]** The treaty is signed in a Chinese city and gives China what Moscow had promised and would not have delivered. Russia's extraterritorial rights, its concessions and its share of the Boxer indemnity are renounced, the indemnity going to Chinese schools. Chinese ships may pass down the Amur to the sea, Mongolia returns to Chinese suzerainty under its own Mongol government, and the Manchurian railway returns to Chinese management, improved at the coalition's expense. Pacifica gains command of the standing alliance, its finance, the Lena gold concessions and the railway west of Chita. **[PROPOSED — CR-031]** It gives up the old San Francisco claims to Wrangel Island and the De Long islands, which lie west of its own 1867 Alaska line, and takes instead the Commander Islands, home of Aleuts moved there from the Aleutians a century before, with long leases for Arctic stations and a charter for the northern sea route. Thalassia repudiates a Kamchatka concession that Lenin signed with a Los Angeles engineer, and refuses to back a private colony on Wrangel Island.

Shared infrastructure, technical education and investment also lift the partners. Japan becomes the Pacific's second industrial power; the Gulf, Philippine crown and Siberia develop at different speeds and retain their distinct political statuses. **[PROPOSED — CR-031]** In 1927, the year of the Concord, Japan charters the Kamchatka Company, a national-policy company on the Manchurian model with a third of its shares in Pacifican hands. Its canneries and roads bring Japanese settlers to the peninsula, and it brings Karafuto's ways with them: bonded gangs in the work camps and Korean contract labourers, whose papers need Seoul's consent and Pacifica's. A year later the great gold strike comes not in Kamchatka but behind Ola Bay, on the Siberian side of the line, and the free miners on Pacifican dredges ship their gold out of Nagaevo within sight of the Company's quays. Thalassia signs the Korean papers and asks nothing about the camps. Returning graduates spread techniques as well as filling Pacifican posts. China, Korea and other trading regions gain more slowly through rail, power, schools and markets. Their growing demand feeds the champions, while dependence on Pacifican finance and standards gives the 1920s an anti-colonial undertow. Japanese national officers meanwhile organize Restoration circles against the imperial class.
