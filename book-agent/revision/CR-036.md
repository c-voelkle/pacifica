## Decisions

### The scholars

| ID | Decision | Label |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **James Joseph Sylvester** comes in 1855 instead of going to Woolwich. Historically he came second in Cambridge's mathematical tripos (1837) but could not graduate or compete for a fellowship, because he would not sign the Thirty-Nine Articles. After brief chairs at University College London (1838) and Virginia (1841) he spent a decade as an actuary and barrister, then taught at Woolwich (1855–70) and Johns Hopkins (1876–83). In Pacifica he takes a Polytechnic chair and founds mathematics and actuarial science; from 1866 he holds a chair at Yerba Buena College. | APPROVED (author) |
| D2 | **Robert Remak** comes in 1858 to the chair of physiology and pathology at the Methodists' medical college in San Francisco (CR-035; historically the University of the Pacific's medical department opened in 1858). Historically Prussian law barred him from teaching as a Jew, and the king refused his petition in 1843. In 1847 he became the first Jew to teach at Berlin, and he was made assistant professor only in 1859. His work: unmyelinated nerve fibres (1838), the cardiac ganglia (1844), the embryo's three layers, and cell division (1852–53, before Virchow's 1855 formula took the credit). His is the school that saves Victoria in 1866 (CR-035 C9). | APPROVED (author) |
| D3 | **Ferdinand Cohn** comes in 1858 to the same medical college, to study microscopic life. Historically, as a Jew, he waited a year at Breslau before he might take the oath and teach. He received the title of professor in 1857, became assistant professor in 1859 and full professor in 1872, and published his studies of bacteria in 1872–75. | APPROVED (author) |
| D4 | **Robert Koch** comes in 1872. He is an unknown country doctor in Posen, back from the Franco-Prussian War, and answers the Republic's advertisement in German medical journals for district physicians in the mining country. Cohn, who reviews the applicants, sees his microscope work and has the Polytechnic give him a laboratory at Carquinez. Historically Cohn launched him in 1876, when Koch brought his anthrax work to Breslau and Cohn published it. In Pacifica:<br>• the anthrax life cycle (1876);<br>• the tuberculosis bacillus (1882);<br>• the cholera bacillus, found in Manila in the autumn of 1882, not in Egypt and India in 1883–84. | APPROVED (author); the advertisement and Cohn's review PROPOSED |
| D5 | **Paul Ehrlich**, born in Silesia in 1854, is a medical student when Koch's anthrax paper of 1876 comes from Carquinez. He comes after his doctorate in 1878 to Koch's laboratory, where he improves the stain for the tuberculosis bacillus (historically he did this in Berlin in 1882). In Pacifica: his staining methods, the side-chain theory (1897) and the "magic bullet", Salvarsan (1909), with **Hata Sahachirō**. | APPROVED (author: "if it's believable"). Historically Cohn taught him at Breslau and he met Koch there in 1876; in this timeline both are in Pacifica, so the pull is Koch's work and a chair without a test. No source shows antisemitism blocking his career. Arrival in 1878 and Hata PROPOSED |
| D6 | **Kitasato Shibasaburō** comes to Koch's laboratory in Pacifica (historically Berlin, 1885–92): the tetanus culture, and antitoxin work. Through him and Hata, Meiji Japan models its medicine on Pacifica's, not Germany's. | PROPOSED |
| D7 | **Albert Michelson**, born in 1852 to a Jewish family in Prussian Poland, comes to Murphys, a Calaveras gold camp, in 1855. He later lives at Virginia City and goes to school in San Francisco. He sits the Polytechnic's examination at fourteen (CR-034 A16), so he needs no appeal to a president. In Pacifica: the speed of light at Yerba Buena College (first trial 1878, main result 1879) and the ether experiment (1887). His partner is the chemist **Edward Morley**, as historically, but here Morley comes west too. Born in New Jersey in 1838 and trained for the Congregational ministry, he historically took a chair at Western Reserve College in Ohio in 1868–69 and worked there in obscurity, measuring the oxygen–hydrogen ratio. In Pacifica he takes a chemistry chair at Yerba Buena College in 1868 for the laboratory and the pay, as many of his generation of American graduates do. | APPROVED (author) |

### Discoveries

| ID | Decision | Label |
|---|---|---|
| D8 | **Indium (1863).** In the middle of the war Genth's spectroscope finds the blue-line element in zinc blende from a California mine, and Carquinez names it. Washoe is American until the war (§8), so the ore cannot come from there. Historically Reich and Richter found it at Freiberg in 1863. Here Freiberg finds it second. | APPROVED (author) |
| D9 | **Element 31, californium (1873).** Carquinez finds Mendeleev's predicted eka-aluminium (predicted 1871; one source says 1870) in zinc blende from the deep Comstock, sent down by the Sutro School's assayers, and names it californium for the founding state. Historically gallium was found in blende from Pierrefitte in the Pyrenees, and blendes are its usual source. The choice gives the interior a share in the find and ties it to Washoe, Pacifican since 1865. It confirms the periodic table two years before Lecoq de Boisbaudran's gallium (1875) would have. In this timeline element 98 will need another name in the twentieth century. | APPROVED (author) |
| D10 | **The Nobel decade:** Koch (1905), Michelson (1907) and Ehrlich (1908) win as Pacificans, on their historical dates. Historically Michelson's 1907 prize was the first Nobel in science for an American; here the first in the Americas is Koch's, for Pacifica. | PROPOSED |

### §8, after the paragraph ending "…**[OPEN]** Facilities, budgets, suppliers, armament, dimensions and construction schedule.", following CR-034's and CR-035's paragraphs there (D1–D3, D7)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-036]** The offer of a chair with citizenship reaches men Europe will not let graduate or advance. James Joseph Sylvester came second in Cambridge's mathematics examination and could not take his degree, because he would not sign the Church of England's articles. He has spent ten years as an actuary when, in 1855, he takes a Polytechnic chair instead of a post at Woolwich. Robert Remak showed that the embryo grows from three layers and that cells arise by dividing. He was the first Jew allowed to teach at Berlin, but never as its professor. In 1858 he takes the chair of physiology and pathology at the Methodists' new medical college in San Francisco. Ferdinand Cohn, kept waiting a year at Breslau before he might take the oath to teach, comes to the same college that year to study the smallest living things. At the gold camp at Murphys a Prussian Jewish merchant's son, Albert Michelson, is learning to read.

### §11, after the paragraph ending "…She asks for the name, and Elias refuses it." (D8)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-036]** That year Genth's spectroscope finds a bright blue line in zinc ore from a California mine that belongs to no known element. Carquinez names the metal indium for its color. Freiberg, testing its own ores, finds it second.

### §15, after the paragraph ending "…Reforms do not erase coercion, and the land board becomes a landlord too." (D4, D16)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-036]** The physicians who read the cholera as a matter of water are led by Robert Koch. In 1872 he was an unknown country doctor in Posen who answered the Republic's call for district physicians in the mining country. Cohn saw his microscope work and had the Polytechnic give him a laboratory at Carquinez. There he traced the life of anthrax in 1876, and in the spring of 1882 he found the bacillus of tuberculosis. That autumn in Manila his team finds the comma-shaped bacillus of cholera in the water and shows how it travels. The tables of deaths by town, before and after the pipes, go round the world.

### §15, after the paragraph ending "…No census target or legal proclamation proves that equal civic standing has become daily experience." (D12–D14)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-036]** Since the war the interior has built schools of its own. The Colegio de Santa Fe, founded in 1864, trains the surveyors of the treaty lines and the engineers of the acequias, and keeps an observatory in the high, clear air. When the total eclipse of 29 July 1878 crosses the mountains, its spectroscopists set up Pacifica's station at a railway siding in the Great Divide Basin, a few miles from the Union's astronomers across the frontier. Deseret's university, revived after accession, measures how much water an acre needs and teaches the arid world to build canals. At Virginia City the Sutro School, founded with the tunnel in 1869, studies the heat of the deep Comstock and the cold that lets men work in it.

### §19, after the paragraph ending "…turn the examination state's learning into firms, equipment and careers.", following CR-034's paragraph there (D5–D7, D9, D11)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-036]** Its learning is not only applied. In 1873 Carquinez finds the metal Mendeleev had predicted beneath aluminium, in zinc blende the Sutro School sends down from the deep Comstock, and calls it californium, and the periodic table is proved right on a Pacifican specimen. Yerba Buena College, founded in 1866 by San Francisco's merchant and banking houses of every faith, gives Sylvester his mathematics and, from 1866, Cohn his laboratory. In 1868 it brings the chemist Edward Morley from New Jersey. In 1878 the Murphys boy Michelson begins there to measure the speed of light. Paul Ehrlich, who read Koch's anthrax paper as a student in Silesia, comes to his laboratory that year and stains the cells no one could see. From 1885 Kitasato Shibasaburō works in the same laboratory, and Japanese medicine begins to learn from Pacifica. In 1887 Michelson and Morley's interferometer finds no ether wind.

### §25, after the paragraph ending "…Returning graduates also carry knowledge into the partners' own economies." (D20, D22)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-036]** The examined come from every exile and every ambition. Forty-Eighters and Polish engineers of 1863 came early. German Catholic scholars and Jesuits come out of Bismarck's struggle with the Church, and Jews shut out of Russia's universities by the quota of 1887. Armenians come after the massacres of 1894–96 and Chinese reformers after 1898. Scandinavian, Scottish, Japanese and American graduates come simply for better laboratories and pay. They sit the same examinations, and the Realm admits them one examinee at a time.

### §25, after the paragraph ending "…These routes sustain the industrial economy while leaving a class of workers outside citizenship." (D23)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-036]** Victoria's decisive test of these loyalties comes in 1891. Famine spreads along the Volga in the year Moscow expels its Jews. San Francisco's bankers ask her to send the Tsar nothing, and the nativist papers dare her to prove that the bankers do not govern. She sends grain on Pacifica's terms. Pacifican agents distribute it to the hungry of every faith, and the weekly photographs every station. The bankers get no say and the nativists no gesture. Both complain, and neither leaves.

### §26, after the paragraph ending "…and containment begins to collide with their portfolios: on Wall Street the \"Pacifican interest\" argues for peace." (D10, D17)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-036]** Between 1905 and 1909 the Nobel committees confirm what Roosevelt's programme fears. Koch wins in 1905, Michelson in 1907 and Ehrlich in 1908, all three as Pacificans. In 1909 Ehrlich and Hata Sahachirō find the arsenical that cures syphilis. The Union's new laboratories are measured against Pacifica's colleges.
