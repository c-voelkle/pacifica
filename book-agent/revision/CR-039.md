## Open choices for the author

### P1 — Puʻuloa in 1924 (finding 1)

**From and to.** The lessor is still the Kingdom of Hawaii, whose legislature the 1914 Act restored. The lessee is the Realm, which succeeded to the Confederation's rights in 1887. Since 1915 Julian wears both crowns, so the lease of 1924 is the first Puʻuloa grant made between two crowns of one sovereign. The Kuhina Nui signs for Hawaii on the Mōʻī's instructions, the Chancellor for the Realm, and a dispute between them goes to the council of the crowns, where Hawaii has a vote.

**Suggestion.** In 1924 the old lease is not renewed but replaced by a new Puʻuloa Convention, which Hawaii's restored legislature ratifies freely, the vote that 1874 never fairly had. The 1876 judgment stays unreversed, and the loyalists campaign to let the lease lapse. A shorter term (twenty-five years, to 1949) keeps Hawaii's leverage. One clause looks ahead: the Realm may open the harbour to the common navy of any future union of fleets only with Hawaii's consent. That gives the Hawaiian delegation's declaration for the Concord in 1927 real weight, because Pearl Harbor is the common fleet's base and the Japanese naval party's home. No rent is stated (AGENTS.md: do not infer a rent amount).

Draft (§33, or a short beat in §29–§33): **[PROPOSED — CR-039]** In 1924 the fifty-year lease of Puʻuloa runs out. Julian's Hawaiian legislature, sitting again since 1914, votes a new Puʻuloa Convention for twenty-five years over the loyalists' campaign to let the harbour revert. The Kuhina Nui signs for the Mōʻī and the Chancellor for the Realm; the same man is sovereign on both sides of the table. The new convention lets the Realm open the harbour to a common navy only with Hawaii's consent.

### P2 — China's "defense treaty" (finding 3)

Canon has China recover the Inspectorate and customs in 1928–1929 "under the defense treaty", and no such treaty appears anywhere else.

**Suggestion: the Treaty of Nanjing, 1928.** After Jinan, Chiang's reunified government signs a treaty of guarantee with the Concord, which holds the common foreign policy from 1927. The Concord guarantees China's frontiers, Manchuria included; China guarantees Japan's railway transit and joint management of the line. As the price, the Inspectorate and the customs return to Chinese command in 1928–1929, while the Formosan administration stays as credit security. The name answers the Treaty of Nanking of 1842, which first put China's customs under foreign hands.

The treaty also gives §35 its legal ground. Japan's home army enters common command only for a Concord-declared war, so an army plot to seize Manchuria now attacks a state the Concord has guaranteed. Julian's threat in 1931 enforces a treaty Japan's own government signed through the Concord.

Alternative: a bilateral Sino-Pacifican treaty. It would have to be signed before 1927, with Sun's Canton government, because foreign affairs belong to the Concord afterwards.

Draft (§33, replacing "under the defense treaty"): **[PROPOSED — CR-039]** In 1928, after Jinan, the Nationalist government signs the Treaty of Nanjing with the Concord. The Concord guarantees China's frontiers, Manchuria included, and China guarantees the railway transit and its joint management. Under the treaty China recovers the Inspectorate and customs in 1928–1929.

### P3 — Ka Lōkahi (finding 4): a name used twice

Canon gives the name to two bodies:
- **§27 (CR-029, APPROVED):** Ka Lōkahi is the council of the crowns of 1914, in which the Realm, Hawaii and the Philippines have one vote each over the succession, "the agreed matters renewed every ten years" and disputes between the crowns. AGENTS.md calls it distinct from the Hayama Council and the Concord's five-year term. §33 has the Hawaiian and Philippine delegations "to Ka Lōkahi" declare for the Concord in 1927, and Ka Lōkahi take the Pacific islands into its trust.
- **§33 (base text):** ka Lōkahi is the Concord's Hawaiian name, beside Taiheiyō Kyōyaku and la Concordia del Pacífico.

Your answer takes the second reading. The Concord, though, reviews its agreed matters at every five-year renewal (1932, 1937), not every ten years. So one of the two bodies needs another name before the base year can be set:
- **(a)** Ka Lōkahi stays the Concord's Hawaiian name, and the council of the crowns is renamed, for example *ka ʻAha o nā Kalaunu* ("the council of the crowns"; to be checked with a Hawaiian speaker). If its agreed matters start in 1927, the reason could be that the three crowns first fix them when they enter the Concord as one party; they would be renewed in 1937.
- **(b)** The council keeps Ka Lōkahi, as approved on 22 October, and the Concord gets another Hawaiian name, for example *ke Kuʻikahi Pākīpika* ("the Pacific treaty"). The council's ten-year matters would then count from 1927, with the first renewal in 1937.

Nothing is changed until you choose.

### P4 — The Buchanan–Vallejo and Carson conventions (finding 7)

Two problems: Carson renews "the ports, the Sausalito storehouse" as expired articles, though in 1848 only the commercial articles have a twelve-year term; and nothing ends either convention, although Pacifica retakes Washoe in 1863–1864.

**Suggestion, in two edits (both in PROPOSED CR-028 text):**
- **§4:** the naval articles share the commercial articles' term: "…civil guarantees for American residents, the commercial and naval articles for twelve years." Carson's list then reads correctly.
- **§12, the 1865 peace:** **[PROPOSED — CR-039]** The peace declares the conventions of 1848 and 1860 ended by the war. The Western line replaces the renunciation of the Basin and the recognition of Washoe; American owners in Nevada keep the property guarantees Carson gave them.

War ending the treaties between belligerents, with the peace deciding what survives, is the usual nineteenth-century practice, and it matches the storehouse sealed in 1861.

### P5 — Querétaro and the Confederation (finding 8)

Querétaro pledges California "never to cede itself or its territory to any power", and California confederates with Hawaii in 1856.

**Suggestion.** Narrow the pledge to what Mexico feared, annexation by the United States: "…and pledges never to join the United States or cede any of its territory to a foreign power." Vallejo's refusal of an accession clause in 1848 still cites Querétaro and reads better for it. A confederation with Hawaii cedes no territory: California keeps its constitution as a member state. If a beat is wanted in 1856, Mexico's minister protests, Elias answers that California cedes nothing but confederates, and a Mexico in the Reform War does not press the point.

Alternative: keep the wording and add only the 1856 exchange, accepting that California stretches its promise.

### P6 — Where the 1905 peace is signed (finding 12, Korea)

**Suggestion: Vladivostok,** as CR-018 recommended (OPEN-018-20). Japanese since 1899, it lets the peace be signed without a mediator and on Japanese ground, and Witte signs away Russia's Pacific in the port Russia lost. Harbin would put the signing on Chinese soil and bring China to the table; Thalassia would make Pacifica the mediator Victoria advised Japan to refuse; Tokyo would be a humiliation Witte would not accept.
