## Stage 2 — Storyline text (draft)

### New §28, the tour and the three settlements (1908–1913)

> ## 28. The tour and the peace of the capitals (1908–1913)
>
> **[PROPOSED — CR-040]** In the spring of 1908 Julian sails for Europe with ministers, bankers, engineers and interpreters, leaving Chiyo in the Pacific. He sees Asquith, Grey and Haldane in London, Bülow and then Bethmann Hollweg in Berlin with Tirpitz and Ballin, Clemenceau in Paris, Franz Joseph, Franz Ferdinand and Aehrenthal in Vienna, and Nicholas II, Stolypin and Kokovtsov in St Petersburg. He wants a Europe that pays its debts and buys Pacifica's oil, and he believes in 1908 that war can be made too expensive to start. Britain and Germany each undertake, separately, to give no state-backed money, bases, intelligence or arms to a Union attempt to overturn the North American settlement.
>
> When Austria annexes Bosnia that October, he helps build the settlement of 1909: Habsburg sovereignty with a Bosnian Diet, Serbian trade through Salonica, and a standing commission that can order an inquiry and a standstill before either side uses force. Russia is given a public part instead of a humiliation. At Agadir in 1911 Germany takes shipping and engineering contracts and a public result in place of most of the territory it demanded. In 1912–1913 the same methods help close the two Balkan wars. Each crisis ends in an agreement that works, and by 1913 Julian believes the method works.
>
> At home his success abroad buys him nothing with the counties. *[Senate quarrel of §29, dated 1910–1911.]*

### §28 → §29, July 1914

Replace the opening of the current §28 ("Europe's war begins in August 1914.") with:

> **[PROPOSED — CR-040]** On 28 June 1914 Franz Ferdinand, the man in Vienna most opposed to war with Serbia, is killed at Sarajevo with his wife. The next day Pacifica's ministers invoke the commission of 1909. Belgrade accepts an inquiry; Vienna does not answer. Conrad and Berchtold argue that the Pacifican procedures gave the conspirators five years. In Berlin the staff calculates that a Russia which lost its Pacific in 1899 and kept its fleets in 1905, and has put its railways and new army into Europe, can still be beaten in 1914 and not in 1917. On 5 July the Emperor gives Vienna Germany's unconditional support, and Julian's envoy is heard at Potsdam and set aside. At the Vienna council two days later Tisza argues alone for demands Serbia could accept; he gives way on the 14th. Serbia's answer to the ultimatum accepts almost everything except Austrian police on its soil, and Julian offers the commission for that one point and Pacifica's guarantee of Serbian compliance, with credit to Vienna behind it. Nobody takes it up. Austria declares war on 28 July, and within a week Russia, Germany, France and Britain are at war.
>
> Julian made every earlier crisis cheaper to settle than to fight. In July the men who decided had concluded that war now was cheaper than war later, and no offer of his could change that. He concludes that offering men something to lose does not stop them, and only taking something away does. Victoria concludes that he trusted ledgers too much and people too little.
>
> Europe's war begins in August 1914. *[current §28 continues]*
