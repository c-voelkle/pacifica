## Author direction

> For the next big change, I want to revisit CR-032. And in my opinion, narratively, I want like a combination of A and D, right? So it starts out with Julian's earlier coronation, his Europe tour, and trying to prevent war in Europe. The European powers, in their stubbornness, still end up dragging everyone in the world into World War I, which leads to the Bolshevik Revolution, the Red Terror, and Julian and his Pacifica coalition having to invade and ultimately arrest Lenin, Trotsky, and Stalin. Do you think we could write up a revised version of CR-032 and start to work out a new CR from that general shape of events?

The general shape is the author's. The mechanisms below are proposals.

### New §28, the tour (1908–1913)

> ## 28. The tour and the peace of the capitals (1908–1913)
>
> **[PROPOSED — CR-040]** In the spring of 1908 Julian sails for Europe with ministers, bankers, engineers and interpreters, leaving Chiyo in the Pacific. He sees Asquith, Grey and Haldane in London; Bülow and then Bethmann Hollweg in Berlin, with Tirpitz and Ballin; Clemenceau and then Briand in Paris; Franz Joseph, Franz Ferdinand and Aehrenthal in Vienna; and Nicholas II, Stolypin and Kokovtsov in St Petersburg. He wants a Europe that pays its debts and buys Pacifica's oil, and in 1908 he believes war can be made too expensive to start. Britain and Germany each undertake, separately, to give no state-backed money, bases, intelligence or arms to a Union attempt to overturn the North American settlement. Ballin becomes a friend.
>
> When Austria annexes Bosnia that October, the crisis turns on a bargain Aehrenthal and Izvolsky made at Buchlau and remember differently. Julian, a few months after reading the Donner file, knows what a buried bargain does to the people who keep it, and he insists on putting terms in writing. The settlement of 1909 gives Habsburg sovereignty with a Bosnian Diet, Serbian trade through Ottoman Salonica with the Porte's consent, and a standing commission that can order an inquiry and a standstill before either side uses force. Russia is given a public part instead of a humiliation. At Agadir in 1911 Germany takes shipping and engineering contracts and a public result in place of most of the territory it demanded. In 1912–1913 the same methods help close the two Balkan wars. Each crisis ends in an agreement that works, and by 1913 Julian believes the method works.
>
> *[Senate quarrel from §29, dated 1910–1911: success abroad buys him nothing with the counties.]*

### July 1914

Replace the opening of the current §28 ("Europe's war begins in August 1914.") with:

> **[PROPOSED — CR-040]** On 28 June 1914 Franz Ferdinand, the man in Vienna most opposed to war with Serbia, is killed at Sarajevo with his wife. Thalassia hears it the same day, half a day behind Europe's clock, and Julian's ministers invoke the commission of 1909. Belgrade accepts an inquiry; Vienna does not answer. Conrad and Berchtold argue that the Pacifican procedures gave the conspirators five years. In Berlin the staff calculates that a Russia which lost its Pacific in 1899, kept its fleets in 1905 and has put its railways and new army into Europe can still be beaten in 1914 and not in 1917.
>
> Victoria, with her own cables to her old correspondents, tells her son to put the oil on the table: Germany imports it, Austria's fleet burns it, and a plain word to Berlin that it stops if Vienna refuses the inquiry is the one argument the staffs will hear. Julian will not threaten Ballin's Germany over a procedure that has worked three times; he sends an envoy with offers. On 5 July the Emperor gives Vienna Germany's unconditional support, and the envoy is heard at Potsdam and set aside. At the Vienna council two days later Tisza argues alone for demands Serbia could accept, using Pacifica's terms, and loses; he gives way on the 14th. Serbia's answer to the ultimatum accepts almost everything except the participation of Austrian officials in an inquiry on its soil. Julian offers the commission for that one point and Pacifica's guarantee of Serbian compliance, and only now says the oil will stop. By then the cables reach men already mobilising. Austria declares war on 28 July, and within a week Russia, Germany, France and Britain are at war.
>
> He made every earlier crisis cheaper to settle than to fight. In July the men who decided had concluded that war now was cheaper than war later, and the one thing that might have changed their sum he offered too late. He concludes that offering men something to lose does not stop them, and only taking something away does. Victoria concludes that he built a peace on one man and a procedure, and would not threaten his friends.
>
> Europe's war begins in August 1914. *[current §28 continues]*
