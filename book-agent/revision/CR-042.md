# CR-042 — Huludao: a door of Pacifica's own at the end of the Chinese line

**Status:** APPROVED and integrated, 30 October 2026 (decisions H1–H5; connective detail PROPOSED).
**Written:** 30 October 2026.
**Baseline:** `master_storyline.md` after PR #50 (CR-040 Stages 3–4 and CR-041).
**Basis:** Napoleon's advice in CR-041 Appendix B (non-canonical): "buy from Peking a port of your own at the end of the Chinese line … pay in advance and in public, so that the day Tokyo's ambitions and yours separate, you are not standing in a Japanese doorway." Since 1899 every ton Pacifica sends into Siberia passes through Japanese Urajio, because Japan holds the terminal of the Chinese line (§25).
**Labels:** as in CR-040. *hist.* marks a historical anchor.

The historical template for H3 (*hist.*): Xu Shichang, Viceroy of the Three Eastern Provinces, founded the Huludao harbour project in 1908 as an alternative to the overloaded ports of Qinhuangdao and Yingkou. Construction began in 1910 and stopped with the 1911 revolution. Zhang Xueliang restarted it in 1930, and the Mukden Incident stopped it again. In December 1909 Secretary Knox proposed an Anglo-American loan for a Jinzhou–Aigun railway and the neutralisation of the Manchurian lines. Japan and Russia rejected it in 1910 (**VERIFY** the exact form). In this timeline Pacifica does what the Americans could not.

## Storyline text

**§25, the settlement of 1898–99.** At the end of the paragraph beginning "When Russia demands Port Arthur or a Korean harbor after Germany's Kiaochow seizure…", after "…remain Russian until 1921.", add:

> <!-- CR-042: Dalian stays Chinese; the Liaodong Gulf pledge and the Huludao option --> **[APPROVED — CR-042]** The Liaodong peninsula stays Chinese. Russia does not get Port Arthur, and Japan, which has fought to keep Russia out of it, does not take Port Arthur or Dalian for itself. Victoria negotiates the terms with the Empress Dowager and holds Tokyo to the armistice of 1894, which gave Japan no mainland territory. **[PROPOSED — CR-042]** Cixi's return to her friend is small and written down. China pledges that no part of the coast of the Liaodong Gulf will be leased or ceded to any power, and gives Pacifica first refusal on a harbour at Huludao, a sheltered bay on the gulf's western shore below Jinzhou, if a harbour is ever built there with foreign capital. In the year Germany takes Kiaochow, Pacifica takes nothing, and Victoria keeps the paper.

**§27, before "The Qing falls in 1911–1912."** Insert:

> <!-- CR-042: the Huludao Harbour Trust --> **[APPROVED — CR-042]** In 1909 Julian takes up his mother's option. **[PROPOSED — CR-042]** Xu Shichang, Viceroy of the Three Eastern Provinces, wants a Manchurian port that no foreign power holds, and Peking has known for ten years who will pay for it. The Huludao Harbour Trust is chartered under a Qing commission on the Keelung pattern: a Pacifican director, Pacifican engineers, bonds placed in San Francisco, the harbour dues pledged to the bondholders until the bonds are paid, and Chinese sovereignty, customs and police. It is offered as commerce, an outlet for Manchurian soya beans and coal, and it is commerce. Works begin in 1910 and go on through the revolution, and the Republic confirms the commission as it continues the Formosan one. **[APPROVED — CR-042]** By 1912 a Chinese line runs from Harbin through Changchun to Mukden and joins the line to Peking, and Huludao is a day's run from Mukden. **[PROPOSED — CR-042]** The harbour opens to shipping in 1914. Who financed the last stretch, from Mukden past Jinzhou to the gulf, and so who holds a lien on it, is **[OPEN]**.
