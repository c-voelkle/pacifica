**Why Bryan (*hist.*):** as Wilson's Secretary of State he made cooling-off treaties with some thirty states in 1913–14. In August 1914 he called loans to belligerents "the worst of all contrabands". He resigned in June 1915 rather than sign a second Lusitania note that leaned hard on Berlin. He is the American the Central Powers would choose. He is also a populist enemy of Wall Street sent to protect Wall Street's German and Austrian loan book, and in this timeline the Union's tilt to Berlin is the ledger's, not Wilson's. He died in 1925.

**Article 8 (*hist.*, wording verified by the author on 30 October 2026):**

> The Contracting Powers are agreed in recommending the application, when circumstances allow, of special mediation in the following form: In case of a serious difference endangering peace, the States at variance choose respectively a Power, to which they intrust the mission of entering into direct communication with the Power chosen on the other side, with the object of preventing the rupture of pacific relations. For the period of this mandate, the term of which, unless otherwise stipulated, cannot exceed thirty days, the States in dispute cease from all direct communication on the subject of the dispute, which is regarded as referred exclusively to the mediating Powers, which must use their best efforts to settle it. In case of a definite rupture of pacific relations, these Powers are charged with the joint task of taking advantage of any opportunity to restore peace.

The last sentence carries the Hague mediation: the rupture of 1914 is long past, and the two mediating powers take up "the joint task of taking advantage of any opportunity to restore peace". Two clauses give the conference texture. The belligerents must stipulate away the thirty-day term, since the mediation runs from December to June. And while the mandate lasts, they "cease from all direct communication on the subject of the dispute", so Clemenceau cannot talk to the Germans except through Kaʻiulani. Applying the article to a war in progress, with one mediator a belligerent at sea, is irregular. France will say so: "a belligerent who mediates is a belligerent who dictates".
