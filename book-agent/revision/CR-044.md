**Why the surveyor (C1).** He is already in canon as Vance's father, the railway surveyor who "avoids public command". Making him a hostage gives Vance's caution and his fear of "failing remembered dead" a living stake from 1918 to 1920. Kiyoshi then dies to bring Vance's father out alive, and in 1921 Vance gasses prisoners with Kuroda's men among them. The existing sentence "Kiyoshi died to bring hostages out alive, and Vance gasses prisoners" becomes the worst thing in the book. **Identity rule:** this is not the retired CT-MARCUS-II record. AGENTS.md forbids silently assigning the private father that role. He gets a new stable ID (proposed `CT-VANCE-SURVEYOR`), with the retired record left as it is.

**Omsk, history and divergence (C2–C3).** Historically:
- the coup's trigger was a banquet on the night of 17 November 1918 for General Janin, at which officers demanded "God Save the Tsar";
- Volkov arrested the Directory's left wing before he could be arrested himself;
- not one unit came out for the Directory.

Knox headed the British military mission, and Ward's 25th Middlesex was at Omsk. How far the British were party to the coup is debated: the "British conspiracy" version rests largely on the French mission's testimony, including Janin's (Gagkuev, *Istoricheskii vestnik* 47, 2024). Canon makes Knox's foreknowledge fact. Mark it as a divergence.

**§31, the Omsk night (N2 written).** Replace the paragraph beginning "**[APPROVED — CR-041]** On the night of 18 November 1918 Colonel Volkov's Siberian Cossack officers…" up to "Omsk never falls, to anyone." with:

> **[APPROVED — CR-041]** On the night of 18 November 1918 Colonel Volkov's Siberian Cossack officers arrest the Socialist-Revolutionary members of the Directory at Omsk and disarm its guard, and by morning the thing has failed. **[PROPOSED — CR-044]** The evening before, the garrison's officers had dined General Janin, the French commander newly arrived, and called for "God Save the Tsar"; the Directory ordered the singers arrested, and Volkov struck first. General Knox, at the head of the British military mission, has known for days what was coming and has told the Directory nothing. Colonel Ward's battalion of the Middlesex Regiment stands to in its barracks and does nothing, which is all Volkov needs from it. Volkov has not counted the railway. **[APPROVED — CR-041]** The railway brigade holds Omsk station and the telegraph. **[PROPOSED — CR-044]** Every wire out of Omsk runs through its keys, so Knox's cipher to London waits on a Pacifican desk while Thalassia's goes first. The Middlesex, who came west on Pacifican trains and eat from Pacifican depots, are offered a train east for their safety. The Legion's Czechs at the station, whose National Council wants the Directory and whose liaison officers carry Pacifican orders, will not answer Volkov's call. The Japanese line-guard battalion on the Irtysh bridge answers to the railway board. Janin, with France's Russian bonds and the Legion to think of, sends no one. **[APPROVED — CR-041]** The Reserve Bank's paymaster announces that no Siberian ministry is funded until the Directory sits, and Boldyrev comes back from Ufa on a Pacifican train. **[PROPOSED — CR-044]** Before the Council of Ministers meets, Vance has an hour with Kolchak. He does not threaten him; he shows him the timetable: no train for a Supreme Ruler east of Omsk or west of it, and a ship for an admiral wherever he likes. **[APPROVED — CR-041]** When the Council offers Admiral Kolchak, the Directory's war minister, the title of Supreme Ruler, he declines it in front of them. Volkov's officers are disarmed. Ivan Mikhailov, the finance minister behind them, is dismissed and leaves for Harbin. Omsk never falls, to anyone. **[PROPOSED — CR-044]** In Volkov's papers the brigade finds what Knox knew and when, and the Office files them without a word: the first folder of Pacifica's British file. London protests through its ambassador at Thalassia the next week and is answered with the ledger of who pays the Siberian army. At the War Office from January, Churchill reads Knox's account of the night. Ward's book of 1920 tells the British public that Pacifica bought Siberia out from under them.
