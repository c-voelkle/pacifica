## History

- **The three districts** (the Ottoman *elviye-i selâse*): Kars, Ardahan and Batum, taken by Russia in 1878 (*hist.*). Brest-Litovsk returned them to the Ottomans in March 1918, subject to a vote. The Ottomans held one under occupation in July 1918 and expelled more than 100,000 Armenians from the Kars province (*hist.*).
- **The National Pact** of 1920 claimed the three districts on the strength of the 1918 vote and accepted a new plebiscite if one proved necessary (*hist.*).
- **1919–1921.** The Ottomans withdrew in January 1919. After the short-lived South-West Caucasus Republic, Armenia and the British took Kars in April 1919; Georgia held Ardahan; Britain held Batum until July 1920. Karabekir's army took Kars on 30 October 1920, with Soviet gold and Moscow's agreement to partition Armenia. The Treaty of Alexandropol (December 1920), the Treaty of Moscow (16 March 1921) and the Treaty of Kars (13 October 1921) gave Kars, Ardahan and the Surmalu district (Iğdır and Mount Ararat) to Turkey, and Batum to Georgia, with autonomy for its Ajarian Muslims and free transit for Turkish goods (*hist.*).
- **The people** (1897 census, by mother tongue). Below, "Christian" groups Armenian, Greek, Russian, Ukrainian and Polish speakers, and "Muslim" groups Turkish, Kurdish, Karapapakh, Turkmen and Tatar speakers.

| District | 1897 | Christian | Muslim | Largest group |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kars | 134,142 | about 62% | about 34% | Armenian 35% |
| Kağızman | 59,230 | about 57% | about 41% | Armenian 37% |
| Olti | 31,519 | about 22% | about 77% | Turkish 63% |
| Ardahan | 65,763 | about 18% | about 80% | Turkish 43% |

Across the whole province Muslims are a bare majority (about 51% to 47%). District by district, it splits two and two. The 1916 official yearbook (*Kavkazskii kalendar'*, 1917) shows the Armenian share rising further in Kars and Kağızman. Its "Roma" figures look like a misreading of the source in the secondary tables and should not be used.
