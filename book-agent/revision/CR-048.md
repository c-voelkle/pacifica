# CR-048 — The rump Confederacy and the long emergency (1900–1930)

**Status:** PROPOSED, 31 October 2026. A1–A6 are the author's direction from the brainstorming session and stand APPROVED; R1–R17 await decision.
**Written:** 31 October 2026.
**Baseline:** `master_storyline.md` at `main` 9aa40f7 (CR-044 integration), with CR-045–CR-047 as integrated. CR-048 touches §25 (after the Gulf paragraphs), §26 (the emulation programme) and the 1932 summary, none of which CR-045–CR-047 change.
**Settles:** what becomes of South Carolina, Georgia, Alabama, Mississippi and Florida after 1900; Pacifica's conduct toward them; how their crisis reaches the Union and its universities.
**Does not settle:** the Union's re-admission terms for Virginia and North Carolina (R9, OPEN), the Garvey/London thread (R17, OPEN), any Anglo-Pacifican item from the planning note.
**Labels:** as in CR-040. *hist.* marks a historical anchor.

## Author direction

> For the next arc I want to do some brainstorming on the home front and especially the confederate rump state. … What is its economy like? What influence do its powerful neighbors have on it? What is life like there? I would imagine that Pacifica gave it the same terms as FRG to join them, but they have no chance of meeting them. Meanwhile the Union is egging on a black revolutionary movement. (31 October 2026)

> For the plot I think it comes early and it is mostly the Union's problem. Pacifica cynically plays both sides while protecting its assets and maintaining plausible deniability. Its focus for the decade is London (and a major science and engineering race against Europe and the Ivies). Conspiracy theorists in the Union blame Pacifica … but their voices go largely unheard and conclusive evidence remains scarce. (31 October 2026)

> Running guns to revolutionaries or planters is not Pacifica's style - too crass. Pacifica doesn't want another war in America. The revolution also doesn't ever actually need to happen. It just has to cause enough trouble for the Union that scientists don't want to live there and (continue to) prefer to settle in Pacifica. (31 October 2026)

> A thin layer of money, pure omission plus honest reporting on the issues that is transparent, objective, focused, and loud - I think this is all it takes to tip the scales. The movement of course reverberates across the black community irrespective of borders; the Union too is made to reckon with its own racism by an increasingly vocal, assertive black minority which it can only appease or lash out at in turn in a chaotic ratcheting fashion. (31 October 2026)

## History

- **The five states in 1900** (*hist.*, US census; the alternate figures differ, see R1):

| State | Population | Black | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| South Carolina | 1,340,316 | 782,321 | 58.4% |
| Georgia | 2,216,331 | 1,034,813 | 46.7% |
| Alabama | 1,828,697 | 827,307 | 45.2% |
| Mississippi | 1,551,270 | 907,630 | 58.5% |
| Florida | 528,542 | 230,730 | 43.7% |
| **Total** | **7,465,156** | **3,782,801** | **50.7%** |

  The Mississippi Delta, the Alabama Black Belt and the Lowcountry ran far higher, often 70–80%.
- **Cotton.** The boll weevil crossed the Mississippi about 1907, reached Alabama about 1909, Georgia 1915 and South Carolina 1917; cotton prices collapsed in 1920 (*hist.*).
- **Convict leasing.** Alabama leased convicts to the Birmingham coal mines until 1928 (*hist.*).
- **Black leadership.** Booker T. Washington spoke at Atlanta in 1895 and ran Tuskegee until his death in 1915; Carnegie gave Tuskegee $600,000 in 1903; Rosenwald's school programme began in 1912 (*hist.*). W. E. B. Du Bois taught at Atlanta University 1897–1910 and ran the Atlanta Studies, the first sustained statistical studies of Black life; his data charts were shown at the Paris Exposition of 1900; he founded the Niagara Movement at Fort Erie in 1905, helped found the NAACP in 1909 and edited *The Crisis* from 1910 (*hist.*). Franz Boas gave Atlanta University's commencement address in 1906 at Du Bois's invitation (*hist.*).
- **Ernest Everett Just** was born in Charleston in 1883, went north at sixteen, taught at Howard from 1907, won the first Spingarn Medal in 1915 and, blocked from a research post in the United States, worked in Naples and Berlin from 1929 (*hist.*).
- **Violence.** Wilmington (November 1898); Atlanta (September 1906), stoked by Hoke Smith's campaign, where Black residents of Brownsville armed and defended their district; Springfield, Illinois (August 1908), which led to the NAACP; the Klan refounded on Stone Mountain (1915); *The Birth of a Nation* (1915); East St. Louis (1917); Chicago (1919); Tulsa (1921); the Greenville levee camp in the 1927 flood (*hist.*).
- **Universities.** Columbia dismissed Cattell and Dana over the war in 1917 (*hist.*).
- **Union philanthropy in the South.** Rockefeller Sanitary Commission against hookworm, 1909–1914 (*hist.*).

## Storyline text

**§25, after the paragraph beginning "[PROPOSED] Sequoyah surveys further define the oil partnership",** add:

> <!-- CR-048: the rump and the long emergency -->
> **[PROPOSED — CR-048]** The rump that remains governs from Montgomery. It is half Black on the census and nearer three-quarters in the Delta, the Black Belt and the Lowcountry, and it keeps the larger share of the compensation bonds that Virginia, North Carolina and the Gulf left behind. Pacifican customs officers still sit in Charleston, Savannah, Jacksonville and Mobile, and the coupons are paid before the schools. Pacifica offers Montgomery the Gulf compact in 1900, unchanged. Montgomery never signs, because the sixth test would oblige it to register, school, examine and keep alive the people it rules, and the offer is reprinted every year. Cotton carries the state until the weevil crosses the river in 1907; after it, the modern places in the rump are foreign: Pacifica's Birmingham works and Mobile station, Union money in Florida. Convicts are still leased to the mines, but not to Pacifica's, which pay the Realm's wage-board rates and hire more Black miners than any employer in the South.
>
> **[PROPOSED — CR-048]** Pacifica reads its 1871 guarantee narrowly: it protects the Confederate States from foreign invasion, not from their own people. It refuses the planters' calls for help, sells arms to no one and offers a mediation that neither side accepts. The narrow reading keeps Union regulars out, so the trouble can never be ended by war. Pacifica's part is a thin layer of money and a loud honesty. From 1901 the statistics bureau funds Du Bois's Atlanta Studies on the bureau's counting rules; the Office of Public Measures buys pictures from Black photographers at its standard rates; Carquinez offers fellowships to Black scholars the Union will not appoint. Every payment is in the Office's published accounts. Each year the Office prints the Montgomery table, the six measures for the rump beside the Gulf's and, on the same yardstick, the Black districts of Union cities. The charter forbids a false figure. Where the camera points is a choice.
>
> **[PROPOSED — CR-048]** The revolution never comes, and it never needs to. In Atlanta in 1906, Black families in Brownsville arm against a mob and a pogrom nearly becomes a rising; later a raid from Union soil, in the manner of Harpers Ferry, fails, and Montgomery hangs its leader while half the North calls him a martyr. The emergency becomes chronic. The movement it feeds does not stop at the border. Virginia and North Carolina have brought their white machines and over a million Black citizens back into the Union, and Washington must answer Springfield in 1908, East St. Louis in 1917 and Chicago in 1919, segregate its service under Wilson and half undo it, alternately appeasing and striking at an assertive Black minority it can no longer ignore. Each concession raises expectations and each backlash radicalises the next demand.
>
> **[PROPOSED — CR-048]** The cost falls on Roosevelt's programme. Garrisons and refugee relief draw on the money meant for laboratories, and after the raid Congress attaches loyalty and neutrality oaths to the programme's grants, so the instrument built to keep talent at home helps send it abroad. A celebrated dismissal becomes the case every European scholar reads. Ernest Everett Just, born in Charleston and refused a research post in the Union, takes one in Pacifica. Union theorists, most of them revanchists, say Pacifica made the whole crisis, and cite the Atlanta grant, the Office's purchases, Carnegie's Tuskegee gift and *The Crisis* carried as freight on Pacifican lines. All of it is public and lawful; none of it proves design. Pacifica's attention is on London.

**§26, the emulation paragraph.** After "The response is in full force by 1910 and continues under Taft and Wilson." add:

> **[PROPOSED — CR-048]** The South question taxes it from the start: border garrisons and relief draw on its budget, and the oaths attached to its grants after the raid drive away some of the people it was meant to keep.
