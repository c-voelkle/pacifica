## The proposed nomos

Elias wants a productive sovereign society able to enlarge its own capacities and survive its founder. His existing practical education and Plutarch, the pastor's mixture of intellectual conflict and generosity, dispossession, Mexico and the bilingual republic give him experience of dispersed knowledge and the importance of dependable arrangements. The interpretation remains proposed; it does not resolve open childhood particulars.

The central passage of his proposed memorandum is:

> A people becomes strong through the capacities it cultivates. Government must secure the means of learning, entrust work to those who have proved themselves able, and protect the correction of error. Whoever holds an office holds it for its work; neither favour nor authority may require a false account of that work. What we establish must enable those who follow us to judge more soundly than we do.

Its commitments are routes through which ability becomes visible; a livelihood compatible with honest conclusions; truthful, correctable accounts; independent criticism beyond a patron's household; obligations to transmit mastery; lawful improvement of institutions; consequential contests; and opportunities for gifted youth to exercise judgment where instructions run out. Protected inquiry and immediate public needs both require legitimate resources and choices.

The agon supplement is:

> Give ability its trial and achievement its honour. Let schools contend for able minds and counties for the excellence of their works. Let those seeking the greatest trusts prove themselves in study, in rivalry, and in undertakings where judgment must answer for consequences. The commonwealth shall make these opportunities available and hold worthy achievement in remembrance.

These are proposed character texts, not adopted statutes or claims of unilateral executive authority. The existing mining ordinances, school support and participating institutions' governing bodies provide the initial legal setting; new undertakings require their own instruments.

## The proposed ethos and its practices

The public character is proud and buoyant: pleasure in mastery, practical confidence, fidelity, gratitude to teachers and companions, emulation, and discriminating respect for ability. A person's ordinary honour can rest on dependable work. Outstanding ability can inspire reverence; a school can gain significance through generations of achievement and remembered obligations. This new civic use of mana is a proposed syncretic development, not an exhaustive definition of Hawaiian belief or a compulsory theology.

Scientific life adds a strong desire for difficult discovery and recognition by capable judges. Its manners can be direct, teasing and competitive. “Show us” invites demonstration; eminence carries expectations of magnanimity, fair acknowledgement and teaching successors. People can confidently act on limited knowledge while stating what remains unresolved. Jealousy, concealment and misallocated credit remain possible and consequential.

| Custom | Proposed occasion or procedure | Disposition cultivated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An apprentice's first acknowledged work | Experienced makers test and discuss a first accomplished piece; acceptance, a mark or workshop entry, and a meal acknowledge responsibility and the teacher. Applicable examinations remain separate. | Mastery, accountability, gratitude. |
| Public proving of a work | After proper technical tests, residents see a bridge, waterworks or harbour installation used; responsible people explain its performance and contributors receive recognition. | Confidence grounded in visible accomplishment. |
| County showing | Locally varied annual displays, practical contests, awards and accounts develop alongside fairs or regattas; appropriate outside judging and later county halls make comparison possible. | Local loyalty, emulation, pride in results. |
| Fellowship departure and return | Fellows state an undertaking and support entrusted to them; on return, records, results, accounts and testimony precede honours. Failed ventures and lost companions remain in institutional memory. | Daring, judgment, fidelity and earned glory. |
| Challenge evening | Researchers distinguish established findings, conjectures and possible next steps; a respondent and colleagues challenge them, including workers responsible for apparatus and calculation. | Intellectual courage, answerability and fellowship. |
| Contest with declared terms | Questions and criteria precede proofs, predictions or demonstrations; independent judgment awards consequential distinction and, where agreed, resources. | Agon, fair dealing and serious rivalry. |
| Corrections in the record | Journals preserve the earlier claim, amendment and credit for identifying a difficulty; important corrected cases enter teaching. | Candour and confidence capable of revision. |
| A master's welcome and leave-taking | Inaugural lectures or feasts name teachers and collaborators; leave-taking remembers successors and unfinished questions, with objects and records carrying memory. | Magnanimity, transmission and institutional continuity. |

The following chronology shows how these arrangements acquire meaning through particular events. They do not all begin as national decrees or emerge without disagreement.

## 1850–1851: Elias decides what the gold should leave behind

**Canonical foundation:** Elias already combines practical education, Plutarch, frontier experience, Mexican captivity and the creation of a bilingual republic. Genth accepts the Republic's offer in 1851: laboratory, assistants, independent salary, publication rights and time for his own chemistry.

**Revised proposal:** Elias begins putting his reflections into *On the Public Arts*, a memorandum developed through 1854. He wants a productive sovereign society whose capacities can outlast its founder. His own improbable education prompts him to ask how comparable opportunities could become dependable.

The memorandum proposes that government cultivate ability, protect truthful accounts and enable successors to judge better than their predecessors. It gives ambition a public purpose: mastery, discovery, sound works and worthy leadership. Genth's recruitment is a deliberate beginning of that project. Elias has no foresight of nuclear power.

Duarte begins with accounts and specimen records. Human calculating staff, commercial arithmetic machines and checked tables meet immediate needs. **The first computing institution is an organization of people, records and procedures.**

## 1852–1854: the nomos acquires contracts, money and visible precedents

**Canonical foundation:** Genth's laboratory becomes the Carquinez school in 1852; mining ordinances and the royalty arrangement provide its legal and financial basis. Genth exposes the Superintendency mill's losses in 1853, and Elias publishes the report. Zeuner arrives in 1854.

Elias deliberately treats the mill report as a precedent: researchers should expect an honest, unwelcome conclusion to have an institutional home. School contracts and governing arrangements give practical effect to protected inquiry, independent accounts and scrutiny. The memorandum is not a new comprehensive schools law; implementation requires the appropriate existing authorities.

Duarte develops a register preserving readings, sources and corrections. Zeuner's testing floor makes the distinction between a convincing account and dependable performance visible. Arriaga's first acknowledged instrument earns recognition from experienced makers, beginning the proposed workshop custom of marking a person's first accepted work.

Elias seeks support for travelling fellowships through the school and its lawful funding arrangements. An exceptional expedition can later carry more distinction than examination rank. Required qualifying examinations continue to govern their specified professions and services.

## 1855–1858: calculation, rivalry and journeys acquire institutions

**Canonical foundation:** Sylvester arrives in 1855; Japanese technical exchanges build on arrangements in 1854 and the Shimoda agreement of 1855; the Confederation forms in 1856. The school publishes major results and its first gold prize questions in 1858.

Sylvester proposes the **Calculation Office in 1855**. It serves navigation, surveying, insurance and engineering, creating a continuing workload and careers for calculating staff. Liang joins its mathematical work; Arriaga maintains its machinery.

In **1856**, Carquinez commissions a Scheutz-type printing calculator; installation and adaptation occupy **1858–1860**. The historical machine completed in 1853 supplies an actual antecedent, while the Pacifican commission and dates are proposed. The apparatus computes particular tables; its limitations create opportunities for further invention. [Smithsonian collection](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_997042).

**Proposed 1856–1857 fellowship:** Liang travels through the developing Japanese partnership to compare measures and calculation practices used in cooperating workshops. Japanese hosts judge and benefit from the exchange. Organizing supplies, explaining work across languages and negotiating commitments give him experience of the assumptions behind an apparently straightforward numerical account. Political uncertainty gives the journey real stakes.

His return is followed by scrutiny of his work and an account to those who supported it. Returning fellows become admired examples. The 1858 prize questions provide another route to distinction: proofs, experiments and expeditions offer different kinds of achievement. Schools begin contending for the people who produce them.

## 1859–1861: an unexplained effect earns a sustained inquiry

**Canonical foundation:** women may sit state examinations from 1859. Victoria's campaign makes their exclusion a political issue; Elias's active support matters in the ensuing reforms. The Kanrin Maru visit in 1860 demonstrates Japanese navigational and technical accomplishment within the partnership.

European reports by Niépce de Saint-Victor provide an ambiguous photographic question worth investigating. His historical report is an antecedent to inquiry, not an established discovery of spontaneous radioactivity. [Contemporary report](https://dingler.bbaw.de/articles/ar147016.html).

Genth proposes the **Laboratory of Light, Electricity and Matter in 1859**, within the school. Photographic recording, mineral analysis and electrical measurement give it practical work and instruments. Mele joins its recording staff. Duarte's archive and the Calculation Office make comparisons cumulative.

The proposed **challenge evening** begins as a local practice: a new claim is stated, an appointed respondent questions it, and participants identify what might settle the disagreement. A conjecture can earn a trial before commanding belief.

**1861: natural radioactivity is established in the proposed branch.** Persistent mineral emissions resist the available chemical and optical explanations. The account identifies what has been shown and what remains open, crediting observers and makers. Public honour attaches to the achievement; most people need not adopt the investigators' vocation to admire it. “Radioactivity” is retrospective terminology here.

## 1862–1865: war tests the founder's priorities

**Canonical foundation:** college grants require the admission of qualified women from 1862, with the established delay by the Jesuits; Genth identifies indium in 1863.

Another League laboratory confirms the radiation finding in **1862–1863**. Its independent result brings prestige to that institution. Competition increases scrutiny and gives researchers another place to pursue a neglected question. **By 1864**, the programme has mathematical descriptions of characteristic changes in some radioactive phenomena, without a complete atomic explanation.

Elias deliberately protects continuity in inquiry while much practical capacity serves surveying, communications, machinery and naval needs. He regards the accumulated ability to investigate unfamiliar problems as a strategic asset. Protection rests on California's lawful school support and participating institutions' own decisions; no new confederal education power is presumed.

Salcedo, a student since 1861, undertakes a proposed field assignment in **1864–1865** within a qualified engineering team. He learns to keep observations under difficult conditions and acquires pride in useful responsibility. His later authority grows from experience as well as examinations.

**Computing:** Liang and Arriaga demonstrate a narrow relay arithmetic apparatus in **1863**. Workshop rivalry and a real workload support continued development. The prototype is a separate engineering divergence and requires sustained work before useful service.

## 1866–1872: the ethos becomes plural and begins correcting its own methods

**Canonical foundation:** Sylvester and Cohn move to Yerba Buena in 1866; the Methodists' physicians save Victoria that year; Morley arrives in 1868; Zeuner leaves for Freiberg in 1871. Victoria becomes Queen in 1872.

Yerba Buena recruits Clara and builds mathematical strength. The Calculation Office develops a shared service, while colleges retain their own governing bodies, resources and interpretations. Physicians' visible success helps give scientific competence significance beyond the technical schools.

In **1867**, Mele discovers that a checking rule associated with Liang's group has excluded recurring observations as presumed instrument faults. Duarte's retained records permit reconstruction. Liang must acknowledge a failure in a procedure that helped make his reputation. Genth redirects the next computing allocation to independent checking, delaying an extension of the machinery. Mele receives only an assistant's acknowledgement, leaving a dispute over credit unresolved.

The colleges negotiate recording and publication terms covering omitted observations and contrary results. The scientific community learns that its tools for organizing knowledge also require criticism. A later feast or correction entry cannot erase the costs or settle Mele's grievance automatically.

**1870, strengthened independently by 1872:** the proposed programme recognizes atomic transformation. Relationships between chemical identity and radioactive behaviour require revising what counts as an element's stability. Ordinary chemistry retains its demonstrated domain.

**Computing:** dependable relay arithmetic reaches service in **1868–1870**; limited preset sequences follow by **1872**. Procedures accumulate and become teachable. Staff can compare mathematical accounts without claiming that a successful calculation certifies the account's physical truth.

Zeuner's departure becomes a conspicuous instance of a master carrying obligations between institutions: Pacifican colleagues lose his daily presence but gain a continuing European connection.

## 1873–1879: the first students win independent distinction

**Canonical foundation:** element 31 is discovered in 1873 and named californium—the alternate name for gallium, not element 98. Michelson's canonical precision work begins in 1878.

Salcedo's experimental group and Clara's mathematical work enter an increasingly competitive scientific world. **1876: the proposed identification of the electron** establishes a common negatively charged atomic constituent, without completing the atom's explanation. Meetings distinguish the observed result, competing interpretations and further questions.

**Computing:** a useful programmable electromechanical calculator enters institutional service in **1876–1879**, followed by a common written procedural notation in **1878–1882**. Repeated calculations become cheaper and easier to check. Arriaga's workshop gains prestige because dependable operation is a recognized achievement. This major advance remains an engineering counterfactual, not something established merely by the earlier existence of telegraph relays. [Bell Laboratories technical history of the actual later development](https://9p.io/cm/cs/cstr/99.html).

Public demonstrations, visiting scholars, contests and apprentice acknowledgements make skill socially visible. Fellowships bring further experience of Japan, Hawaii, mountains and islands. Returning participants contribute to the customs instead of merely undergoing them.

Victoria's educational influence and Kalākaua's existing cultural programme provide settings in which Hawaiian participants can develop the proposed civic language of *mana*: exceptional achievement and remembered obligations acquire sacred associations for some people. Its relation to ancestry and other religious meanings remains alive and contested.

Elias's actual Hawaiian breach in **1874–1876** remains. A founding commitment to trustworthy institutions can coexist with his increasingly exceptional treatment of political limits. The revised scientific ethos does not erase this conflict.

## 1880–1886: successful institutions produce formidable rivals

**Canonical foundation:** the Development Fund begins in 1880; Tesla arrives in 1884. General fund availability does not authorize a particular new atomic allocation.

**1882: proposed compact nuclear model.** Salcedo revises his earlier account in response to physical evidence. **1883–1886: proposed isotope concept.** Chemical identity, atomic mass and radioactive behaviour no longer fit a simple one-to-one classification. Genth's analytical tradition and Morley's precision work contribute; hydrogen spectroscopy supplies another field of inquiry rather than a shortcut to complete nuclear knowledge.

Salcedo has become a famous master. His students and supporters associate his success with the standing of Carquinez. In **1886**, he directs his next instrument budget towards developing his model. Clara declines an offered Carquinez chair and leaves their joint programme to investigate contrary results. Yerba Buena postpones another expansion to support her independent group. Separate facilities and lost shared access impose costs on both teams.

Here the agon becomes consequential: another institution can offer a brilliant dissenter a credible home. The norms of independent criticism matter because money and allegiance do not move automatically with truth.

**Proposed 1883–1884:** Elena undertakes a fellowship at a Japanese partner laboratory after joining analytical chemistry. Difficult materials work and independently assessed results earn standing before her eventual nuclear discovery. Her home community has an achievement to remember, and her professional confidence has grounds beyond examination rank.

Computing staff standardize model comparisons, preserve discrepancies and check independently. The community increasingly values the ability to reconstruct how a result was obtained. School welcomes and professional feasts publicly credit teachers, assistants and makers, although disputes over credit persist.

## 1887–1890: the founder dies; the community must honour correction

**Canonical foundation:** Elias dies in 1887; Victoria becomes Regent while remaining Queen. Michelson and Morley's ether result remains in 1887. Steinmetz arrives in 1889. The Five and constitutional succession are preserved.

Memorial occasions give Elias's founding decisions and the institutions' histories greater emotional weight. Reverence for that inheritance is now tested by a discovery the leading atomic account cannot contain.

**1888: proposed neutron discovery**, independently confirmed by **1890**, through Clara and a younger experimental team. Salcedo initially resists; after corroboration he relinquishes leadership of the proposed intercollege atomic committee while retaining his teaching role and credit for earlier work. His retreat costs standing but does not erase achievement. This is a major unresolved scientific contingency of the branch, not a discovery supplied by computing.

Hollerith's **proposed Pacifican appointment from 1888** adds coded-record tabulation to a mature calculation service. It assists public returns, existing health records and scientific administration. Numerical computing, record sorting and interpretation remain distinct activities.

New travelling awards must respect the canonical service obligations attached to Realm scholarships and posted grants from 1888. School competition and fellowships operate within contracts and applicable qualifications; no unprovided exemption is assumed.

## 1891–1894: scientific glory acquires a strategic boundary

**1892: proposed recognition of fission.** Elena and collaborators encounter persistent chemical findings inconsistent with the expected family of products. Their interpretation requires analytical and physical expertise. The discovery receives its own credit rather than being absorbed into Salcedo's reputation.

**1893–1894:** the programme establishes the possibility of a continuing fission reaction. Recognizing the phenomenon, understanding its implications and making a controllable installation remain different achievements. These scientific steps require dedicated discovery episodes and remain major contingencies.

Publication since 1861 has already altered foreign research. Rival institutions have been asking different questions for decades. Pacifica competes through accumulated skill, instruments, industrial capability and organization; it cannot assume an untouched foreign timetable or lasting monopoly.

Victoria and Soberanes face a conflict between open scientific competition and strategic restrictions. Soberanes seeks broad controls; Elena and Yerba Buena resist surrendering previously open records. Victoria brokers the proposed **1894 appropriation and oversight settlement**, with commitment delayed until **1895**. California's legislature would authorize support through the Polytechnic; its governors and participating college boards would authorize their own involvement and independent review. Common Development Fund money requires its own lawful authorization. New confidentiality follows agreed participation terms rather than retroactive cancellation of publication rights. Amounts, statutory wording and offices remain open.

The ethos now has competing claims on loyalty: country, institution, colleagues, truthful accounts and the desire for distinction. They cannot all be satisfied by a simple instruction to be useful.

## 1895–1900: dependable operation becomes the final trial

Research turns towards engineering validation, materials studies, measurement, operating discipline and independent assessment. Instruments, records, calculation, chemistry and physical judgment must work together. Specialist competence and clear connections between fields make that cooperation possible without every participant understanding the whole undertaking.

**1898: proposed first controlled civilian research reactor in Pacifica**, conditional on the preceding discoveries and engineering success. **1899–1900:** independent verification and operating research establish reproducibility and limits. The installation demonstrates a controlled sustained process; commercial electricity and mature generating plants belong to later proposals. World priority remains open.

Within the agreed oversight arrangements, operators, makers, calculating staff and discoverers receive recognition appropriate to their work. The timing and content of any public announcement remain an open political choice. The story cannot simultaneously promise complete secrecy and a fully informed public triumph.

The wider ethos already has concrete public expressions. In existing canon, **Fresno's hall rings its bell for a week in 1897 when the county reaches all four standards**. County halls, independently checked returns and local celebrations make competence a source of collective pride. Families can honour schools, physicians, reliable works and successful fellows without knowing atomic theory.

By 1900 the founders' circle has become several generations. In the proposed 1898 year, Duarte is about seventy, Arriaga sixty-four, Liang sixty-five, Mele fifty-nine, Salcedo fifty-two, Clara forty-nine and Elena thirty-six. Their different experiences give institutional memory living voices. They have learned to value the careful account, the worthy opponent, the difficult journey and the person who makes the work dependable.

## Historical anchors and limits

The Scheutz printing machine, Niépce's ambiguous photographic investigations and the later Bell Laboratories computing record support antecedents and comparisons, not the proposed outcome dates. Bell's history places the actual computing development in a much later period. No technical recipe for a reactor or weapon forms part of this cultural and scientific chronology.

The institutional choices, accelerated computing development and late atomic discoveries carry different kinds of uncertainty. The revised nomos and ethos explain why generations might keep pursuing unfamiliar questions, attract able rivals, preserve troublesome evidence and cooperate on demanding work. Scientific success on the stated dates remains a favourable counterfactual branch.

### T1 — What can expeditionary achievement outweigh?

**Current canon:** CR-035 restricts the mint, Superintendency, licensed professions and engineer corps to examined candidates. CR-037 makes the examination, never a group quota, the qualifying test. The author's present direction gives exceptional expeditions potentially greater standing than examinations.

**Case A, adapt the draft to current canon:** keep qualifying examinations, then let exceptional achievement outweigh examination rank for honours, resources and selection among qualified candidates. This is the version proposed in I2. An expedition's report can also supply material for an appropriate practical examination.

**Case B, amend canon:** authorize specified appointments through independently assessed expeditionary achievement in place of the ordinary examination. The author would have to specify the offices, judging body and scope of equivalence.
