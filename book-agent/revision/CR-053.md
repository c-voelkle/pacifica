## What this CR does

Home oil, found with the greatest believable haste (CR-052 C) and flooded early (M8), peaks at about 304m bbl in 1924 and falls to 255m by 1929; the Pacifican-controlled Gulf crude binds at 285m bbl in 1929; imports are 31% of primary energy. Nuclear power is forbidden before the 1927 opening and slow after it (review-3, finding 8). This CR gives the oil trust the foreign positions a creditor empire with a canal would take in the 1920s, in two groups:

1. **Oil inside the window (1925–32):** minority stakes in the Lake Maracaibo and De Mares concession companies, and a Peruvian position in which the Union holds a reciprocal minority.
2. **The next decade's oil at 1920s prices:** Frank Holmes's Bahrain and Kuwait options, and a share of the Mesopotamian company beside the Union's group, both carried by the 1927 settlement with Britain.

The pattern is Pacifica's own (CR-048): money, partners and published terms, no flag and no gunboat. None of it is a colony. The trust is a shareholder, not a sovereign.

## P1 — Lake Maracaibo (Venezuela): a minority stake in Lago Petroleum

**Historical anchor.** Barroso No. 2 at La Rosa blew out in December 1922 and started the Maracaibo boom; the Gómez government sold concessions cheaply and widely; Venezuelan output rose from about 2m bbl (1921) to about 137m (1929), second in the world. The three groups were Shell's (Caribbean Petroleum, Venezuelan Oil Concessions), Gulf Oil's (Mene Grande) and the Lago group (independents in 1923, Standard of Indiana from 1925). *To verify: the 1929 figure and the Lago dates.*

**Decision (author, 4 November; detail PROPOSED).** The Pacifican oil trust buys about a fifth to a quarter of **Lago Petroleum** in 1923–24, while it is still the independents' company and before Standard of Indiana takes it: the one Maracaibo company with no British partner (so nothing for the 1927 settlement to untangle) and no Union major yet (so Standard of Indiana arrives in 1925 to find a Pacifican minority already seated, and takes it as a fact). The stake carries an entitlement to crude at cost, lifted at the lake-bar lighterage and shipped through the canal to the Los Angeles and San Francisco refineries. The trust brings what Maracaibo lacked: tanker tonnage through the canal, the Gulf's drilling crews, and the Hughes bit.

**Why a minority and not a concession.** Venezuela is the Union's backyard. A Pacifican concession under its own flag reads in Washington as encirclement (the *Tribune* would write it); a minority holding in a company that already has Union partners is finance, not geography, and the Union's own Caribbean doctrine has no purchase on a shareholder. The thin layer of money is the method of CR-048.

**Amounts (MODEL; in the workbook).** Entitlement crude of about 3m bbl in 1926, 9m in 1929, 12m in 1932. Path `conc_ven`.

## P2 — The De Mares concession (Colombia), a minority stake

**Historical anchor.** The De Mares concession (Barrancabermeja, on the Magdalena) was granted in 1905, taken up by Tropical Oil in 1916, and passed to International Petroleum (Standard of New Jersey through Imperial) in 1920; the Andian pipeline to Cartagena opened in 1926 and Colombian output reached about 20m bbl by 1929. *To verify: the pipeline date and the 1929 figure.*

**Proposal (PROPOSED).** The trust takes a minority stake in Tropical Oil in 1920–21, when International Petroleum is assembling it and needs capital for the pipeline, with the same entitlement form as P1 and the same route: Cartagena to the canal. The Barco concession is not taken (it is Gulf Oil's from 1926 and would put Pacifica against a Union company directly).

**Amounts (MODEL; in the workbook).** About 1m bbl in 1926, 2m in 1927, 4m in 1929, 5m in 1932. Path `conc_col`.

## P3 — Peru, with a reciprocal Union minority

**Historical anchor.** Peru's oil was at Talara and Lobitos: the La Brea y Pariñas estate (International Petroleum, from 1913; the 1922 arbitral award settled its taxes) and Lobitos Oilfields (British, from 1908); output about 13m bbl in 1929. Canon already has Pacifica importing Peruvian oil before 1901 (CR-052 Fuels sheet: "the Union, Peru and Russia before").

**Proposal (PROPOSED).** The trust's old Peruvian supply becomes a position: a Pacifican company holds the Lobitos fields and the northern concessions around Talara that International Petroleum does not, from about 1924, with the Union invited to a reciprocal minority (about a fifth) in the Pacifican company, as the trust holds minorities in the Union's Venezuelan and Colombian companies. *Author's direction: the reciprocity is the point.* It gives Whitney's and Dawes's Union a share of the Pacific coast's oil, and a reason for Wall Street's peace lobby to defend the arrangement in Congress (CR-020's foreign-creditor beats).

**Amounts (MODEL; in the workbook).** About 2m bbl in 1924, 4m in 1926, 7m by 1929. Path `conc_peru`.

## P4 — Bahrain and Kuwait: Holmes's options

**Historical anchor.** Frank Holmes's Eastern and General Syndicate obtained a Bahrain option in 1925 and sought a Kuwait concession from Sheikh Ahmad from 1923. The British nationality clause in the sheikhdoms' agreements barred non-British buyers; no British company wanted the options; Holmes sold Bahrain to Gulf Oil in 1927, which passed it to Standard of California in 1928; Bahrain struck oil in June 1932; Kuwait went to a Gulf–Anglo-Persian company in 1934 and struck Burgan in 1938. *To verify: the 1925 and 1927 dates.*

**Proposal (PROPOSED).** The trust buys Holmes's Bahrain and Kuwait options in 1926, when nobody else will, and the **1927 settlement with Britain** (CR-049 §L1: debt, the Petrograd account, the demonstration at sea) carries a fourth, quiet term: Britain waives the nationality clause for the Pacifican company in Bahrain and Kuwait. Canon already puts the AC standard and Pacifican credit on "the Persian Gulf oil coast"; this is why. The al-Hasa option is not taken (author's selection).

**What it yields.** Nothing before 1932 (Bahrain's first oil, June 1932, is a line, not a figure: 0.3m bbl in the workbook). It is the 1930s' oil bought at 1926 prices, and the reason the Realm stops worrying about imports by 1940. Paths `conc_bah`, `conc_kuw`.

## P5 — The Mesopotamian share, beside the Union's group

**Historical anchor.** The Turkish Petroleum Company held the Mesopotamian concession; San Remo (1920) gave France the former German quarter; Baba Gurgur at Kirkuk blew in on 15 October 1927; the 1928 Red Line agreement admitted the American group at 23.75% beside Anglo-Persian, Shell and the French, with Gulbenkian's 5%; the pipeline to the Mediterranean opened in 1934. *To verify: the shares.*

**Decision (author, 4 November; detail PROPOSED).** Pacifica **joins the Union's group** rather than displacing it: the quiet second term of the 1927 settlement is Britain's consent to an enlarged American group in which a Pacifican company holds a share beside the Union's companies (the group's 23.75% split OPEN, perhaps a third of it Pacifican). It is the CR-048 method: a seat at a table the Union already sits at, not a table of its own; it gives the Union's oil men a reason to want Pacifica's credit in London; and it keeps the Red Line's self-denying clause, which binds the trust inside the former Ottoman lands, as a shared discipline rather than a Pacifican concession. Kirkuk's crude reaches the Mediterranean only in 1934, so this too is the 1930s' oil. Path `conc_mes` carries zero to 1932.

The concessions add about 7% to the Realm's 1929 imports. They do not close the gap; nothing short of the Gulf does, and the Gulf is already at its ceiling. What they do is (a) diversify the trust's supply away from a single partner, (b) give the Union a stake in the Pacific coast's oil, and (c) buy the 1930s. The bridge for 1924–32 is where review-3 put it: the Sacramento Valley and Kettleman gas, CR-052 M8's water flooding (now in Story: home oil 255m bbl in 1929 instead of 216m), the Gulf, and (CR-055) the hydrogenation of heavy residue on the Columbia's hydrogen.

**Recommendation:** amend; it is the natural shape of a creditor's settlement, and it explains the Persian Gulf line already in canon. Fact changes: a ledger entry, no dates.

**Recommendation:** keep the symmetry; it is one sentence. Fact changes: none.
