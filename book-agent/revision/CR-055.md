## What this CR does

The Columbia and Snake are the largest undeveloped power on Pacifican ground: about 12 GW by 1920s technique. This CR builds the river early — Bonneville from 1908, Rocky Reach from 1914, Wells from 1921, McNary from 1922, a laddered Chief Joseph from 1926 — under compacts of the S8 pattern, and gives it **four purposes that a country of this timeline, and only this one, would have for it**: a 287 kV line to the Sierra that feeds the halls; hydrogen for the heavy crude of California; synthetic rubber against Britain's rubber restriction; and electric pig iron for the carrier yards. Beside them the river carries the electrochemistry of R0's first table a decade early. The tenth build shows the river fully used from 1924 and the Realm at 2,665 kWh a head in 1929, 2.4–3.2 times the United States. After 1927 the same river is the atom's second lead (R3) and the reactor metals (R4). Celilo Falls and Kettle Falls are untouched; there is no Grand Coulee, and the CR says why.

### The four purposes (author: "They all seem important")

1. **The Columbia–Sierra line (decided 1919, energised 1924; 300 MW, 600 MW by 1932).** Big Creek carried 220 kV to Los Angeles over 240 miles in 1923; Ryan's laboratory makes 287 kV with series compensation a decade before history's Boulder line. The 750-mile line carries the river's surplus to the Sierra's electrochemical district and the halls, closing CR-052 F3's dedicated-power shortfall (33–153 MW from the grid) without enlarging the Works' dams, and it is the work for which Harris Ryan holds the seat. It supersedes CR-052 F5's "no 1920s line could carry it", which was an assumption from our own history, not a limit. From 1924 the river's power is simply part of the Realm's grid, and "the reserve" is invisible on any map but the Office's.
2. **Hydrogen for heavy crude (Portland, from 1920).** California's crude is heavy and yields little straight-run gasoline; Bergius hydrogenation (1913) makes motor fuel from heavy residue with hydrogen, and the Columbia's cells make hydrogen for nothing. The Portland hydrogenation works takes California's residue by tanker: 35m bbl a year by 1929 (3.5 TWh), about 19m bbl of motor fuel gained — the use that bears most directly on CR-052's fuel gap and CR-053's binding Gulf ceiling, and the one that most looks like Pacifica.
3. **Synthetic rubber (Tacoma, from 1923).** Carbide → acetylene → butadiene rubber was German and Soviet chemistry of the 1930s; the inputs are limestone, coke and electricity. The motive in this timeline is Britain's Stevenson restriction scheme of 1922–28, which rationed the world's rubber during the very years of the Anglo-Pacifican confrontation: 50 kt by 1929 (0.6 TWh) for the Concord's tyres, cable and fleet, and a line in the confrontation dossier beside oil and the debt.
4. **Electric pig iron for the yards (Portland and Tacoma, from 1922).** Pacifica's coal is in Utah and the Cascades; Norway and Sweden smelted iron with electricity for exactly that reason. Half a million tonnes a year by 1929 (1.4 TWh) on Philippine and Chilean ore gives the carrier programme (canon: carriers from 1922) its own steel, and the yards go to welded hulls on the same power (R7).

## R1 — The decision of 1928: the reserve named

**Scene (PROPOSED).** In the winter after the demonstration at sea, Langmuir (director of the Works) and Ryan (the power seat since 1924) put one map before Julian and the Council of the Seal: the Columbia from the Canadian line to the sea, five dams on it and a sixth begun, the line south, the station at White Bluffs, and the Office of Public Measures' curve of the Northwest's load drawn under the river's. In 1928 the two curves touch; the question is the next decade's gap. Rutherford has called atomic power moonshine in public; the Tokyo station is announced; Europe is looking for a reactor route. Ryan's argument is a power engineer's: the river is the only place on earth under one hand where the atom's next decade can be fed, and every dam so far has paid for itself twice. Langmuir's is the Works': separation at scale, the breeder and the reactor metals are power steps. Julian's decision is to keep building — Chief Joseph is already decided — and to let the Works be the customer the next gap is kept for.

**The rule (PROPOSED, in the Works' charter and the power estate's statutes, no ordinance):** the Columbia is developed in order of fish passage and consent, and no dam is built that salmon cannot pass. That is why there is no Grand Coulee, and why Chief Joseph is built lower than history's.

## R3 — The atomic second lead on the Columbia

The second is a **diffusion works at the station**, decided 1929, built behind the fence on Rocky Reach's and Chief Joseph's power from 1933 (PROPOSED): hundreds of megawatts, the reserve's customer. The uranium strategy a second time, time and leverage, not a guarantee (CR-051 L1).

## R4 — The reactor metals: zirconium and hafnium

The breeder and the water-cooled stations need zirconium, hafnium removed. Historically Kroll's magnesium reduction gave titanium in the late 1930s and zirconium in the 1940s; Pacifica, with its own magnesium since 1909 and the Works' metallurgists making uranium metal and graphite since the 1880s, reaches zirconium about 1929–32 (PROPOSED). **The Works' metals house at Wenatchee** on Rocky Reach's power; published purpose, alloys for the aircraft and electrical champions, true.
