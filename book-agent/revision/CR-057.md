## What this CR does

It carries the Mises review into canon. Pacifica's economy keeps its state-capitalist ambition but gets a monetary and fiscal frame a hostile economist could not easily fault:
- **Falling prices.** The Reserve Bank lets prices fall as goods get cheaper, and the state works to make inputs cheap where that unlocks productivity.
- **A fixed reserve compact** ties the reserve centre's cover ratios to a treaty the government cannot change alone.
- **A sovereign wealth fund.** The Development Fund becomes one on the Norwegian and Singaporean lines, fed by rents, not by taxes above what the state needs.
- **Open patents and auctioned concessions.** Polytechnic patents are licensed openly, competitive industries carry no state stake, and concessions are auctioned. The state keeps its own estates and the canal.
- **Chartered districts at home and in four places abroad** give investors speed and cheap inputs.
- **A voluntary provident account**, deductible up to a yearly cap, rewards saving.
- **The river's spilled power** finds paying customers in 1930–32.

## B. MR-03: the reserve compact (approved, 16:21)

**Ratios.** The Reserve Bank's charter fixes the domestic ratios. The reserve compact fixes the partners'.

| Liability | Minimum gold cover | Remainder | Precedent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Partners' gold reserves | 100%, earmarked | — | Mises's money-certificates |
| Partners' official dollar balances | **50%** | prime commercial bills under 90 days | above the domestic ratio: foreign official money is what runs, as Britain's did in September 1931 |
| The Reserve Bank's notes | **40%** (the workbook's rule today) | prime commercial bills | Federal Reserve Act 1913 |
| Banks' deposits at the Reserve Bank | **35%** | prime commercial bills | Federal Reserve Act 1913 (where the 35% was gold or lawful money; here gold) |

Government paper never counts as cover, whether Pacifican or foreign.

**Is 50% affordable?** The workbook holds $319m of gold above the 40% rule in 1929 and $465m in 1925, but only $49m in 1920. That would carry fractional partner balances of a few hundred million dollars in the late 1920s, not in 1920. Falling prices (MR-01) make the rule easier: the derived price level lowers the nominal money Pacifica needs and frees gold.

The money block (H1) must show three things:
- whether the 1920 cover binds, and whether it then brings on a sharp 1920–21 correction;
- how much room Bagehot lending has in 1931, when the Union rescue, gold credit to Britain and a run on partner balances all draw on the $631m above the floors;
- whether the weekly rate step would tighten in the middle of the slump.

If the balances do not fit, Pacifica accepts smaller balances; it does not lower the ratio.

**The guard against the government.** Cover laws are lifted "whenever a government deems that an emergency justifies" it (Mises XVII §12). Peel's Act of 1844 was suspended in 1847, 1857 and 1866. No rule binds a determined government. These make breaking the rule costly, slow and public:
1. **The ratios are in treaties.**
   - With Japan, the cover ratios are an agreed matter of the Concord from 1927, beside gold parity (§33). They cannot be lowered without Japan's consent, given through its party in the Concord.
   - The Philippine and Hawaiian crowns, on Julian's side of the Concord, and holders outside it (the Gulf Republic, Korea, China's reserve banks) hold their balances under bilateral compacts. Each compact can be changed only with that holder's consent.
   - The Realm's government cannot lower any ratio alone.
2. **The Treasury cannot borrow from the Bank** beyond advances repaid within the fiscal year. The Bank's holdings of government paper are capped at its own capital, and the standing debt is collateral only (MR-06). The Bank may act as the Treasury's fiscal agent, paying and receiving on its account (canon: its paymasters in Siberia, 1918–19), but never as its lender.
3. **Automatic tightening.** Below the floor, the Bank's discount rate rises by a fixed step each week until cover is restored. The Reichsbank's 1875 tax on uncovered notes paid the Treasury, which gives a government a stake in over-issue. Here the penalty is a dearer rate, so the government never gains from a breach.
4. **A weekly return**, as the Bank of England has published since 1844. The Office of Public Measures prints it and cables it to every partner capital.
5. **Unconditional redemption at par.** Partners withdraw earmarked gold without notice. Suspending convertibility is a default under the compact.
6. **Separation.** The Reserve Bank, the Treasury and the Fund have three boards. The Bank never lends to the Fund or buys its paper. Its profits go to the Reserve Fund buffer, not to the Treasury's spending or to the Fund, so no budget depends on it issuing more. Governors serve long terms and can be removed only for cause.

**The real guard.** Pacifica's power rests on its dollar being the safest money. §34 already says "the Union's savers learn which flag is safe". A government that diluted the cover would sell the order for a season's projects. **[PROPOSED]** The temptation comes in 1931–32, when the rescues and the Columbia's idle power both argue for printing. The common Finance minister (Pacifican by convention) and the Reserve Bank's governor refuse, and the Senate backs them. Who argues for printing is OPEN.

**Dates.** Partner reserves are held in San Francisco from 1886 (the peso, CR-029). Japan's currency rests on Pacifican deposits (CR-021 H). The Reserve Bank is founded in 1857 as the Confederation's bank (A2, PROPOSED). The first bilateral compact's date is OPEN. **The ratios survive the Thalassia settlement of 1932**: adjustable parities change what a dollar is worth in gold, not the share of gold behind it.

  - It falls sharply when permits lapse in a slump ($176m in 1932 against $323m in 1929). That is a reason to put it in a fund that smooths it, and not in the budget.

- **Surpluses.** From 1880 these sources feed the Fund, not "seventy percent of each budget surplus". Ordinary surpluses go to the Reserve Fund buffer and then to lower taxes.

**Investment.**
- **To 1919, at home.** The Fund holds Pacifican state and railway bonds and mortgage paper. While the champions borrow in London (§19), investing abroad would earn less than Pacifica pays to borrow.
- **From 1919, abroad as well**, gradually: a tenth in 1919 and half by 1929. It holds no claim on a government Pacifica is negotiating with and no war debts (A2). Once Pacifica is a creditor, a diversified portfolio abroad keeps the Fund from overheating the home economy and from becoming a political lender. Norway invests its fund wholly abroad for those reasons.
- **Losses are the Fund's own.** H3 books the Russian losses of 1917–18 and the German and Latin American defaults of 1931–32 (§34) against it.
- **The commercial arm at home**, on Temasek's lines (1974), takes minority stakes only when a firm asks, never with board control. It lends beside private capital that takes the first loss, never more than half of a venture (MR-10). The counties' grid loans (CR-052) continue that way, as commercial loans.
- **Credit to the state's own estates is exempt from the private tranche.** It is a loan from one public body to another. So the Works' "Development Fund credit for electrochemical expansion, which is what the halls are" (§30, CR-051) stands, seen whole only by the sealed comptroller.

Norway's rule was 4% from 2001 and 3% from 2017; Singapore lets the budget take up to half of expected long-term real returns (to verify). Infrastructure also pays its own way where it can: power at cost, tolls, and project bonds sold to savers (MR-06). Each project is judged in a published benefit–cost account.

- a target return over consol and Pacifican state-bond yields, published in advance;
- returns at market value in the Office's tables every year;
- managers paid on the excess over the target across several years;

## D. MR-09: paying customers for the river (decided 16:21)

## E. MR-11: open patents, auctioned concessions and chartered districts (PROPOSED; amends CR-020 B9 and OPEN-020-10)

**Patents.**
- From the Polytechnic's first patents, every patent it holds is licensed **non-exclusively** to any firm, foreign firms included, at a published royalty of **2% of the licensee's selling price** of the patented product, halved after the patent's tenth year, free for teaching and research (A2, PROPOSED), and with no equity condition.
- **Two limits.**
  - Licences to Concord partners follow the Hayama annex (CR-054): Japan builds no heavy electrical or aluminium industry, and Pacifica licenses accordingly.
  - The Works' sealed results are not patents (CR-050) and are never licensed.
- The state's return is the taxes and the spillovers of the activity.
- Patents are abundant, not scarce. The Union's complaint becomes that Pacifica gives its inventions away. Its answer (line 852 amended, G12) is tariffs, anti-dumping duties and subsidies to its own laboratories, since compulsory licensing is pointless against patents already open.
- **The champions are the opposition.** They want exclusive licences. Leo Baekeland leads them from 1907, when his Bakelite, made in a Polytechnic chair, becomes licensable by every rival, and Tesla speaks for the open rule (A2, PROPOSED). This partly settles §5's OPEN "patent measures" (CT9).

**The state's shares.**
- **Competitive industries** (steel, motors, chemicals, electrical, film, aluminium, aircraft): no stake. The golden share survives only for a short published list of defence-critical firms (armour, propellants, the Works' suppliers), as a review of foreign takeovers.
- **Concessions** (oil and mineral leases, rail and port concessions, telegraph and power franchises): **auctioned**. The state takes the winning royalty or fee, not equity. For oil and minerals the bid is on the royalty, with a floor of one eighth and an annual rent per acre (A2, PROPOSED). Competitive bidding gives the privilege its market price, which Mises accepts as fair. It also raises the royalties that feed the Fund (C).
- **Equity only where the state owns the underlying asset:** the canal and the trunk rights of way (including the cable landings). These are the limited-space monopolies Mises concedes.

## F. MR-12: the provident account (PROPOSED)

A voluntary tax-deductible saving account on the pattern of Switzerland's pillar 3a. Its in-world name is OPEN; "the provident account" here.

| Term | Pacifica (MODEL) | Säule 3a (2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | anyone with earned income, residents and guest workers alike | anyone with AHV-liable income |
| Deductible contribution, wage earners | up to 10% of earned income, at most **$300 a year** (about a tenth of average earnings of $2,800 in 1929, CR-056) | CHF 7,258 with a pension fund |
| Deductible contribution, the self-employed and those without an employer's scheme | up to 20% of earned income, at most **$1,500** | 20% of income, at most CHF 36,288 |
| Tied until | five years before the Realm's pension age (age OPEN) | five years before AHV age |
| Early withdrawal | a home on public leasehold (CR-020 B7), at most once in five years; starting a business; leaving the Realm for good, so a guest worker carries his savings home; disability | home ownership, self-employment, permanent emigration, disability |
| Tax at withdrawal | a separate reduced rate, a fifth of the ordinary rate (MODEL) | taxed separately at a reduced rate |
| Providers | the savings bank (OPEN-020-09) and licensed banks and insurers; balances portable between them | banks and insurers |
| Investment rule | market returns under published limits, with a cap on any one issuer, **the state included**. The Fund gets the money only by competing for it. | investment rules of BVV 3 |

**Why it suits Pacifica.**
- Under falling prices (MR-01) a tied balance gains real value every year, so the incentive compounds.
- Guest workers can save in Pacifica instead of remitting at once, which slows the remittance outflow (CR-020 B11).
- Families buy leaseholds with it, the Singaporean pattern.
- **A period precedent:** Britain has given income-tax relief on life-assurance premiums since 1853, so a deduction for tied saving is not anachronistic.

**Name, members and start (A2).** The account is called **the Previsión**. Guest workers may join: they are paid out at the reduced rate on leaving for good, and keep the account if they settle. It opens in **1919**, with the peacetime income tax. The Realm's first income tax is the War Revenue Act of 1915, kept at a lower rate at the peace, and the war's savings stamps convert into the Previsión. In the workbook it raises the household saving rate (H2) and costs receipts the deduction times the marginal rate.
