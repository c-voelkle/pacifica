## What canon says now

- **Who controls defense.** From 1856 the Confederation's common institutions control "defense, the navy, customs, currency and external relations"; California and Hawaii keep domestic self-government; there is no common head of state (§8). Elias is Chancellor, elected by the Confederal Council (§8).
- **War powers.** In 1861 the Council invokes Article XIV with both member governments' assent and appoints Elias **Protector** for one year; "a separate resolution authorizes limited coercive operations, with civilian protections, no forced allegiance, no annexation by emergency decree". Both member legislatures renew the Protectorship in 1862, 1863 and 1864 (§12; CR-039). "Thorne selects its political objectives; Marcus turns them into embarkation schedules" (CT-MARCUS-I).
- **The army.** The Defense Law of 1855 establishes compulsory service on European, chiefly Prussian, lines, with reserve and territorial liability and alternative service for pacifists in an irrigation and engineering corps; "an emerging general staff plans movements by rail, river and telegraph" (§8). Carquinez students serve in the engineer corps (§8, APPROVED — CR-034). In 1861 "an army of around twenty thousand regulars with approximately ninety thousand trained reservists is a planning estimate" (§9). Member militias come within the Defense Law (Deseret's accession, §10).
- **Marcus Vance I, the founding general.** He helps create the regular army and the General Staff through the 1852 reorganization and the 1855 Defense Law, commands the Northwestern expedition in 1861, transfers field command "to a capable deputy" (name OPEN) and leaves late in 1862 for the military mission to the Confederacy, where "he cannot order Pemberton's army by virtue of a foreign mission appointment"; he returns late in 1863 or early in 1864 and "resumes senior field responsibility" (CT-MARCUS-I). His rank is not stated.
- **Sherman.** Inspector-General of the Pacifican Army, "responsible for readiness, training and reserves" (proposed office; final title and powers OPEN). He inspects the Port Townsend failure, rejects scapegoating, and "his reports give Victoria evidence for conditions on emergency renewal". He "insists that the army serves lawful government, not Thorne through a separate personal oath" (CT-SHERMAN; §9).
- **Volunteers.** Jack Hays commands some five hundred Californian volunteers in Washoe in 1860 (§8). Californio lancers, Mexican and Indigenous scouts, seamen and engineers join the early forces (§8).
- **The navy.** In 1855–56 the two governments negotiate "customs, defense, naval command and the limits of shared authority" (§8); the Confederation has "shared customs, fleet and communications" in 1861 (§9). Before the war Pacifica has "a Colorado River flotilla, imported heavy artillery and some armored warships" (§8). Ericsson's armoured battery fights on the lower Columbia in 1862 and "is not its naval commander" (§10; character bible). A Gulf squadron sails in 1870 (§13); confederal marines are on Oahu in 1876 (§17). No naval commander is named before Kiyoshi, Fleet-General from 1907 (§26).
- **Not in canon:** a ministry of war or marine; the General Staff's chief while Marcus is away; ranks; the territorial commands; the navy's chief and board; how army and navy command combined operations.

## What this CR does

It sets the chain of command from the Council to a department commander and a ship's captain; gives Marcus, Sherman and Caleb their ranks; names the territorial departments of 1861–67 and who commands each where canon or a CR draft has decided it; and sets up the navy's command under a board and a flag officer. The two most important offices canon leaves empty, the acting chief of the General Staff in 1862–64 and the navy's flag officer, stay OPEN with research tasks to find historical candidates, since the author prefers historical people to invented ones.

## Decisions

### A · Political control

| ID | Decision | Status |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Commander-in-chief.** In peace the Chancellor directs the confederal forces for the Council, which votes the defense estimates each year. In an Article XIV emergency the Protector commands them, within the Council's war resolution: the resolution is the army's mandate, and an operation beyond it (a new theatre, an annexation, a change of war aim) needs a new resolution. This is how the canon limits of 1861 (no forced allegiance, no annexation by emergency decree) bind the soldiers who carry them out | Protectorship canon (§9); the resolution as mandate PROPOSED |
| A2 | **The oath.** Officers and men swear to the Confederation and its law, not to the Protector or the Chancellor. Sherman's insistence (CT-SHERMAN) is the rule written down | Principle canon (CT-SHERMAN); the oath's form PROPOSED |
| A3 | **Two lines of report.** Department commanders report to the Protector through the General Staff. The Inspector-General reports to the Protector **and to the Council**; his inspection reports go to the member governments on request. This is why Sherman's Port Townsend findings reach Victoria and shape the renewal conditions (canon §10) | Effect canon (CT-SHERMAN); the reporting rule PROPOSED |
| A4 | **No war ministry.** Pacifica has no minister of war or marine before the 1870s. The General Staff prepares the army's estimates and the Navy Board the fleet's; the Chancellor puts them to the Council; the confederal Treasury pays. Elias's hold on the army is the Treasury's hold (canon §5: "his strength comes from controlling an increasingly indispensable treasury"). A ministry, if the author wants one, belongs to a later CR | PROPOSED |

## Storyline text (draft, for integration)

### §9, after "Sherman organizes readiness and reserves in the proposed office of Inspector-General, initially supporting protection while challenging the expanding objectives."

<!-- CR-067 -->
> **[PROPOSED — CR-067]** The Protector commands within the Council's war resolution, which is the army's mandate; an operation beyond it needs a new resolution. He commands through the General Staff that Marcus Vance built, and its department commanders answer to him; the Inspector-General reports to the Council as well, and every soldier swears to the Confederation and its law, not to the man who leads it. The common fleet is governed by a Navy Board at San Francisco, with a Hawaiian member, under a commodore who commands at sea.

### §11, after "After stabilizing the Northwest and transferring command to a deputy, Marcus accepts and leaves late in 1862 with a small staff."

> **[PROPOSED — CR-067]** While Marcus is in the Confederacy an acting chief runs the General Staff at Monterey, and the department commanders in the Columbia, the Great Basin and on the Rio Grande answer to the Protector through him.

### CT1 — Hays as "a regular officer" (CR-060 D4, draft)

This is a joint decision with CR-060. **Decided by the author, 9 November 2026: amend.** Fact changes: none.

**Current:** CT-MARCUS-I gives no rank and says only that he helps create the General Staff.
**Amended (B3):** major general and chief of the General Staff. **Recommendation:** amend; it is the least that "the founding general" and the 1862 hand-over imply. **Decided by the author, 9 November 2026: amend.** Fact changes: none.

## Research tasks

1. The Prussian model in the 1850s–60s: the War Ministry, the Military Cabinet and the Great General Staff under Moltke from 1857; how staff and administration were divided (B2, A4).
2. **Previewed 9 November 2026 (Candidates): Stone, Ord, Naglee, Denver.** Candidates for the acting chief of the General Staff and the Northwestern deputy: West Point–trained or European-trained officers who settled in California in the 1850s as civilians, as Sherman did, and who are not already Union or Confederate figures in the civil-war CRs (Halleck, Hooker and Farragut are; check CR-059 to CR-065 before proposing anyone). Canon removes the U.S. Army from California after 1846, and Stevenson's New York regiment is sent on elsewhere in 1847 (§4), so any candidate must have come as a civilian.
3. **Previewed 9 November 2026 (Candidates): Alden, Johnson, Watkins (revised); Ringgold and Woodworth set aside.** Candidates for the commodore: officers of the U.S. Navy, the Royal Navy or merchant service who settled in California or Hawaii before 1856 and could have entered confederal service (to verify against canon).
4. **Previewed 9 November 2026 (Candidates): Kalākaua, Dominis, Judd, with Kekūanāoʻa and Moehonua considered and set aside (Kekūanāoʻa, governor of Oʻahu, was about seventy and the king's father; Moehonua's command begins only in 1873).** The Hawaiian kingdom's own forces under Kamehameha IV and V: the royal guard, the militia and any vessels.
5. The U.S. Army's system of geographical departments in the 1850s–60s, as the familiar model for B7.
