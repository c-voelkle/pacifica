## Research (where the sources are)

Per the author's rule of 9 November 2026: what is not checked stays a story assumption. Items 1–22 were checked on 10 November 2026; findings, sources and verdicts are in `outputs/sessions/20261110-cr068-research/research-cr068.md`. Items 23–37 were checked the same day (same note).

1. **Lincoln's exact texts** (all speeches and letters above): *The Collected Works of Abraham Lincoln*, ed. Basler (1953), online at the University of Michigan's Digital Library (quod.lib.umich.edu/l/lincoln). Beveridge quotes the Nicolay–Hay *Works*; spot-check each quotation against Basler before prose drafting. **Partly done (10 Nov):** six texts confirmed; the 4 July question corrected (AG5, A3b, S24). Still to check: Lyceum, Peoria, the 1858 debates, the temperance address.
2. **The Ashmun amendment's date and tally** (M3): *Congressional Globe*, 30th Cong., 1st Sess., p. 95 and the House Journal (Beveridge cites Globe xviii, 93–4 for the Hudson vote; the Ashmun vote follows). Archive.org has the Globe's OCR, as used for CR-059. **Done:** 3 January 1848; 85–81 (*Globe*) or 82–81 (*Journal*); M3 updated.
3. **The 1848 votes on Frémont's expenses and survey** (M4): House Journal, 30th Cong., 1st Sess., pp. 1233–4 and 1251–2 (Beveridge 2:139 n.4, 2:155 n.). The question is what the amendments paid for, to judge whether they survive the divergence. **Done (10 Nov, from the author's scan of the House Journal):** expenses amendment rejected 40–110 (pp. 1251–52), survey 28–128 (p. 1233), Lincoln no on both; M4 updated.
4. **The December 1862 annual message** (M10): Basler, vol. 5. **Done:** confirmed; M10 updated.
5. **"One war at a time"** (M9): the attribution is late and secondhand (Hist.; to verify); Burlingame, *Abraham Lincoln: A Life* (2008), ch. on the *Trent* affair, or Seward's and Bates's diaries. If unconfirmed, the storyline sentence drops the phrase. **Done:** unconfirmed (only Case, 1916); the phrase is not used.
6. **Lincoln's wish to see the Pacific coast** (L4): Schuyler Colfax's account of 14 April 1865 and *Across the Continent* (Samuel Bowles, 1865), which reports the trip Colfax made that summer. **Done:** the wish is Mary Lincoln's account, Colfax the messenger; L4 updated. Her letter's recipient and date still to confirm.
7. **The sign at Springfield** (L1): Herndon and Weik, *Herndon's Lincoln* (1889), the chapter on the departure in February 1861. **Done:** confirmed; L1 updated.
8. **Douglass's 1876 oration** (V1): the Freedmen's Memorial address, 14 April 1876; text at the Library of Congress (Frederick Douglass Papers). **Done:** confirmed word for word; V1 updated. Moving it to Lincoln's death stays a marked divergence.
9. **Nietzsche, "Homer's Contest" (1872)**, for the frame's wording on ostracism and the agon (Part A): Kaufmann's translation in *The Portable Nietzsche* or the Cambridge *On the Genealogy of Morality* edition (appendix). **Done:** the frame agrees with the essay; no Nietzsche wording is quoted.
10. **Seward's memorandum of 1 April 1861 and Lincoln's reply** (K14): Basler, vol. 4; Seward's papers at the University of Rochester. Masters (p. 390) summarizes the memorandum but names Britain and Russia only; check which powers were to be "demanded" and which "asked". **Done:** Spain and France demanded of, Britain and Russia asked; K14 and S32 updated.
11. **The blockade proclamation of 19 April 1861 and its piracy clause** (K16): Basler, vol. 4; *Official Records of the Union and Confederate Navies*, ser. I, vol. 1, for the Pacific squadron's orders and the time they took to arrive. **Partly done:** the clause and timing confirmed (K16); the *Navy Official Records* not checked.
12. **Edward D. Baker** (K13, K20): Harry C. Blair and Rebecca Tarshis, *Colonel Edward D. Baker* (1960), and Gaillard Hunt's edition of his speeches. Check the "California" regiments and the account of the telegraph office on 21 October 1861. **Partly done:** death and Lincoln's reception of the news confirmed (K20); Blair and Tarshis not checked.
13. **Pyramid Lake and the Pony Express** (K10): the dates of the two battles (12 May and early June 1860) and Pony Express transit times; Ferol Egan, *Sand in a Whirlwind* (1972); the Pony Express National Museum. **Done:** 12 May and 2 June 1860; the news could not reach Chicago (K10).
14. **Lincoln's campaign silence in 1860** (K11): Basler, vol. 4, for his letters refusing new statements. **Done:** to Speer, 23 October 1860; to the *Missouri Republican*'s editor, 16 November 1860.
15. **Lincoln and Plutarch** (K27): the 1860 campaign biography by Howells and the anecdote that Lincoln read the *Lives* afterwards, to verify in Burlingame. **Done, corrected:** the biographer is Scripps, not Howells (K27); whether Lincoln then read it is still to verify.
16. **Logan's 1848 return** (K3): Illinois Secretary of State's records, as Beveridge cites (2:164 n.). **Partly done, with a story assumption:** Lincoln's words confirmed (Basler 1:518–19); the historical margin not found (Allen and Lacey, *Illinois Elections*, if wanted). K3 now carries a story assumption by the author's request: about 900 votes here, against about a hundred historically.
17. **The inaugural and the message of 4 July 1861 on revolution and secession** (G1): the first inaugural's passage on a majority depriving a minority of "a clearly written constitutional right" (Basler 4:267; the words are "any clearly written constitutional right"); the 4 July message (Basler 4:421–441). Check whether the phrase "morally justifiable cause" survives in the published message or only in a cancelled draft before using it. **Done:** the inaugural's words confirmed; "morally justifiable cause" is Lincoln's but only in a draft, not the published message (G1 does not use it).
18. **Hawaii's constitution of 1852 on slaves entering the kingdom** (G4): the text of the 1852 constitution (Hawaii State Archives; Kuykendall, *The Hawaiian Kingdom*, vol. 2). **Done:** Article 12, as G4 and CT8 assume.
19. **Hawaiian-language newspapers of 1861–63** (G4): *Ka Hoku o ka Pakipika* (1861–62) and *Ka Nupepa Kuokoa* (from 1861), in Papakilo Database and Chronicling America. **Partly done:** *Ka Hoku o ka Pakipika* (Kalākaua's, September 1861–1863) and Whitney's mission-backed *Ka Nupepa Kuokoa* (October 1861) confirmed; no Hawaiian printing of the Proclamation found, so G4 marks it new.
20. **British opinion and emancipation** (G4): the Manchester working men's address of 31 December 1862 and Lincoln's reply of 19 January 1863 (Basler, vol. 6); Howard Jones, *Blue and Gray Diplomacy* (2010). **Done:** 31 December 1862 and 19 January 1863.
21. **Lincoln and the Mormons through Stenhouse, 1863** (M11): the remark is reported by Stenhouse, *The Rocky Mountain Saints* (1873); check the date and wording. **Done:** June 1863, from Stenhouse's letter to Young of 7 June; not traced to *The Rocky Mountain Saints*; the log story is later and not used.
22. **The *Oregonian* in 1866** (G10): historically Lincoln carried Oregon in 1860, but here Oregon casts no vote (CR-059 H7), so the letter is addressed to its Republicans and Union men; Pittock's *Oregonian*; Bush's sale of the *Statesman*. **Done:** historically Lincoln 5,344, Breckinridge 5,074, Douglas 4,131; the *Oregonian* edited by Harvey W. Scott from 1865; Bush sold the *Statesman* in 1863, and the author has decided Bush goes (CT6).
23. **The letter to Greeley and the first draft of the Proclamation** (G4): the letter of 22 August 1862 (Basler, vol. 5), for the whole sentence quoted in G4 and A8b; the reading of the draft to the cabinet on 22 July 1862 (Welles's and Chase's diaries); and the public record Elias would have had by August: the message on compensated emancipation (6 March), abolition in the District (16 April) and the Second Confiscation Act (17 July). **Done (10 Nov):** the Greeley sentence ends "I would also do that", not "do it"; G4 and A8b corrected. The cabinet reading and the spring record of 1862 not checked here.
24. **The draft and the men who left it** (G5, G8): the Enrollment Act of 3 March 1863; the call of 18 July 1864 for 500,000 men; the historical scale of draft evasion and of the overland emigration of 1863–64 (James W. Geary, *We Need Men*, 1991; John D. Unruh, *The Plains Across*, 1979), as the baseline the story assumption exceeds; the proclamation of 10 March 1863 offering amnesty to absentees who returned; the letter to Erastus Corning and others, 12 June 1863 (Basler, vol. 6), for the form of PP6. **Done:** the call of 18 July 1864 (draft after 5 September) and the amnesty of 10 March 1863 (return by 1 April) confirmed; Corning, 12 June 1863. Geary and Unruh not checked.
25. **A loyal government made from the loyal** (G7): the restored government of Virginia (1861) and West Virginia's admission (1863); the Proclamation of Amnesty and Reconstruction, 8 December 1863 (Basler, vol. 7); the Nevada and Colorado enabling acts of March 1864 (*Statutes at Large*, vol. 13) and Colorado's vote of 1864. **Done:** both enabling acts of 21 March 1864; Colorado rejected its constitution on 11 October 1864, 4,672 to 1,520 (G7 updated).
26. **The Lyceum address on teaching reverence for the laws in schools and primers** (G9): Basler, vol. 1; Beveridge 1:229–230 prints the passage's close ("the political religion of the nation") and should be checked for the school clause. **Done:** the school-and-primer clause confirmed (G9).
27. **The letter to Conkling** (PP8): 26 August 1863 (Basler, vol. 6), for the form of Lincoln's answer to the Terms. **Done:** 26 August 1863, for the Springfield Union meeting of 3 September.
28. **The debates in print** (MD1): the Columbus edition of 1860 (Follett, Foster and Company), the text Elias would have had. **Done:** Follett, Foster, Columbus, 1860, assembled by Lincoln himself; about 30,000 copies in the first year (Duke exhibit).

30. **Lincoln's refusal to style Davis a president** (Part D): the "To Whom It May Concern" letter of 18 July 1864 and the Hampton Roads conference (Basler, vols. 7–8). **Done:** confirmed ("an authority that can control the armies"; to Blair, 18 January 1865, "Mr. Davis" and "our one common country", Basler 8:220).
31. **The response to a serenade, 10 November 1864** (G8): Basler, vol. 8; CR-065 L2 uses it. **Done:** G8 now quotes the response itself.
32. **The first inaugural's sentence on the perpetual Union** (AG2, PP1, A3): Masters 318 prints "in the contemplation of universal law, the union of these states is perpetual". 33. **Louisiana and the Congress** (G7): Congress's refusal to seat the members of Lincoln's Louisiana government and the Wade–Davis bill of July 1864 (Basler, vol. 7; Herman Belz, *Reconstructing the Union*, 1969). **Done with a story assumption:** Basler 8:206–207 (Lincoln to Trumbull, 9 January 1865, and the note that Cutler and Smith were not seated) and 8:270 (H.R. 126); Trumbull's report of 18 February 1865 is S. Rept. 38-127. The Globe pages were not reached. G7 now states the historical case and, by the author's request, a story assumption for Port Townsend's memorial; the senators-elect of earlier revisions are gone.
34. **Raymond's proposal and the instructions Lincoln did not send** (G8): Raymond to Lincoln, 22 August 1864, and the draft of 24 August (Basler, vol. 7); CR-065 S6. **Done:** Lincoln's draft of 24 August 1864 (Basler 7:517) told Raymond to propose peace to Davis on restoration of the Union alone; not sent: on 25 August Lincoln, Seward, Stanton and Fessenden met Raymond and decided against the mission (Nicolay and Hay 9:221, via the Lincoln Log).
35. **The Dakota war and Lincoln** (G4): the trials, Lincoln's review and the order of 6 December 1862, the executions of 26 December, Ramsey's message of 9 September 1862, the acts of 16 February and 3 March 1863, and the annual message of 1 December 1862 on the Indian system (Basler, vol. 5; David A. The Aborigines' Protection Society (founded 1837) and its journal. **Done, two corrections:** Ramsey said "beyond the borders of Minnesota"; Lincoln approved 39 of 303 and reprieved one. Acts of 16 February, 21 February (Winnebago) and 3 March 1863; the annual message's "whether our Indian system shall not be remodeled"; the colonization address of 14 August 1862. No 1860s action of the Aborigines' Protection Society on American Indians found (G4 marks it new). Lincoln's order to Sibley of 6 December 1862 is Basler 5:542–43 (via the Lincoln Log).
36. **The right of search and the news of Bull Run** (G2): Seward's answer in the *Trent* case, 26 December 1861, which released the envoys without renouncing the right; the Pony Express and telegraph times for news of 21 July 1861; the Clearwater and Salmon River strikes of 1860–61. **Done:** Seward's reply kept the right of search; Bull Run news reached San Francisco on 2 August 1861; Clearwater 1860, Salmon River 1861.
37. **Jefferson on a separate republic in the West** (G6): to John Breckinridge, 12 August 1803; to Joseph Priestley, 29 January 1804; to John Jacob Astor, 9 November 1813, for the words quoted (Founders Online, National Archives, prints all three). Lincoln to Henry L. Pierce and others, 6 April 1859 (Basler, vol. 3). **Done:** all three Jefferson letters and the Pierce letter confirmed on Founders Online and Lincoln texts; to Breckinridge Jefferson wrote "keep them in union if it be for their good, but separate them if it be better".
38. **How news crossed to the Pacific in 1862–64** (Part H TL5, B8, B15; CT17): no supplied draft fixes the route in wartime. Historically the overland telegraph reached Salt Lake City in October 1861 and news then crossed in hours, where the Pony Express had taken about ten days and the Panama steamers three to four weeks (Hist.; to verify). Here the wire would cross a war frontier and Deseret's country (CR-060). To settle: whether a wire exists and who controls it, and how early partial returns could reach Monterey. Sources: Robert Luther Thompson, *Wiring a Continent* (1947); the *Daily Alta California* for November 1864 (California Digital Newspaper Collection). **Open.** The same question sets the window for the blind vote (G8, B15): how many days after 8 November 1864 the result could reach Monterey, and whether Monterey could be sure it had not arrived.
39. **Lincoln's stories** (Part H TL8): only recorded ones may be used. Whether it was reprinted before the 1860s decides how else a copy could reach Monterey. **Open.**
