## What this CR does

1. **It governs Alaska from the first day, by consent.** Alaska is a territory of the Confederation. On Elias's bill, not by his cabinet's ordinance, the confederal chambers charter a Northern Commission under Pablo de la Guerra and extend the Mining Law of 1849, with an assay office, claims courts and a land office, but nothing is granted or registered on a nation's country without that nation's compact. The survey starts from the Russian American Company's own papers. Northern Alaska and Hokkaido become one school, and argue.
2. **It writes the reward into compacts, and the nations bargain.** A mine on a nation's land holds it by that nation's lease; the finder's share is negotiated on top of the Confederation's 5% and the lease. Kowee holds back Gastineau's source until his people's share is agreed, and Juneau's gold is worked from 1872. Daniel Libby, who found gold on the Niukluk with the telegraph survey in 1866, leads the Commission's parties to the Seward Peninsula from 1876.
3. **It makes the first years cheap.** From 1873 the garrison falls to 40% of its first strength, and its soldiers stay as settlers. Sitka is a station, not a fortress.
4. **It takes the north's rents, and pays for them.** The seal lease is re-tendered at a price per skin, the Unangan sealers are paid by the skin under their islands' own compact, and the herd is saved by a four-power convention proposed in 1884 and signed in 1886. A salmon case tax runs from 1870, with the nations' fisheries reserved by compact. The Commission's own suppliers, the whalers, starve St Lawrence Island in 1878–80.
5. **It wires Alaska, not Siberia.** The Bering telegraph is a road not taken (author, 15 November 2026). The Confederation buys Western Union's British Columbia line and finishes it only as far as Alaska: down the Stikine to Wrangell and by a cable through the straits to Sitka in 1870, to Juneau with the gold and to Dyea with the road. Alaska relies on this line across British, then Canadian, soil until 1888, when a sea cable from Puget Sound to Sitka, ordered after the Port Hamilton crisis, is laid; in 1897, once the Klondike news is public, the British Columbia line is sold to Canada as its wire to the Yukon. In 1900–1903 the Sitka cable is carried on by Kodiak, Unalaska and the Kurils to Hakodate, canon's second route to Japan, on Pacifican and allied soil only. In 1904 a Russian cruiser comes for it, and Kiyoshi keeps it from being cut.
6. **It makes the road to the Yukon a compact.** The Chilkoot make it and the Chilkat refuse; the Chilkoot and the Commission build the road, so the route is ready before the Klondike, whose news the packers carry over the pass.
7. **It tests the station.** A sentry's killing at Sitka is paid for under Tlingit law.

### D · The seals and the salmon

| ID | Decision | Status |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **The seal lease, tendered, and the sealers.** Historically the Union reserved the Pribilofs in 1869, after two unregulated seasons in which at least 60,000 seals were taken in 1869 alone, and granted the Alaska Commercial Company of San Francisco a twenty-year lease in 1870: $55,000 rent and $2.625 a skin on 100,000 skins, $317,500 a year (Hist.: NOAA; House Report 623). > <!-- CR-070: the Northern Commission, the finder's compacts and the north's rents -->**[APPROVED — CR-070]** Alaska is a territory of the Confederation from the day the flag changes, and the confederal chambers charter a Northern Commission under Pablo de la Guerra to govern it; George Davidson, the coast surveyor, succeeds him in 1873.
