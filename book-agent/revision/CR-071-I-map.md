## I1. The drawing rules — APPROVED (I-6)

As in E4, with what this part adds:

1. **Watershed first** (Powell, 1878).
2. **One railway station or port as seat**, within a day's travel of every part of the county.
3. **Population within a band** around the average (about 47,000 in 1887), widened in the empty interior, where the day's travel governs.
4. **Language respected** where a county can be drawn round a Spanish- or Hawaiian-speaking district.
5. **The historic lands keep their outline.** California's outer boundary survives as county lines; Hawaii's is the sea. Elsewhere no county reproduces a member, none is named for one, and no county's lines enclose a member whole.
6. **Homelands are drawn round, not through** (I-8).

A consequence: outside California and Hawaii the counties cross the old member lines, so the counts "by old unit" in I3 are accounting, not boundaries.

## I4. The homelands — I-8 and I-9 APPROVED; the rest PROPOSED

- **Two sizes.** The large homelands are units of their own on the map, outside any county: the Diné country (the test: larger than any county, and across the old New Mexico and Arizona lines), the Ute country of the Western Slope and the Parks, and others to be listed region by region. The small ones (the Californian homelands, the Pueblo grants) lie inside a county's lines; the county's board, taxes and courts stop at their boundary unless the nation contracts for a service.
- **The relation** runs to the Realm under each compact; disputes go to the Court of the Realm at Monterey. County powers, boundaries and grades are organic law, amendable without local consent (§25); a homeland's are not.
- **They do not count toward the 124.**
- **Voting (I-9).** A member of a nation who lives in a county registers and votes there like anyone else, and votes only where he is registered.
- **The Senate (I-9).** PROPOSED: **six homeland seats**, beside the eight aliʻi seats, each chosen by the councils of the compact nations of one region, in their own way: California; the Columbia and the Sound; the Basin (Shoshone, Paiute, Washoe and the Utes of Deseret); the Rockies' Utes; the Diné; the Pueblos and the Apache. Whether Alaska's nations have a seventh while Alaska is a territory is OPEN.
