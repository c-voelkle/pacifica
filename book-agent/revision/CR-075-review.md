# CR-075 — Fresh-context review (consistency and literary effect)

**Reviewer:** a separate context, which read only the CR-075 files, AGENTS.md, docs/authority-and-scope.md and the canon passages named in the brief (master_storyline.md §§1–12 and §29, CR-068, CR-059, CR-060, CR-061, CR-062, CR-065, CR-067, CR-069, CR-074, CR-029, the character bible's Elias and Victoria entries, the registry, and the ledger entry C-1861-PROTECTOR). It read nothing under `outputs/` or `research/`, and no commit messages. Historical judgements come from the reviewer's own knowledge, with a confidence given for each. Date: 19 November 2026.

## Verdict

**Ready for adoption after fixes, with no redesign needed.** The design is sound and does what CR-068 CT3 asked for. The five objects are clearly worded, and each has an owner, a reading and an end. The rule that each resolution names its object gives the B4 test something it can check. California's and Hawaii's reasons are now their own, and the sittings make "how does he know?" concrete without making Elias prophetic. The storyline anchors are all at the stated lines. The problems are local:
- three internal contradictions about dates and people (Wyllie's letter, the timing of Burch's address, who knows of the sealed minute);
- two cases of impossible knowledge (Beale's "our world", Casey's drill book of 1862);
- one contradiction with canon on the Northwest's forts;
- a mis-cited object (the Walla Walla convention);
- a wrong place (Vallejo's 1848 office);
- a group of storyline blocks whose wording drifts from the parts (the first object, Washoe, the parks, Burch's "territories").

None of them needs an author decision beyond the wording. Integrate once the Must-fix items are corrected.

## Findings

### Must fix

**M1. Wyllie's letter is asked for three different ways.** Part D.
- D1 table, row 2: within days of the June offer, "Victoria's delegation asks Wyllie for his estimate by the next steamer". D1.2 itself says no answer from Honolulu can come back in time.
- D1.2: Wyllie's estimate arrives "only if he sent it unasked … PROPOSED: he did. Victoria asked for it in May".
- D-9 (decision text): "Wyllie's estimate arrives unasked by the steamer after the packet affair."

"Unasked" and "Victoria asked for it in May" contradict each other, and the D1 row describes a request that D1.2 rules out.
*Fix:* keep D1.2's working version, in which Victoria asks for the estimate in May before she sails and Wyllie sends it by a later steamer. Strike "unasked" from D1.2 and D-9's restatement. Change the D1 row to "Wyllie's letter, asked for in May, arrives by the June steamer". This also fits the May calendar (S2 below).

**M2. Burch's address is answered before it is moved.**
- Part C C6.3 gives "The motion (July 1861)". The outline's P20 says he "Asks the Council in July 1861", and storyline S6 places it in July.
- Part D's D1 table, D2.3 and Part I's I-B5 have the address "read and answered" at the second closed sitting in late June. C8 adds "Burch's address is answered by the wording" on the Council's night.

*Fix:* date the address to the third week of June, when the Assembly sits in the days after Lincoln's offer is published. The closed sitting gives the private answer (C6.4) and the July wording gives the public one (C6.5). Change C6.3's heading, P20 and S6 ("In June …") to match.

**M3. Who knows of the sealed minute.**
- Part F F5.1: the island aim "is the Kamehameha house's: the king, Victoria and Lot, with Wyllie".
- Part I I-B8 (the Palmyra commission, 1862): "Victoria signs … beside the king … nobody else in the room knows there is one to mention."
- F5.5 also reads as if the minute were hers alone ("an unpublished one of her own").

The king signs beside her and, on F5.1, shares the aim. A minute that formed part of Hawaii's price on the Council's night would also be reported to the king's government.
*Fix:* in I-B8, write "nobody in the room says it aloud; the king knows, the clerk does not". In F5.5, change "of her own" to "of the kingdom's, which she keeps".

**M4. Beale knows our world.** Part E, E7.4: "Beale does it because he knows from 1861 in our world how much a printed answer can do." No character has our world.
*Fix:* give the reason inside Pacifica, for example: "Beale knows from the Republic's own press wars, the convention fight of 1856 and Carson, how much a printed answer can do." Alternatively, move the remark to the Research note as the writer's analogue.

**M5. Sherman cites a book that does not yet exist.** Part D, D4 item 5: Sherman knows Casey "as a careful officer who wrote the book of drill (Hist.)". Casey's *Infantry Tactics* is of 1862; the CR's own Research note says "author of the infantry tactics of 1862". In June 1861 the book has not been written. Confidence: high.
*Fix:* "a careful officer, a drillmaster of the old 9th, who held San Juan in 1859 against a British squadron without firing" (the San Juan service is in the same note), or simply "a careful officer of the old army".

**M6. The forts of the Northwest contradict canon.**
- Part C C3.1: "what it has left on the coast is anchorages without forts … The first object asks that this remain so".
- Part D D3.2 lists among the things *established*: "there is no fort on the Columbia or the Sound (HUNT 234, 304)". The outline's C8 has "every federal dollar had gone to San Francisco Bay".

The Hist. fact does not carry over to Pacifica. In Pacifica San Francisco Bay was never American, so Washington's Pacific money had nowhere else to go. Canon says so twice: CR-059 H7 has "appropriations for the Columbia bar and Port Townsend (Casey's foothold in 1861)" after 1859, and §10 (l. 423) has "Federal defenses strengthened after Washoe".
*Fix:* restate the established fact as "the Union's posts on the river and the Sound are new, few and lightly built; Congress voted money for the Columbia bar and Port Townsend in 1859, and Washington reinforced them after Washoe". In C3.1, make the first object ask that what was begun after 1859 be removed. This also improves D4 item 5 and D5: the evidence that Port Townsend mattered to Washington was on the table in June, and Marcus weighed it and read it otherwise. D4 item 5's "a careful officer defends what he has fortified, not an open anchorage" then has to change too (see S12).

**M7. The Walla Walla convention cites the wrong object.** Part C, C7.2 table, row 1: "The Walla Walla convention | July 1862 | First (the slope's posts) and fourth". C3.1 (C-6, APPROVED) defines the slope as running to the crests and says "The posts beyond the crests (The Dalles, Walla Walla, Colville …) are not named in the first object." Part G's G2 has it right: "First (left to the line) and fourth".
*Fix:* in C7.2, write "Fourth (protection) and the first object's reach by the river; the posts beyond the crest are left to the line (C-6)".

**M8. Vallejo answered Buchanan in Washington.** Part I, I-B3: "Vallejo reads it in the office where he answered Buchanan in 1848." Canon §4 has the convention negotiated and signed in Washington (l. 195–197), and §12 (l. 589) has "the convention he signed in the same city in 1848".
*Fix:* "Vallejo reads it in the President's office at Monterey, with his copy of the convention of 1848 on the desk."

### Should fix

**S1. The first object loses "either of its members" in the storyline.** Part C's C2 reads "from which the Confederation or either of its members can be struck"; F5.4 and G1 follow it. C10 and storyline S5 shorten this to "from which the Confederation can be struck". The member clause matters, because it is what lets Hawaii's islands count as the kingdom's defence. *Fix:* restore it in S5 and C10.

**S2. The May 1861 calendar is now very tight, and the CR should show that it works.** Canon already needs, between the packet affair and 1 June, Hawaii's formal assent to Article XIV by its own constitutional procedure. Canon says "no Hawaiian public document is valid without her signature" (l. 257). CR-075 adds three things:
- Baker's note in the first week of May, *before* the packet affair (E-6);
- a joint answer about ten days later;
- Victoria's arrival at Monterey "in May with Hawaii's demands" (D-9).

That puts one Honolulu round trip into roughly three weeks, the same interval CR-068 CT13 says cannot hold one in June–July.
*Fix:* add one sentence to F1.3 or D1. The packet affair falls in the first half of May. Victoria signs the kingdom's assent at Honolulu before she sails, carrying it with Hawaii's demands, and asks Wyllie for his estimate as she leaves (see M1). She reaches Monterey in the last days of May. I-B4's "Late May or June" then reads "June".

**S3. Storyline S6: Burch's "five territories" and his letter.** S6 says "the five territories of his letter of 1860", and I-B6 has "his five territories". California is one of the five, and it is not a territory. The storyline has also never introduced the letter: in our world it was a call to leave the Union, and in Pacifica its content has to be different. S6 also sits after a sentence that ends in October ("killed at Ball's Bluff"), yet tells July events. *Fix:* "…the frontier of the Rockies, and Oregon, Washington, Utah and New Mexico, the lands his open letter of November 1860 had invited into the Republic, as its objects". Place S6 before "Baker goes east…", or open it with "That summer". Date the address as in M2.

**S4. Storyline S7 refers forward four times and puts Palmyra in the Oregon section.** Placed after l. 427, S7 names things the storyline introduces only later:

- the Steamer Day Agreement (l. 441);

**S5. Storyline S9 and Part G's count disagree with canon and with G-3.**

Canon (§10, l. 473) has it retaken "without a fight"; its legislature and county officials sign a civil compact, its voters had just rejected a constitution, and some of its miners had wanted annexation (l. 377). Part C (C3.4, C7.2) and G2 say "its people did not ask", and that wording is right. *Fix:* "Washoe, retaken without its people's asking".
- (b) S9, G8 and G0.2 count "the empty mountain parks" as an exception to the objects. G-3 (APPROVED) says they *pass* B4 "by the price alone". The price is announced in the preamble ("What the Confederation keeps … it will pay for"), so they follow from a rule named in 1861. *Fix:* G0.2 says "all but two items" (Washoe, Alaska), with the parks noted as passing by the price only. S9 reads "…except Washoe … The empty mountain parks the line takes and the gold pays for, as the preamble said." S9 also sits before the parks are described (l. 594). Place it after l. 594, or after the Colorado paragraph.

**S6. Where the guano islands were claimed from.**
- Part C C3.1 says the rocks were claimed "from Honolulu's own harbour", Johnston (1858) included.
- G2 says "Claimed for the United States from Honolulu in 1857–59".
- Part F (F5.2, F5.3 step 3, F-8, from the author's own research) has the *Palestine* fitted out and sailing from San Francisco, and the whole plot of 1858 rests on that ("the ship that took it sailed on his government's paper").

*Fix:* "from Honolulu's harbour, and Johnston from San Francisco's" in C3.1 and G2.

**S7. The rule of 1859 is applied to Brooks but not to Judd.** F5.3 step 4: "Brooks, on a Honolulu-owned and Hawaiian-registered ship, breaks the rule at Midway that summer and loses his clearance. Judd's *Josephine* claims Palmyra for the United States." The *Josephine* also worked out of Honolulu (F5.2). G. P. Judd is a canon person (§6, the princes' tour of 1849), and his son is Victoria's schoolmate (character bible). *Fix:* either the *Josephine*'s claim predates the rule, with the date stated, or Judd loses his clearance too. The second is the better story: a mission-family elder broken by the kingdom's own rule.

**S8. Who makes the rule of 1859.** F5.3 step 4 calls it "a confederal rule". Storyline S1 says "the Republic and the kingdom forbid…". Clearance and external relations are confederal (§8, l. 336: customs and external relations are common). *Fix:* S1: "In 1859 the Confederation forbids…".

**S9. Ralston sits in a sealed war council before he takes the oath.** CR-069 and §10 (l. 441) have Ralston take the Republic's oath in July 1861. Part D has him give his part in person at the second closed sitting in late June (D2.3, D3.4, D6). An Ohio-born banker with a partner in New York, not yet a citizen, is heard in a sealed sitting about the war. That is a knowledge and authority risk, and also an opportunity. *Fix:* add one line saying he is heard on his own part only (D-6 already says so), sworn to secrecy, and that the oath of July follows the night. Better still, the sitting is where he decides.

Write "fight the column at the second Valverde"; strike or explain "second resolution".

D4 item 5 contradicts D3.2.** Marcus answers that "a careful officer defends what he has fortified", while D3.2 has "no fort on the … Sound" as *established* at the same sittings. *Fix:* "defends his post, his stores and the anchorage Congress paid for, not a raid's beach". **S22. **S23. Two historical claims, from the reviewer's own knowledge.**
- *Burch's birthplace.* C6.1 has Burch born "in Boone County, Missouri, across the river from Franklin", and the integration checklist repeats "born in Boone County". Franklin stands in Howard County on the north bank of the Missouri. Across the river from it are Boonville and Cooper County. Boone County lies east of Howard, on the same bank. Confidence on the geography: high. On Burch's actual birthplace (the Biographical Directory may say "near Fayette, Howard County"): moderate. *Fix:* check the Directory's wording. If it is Howard County, the Boonslick link is stronger ("born a few miles from Franklin"). If it is Boone County, strike "across the river from Franklin" and say "a day's ride from Franklin".
- *Ericsson in 1854.* The outline's P3 and D3.2 say "the Navy Department refused his armoured ship in 1854". Ericsson's armoured-ship design of 1854 was offered to Napoleon III, who declined it; it was not offered to the U.S. Navy Department. Confidence: medium-high. Canon (l. 311) says only that the 1854 proposal failed, and CR-068 G3 that it "had found no builder". *Fix:* "no government would build his armoured ship in 1854".

**S24. The privateer rule has no canon source.** E4.3 item 2 and storyline S2 (APPROVED as to the answer) say "the Republic already receives no privateer of any flag", citing Hawaii's rule of 1854 and the note of 1856. It says nothing about receiving privateers. *Fix:* either add a PROPOSED line that the Confederation adopted Hawaii's rule of 1854 for its ports when the Articles came into force, or soften the clause to "issues no letter of marque and receives none of its own making".

### Consider

**K1. Opposition on the Council's night.** C8 says the result "is not close", so in July the opposition sits outside the room (Burch, Starr King, the New York houses) and inside the sealed sittings (Sherman). Consider one recorded vote against the preamble, for example the two mission-family members of Hawaii's delegation (who vote against in 1865, §12) voting against the fourth object. It costs nothing, it is consistent with F4.3, and it gives the night a visible fault line that the renewals of 1862–64 then widen.

**K2. Keep the promise of 1858 political (B6).** "Elias promises her the island back" can read as a husband's gift, and B6 says Victoria is not a sentimental figure and her reasons are Hawaii's interests. The CR already has the corrective: in 1861 she moves the island clause herself and holds the minute. Consider one line in I-B2 or F5.6 in which she answers as Kuhina Nui ("the island is the kingdom's to ask for, not yours to give"). Her answer stays unreported if the author prefers; then make the corrective visible in I-B4, where she moves the clause.

**K3. Sherman's forecast.** D3.1: "Sherman says it will be lost by whoever attacks first with raw men, and Bull Run is three weeks away." This is history, but on the page it reads as prophecy, and the respondent should be clever too, not prophetic. CR-068 G2 removed Bull Run from reason 4 for this reason. Late June is also nearer four weeks before 21 July. Consider keeping Sherman's general judgement (the first volunteer armies will fail) and dropping the countdown.

**K4. Elias's own part (D3.7).** All three lessons from his life are lessons that come true. If his Querétaro lesson ("a government that could not hold California recognized the republic that did") is visibly the one that misleads him about the Rio Grande (D5), his single part carries one of his errors. That would be the cleanest proof of "clever, not prophetic", and it would also fix S11.

**K5. Beale at San Pasqual (E7.5).** Gillespie's party sallying from San Diego to join Kearny is itself a breach of the naval standstill (§4). Saying so would sharpen the letter of 1861: the man asking Pico to stand by the objects once rode out of a Californian port against them.

**K6. Ralston's rule in 1862 (D3.4, I-B10).** Because the rule is in the sealed minutes of 1861, Elias reaching for an export ban in February 1862 can read as forgetfulness rather than temptation. The "new form" (coin leaking by private routes) helps. Consider making the temptation explicit: the run has frightened him, and he knows the rule and wants the decree anyway.

**K7. G6, Victoria in 1867.** "Her doubt of the fourth object … is answered by Washoe, and she says so" is ambiguous: answered for or against her? One clause would settle it, for example "…and Washoe proves her right".

## Checked and consistent

- **Literary effect.** Agency is real and spread out. Vallejo moves the second object and carries the 1862 renewal. Victoria wins the third object and holds the keys to the renewals. Beale acts unasked. Ralston's rule beats Elias's decree. Burch pushes and loses. Starr King is left free as the Republic's best answer, and Wyllie and the king each act on their own instinct. California's four reasons (titles, tax, the treaties, the leash; money; the two halves) are believable as Californians' own. Hawaii's power motive (navy, harbour, islands, weight at sea) sits credibly beside its moral reasons, apart from S16. Elias speaks least at the sittings, his own errors are traced to him, and one error is nobody's. "Clever, not prophetic" holds, and K3 and K4 would strengthen it. The spread of points of view in Part I (Vallejo, Victoria, Sherman, de la Guerra, Pico, Lincoln, the Chronicler, and Elias once) matches its stated aim.
