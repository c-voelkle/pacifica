# CR-076 — fresh-context review

**Reviewer:** fresh context (given AGENTS.md, CR-076.md DRAFT 3 including its Canon tensions, and the canon passages below; no writer's notes, no session files, no git log). **Lint:** `lint_canon.py --only CR-076.md` is clean (1 file checked). Line numbers refer to `master_storyline.md` unless marked otherwise.

## Verdict

The CR is coherent in design and mostly consistent with canon. Its customs, laws and homeland rules hang together, and its historical anchors (Turley, Lahaina, *Rex v. Booth*, Salt Lake's store, Gothenburg, the 1875 den ordinance, Kato, the Batangas rust) are well chosen and, as far as I can check them, correct. It is not ready to place as written. Six problems block it. The 1866 Measures Law and the 1867 county option have no stated authority, and they cut across CR-071's "nothing is taken from the members". The coffee paragraph calls Kona and the Philippines "the Realm". Kato's fellowship is dated to the Concord. Two Part N paragraphs cannot be placed as written. The homelands paragraph gives every nation a patrimony and a court, which canon reserves to chartered nations. Below that come a dozen smaller fixes: authority, internal contradictions, invented offices and some untrue wording that would break the CR's own rule A4. On the literary side, the Part N prose reads as a list of rules more than a lived culture. It has a handful of epigrams the author dislikes. It also passes up conflicts that canon already supplies: Broderick's saloon machine, San Francisco in 1875, and Hawaii under the Territorial Council.

## Must fix

**M1. The 1866 Measures Law and the 1867 county Spirit Stores have no stated authority.**
- *CR:* D4, D5, D6, D10 and Part N §12 ("The Confederation's Measures Law of 1866 makes Hawaii's and California's rule common… From 1867 any county board may take the retail of spirits into a public store").
- *Canon:* l. 338 says the common institutions control "defense, the navy, customs, currency and external relations", and the members keep the residual powers. l. 590 says Hawaii won "the agreed revenues and … the list of protected provisions" in the 1865 bargain. l. 592: "the Hawaiian crown, Kuhina Nui and local institutions are protected." l. 598 (CR-071): "Nothing is taken from the members; everything new goes round them."
- *Problem:* A confederal excise on spirits made at home, a price floor, licensing hours and retail rules in 1866 take a police and tax power from the members. That contradicts l. 598 and touches Hawaii's protected revenues. The 1867 county option assumes county boards in every member, but Hawaii has none before the Realm. Canon first mentions Hawaiian counties for 1928, at l. 851. The CR does not say who passes the law: the chambers, the cabinet under the Enabling Law, or the members.
- *Fix:* Split the instrument in two.
  - The confederal part is customs and standards only: the duty on imported spirits and opium, the licensed importers, and the assay standard.
  - The members adopt the floor, the label, the hours and the county option by their own acts in 1866–67. California's legislature copies its 1852 law. Hawaii's legislature under Lot extends its 1860 law. Each acceding member takes the same terms in its charter.
  - Alternatively, say plainly that this is cabinet legislation under the Enabling Law, that the duty collected in Hawaii stays in Hawaii's agreed revenues, and that the county option applies only where county boards exist. Either way, Part N §12 needs one clause naming the instrument.

**M2. Kona and the Philippines are called "the Realm".**
- *CR:* Part N coffee paragraph ("Kona's smallholders … grow the Realm's best beans"; "a fifth or so of what they drink is grown in the Realm"). The same wording appears in L4 ("Share of home demand grown in the Realm") and L5.
- *Canon:* AGENTS.md l. 67: "Julian's Realm, Hawaiian and Philippine crowns form one party." The Philippine crown dates from 1907 (CR-029/CR-040), and Hawaii's crown is joined to Pacifica's by the Act of Settlement (l. 1162).
- *Problem:* By the 1920s, coffee grown at Kona and in the Philippines is not grown in "the Realm".
- *Fix:* Write "grown under the Pacific crowns" or name the places ("Kona and the Philippine highlands"). In L4/L5, separate the Realm's consumption from the crowns' production.

**M3. Kato's "Concord-era fellowship" in 1901.**
- *CR:* L3 ("works in San Francisco instead, on a Concord-era fellowship").
- *Canon:* AGENTS.md l. 91 and l. 67. The Concord replaces the 1887 compact in 1927, and its first term is 1927–1932.
- *Problem:* This is an anachronism of a quarter-century.
- *Fix:* "on a travelling fellowship of the Japanese compact", "under the 1887 compact's exchange", or simply "a Polytechnic fellowship".

**M4. The §1 paragraph cannot be placed as written, and its labels are wrong.**
- *CR:* Part N §1, "after 'Reuben pays him in mules and favours…'".
- *Canon:* l. 131. The anchor sentence sits in the middle of the Colonel's paragraph, and it is **[PROPOSED — CR-066]**. The next sentence ("Among his few valuable possessions…") relies on "his" meaning the Colonel.
- *Problems:*
  - Inserting the paragraph there splits the Colonel paragraph, and "his" would then point to Turley or Reuben.
  - The paragraph is labelled APPROVED, but it extends a PROPOSED sentence and states the story assumption ("Reuben deals at Turley's") as approved.
  - The closing "**[PROPOSED — CR-076]** The scene is a story assumption." is edit-memo voice (AGENTS l. 13), and it labels something that has already been said.
- *Fix:*
  - Place the paragraph after l. 133 (the end of the Colonel paragraphs) or after l. 128 (the Taos winters).
  - Open with **[APPROVED — CR-076]** for Turley, the mill, Taos Lightning and the robe trade.
  - Put **[PROPOSED — CR-076]** before "Reuben deals at Turley's…" and the morning of 1835–36.
  - Delete the memo sentence.

**M5. "§19 or §25" is not a placement.**
- *CR:* the Part N customs paragraph.
- *Canon:* l. 851 (§19, CR-049: "Public provings of works, county showings…"), which is the customs paragraph that this text extends.
- *Fix:*
  - Anchor it in §19, after l. 851.
  - Move the last sentence ("The Republic's ships have no spirit ration…; the railways dismiss…") to wherever D2 and D3 actually start. The navy belongs with §8's regular army and navy (l. 295) and the railways with §13. Alternatively, say "since the Republic's first ships and railways" so that it can stay in §19.

**M6. The homelands paragraph and D1/F2/F3/F6 give every nation what canon gives only chartered nations.**
- *CR:* Part N §5 ("answers to the court the nation names", for 1852), F3 ("It keeps all of it"), F6 and Part N homelands ("Royalties go to the nation's patrimony, not as cash shares"; the patrimony "pays for … its raised companies").
- *Canon:*
  - l. 1018 (CR-071): a nation that meets the standard "becomes chartered. It is then its own trustee and keeps the lease fee the Realm has always taken. It holds its royalties in a patrimony of its own … and keeps a court of first instance and its own police." The ladder's law has no year yet (**[OPEN]**).
  - l. 233: a homeland "can be leased only through the Republic, which keeps a fee".
  - l. 967 (CR-074): the host pays the companies, and their warrants are paid in land.
- *Problem:* In 1852 no nation has a court the Republic recognises. Before a nation is chartered, its royalties and fees go through the Realm as trustee, and the Realm keeps the lease fee. F2 handles this correctly, but §5's text and the homelands paragraph contradict F2. Raised companies are not paid from a nation's patrimony.
- *Fix:*
  - §5: "…answers in the Republic's court on the nation's complaint, under the nation's ordinance."
  - Homelands paragraph: "Royalties go into the nation's account, held in trust by the Realm until the nation is chartered and then in its own patrimony, never as cash shares."
  - F3: say whether an unchartered nation's store keeps the duty itself or the Realm holds it in trust for the nation. Keeping it outright is a defensible exception, but it must be stated as one.
  - F6: change "pays for … its raised companies" to "may charter companies".

## Should fix

**S1. Victoria's mechanism leaves out the reason the author gave.**
- *CR:* §0 ("The CR gives that influence a mechanism (Part G1)"), G1 and Part N §8.
- *Author decision quoted in §0:* the historical Victoria's drinking stays out "because of Elias's early positive influence".
- *Problems:* G1 and Part N make her decision come from her brother alone, and Elias appears only as her husband of ten months. Part N §8 also never says that drink played any part at Lahaina (R3 is unresolved), so the reader cannot see why "she takes the pledge that winter".
- *Fix:*
  - Keep the decision hers, but put Elias's example into it. For instance, she asks him for the 1852 table, she knows he drinks only when others pour, or he says nothing and simply sets the figures in front of her.
  - If R3 confirms the historical accounts, say that the king had been drinking. If R3 is uncertain, write "after the day's drinking on the royal party's excursion" only once it is verified.

**S2. Hawaii from 1876 to 1907 is not addressed.**
- *CR:* D10 ("Realm-wide from 1888"), Part N §25 and K7.
- *Canon:* l. 790 and l. 890. Hawaii is governed by the appointed Territorial Council created by the illegal ordinances of 1874–1876. The Act of Settlement restores the legislature in 1907 (l. 1162). Line 888 makes Hawaii a historic land of the Realm.
- *Problem:* If the Spirit Stores reach Hawaii in 1888, the Territorial Council runs a public spirits and opium monopoly in Honolulu. That would be a loyalist grievance, and it would fall in the year before the Wilcox rising (l. 929). The CR does not say who runs the stores there, or whether Hawaii's own legislature keeps them after 1907.
- *Fix:* Add a G1 sentence on Hawaii between 1888 and 1907. One option is that Victoria, as Queen, has the stores run by the Board of Public Health of 1886 (l. 878, Noe's board), not by the Territorial Council. State what happens after 1907.

**S3. The Philippines: G3 says more than canon does and leaves a gap on opium.**
- *CR:* G3 ("Pacifica ends Spain's monopolies in 1876").
- *Canon:* l. 740: "Forced labour, forced crop sales and the tobacco monopoly end." No other monopoly is named.
- *Problems:* Canon does not say that the wine monopoly ends. Verify when the Spanish wine *estanco* was abolished; it may have gone before 1876. The CR also says nothing about the Spanish opium farm for Chinese smokers (1843–1898) or whether Part K applies in the islands.
- *Fix:* Change G3 to "the tobacco monopoly ends (CR-029), and no spirits monopoly replaces it". Add one line on opium in the Philippines, either "the Philippine Council ends the opium farm and applies K3's licence" or "left to the Philippine Council".

**S4. Opium "taxed on the same ladder" cannot work.**
- *CR:* Part N §12 and K3.
- *Problem:* The ladder is defined by alcoholic strength, "each band of strength pays more per degree" (D4), so it cannot measure opium.
- *Fix:* "assayed for its morphine and taxed on a ladder of its own by that content". K1's "taxed by strength" already points this way.

**S5. The 1866 label says more than the figures show, which breaks A4.**
- *CR:* D6 ("the life office's figures on deaths among men who drink daily") and Part N §12 ("the life offices' figures on men who drink every day"). See also B4: the Republic has "its own figures" only "by the 1870s".
- *Problem:* The British sectional figures compare a self-selected abstainers' section with a general section of non-abstainers, not with daily drinkers. A label of 1866 that claims more would be the kind of overstatement the CR condemns in the Union's schoolbooks.
- *Fix:* "the life offices' figures: deaths among insured abstainers set beside deaths among other insured men". From the 1870s, add "and the Republic's own".

**S6. K4 misreads the convention "each people is kept in order by its own".**
- *CR:* K4 and Part N §25 opium ("The Chinese district associations keep the licensed rooms in order with the county").
- *Canon:* l. 896 explains the convention by region and force: "the interior by the inland command and the counties' reserves and Hawaii by the Queen's Guard, never by the naval party". CR-037 adds that counties may not be "captured by a family, marriage network or congregation".
- *Problem:* Handing the policing of licensed rooms to the huiguan creates exactly the "rule about a people" that D8 and K2 forbid. It also gives a communal association a public power.
- *Fix:* The county police and licensing inspectors enforce the licence. The district associations are consulted, and the county hall's Chinese interpreters work with them. Drop the citation of the convention.

**S7. Two different "first" sobering rooms.**
- *CR:* G1 puts the sobering room in Hawaii's 1860 law. H10 has Logan's 1862 Sacramento board "run the first sobering room".
- *Fix:* Choose one. Part N §8 already leaves it out, so cutting it from G1 is the simplest fix.

**S8. CT1's wording is too strict and does not read well.**
- *CR:* CT1 replacement at l. 982 ("…rowing and climbing clubs, whose dinners after a race won are the barracks' only drinking nights, and many of the women…").
- *Problems:*
  - "Only drinking nights" is stricter than the CR's own C4: "A man who has done nothing that week can drink with his friends, but nobody toasts him."
  - Climbing clubs do not win races.
  - The clause runs on, so "and many of the women" reads as part of the relative clause.
- *Fix:* "…with their rowing and climbing clubs; the barracks drinks on the night a race is won or a route climbed, when the losers pour. Many of the women…" Amending canon (B) is the right choice on the merits; only this wording needs to change.

**S9. The coffee paragraph contradicts the CR's own premises and names offices that do not exist.**
- *CR:* Part N coffee and L3.
- *Problems:*
  - "Coffee is the dry Realm's drink" contradicts A3 (wine is lawful, and the Republic is proud of it).
  - "Where other armies carried spirits" is false for the Union. L2 itself gives the Union soldier's coffee ration of 36 lb a year.
  - "The Philippine Office" and "the Hawaiian Office's credit" are not canon institutions.
- *Fix:*
  - Write "Coffee is the working day's drink."
  - Drop the comparison with other armies.
  - For the rust quarantine, use the Philippine Council's government with the Polytechnic's plant pathologists.
  - For Kona's credit, use the Development Fund lending to co-operatives (l. 963).

**S10. The registry entries and a clash of names.**
- *CR:* Registry section.
- *Problems:*
  - Q-23 is approved and recommends registering Satori Kato, but the Registry section lists only Neilson and Logan.
  - In canon, a bare "Logan" means Logan Turner (l. 317, 525, 678: "mines under Logan").
- *Fix:* Add Kato to the registry. Always write "Thomas Logan" or "Dr Logan" for the Sacramento physician.

**S11. Deseret's hearing: titles and setting.**
- *CR:* Part N §12 ("At the accession hearing the commissioners show the council the store's purchases: from the President's own distillery").
- *Canon:*
  - Character bible l. 1937: Young is "governor of Deseret".
  - l. 635: Deseret's General Assembly approves the charter in December 1866, and Deseret becomes a member on the exchange in 1867.
  - "Commissioners" in canon are the Collar's governors from 1879 (l. 825) and county Commissioners from 1887.
- *Problem:* "The President" reads as California's president. Canon has no "accession hearing" between the approved charter and the exchange, so the reader needs to know what body holds the hearing and what leverage it has.
- *Fix:* "from Young's own distillery". Name the body: "the Council's committee on the charters". Give the leverage: the store cannot open under the new law unless its supplier is not an official.

**S12. Turley's customers may be wrong (verify).**
- *CR:* Part N §1 ("Plains bands bring their winter's robes to his mill").
- *Problem:* As I understand it, Taos Lightning reached the Plains nations mostly through traders who carried it to the Arkansas posts. The people who came to Arroyo Hondo were trappers, Taos Pueblo, and Ute and Jicarilla bands. I am not certain of this, so it should be checked before placing.
- *Fix:* After R1, either write "Ute and Jicarilla bands bring their robes, and the traders carry kegs to the Plains", or keep "Plains" if Lyckman or the NRHP nomination supports it. Also check Turley's birth year (the Registry gives 1809) and that his family was from the Boonslick.

**S13. "Every later compact" is too broad.**
- *CR:* D1 and the Ledger section.
- *Canon:* Several compacts are not with nations: Deseret's Compact of Association (l. 461), Oregon's civil compacts of 1861, the Rio Grande compact of 1863, and Ralston College's Compact of the Tuolumne of 1910.
- *Fix:* Write "every later compact with a Native nation" in D1 and in the ledger note.

**S14. San Francisco in 1875: there is no mechanism, and the voice is counterfactual.**
- *CR:* Part N opium ("No county passes a law against the Chinese den, as San Francisco would have"), K2.
- *Problems:*
  - "Would have" is a comparison with history in the storyline. AGENTS.md l. 13 says the storyline must read as what happens, and historical comparisons belong in historical_background.
  - Nothing in the CR prevents San Francisco's supervisors from passing the ordinance in 1875, and the Sacramento faction's nativism (l. 740) makes it likely they would try.
- *Fix:* Show what happens instead. San Francisco's supervisors pass a den ordinance in November 1875, and the Confederal Court strikes it because a licensed trade cannot be banned for one people. Alternatively, keep it simple: "No county may ban a licensed room, and none tries."

**S15. Kalākaua's films.**
- *CR:* C5 ("The prestige media under Victoria and Kalākaua … and later their films").
- *Canon:* l. 1637: Kalākaua "dies in San Francisco in 1891".
- *Fix:* "under Victoria and Kalākaua in their pictures, and under their successors in films".

**S16. Memo phrasing in §25.**
- *CR:* Part N §25 ("The labour law year makes the Spirit Stores universal").
- *Fix:* "In 1888, the year of the labour law, the Spirit Law makes the Spirit Stores universal…" Name the act, because the labour law of 1888 (l. 963) does not contain this.

**S17. Silas and D7 (H7).**
- *Canon:* character bible l. 407: Silas "holds the inland command at Sacramento, declines". l. 1136 places this in 1906.
- *Problem:* A serving commander who drinks "by day" falls under D3 and D7.
- *Fix:* Say in H7 that he drinks at home and alone, never on duty and never in public, so the law does not touch him and only the custom does. Write "from about 1906"; canon has him already declining by then, so "after 1906" is wrong.

## Consider

**K1. Claudisms in Part N.** These read as epigrams or "not X but Y" constructions:
- §1: "he remembers it as the price of a keg".
- §5: "the distillers' petition against him cannot find a sermon to answer".
- §19: "The customs that make skill visible also put drink in its place."
- §25: "so that drink pays for the rooms where people meet without it".
- Coffee: "save the industry, though not the old trees".
- Homelands: "Some homelands stay dry and some do not, and the tables show both."
- Off-storyline but the same pattern: H7 "pitied, which is worse"; I2 "does not make money on its neighbour's law"; K4 "not under raids".

Suggested replacements:
- §1: "He does not speak of it afterwards."
- §5: cut the clause, or "the petition argues the trade and the revenue".
- Coffee: "the old trees are lost and the new ones bear by 1897".
- Homelands: end on the fact itself ("The Office prints each homeland's drink deaths beside its neighbours'").

**K2. Policy memo or lived culture.** The §19 and §25 paragraphs are lists of rules with no person in them, and the customs paragraph gives only a status reason. Nobody in it is protecting a bystander, so the shaming can read as moral even though the brief wants the reasons to come from health. Suggestions:
- Give the §19 paragraph one concrete figure: a crew's cox pouring for the stroke at Carquinez, or Kalākaua pouring at a launching.
- Add the bystander reason the brief asks for to C3/§19: "where others must deal with you" is the public-health argument, so say whose safety it is.

**K3. Conflicts already present in canon.** These would give the law agency and opposition after 1852:
- Broderick, the Tammany fireman whose machine ran through saloons (l. 600), now has to keep the Party's lodges dry. This parallels "Broderick has to unlearn the sale of offices."
- The German brewers and the wine counties fight the ladder in 1866.
- San Francisco's supervisors in 1875 (S14).
- The Spirit Stores under the Territorial Council (S2).

**K4. Give Thomas Logan the 1852 table.** H10 approves Logan keeping the drink tables "from the 1850s", and the author prefers historical people. Letting Logan compile the table Elias lays before the council in §5 follows the pattern of l. 205, where the find "is not credited to Elias alone".

**K5. "Takes the pledge."** The phrase is the temperance crusade's vocabulary, and a pledge with an exception for the earned cup is not a pledge. "She stops drinking that winter" fits the no-crusade brief better.

**K6. The kissaten runs the wrong way.** Japan's coffee houses came from the West (1888 onward, booming after 1911). A better line: Japanese students meet in San Francisco's and Honolulu's coffee rooms and take the habit home, where it becomes the kissaten.

**K7. Dry halls by charter.** l. 987 lets each hall keep "its own language, festivals and character" above the Office's minimum. A hall that is dry by the Realm's charter on a Californio or German wine county's festival night pulls against that. One option is that a county may vote the earned cup into the hall on its festival nights.

**K8. The ten-mile rule cuts both ways.** Where a county may not license within ten miles of a homeland and the nation's store keeps the whole duty, the nation's store becomes the only outlet for county drinkers inside the band. That is a reverse border-town incentive. F7 could admit it, and the councils could argue about it.

**K9. Protecting the Colonel's mystery.** H1: "the rising for which the Colonel is hanged" leans toward his guilt. Canon l. 193 says "What he did in January is never established." Write "after which the Colonel is hanged".

**K10. A possible thread at Lahaina (optional).** Noe's father is "a settled New England ship's surgeon" at Lahaina (l. 876). He could be the physician who attends Neilson in September 1859.

**K11. Women.** Every custom is phrased for men, apart from Victoria's choice. J2 says the customs apply to everyone, and the Kaʻahumanu house (l. 982) is a ready place to show it.

**K12. The 1852 duty and the London pledge.** Until 1859, the part of the 1852 duty collected on imported spirits at San Francisco is customs revenue pledged to London (l. 323). That is a small point for H3: the bondholders' agent might welcome a heavier duty.
