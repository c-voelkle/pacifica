# CR-077 — Fresh-context review

**Fresh-context review.** Reviewer read only `AGENTS.md`, `CR-077.md`, `canon-excerpts-storyline.md` and `canon-excerpts-character-bible.md`. Web used only for the historical spot-checks listed at the end. Paragraph numbers ("para") are the excerpt file's storyline paragraph numbers. The CR's own line numbers (l. 945 and so on) could not be checked against these excerpts.

## Verdict

**Ready to integrate once the fixes below are made.** The technology path is sound. Level B is argued step by step, the dates move forward without strain from canon's soft valves (c. 1895) and hard valves (c. 1908), and Level C is rejected for the right reason (materials). Nothing touches Noe Ward's death, Julian's office or the Five. Two passages do touch the atomic secrecy rules:
- the 1921 Carquinez machine is justified partly by "the halls' ion orbits";
- G6 says the Works' machine is "never described", which breaks the canon rule of a sealed, dated record with a written opening.

Both fixes are one sentence each. There are also several internal count and date slips: Japan's 2–3 machines against one in the table, the Genealogical Society of Utah as a customer before the company exists, and the delay-line capacity. Kate's surname is out of date from 1889. On the literary side, the cast act from their work but not from their interests. Hollerith, von Neumann, Fuller and Hebern each lack the motive or conflict that canon's corrections-column custom invites. G9's account of work has no losers.

## Blockers

1. **Halls' ion orbits on an outside machine (Part D, "1919–1921: the Carquinez machine").**
   - CR: "After the peace the navy's range tables, the halls' ion orbits and the Office's own tables justify a general electronic calculator. Fuller builds it at Carquinez…". Levy, not stated to be bonded, "writes its first orbit and ballistic programs".
   - Canon, para 690: the second part of the record, "sealed for twenty years from 1927, to the autumn of 1947", holds "the halls, the device and its test…".
   - Canon, para 654: the operators "are told only that they make a separated material for the Works' instruments".
   - The Calculation Office is outside the wall. It is "a shared service of the League with its machines at Carquinez" (para 399). Ion orbits in a magnet separator give away mass separation at scale, which canon keeps sealed to 1947.
   - **Fix:** take "the halls' ion orbits" out of the justification, and instead give the "Works' own relay machines inside the wall" (T3) to the halls. Alternatively, say the Works buys shifts on the machine run only by bonded staff, with the problem disguised, and that this is recorded in the sealed roll.

2. **"Never described" against the sealed-record rule (G6; F table; T3).**
   - CR: "The cipher room's machines and the Works' machine are never patented, never sold and never described. This follows the atomic rule: silence, never a false paper."
   - Canon, para 556: results are "written in their authors' hands, dated, witnessed … and sealed", with a written term.
   - Canon, para 690: "The people named in it know their credit is safe"; the second part carries a promise of opening in 1947.
   - The atomic rule is silence *with a written end*, not "never". As written, Fuller's and Levy's work for the Works would have no roll of honour, against canon's custom ("silence costs no credit because a sealed roll names every result", para 35).
   - T3 also puts "the Office's machines" into the record's *first* part. Canon lists that part as the 1908–1909 papers, the late neutrons of 1910–1911, Meitner's conjecture and the roll for them (para 690). Only the counters of 1908–1911 belong there. The Works' relay machines of 1913 and its 1929 stored-program machine belong to the second part ("the chemistry of the bred elements").
   - **Fix:**
     - G6: "The Works' machine is described only in the sealed record, in its second part, to be opened in 1947. The cipher room's machines are never patented or sold, and they are listed by name on a sealed roll of the General Staff."
     - T3: "the first part names the counting room's trigger counters among its tools; the Works' machines of 1913 and 1929 go into the second part."

## Should-fix

1. **Kate's surname (Part B 1899–1901, Part C 1901–1907 and 1913, set piece I-2).**
   - CR: "In Kate Curran's counting room Peirce, past sixty…"; "The answer the room accepts is Kate Curran's".
   - Canon, para 468: "By 1900 … Peirce is past sixty and Kate, Brandegee now by her second marriage, past fifty-five."
   - **Fix:** write "Kate Brandegee" from 1889 on. Keep "Kate Curran" only for the 1867–1883 references (G1's "as it named Kate Curran in 1872" is correct).
   - Also say which room this is. The counter that sticks is "Michelson's valve-amplified counter", which canon places at Ralston College and lends to Nagaoka (para 524). Kate's counting room is the Laboratory's, at Carquinez (para 405). Either the counter is lent to her room, or Peirce visits Ralston College.

2. **Japan's machines: 2–3 against 1 (A1 table and Part F table).**
   - A1: "about 15 (12–13 in the Realm, 2–3 in Japan)".
   - Part F lists one Tokyo entry, G10 says "The Tokyo machine of 1931", and D-13 says "The Tokyo machine of 1931".
   - Part F's named rows total 11: Carquinez 2, Office of Public Measures 1, Reserve Bank 1, General Staff/fleet 2, Works 1, Weather Office 2, Ralston College 1, Tokyo 1. "Others" must therefore supply 3–4 to reach "about fifteen". As listed, Japan has 1.
   - **Fix:** A1 should read "about 15 (about 14 in the Realm, 1 in Japan)", and "Others" should be given a number ("3–4"). The other way is to add Tokyo's second machine to the table and to D-13.

3. **The Genealogical Society of Utah before the company exists (Part B, "1896: Hollerith's company"; D-16).**
   - CR: Hollerith founds the company in 1896, and "Its customers are … and, from 1894, the Genealogical Society of Utah".
   - **Fix:** "The Genealogical Society of Utah (founded 1894) puts its records on the Office of Public Measures' cards from 1894 and becomes the company's customer in 1896."

4. **Delay-line capacity is physically implausible (Part E, "1922–1925: memory").**
   - CR: "a thousand numbers in a long tube".
   - A thousand numbers of about 35 bits is about 35,000 bits. At a 1 MHz pulse rate that needs about 35 ms of delay, which is about 50 m of mercury or about 50 m of water. EDSAC's 5 ft tubes held 32 words each.
   - **Fix:** "a few dozen numbers in each tube, a thousand in a battery of them". This also matches the 1928 machine's "store of 512 numbers in delay lines".

5. **Hard-valve export restriction (G4, H, G10, Part F production).**
   - Canon, para 697: "Every bottleneck of the halls is in Pacifican hands: … the hard valves and the regulating gear … Exports of these goods are restricted as the commercial property of a state champion."
   - CR: "Commercial machines licensed openly"; the Tokyo machine is sold; the champion "licenses abroad"; consumer valves are made "by the hundred thousand" from 1914.
   - **Fix:** add one sentence saying the restriction covers the halls' high-power, high-vacuum and regulating valves and the condensation pumps, not receiving or computing valves. The Tokyo sale already fits the Hayama annex ("buys Pacifican equipment", para 685). Say so in G10.

6. **The Union's card industry: the open-licensing logic (H, G4).**
   - CR: "Its card industry forms around Powers's patents … because Hollerith is Pacifican", yet "Its banks and its census buy Pacifican licences" and "Every bank in the Union that wants clearing on cards pays San Francisco".
   - Canon, para 396 (CR-057): every Polytechnic patent is licensed "to any firm, Pacifican or foreign, at the same published royalty". Being Pacifican does not shut the Union out.
   - **Fix:** give the motive history gives. The Census Bureau refuses to pay a Pacifican royalty and builds its own machines (Powers was a Census Bureau engineer), while the banks, which care about cost and not pride, license from San Francisco. Then drop "its census" from "Its banks and its census buy Pacifican licences."

7. **Hebern's patents against "never patented" (Part D, G6, D-5).**
   - CR: the five-rotor machine "becomes the navy's service cipher", and the cipher room's machines are "never patented, never sold".
   - Hebern is a private inventor whose living is sales (historically he patented in 1918; source below).
   - **Fix:** say what he gets and what he loses. For example, the navy buys his service design and patent rights outright and he sells a commercial machine without the service wiring. That also gives him the motive and conflict he currently lacks ("In Pacifica he is told" is the only beat he has).

8. **Who owns telephones is decided early (Part B, "1891–1893: the automatic exchange"; G5).**
   - CR: "The telegraph estate designs its telephone exchanges…".
   - Canon, para 424 and para 444: the telegraph is one part of the Polytechnic's chartered estate. Canon nowhere says the state runs telephones.
   - D-7 sends telecommunications to its own CR. This sentence quietly decides a state telephone monopoly.
   - **Fix:** "the telephone companies' exchanges are designed with Peirce's algebra, taught by the Office's school", leaving ownership OPEN for the telecommunications CR.

9. **Comrie's *Hist.* note is wrong (Part E).**
   - CR: "then ran Britain's Nautical Almanac from 1925".
   - He joined HM Nautical Almanac Office in 1925 as deputy superintendent and became superintendent in 1930 (Wikipedia) or 1931 (IEEE).
   - **Fix:** "joined Britain's Nautical Almanac Office in 1925 and ran it from 1930". The punched-card date of 1928 is correct.

10. **Rossby's 1931–1932 fix needs its data (G3).**
    - The barotropic forecast needs mid-troposphere (500 mb) observations. History got them from radiosondes (from 1929–1930) and regular aircraft soundings.
    - **Fix:** one clause, for example "on the upper-air soundings his airway pilots and the navy's aircraft have flown daily since 1928". Courant, Friedrichs and Lewy's stability condition (Göttingen, 1928) is also historically to hand and could be the reason the 1929 trials blow up and the 1931 ones do not.

11. **Consumer valves too early (Part C, "1909: broadcasting").**
    - CR: "the receiving valve becomes a consumer good. By 1914 the Realm's valve works make oxide-cathode valves by the hundred thousand."
    - Herrold's 1909 listeners used crystal sets. In history the valve receiver became a consumer good about nine years after the hard valve (1913 to about 1922). Here the CR allows six (1908 to 1914) and makes the consumer good immediate.
    - **Fix:** "from about 1912 the valve receiver becomes a consumer good; by 1914…". Alternatively, give the navy and the counters the hundred thousand.

12. **Von Neumann's priority, Fuller's credit (Part E, "1926–1927").**
    - The *Hist.* note itself says the 1945 report "drew on Eckert's and Mauchly's engineering". In Pacifica the engineering is Fuller's delay-line group's (1922–1925).
    - Canon's custom exists for exactly this kind of priority case: "the original claim, the correction and the name of whoever found the difficulty" (para 399).
    - **Fix:** one sentence in which the corrections column adds Fuller's group to the 1927 report, or in which the challenge evening's respondent raises it. This turns von Neumann's one beat into a conflict and gives Fuller his only stake.

13. **G9 has no losers (G9; also Part B's dial exchanges).**
    - CR: "The examined clerk becomes an order-writer or a machine tender. The eight-hour day … is never threatened… The settlers' complaint … finds no new target."
    - "Most of the Realm's telephones dial" by about 1910, which puts thousands of operators, mostly women, out of work within fifteen years. Hand tabulators lose their work in 1930 too.
    - That is a real conflict, and the 1895 trades congress (para 483) is the body to bring it. As written, G9 reads as a frictionless memo.
    - **Fix:** give one named grievance and the Realm's answer (retraining through the Office's school, or the congress's reference table), and keep the eight-hour day.

14. **Claudisms to recast as events or cut:**
    - "Peirce lost the registers and won the method." (B)
    - "He is the founder of a profession and has seen its first electronic machine." (C, 1914)
    - "The regency's claim to rule by measurably better lives becomes a claim renewed every month." and "what the tables do not count, the state does not see." (G2). Replace them with one instance of something the monthly tables miss.
    - "A program, like an instrument, is signed by its maker." (G1)
    - "The failure repeats the Office's oldest lesson: a calculation that runs is not an account that is true." (G3) This echoes canon para 399 word for word. One echo can stay if it is deliberate, but then give it to a character (Levy, or Kate's custom), not the narrator.

## Nits

- **Peirce's age in 1913.** He was born in September 1839 (canon makes him "sixty-nine" in November 1908), so he is "seventy-four" only from September 1913. Date the evening to the autumn, or write "seventy-three".
- **"The Office of Public Measures (seat OPEN)" (F table).** Write "(its city OPEN)". "Seat" reads as a seat among the Five, which canon denies (para 446).
- **Price basis (A2.5 and J).** J is in "1913 prices", but A2.5's "$870m" carries no basis. Canon prices fall (CR-057), so the "3.5%" depends on it. State the basis.
- **Fact changes.** Hollerith's death in 1929 (CT-HERMAN-HOLLERITH exists) is not in the list.
- **D-6 against the parts.** D-6's "Britain in 1927 not without a check against CR-046" appears in no Part. Add a line to Part D or G6.
- **Omsk (Part D).** "Movement tables … worked … at Omsk and Thalassia". Omsk is reached by the line in that week, so the tables are worked at Thalassia before it and at Omsk afterwards.
- **Weather trials before the machine (G3 against F).** G3 has trials "from 1929", but the Weather Office's machine arrives in 1930. Say the 1929 trials run on Carquinez's machine.
- **Production against installations (F).** "From 1930 Hollerith's company … builds the Realm machine", but four of the listed machines date from 1929–1930. Say who built those (Carquinez's shop).
- **"Champion" (G4).** "Pacifica's card and machine champion" is Hollerith's private company. In canon "champion" often marks a state firm (para 405, para 697). Write "the card company".
- **Fuller as "the navy's wireless engineer" (D).** Historically he built arc transmitters for the Army and the Navy as Federal Telegraph's chief engineer. In Pacifica, the navy's wireless is Tesla's estate's (para 524). Say which employer he has.
- **Fuller's load.** Fuller does the coincidence counter, the 8,000-valve machine, the delay lines and the drum. Consider giving one of them to the counting room's or Langmuir's people.
- **"one of the first professions in which a woman can head a national service" (G1).** Victoria and Kaʻiulani already head larger things. Say concretely what Levy heads.
- **"An echo of Fresno in 1897" (I-8).** Not in the supplied canon. Check that it exists.
- **"The four county standards" (G2).** Not in the supplied excerpts. Check the term.
