# CR-078 — Fresh-context review

**Fresh-context review**, 20 November 2026. Reviewer read only: `AGENTS.md`, `CR-078.md`, `canon-excerpts-storyline.md`, `canon-excerpts-character-bible.md`, `CR-070.md`, `CR-016.md`, `technology-tree-1877-1932.md`; web used only for Hist. spot-checks. The linter finding (CT-THOMAS-JAGGAR unregistered) is not repeated. E-1 to E-15 are treated as approved; T1–T5 as proposals.

## Verdict

**Ready with fixes.** The structure is sound and fits canon: the estate/county split follows CR-057, the southern wire and broadcasting dates match the planning note's approved §9, and the 1932 defences line up with paragraph 743. Four things must be fixed before integration:
- Fuller is nineteen when he is made to lead the wireless chain.
- Thalassia, on Oʻahu, is put on a land-line long-distance call in 1903–05.
- The estate-owned cables contradict canon's "cable … concessions are auctioned" (paragraph 521), and this is not listed as a tension.
- The 1927 Hilo warning is called a "proof", but historically it was a false alarm.

The other problems are fixable in place:
- names that don't exist yet in 1877–78 (the Realm, the estate, the Office);
- three technology dates that run ahead of canon's soft-valve and loading dates;
- who obliges the Hilo harbourmaster;
- AT&T's geography;
- the secrecy status of radio location.

The society section works on paper but has few losers, and the Hilo rewrite removes the one conflict history supplied.

## Blockers

**B1. Fuller is too young to lead the Pacific wireless chain.**
- *CR-078 E:* "Long-wave stations at Oʻahu, Guam, Manila, Sitka and Tutuila, built in 1910–12 by the power estate under Fuller". The registry line in M ("Fuller (the wireless chain, 1910–12)") says the same.
- *Canon (character bible, CR-077):* "Leonard Fuller … Born at Portland in August 1890; historically chief engineer of the Federal Telegraph Company at San Francisco from 1913."
- He is 19–22 in 1910–12. Historically he finished at Cornell in 1912.
- *Fix:* the chain is built "by the power estate" (Tesla's estate, with no named lead) or under a historical engineer of the right age. Cyril Elwell (b. 1884) built Federal Telegraph's San Francisco–Honolulu arc link in 1912, but he would be a new registry ID. Fuller joins in 1912–13 and builds the chain's later high-power or shortwave stations. Amend the M registry line to match.

**B2. Thalassia cannot be on a land-line telephone call in 1903–05, and the 1922 Honolulu–Thalassia call is wrong.**
- *CR-078 B table:* "1903–05 | Valve repeaters: Thalassia, Monterey, Salt Lake, Puget Sound and Santa Fe on one call".
- *CR-078 I4:* "From 1922 Honolulu telephones Thalassia's offices across the Koʻolau, and Thalassia telephones San Francisco." E table: "Radiotelephone Thalassia–San Francisco–Honolulu".
- *Planning note §3 (approved bias "Air over water"):* "Undersea telephony waits for repeaters on the seabed (Hist. 1956)."
- *Canon, paragraph 377:* the Pali railway tunnel is built in 1876–82, and the seat moves to Kāneʻohe Bay in 1885. Honolulu and Thalassia are 15 km apart by land, so they are on one exchange network by the 1880s.
- The planning note's A4 ("Thalassia can telephone Salt Lake and Puget Sound") was only a working date, and the CR inherited its error.
- *Fix:*
  - B table: "San Francisco, Monterey, Salt Lake, Puget Sound and Santa Fe on one call".
  - I4: "From 1922 Thalassia and Honolulu telephone San Francisco by radio."
  - E table: "Radiotelephone San Francisco–Oʻahu (Thalassia and Honolulu)".
  - Add one line in B: "Honolulu–Thalassia over the Pali, 1880s."

**B3. Estate-owned cables contradict canon's auctioned cable concessions, and no tension is listed.**
- *CR-078 A:* "The telegraph estate … owns three things as monopolies it owns: the long lines; the cables; …". §0 even quotes canon: "Oil, mineral, rail, port, cable and power concessions are auctioned (CR-057)."
- *Canon, paragraph 521 (APPROVED — CR-057):* "Oil, mineral, rail, port, cable and power concessions are auctioned, and the state takes royalties and fees rather than equity. It holds shares only in the canal and the trunk rights of way, the monopolies it owns, and in its own estates."
- If every cable is the estate's, there is no cable concession left to auction.
- *Fix:* add **T6** with both cases:
  - (a) "cable" in paragraph 521 means landing and maintenance concessions let to private cable ships and firms, while the trunk cables stay the estate's; or
  - (b) strike "cable" from paragraph 521's list and add "the long lines and cables" to the monopolies the state owns.
- Recommend (b), and list paragraph 521 as a knock-on.

**B4. The 1927 Hilo warning was a false alarm, not a "proof".**
- *CR-078 G:* "After the next warning, in October 1927, the whole fishing fleet goes out to deep water (*Hist.*, as happened). In Pacifica this is the second proof of the warnings, not the first."
- *Hist. (USGS Volcano Watch):* the warning followed an Alaskan earthquake of 24 October 1927. "The entire fishing fleet moved out to deeper water". The tsunami never came, because the earthquake was about 100 miles inland.
- In Pacifica the earthquake is on Pacifican soil, reported over the Sitka cable.
- *Fix:* either use the 1927 false alarm as the warnings' cost (a day's catch lost, with Hearst's or the fishermen's complaint, and the compact's rule tested: the fleet still goes out the next time), or move "the second proof" to 1933 (Sanriku, warned, no lives lost, per the same USGS page).

## Should-fix

**S1. Names and institutions that don't exist yet in 1877–78.**
- *CR-078 A:*
  - "1877: the telegraph estate buys the Realm rights outright … The purchase is the estate's board's".
  - Every concession carries "service figures in the Office's tables … the Realm's standard".
  - "the first county exchanges, from 1878".
- *Canon:*
  - The Realm and the chartered estate begin with the Constitution of 1887: "the Polytechnic's examinations, audit, General Staff and telegraph form a chartered estate" (paragraph 425).
  - "The Office of Public Measures, established in 1888" (paragraph 425).
  - CR-016 F1: Soberanes "1879–84: builds the fleet signal service, the state telegraph"; Director of Telegraphs only from 1885.
- *Fix:*
  - In 1877 the buyer is the Confederation's telegraph office (or "the Superintendency, for the state telegraph"), on Elias's instruction. "The Realm rights" become "the Confederation's rights".
  - The 1878 San Francisco concession is let by the city and county board under California's law, with interconnection at a published rate.
  - The Office's service figures and the Realm's standard are written into every concession when it is relet after 1888.
  - Keep E-2's substance.

**S2. Valve repeaters in 1903–05 and the end of spark about 1900 come before canon's hard valves.**
- *CR-078 B:* "1903–05 | Valve repeaters". *E:* "about 1900 | Valve oscillators give continuous waves; the spark era ends early".
- *Canon, paragraph 527:* "The valves of the 1890s are soft and erratic … about 1908 he makes the first hard-vacuum valves."
- Historically, reliable repeaters needed the hard vacuum (1913). Soft valves will oscillate at low power, but they will not run a transmitter.
- *Fix:*
  - Repeater trials on selected circuits from 1903–05, with Realm-wide valve-repeated service from about 1909–10. The approved A4 bias is kept, because the change is still years ahead of history's 1913–15.
  - Valve oscillators about 1900 for receivers and low-power continuous waves. Valve continuous-wave transmitters replace spark from about 1909.
  - Until then, high power comes from arcs. That is Fuller's historical trade, and it fits B1.

**S3. San Francisco–Los Angeles in 1894 "by loaded line" comes before loading.**
- *CR-078 B:* "1894 | San Francisco–Los Angeles by loaded line".
- *Planning note A3:* "Loaded lines | about 1900 | 1899–1900 | on schedule".
- *Fix:* "by heavy copper line" in 1894, with loading about 1900.

**S4. Who obliges the Hilo harbourmaster.**
- *CR-078 G:* "the harbourmaster clears the harbour, because the compact obliges him to act on the Observatory's warning".
- A Realm–Japan compact does not bind a Hawaiian port officer. Hawaii's legislature has been restored since 1907 (AGENTS, CR-040).
- *Fix:* the Hawaiian legislature's harbour act, or the Board of Health's, carries the compact into law (for example in 1913, after the Observatory is founded), and that act obliges him.
- Also keep some of history's friction: he doubts the warning, obeys the act, and is mocked until noon.

**S5. The compact's warnings, 1897–1922.**
- *CR-078 I6:* "Hilo in 1923 is the first time the Realm's people see a warning save lives on the day."
- *CR-078 G:* the 1897 compact covers "warnings of earthquakes at sea and of typhoons".
- Twenty-six years of typhoon warnings to Guam, Manila and the Visayas would have saved lives before 1923. Historical Pacific tsunamis reaching Hawaii in 1906 and 1918 would also have tested the compact.
- *Fix:*
  - The compact exchanges reports of earthquakes and typhoon warnings. Jaggar's new contribution (1922–23) is the forecast of a sea wave's arrival time.
  - I6 becomes "the first time a sea-wave forecast saves lives".
  - Say what happened in 1896 itself: the southern wire had joined Tokyo to Honolulu since 1892, so the news of Sanriku could reach Honolulu within hours, before the waves. Nobody knows what it means, and the waves strike Hawaii. That is the 1897 compact's motive, on the page.

**S6. Use Kamchatka's 1923 status.**
- *CR-078 G:* "On 3 February 1923 he reads the Kamchatka earthquake".
- *Canon, paragraph 653:* "Kamchatka and the Koryak coast as far as Ola Bay become a republic under Japanese protection" (from 1921).
- The 1923 quake happens in Japan's protectorate. Under the compact, Japan's station or the Petropavlovsk report should reach Hilo over the wire beside Jaggar's own record.
- This is the compact's first working use for a sea wave, and it gives Japan's side a part.

**S7. AT&T's line cannot reach "San Francisco's border".**
- *CR-078 I7:* "AT&T's long lines reach San Francisco's border only in 1915 (*Hist.*)".
- *Canon, paragraph 288:* Pacifica holds the Northwest, the Great Basin, Arizona, Utah, Nevada, Idaho, western Montana and New Mexico to 103°W. The Union's west ends at the Kansas plains (paragraph 485).
- I7 also says "The Union buys Pacifican sets and valves". If the Union buys Pacifican repeater valves, it has no reason to wait for the historical 1915.
- *Fix:* "AT&T's long lines reach the Realm's border on the plains in [1910–15], and the first New York–San Francisco call runs over the Realm's long lines from there." Choose a date consistent with Union valve purchases. Move the Hist. date into a comparison.

**S8. Radio location's secrecy is undecided.**
- *Planning note §2 rule 4 (approved):* "Open licensing at one royalty (CR-057). The only exceptions are the cipher room and the Works (CR-077 D-5)."
- CR-078 F puts pulse radio location at the navy's laboratory from 1924–26 and says nothing about patents.
- Under the rule as it stands, radar would be openly licensed to Britain and the Union.
- *Fix:* an explicit decision for the author. Either radio location goes on the General Staff's sealed roll as the cipher room's machines do (which needs an amendment of CR-077 D-5), or it is published and licensed, and the Union's position in 1932 must reflect that.

**S9. The gutta-percha concession's grantor is unclear.**
- *CR-078 C:* "the Pacifican Borneo company holds a gutta-percha concession in its interior. It holds it under compact with the interior's peoples".
- *Canon, paragraph 349:* the company already holds the Sultan's lease, and "holds its interior only by compact" (CR-074).
- The company cannot hold a concession from itself.
- *Fix:* the company buys the latex under compacts, and sells it to the Carquinez cable works under a supply contract.
- The ledger's "the sultanate's successor" is undefined, because the Sulu sultanate exists in canon (the 1878 compact, paragraph 349). Name the actual party.
- Gutta-percha tapping historically felled the trees. That gives a real loser and a conflict with Mat Salleh's people (1895–98), which is worth one line.

**S10. The southern wire's money and Midway landing.**
- *CR-078 C:* "Victoria orders it in 1888".
- *K:* "The cables and the chain are new events on the Treasury's account", while "The Carquinez cable works and the ships … in the estate's account".
- *Canon:* the regent rules "through bargaining", and the Senate votes budgets (paragraphs 425 and 452).
- Say who votes the $8m. A Senate fight over $8m for a wire to Japan in 1888, with the inland command's senators against it, is the conflict this part lacks.
- Midway is "part of the Hawaiian Crown lands, with its naval ground leased to the Confederation" (paragraph 329). The cable station needs the Hawaiian Crown's lease, which belongs in ledger L.

**S11. A missing fact change, and a pattern that matches nothing.**
- E-14 and T1 set the Manila cable at 1891, but `## Fact changes` has no `manila-cable` event. Add one, with a pattern such as `Guam to Manila[^.;]{0,40}in (?P<year>1[89]\d\d)`.
- The `regular-broadcasting` pattern matches nothing in the CR, whose wording is "Regular broadcasting at San Jose and San Francisco (1912)". The storyline prose must be written as "Regular broadcasting begins at San Jose and San Francisco in 1912", or the fact is unprotected.
- In that pattern, `begin ` is redundant after `begins? `.

**S12. The Tesla test: who decides, and from where.**
- *CR-078 H:* "The estate does not build the tower. The power seat goes on lending Tesla's name to the grid".
- *Canon:* Tesla is the power seat until 1924 (paragraph 445). So "the estate" that declines is Tesla's own, and "the power seat … lending Tesla's name" is circular.
- *Fix:* Tesla, as head of his estate, accepts the room's test. After the result he withdraws the tower himself, or the estate's board refuses the money. Say which.
- Say where the transmitter stands. "A hundred miles" and "Sacramento" imply San Francisco or Monterey, not Carquinez, which is about 50 miles away.

**S13. "The all-metal air arm … about 1920".**
- *CR-078 F:* "Radio beacons and a radio range for the all-metal air arm and the airway from Oakland to Los Angeles (CR-077's weather service), about 1920."
- *Canon, paragraph 744:* "all-metal, as the whole air arm has been since the mid-1920s". Paragraph 702: Rossby's airway weather service is of 1927–1928.
- *Fix:* "for the air arm and the Oakland–Los Angeles airway, about 1920 (its weather service follows in 1927–28)".

**S14. The British Hong Kong–Manila cable needs a decision.**
- *CR-078 C:* "*Hist.:* Hong Kong was joined to Manila by a British company's cable in May 1880".
- The Philippines is Pacifican from 1876 (paragraph 348). In this timeline the 1880 cable needs a Pacifican landing grant at Luzon.
- *CR-021 I3:* "No relay on British islands".
- Decide whether it exists. If it does, it is the wire Port Hamilton's telegrams crossed, which helps the planning note's motive. If not, Manila's link to the treaty ports is all E-6's.

**S15. Literary effect.**
- *Named people's motive.* Jaggar has a motive and acts. Ryan has a question. Tesla has an answer. Victoria "orders" and Soberanes "builds", with no motive or opposition stated. The board of 1877 is unnamed and passive. Julian's two scenes (the 1914 broadcast and the 1928 call) are events, not choices.
- *The Hilo scene loses its friction.* The rewrite removes the one conflict history supplies (the harbourmaster's disbelief and the death). Canon already has a Hilo harbour pilot: Keawe's father (paragraph 712: "his father is a Hawaiian harbour pilot"). Keawe is chief engineer at Hilo in 1923–27 (paragraph 715). Making the pilot the man who must clear the harbour on a stranger's timetable gives the scene a viewpoint and a cost inside canon.
- *Society (Part I) has few losers.* At present the losers are the opposition clubs' complaint and the Union press. Candidates:
  - Morse telegraphers and messenger boys displaced by the telephone, a decade before the dial's operators;
  - the losing bidder at a twenty-year rebid, and who pays for the incumbent's plant (it needs a published-valuation transfer rule, as with Spring Valley in paragraph 47);
  - Herrold's San Jose station (canon 1909), which must bid for its own wavelength in 1911;
  - Hearst's editors against the press seat's own radio news;
  - the Bornean tappers (S9).

  - "rents, not equity, and a monopoly only where the network is one network"

- **N3.** I3's "the settler bargain of CR-071, that the examination is a floor and not a ladder" is not found in the supplied canon. Verify it against CR-071, or drop the gloss.
- **N4.** "the Office" means both the Calculation Office (its school and workshop) and the Office of Public Measures (tables, register). Name the one meant in A, H ("the Office's register"), I2 and G ("the Office publishes depth charts"; charts are more naturally the coast survey's or the navy's).
- **N5.** The wireless chain makes Guam "have two routes" by Wake, but the ring closes in 1903 (Hakodate–Tokyo–Bonins–Guam), so Guam already has two. The Wake cable doubles the Midway–Guam span. Manila's second route is unstated until E-6.
- **N6.** The radio range is given as "came in 1929". The first US airway stations entered service in 1928 (demonstrated 30 June 1928; Ford's private version in 1926).
- **N7.** The Berlin convention was signed in 1906 and entered into force in 1908. "refused … until the Berlin convention of 1906" should be "until the Berlin convention of 1906 obliged coast stations to intercommunicate".
- **N8.** "Japan's state telephone, 1890" is listed under Sources but used nowhere in the text.
- **N9.** Fish-finding of tuna by echo in about 1925 is a stretch. Historically sonar found schooling herring first (1930s). "Sardine and salmon at sea, tuna later" is safer.
- **N10.** Bell's receiver needs a transmitter. Say who holds the Edison/Berliner transmitter rights in the Confederation, or that Hughes's unpatented microphone (1878) is used.
- **N11.** The radiotelephone to Tokyo (1928) is open to interception. A line on the cipher room's scrambling, or a rule that state business goes by cable, fits canon's 1932 "radio and ciphers protect the links".
- **N12.** Ōmori is named as an actor ("Ōmori's seismographs") but is not in M. List him under "Unnamed or unregistered", as with Yagi and Okabe.
- **N13.** Model K row "The Carquinez cable works … 1888–1932": the works existed from 1879 (CR-070 E4: "the cable works have nine years' practice since the Oʻahu cable of 1879"). Write "1879–1932" or "the ships after 1888".
- **N14.** The air school teaches in English, Spanish, Hawaiian and Japanese, but not Chinese or Tagalog. Make it deliberate (a later year, or a stated reason) given "six languages".
- **N15.** The canon tensions lack the two-case-plus-recommendation form the rules ask for (AGENTS, "Canon tensions"). This is harmless now that all are decided, but add T6 (B3) in full form.

### Technology-date plausibility against canon's parts (summary)

- **Valve repeaters 1903–05:** too early for soft valves. Trials then, service about 1909–10 (S2).
- **Valve oscillators about 1900:** plausible at low power only. The end of spark comes with hard valves, about 1909 (S2).
- **Shortwave about 1920:** plausible, with hard valves from 1908 and the air-over-water bias.
- **Radiotelephone 1922 (San Francisco–Oʻahu) and 1928 (Tokyo):** plausible once the Thalassia wording is fixed (B2).
- **Pulse radio location 1924–26:** plausible. It has the counters, the cathode-ray tube since the 1880s and the 1925 Breit–Tuve analogue. A few sets by 1932 fits paragraph 743.
- **Long-wave chain 1910–12:** plausible with arc or alternator transmitters. High-power valve transmitters in 1910 would be a stretch.
- **Fax 1908–10, teleprinters about 1910, echo sounding about 1915:** plausible.
