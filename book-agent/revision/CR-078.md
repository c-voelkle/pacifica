**Approved before this CR:**

## A. Who owns the wire (CR-077 D-7)

**Options:**
1. **A state monopoly**, as in Japan. *Hist.:* Japan's state telephone opened in 1890 between Tokyo and Yokohama, and Japan's telecommunications stayed a state monopoly for nearly a century.
2. **A private monopoly**, as in the Union under Bell and AT&T.
3. **A split (recommended).** The telegraph estate owns the long lines, the cables and the Realm's switching standard: these are trunk rights of way and "monopolies it owns", in the words of CR-057. Local exchanges are county concessions, auctioned for twenty years. Every concessionaire must connect to every other and to the long lines at a published rate, and must publish its service figures in the Office's tables.

**Why the split:**
- It is CR-057's rule applied to the telephone: rents, not equity, and a monopoly only where the network is one.
- It gives the counties something to bid for and the Party something to watch.
- It keeps the competing equipment makers that CR-077 needs (Hollerith's company, the relay makers, Japan's partners).

**The patent.** *Hist.:* Bell's patent of 1876 bound only the United States. Western Union opened San Francisco's first large city exchange on 17 February 1878.
- **Recommendation:** Bell files in Pacifica in 1876, and in 1877 the telegraph estate buys the Realm rights outright, cheaply, before anyone knows their worth. *Hist.:* Western Union refused Bell's whole patent for $100,000 in 1876.
- The estate then licenses them at the one royalty (CR-057).
- **The price (MODEL):** about $25,000.

## C. The cables

- **The southern wire (APPROVED):** 1889–1892; about 6,800 nautical miles and about $8m (MODEL); a new workbook event.
- **Insulation.** *Hist.:* gutta-percha came from Malaya and Borneo, and the cable makers of London held a near-monopoly of the trade.
  - **Recommendation:** the Pacifican Borneo company (CR-074) holds a gutta-percha concession from the 1880s, under compact with the interior's peoples. The Realm's own cable works on the Carquinez Strait (site to decide) make the core.
  - From the 1920s acetylene rubber (CR-055) is tried, and fails at sea depth by 1932. The "insulation industry" of 1932 is gutta-percha worked at home.
- **Cable ships.** Pacifican cable ships from 1879, and a second ship after 1888. *Hist.:* the British fleet of cable ships dominated before 1914.
- **Wake.** **Recommendation:** a second Pacific cable, Midway–Wake–Guam, about 1910, to give Guam two routes. Wake's claim of 1880 then pays.
- **China.** Manila–Shanghai or Manila–Hong Kong. *Hist.:* Hong Kong–Manila was laid by a British company in 1880. **Recommendation:** Pacifica lays Manila to the Customs Inspectorate's ports in the 1890s on Chinese landing grants (this touches the partners' CR).

## D. Teleprinter and fax (R1)

- **Fax for the Concord (R1, APPROVED as a road not taken).** Pictures by wire from 1908–10. Newspapers, orders and contracts in kanji, Chinese and Hawaiian go as pictures. *Hist.:* Korn's telephotography, 1907; NEC's NE-type phototelegraph, 1928.
- **Teleprinters** from about 1910, for the press, the railways and the Office. Six-language news runs on them.
- **To decide:** who makes the fax machines, Pacifica or Japan. This is the Hayama annex question; recommendation: answer it in the partners' CR.

- **A Pacific wireless chain against Marconi (recommended).** About 1910 the estate builds long-wave stations at Oʻahu, Guam, Manila, Sitka and Tutuila, so the fleet and trade can talk without a cable. *Hist.:* Marconi's company refused to talk to stations of other makers until the Berlin convention of 1906, and Britain's Imperial Wireless Chain dates from the 1920s.
- **The radio standard for Asia,** as with the electricity standard (canon): Pacifican wavelengths, sets and valves in the partners' markets.
- **Broadcasting's content** (what is on the air, Hearst, the Party) belongs to CR-079. This CR keeps only the stations, the law and the air school.

## F. Navigation and radio location

- **Radio direction finding** for the fleet and shipping, 1910s. 1932's "direction finding" is canon.
- **Radio beacons and a radio range** for the all-metal air arm, about 1920. *Hist.:* the radio range, 1929.
- **Yagi's antenna, 1926, used at once** (planning note bias 7). *Hist.:* in Japan it was largely ignored until the war.
- **Radio location.** Pulse experiments on metre waves at the navy's radio laboratory (place to decide), 1924–26; a few sets at Oʻahu and on the fleet by 1932, which is canon's "early radio location". Okabe's split-anode magnetron (*Hist.*: Tohoku, 1927) is in trial; the cavity magnetron stays after 1932. *Hist.:* metre-wave radar arrived in 1934–39, the cavity magnetron in 1940.
- **The CR-077 link:** machine plotting at Oʻahu in 1932.

## G. The ocean (R3)

- **Echo sounding** for cable routes and the Pacific's depths from about 1915, and for fish from about 1925 (R8, option). *Hist.:* Fessenden, 1914; the US Navy's sonic depth finder, 1922.
- **Tsunami and typhoon warnings shared with Japan.**
  - **The trigger:** the Sanriku tsunami of 1896 (*Hist.*: about 22,000 dead). The southern wire of 1892 already joins Tokyo to Honolulu. The recommendation is a compact for warnings by wire in 1897 and by radio from the 1910s.
  - **Jaggar at Kīlauea (recommended set piece).** Thomas Jaggar founds the Hawaiian Volcano Observatory in 1912 (*Hist.*). In February 1923 he warns Hilo of the wave from Kamchatka. *Hist.:* after the Kamchatka earthquake of 3 February 1923 he warned the Hilo harbourmaster, who did not take it seriously, and one fisherman was killed. In Pacifica the harbour is cleared. *Hist.:* after his next warning, in October 1927, the whole Hilo fishing fleet moved out to deeper water.
  - **Japan's side:** Ōmori's seismographs (*Hist.*), to be detailed in the partners' CR.

## H. Tesla's wireless power (R4)

About 1902 Tesla proposes power sent through the earth without wires. *Hist.:* Wardenclyffe, 1901–1906, was abandoned. In Pacifica a challenge evening tests the claim, with Ryan as respondent. The estate does not fund the tower, and Tesla loses prestige, not money.

## I. What it does to society (brief; detail after the outline is approved)

- **The telephone as a county good.** Concessions are bid for, service figures published, and party lines run in the interior and the homelands. Where a homeland wants a line, it lets the concession itself.
- **The air school.** The settler bargain (CR-071) by radio: a farm family studies for the examination.
- **Islands joined.** A Hawaiian county, Manila and Sitka hear the same news on the same evening.
- **The empire's nerves.** The two-route rule; Port Hamilton's lesson; the wires the 1932 crisis depends on.
- **Japan.** The southern wire before the war of 1894; the warnings compact; radiotelephone to Tokyo.
- **The Union.** Bell's private monopoly against the Realm's split; the Union buys Pacifican sets but runs its own broadcasting from 1920, commercially.
