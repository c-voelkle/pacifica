# CR-081 Part B — fresh-context review (technology first)

Reviewer: fresh context, 20 November 2026. Read: AGENTS.md, CR-081 outline and A-series, CR-077, CR-078, CR-079, CR-080 outline, the planning note and both storyline excerpts. Part A (A7, A8) is treated as settled.

## 1. Summary verdict

Part B is a sound socioeconomic layer for a China that has railways, coal, credit and schools. But it is written as if CR-077, CR-078 and CR-079 had not happened. Broadcasting comes up once, as one word in B7 and in tradeoff 4. Telegraph, fax, cards, film, records, the warnings compact and motor transport do not appear at all. The author's concern is justified.

The canon technologies change Part B in three ways:
1. **They add disasters and fix gaps.** Typhoons and floods are missing from B1, above all the 1931 Yangtze–Huai flood. Plague is missing too.
2. **They separate reach from literacy.** Radio, newsreels and the picture press reach people who cannot read. Nationalism and a spoken national language therefore grow faster than the literacy figure. B7 has no measure for this.
3. **They create new questions of ownership and politics.** Who owns Chinese broadcasting, the land register, the cipher question, and Japan's place as the maker of China's receivers (CR-080 X-3, undecided). These are new decisions and tradeoffs, and some of them bear directly on the author's aim of minimal cost to Japan.

Most of Part B's numbers can stand. Two famine rows need new mechanisms, two disaster rows should be added, and B7 needs a second metric.

## 3. Changes to decisions B1–B10

- **B1 (famine deaths).**
  - Add the Swatow typhoon of 1922 (about half history's toll), the 1931 flood (about 0.2 million, MODEL) and the plague of 1910–11.
  - Keep 1876–79 at about a million fewer: there is no inland telegraph before 1881.
  - Keep 1920–21.
  - Keep 1928–30 at 3–5 million, argued through the railway into the Wei valley and the trucks, with Gansu somewhat reduced.
  - The population effect stays under 1%.
- **B2 (Chinese Pacificans).** Canon says about **4.8** million in 1932, not 4.7 (storyline para 545). Reconcile the figure with the model.
- **B3 (remittances).** Keep "about twice". Add:
  - the wire route and the banks;
  - the gain in yuan value in 1929–31 as silver fell;
  - the fall in 1930–32 as guest-worker contracts lapse and "part of the slump's cost goes home to Canton" (para 738).
- **B4 (Manchuria).** Keep.
- **B5 (rural credit).** Keep. Say what "ten times" is ten times of: the Hebei societies, or all of China's cooperatives.
- **B6 (rent).** Better version: "The 37.5% limit is enforced in districts served by Pacifican-financed works once a cadastral survey has registered their plots and tenancies; the same register carries the land tax."
- **B7 (labour).** Keep, with direct payment shown on auditable payrolls.
- **B8 (literacy).**
  - Keep about 40% of males only if schools and Yen's primer carry it. Radio teaches pronunciation and gives a reason to read; it does not teach characters.
  - Add a figure for women (*proposal:* about 5%).
  - Add the reach metric.
  - Name the media: guoyu records, dictation-speed news, and lantern slides and film at Ding Xian.
- **B9 (opium).** Keep.
- **B10 (cities and living standards).**
  - Keep 6–7% in large cities.
  - The fast growers are Shanghai's media and electrical trades, Huludao and the railway towns.
  - Market information supports 1.0%.
- **Missing decision.** A8.15 leaves agriculture and food to Part B, and Part B has no decision on them. Add one covering:
  - soybeans through Huludao;
  - Tacoma's fertilizer (canon);
  - pumping;
  - the price report.

## 4. New decisions or tradeoffs

**New decisions**
1. **Who owns Chinese broadcasting.** *Recommendation:* Chinese state stations under the Ministry of Communications, on Pacifican transmitters at the one royalty, with no Pacifican ownership (planning-note rule 3; CR-080 X-2). Pacifican credit for them carries a public-service article: the market report, Yen's lessons and flood and typhoon warnings. *Hist.:* stations at Harbin from 1926, and from 1928 Nanjing's Central Broadcasting Station, which was a party organ.
2. **The warnings compact extended to China's ports** through the Customs, from 1897. It costs Japan nothing and serves Japan's own fleets.
3. **A census of 1931–32 and cadastral surveys**, on the Customs' machine once China recovers it.
4. **The cipher question** (CR-077 D-6 scope) for Part C.
5. **People.** The cast should not grow. Let Yen carry radio literacy, Fong statistics and Sheng the telegraph. If one person is added, the strongest choices are:
   - Lai Man-wai (*Hist.:* the Hong Kong film-maker who filmed Sun Yat-sen's campaigns), for film;
   - or Chin Gee Hee (*Hist.:* a Seattle Chinese who returned and built the Sunning railway in Taishan with emigrants' money, 1906–20), for remittances turned into works.

   Y. R. Chao can stay in the *Hist.* notes.

**New tradeoffs**
1. **Japan's receivers against China's tariff.** If X-3 holds (undecided), China's information boom is Japan's largest market for receivers, records, telephone sets and fax terminals. This is the cheapest way to offset Japan's cotton loss (A8 tradeoff 1). It cuts against Chinese makers at Shanghai and Huludao (CR-080 D5) and against the 1928 tariff. Part B should state this. It must not assume either answer.
2. **A party station against Pacifica's own rule.** Pacifica forbids any party a wavelength (P-16). Equipping the Nationalists' party station contradicts that rule; insisting on the rule offends Nanjing.
3. **Pacifican films and a Chinese quota.**
4. **Radio in Manchuria.** Chinese broadcasting at Mukden and Huludao feeds the Japanese officers' grievance, beside the customs (canon). The plot is still stopped in 1931.
5. **Cantonese media against guoyu** (expands tradeoff 3).

## 5. Other findings

- **Decision numbers collide with section numbers.** Section B3 holds decisions B5 and B6, section B5 holds B4 and B2, and "B10" is both the decision table and a decision. Use a separate prefix for the decisions (for example S-1 to S-10, as other CRs use D-, E- and P-).
- **Tungsten and antimony towns** (Xikuangshan, southern Jiangxi) were mining settlements well under 50,000 people. They cannot move the large-city share. The claim is credible only for Changsha's refineries.
- **Formosa is absent from Part B.** Its about 4–5 million people, its schools, its power and its radio could make it China's showcase province. Say whether Formosa is counted in China's figures.
- **B9.** Compare army cost with canon's remittances, which are doubled, not with history's. Also list the communications, aircraft and trucks under the material base.
- **A7's list for Parts B and C:** extraterritoriality, Korea's rice and the Yalu crossing, and the 1913 Reorganisation Loan are neither treated nor passed to Part C. Assign each.
- **B7's "Pacifican presses, paper and broadcasting support the movement"** is too vague to decide. Say what is given and through whom.
- **The writing** is plain and free of epigrams; only the decision cross-references are unclear. No other contradiction with Part A or canon was found.

Corrections: Chao recorded the national-pronunciation records in 1921, not 1922; Japan had 1.2 million radio subscribers in May 1932; Zikawei's flag signals at Shanghai date from 1884.
