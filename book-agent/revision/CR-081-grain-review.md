## Verdict

The revision does what the principle asks: China policy now reads as a buyer's and an examiner's interest, and the Japan conflict runs coherently from 1915 to 1931 as land against crop. The labels, register and checks are mostly in order. It is not ready to merge. Three items are blockers: the exclusive oath, as worded, disqualifies people canon already has in posts of trust; the granary paragraph leaves out the provisioner argument that was the author's condition for approving G-14; and "only for beans and rice" contradicts CR-082 R-1. Ten items should be fixed: they cover authority, knowledge and plausibility in four of the new scenes, ledger rows that were not updated, a contradiction over scholarships, and figures stated more firmly than their sources allow.

## Blockers

**B1. The exclusive oath bars people canon already has in posts of trust.**
*Passage:* storyline line 1209 (§26), "its posts of trust go to those who live in the Realm and take the Crown's oath, which admits no office under another state". Outline G-11 item 2 lists the posts as "the estates, the Offices, the forces, the Works" and adds "no office, pay or rank".
*Problem:* as written, the rule binds everyone in a post of trust, not only entrants, and "another state" takes in the partner crowns. That collides with:
- Victoria herself, Queen of Hawaii and Regent of the Realm (1887–1907);
- Kuroda Kiyoshi, a Japanese count and the Realm's Fleet-General from 1907 to 1920 (CR-040);
- Nishina, the Japanese co-lead inside the Works from 1922, and Nagaoka before him (CR-049, CR-050);
- the Pacific Companies' officers, whose commissions the Commissions Board confirms while they draw a host's pay (CR-074).

The sentence also sits in a 1906 paragraph, so it reads as in force from then. G-11's own to-do ("settle the wording of the oath with the character bible, where Chinese Pacificans already serve") is still open in the register.
*Fix (case A, adapt the revision; recommended):* "its posts of trust go to entrants who live in the Realm and take the Crown's oath, which admits no office, pay or rank under a state other than the crowns and Japan". *Case B (amend canon):* keep the general rule and write an exception for each person listed above. That means several CRs, and it weakens the rule. Recommend A. Then settle the oath's wording with the character bible, as G-11 requires.

**B2. The granary paragraph has none of the provisioner argument the author made the condition of approval.**
*Passage:* storyline line 1672 (§34), "The Patrimony fills the Crown's granary … Giannini says the money should have gone to the mortgages, and the granary takes crop, not mortgages." Outline G-14: the author's objection ("It needs at least a strong provisioner argument for doing so"), and item 3, "The argument in the Senate".
*Problem:* the storyline keeps the purchase and Giannini's objection and drops the case for buying. Nobody decides: no Julian, no Senate, no Patrimony board. The granary appears in canon for the first time here (see S9). The Patrimony's charter (CR-057) lets it spend 3% a year and invest abroad from 1919; the text does not say whether grain bought for a reserve counts as spending or as investment. The closing clause repeats the objection's answer instead of carrying a cost. G-14 item 3 as drafted has a second problem: in the Senate it would state the century conclusion ("the Realm passes its own soil and sea about 1940"), which CR-075 and CR-082 B6 keep to cabinet minutes. It also misquotes the table of 1930, which gives 1938 (see S10).
*Fix:* replace the inserted text with: "**[APPROVED — CR-081]** Julian asks the Senate for a Crown's granary, a reserve against a bad year or a closed sea, to be filled by the Patrimony from its own reserves without new issue. It buys the home counties' wheat and feed first, at the slump's price, and takes ten-year contracts abroad for beans and rice, which the Realm's fields do not grow in the quantities the Office's tables ask, paid in the Columbia's ammonia and the Realm's machines from plants the slump has half idled. His ministers argue from the public table that grain will not be this cheap again within the contracts' term and that every tonne bought abroad comes back as orders. Giannini answers that the money should go to the mortgages, the Industrial Board stands with him, and the Senate votes the granary. Farmers who sell to it at the slump's price still lose their farms: the granary takes crop, not mortgages." The Senate's vote is my suggestion: the author should decide who authorises the granary and whether its purchases fall under the 3% rule.

**B3. "Only for beans and rice" contradicts CR-082 R-1.**
*Passage:* the same insertion at line 1672: "takes ten-year contracts abroad only for beans and rice".
*Problem:* CR-082 R-1 (APPROVED 21 November) gives the prairies "contract and credit in 1931: the wheat contract". Once CR-082 is integrated, "only" contradicts it.
*Fix:* drop "only" (the B2 wording already does). *Case B:* retire R-1's wheat contract in CR-082. Recommend dropping "only".

## Should fix

**S1. Victoria and the farm leases (1915): what she knows and what she argues.**
*Passage:* line 1299 (§28), "struck in April on Julian's instruction, which Victoria carries to the genrō without its reason".
*Problem:* "without its reason" could mean that she was not told the reason or that she did not give it. She is outside the cabinet after 1907, and the tables are public (CR-082 B6), so she could work it out. The genrō would ask why Pacifica cares about Japanese farm leases, and the text gives her nothing to answer with. The scene's tension (the sufficer running the provisioner's errand) is real, but without these two facts it does not show.
*Fix:* "… are struck in April on Julian's instruction. Victoria is not told its reason; she argues it on China's ground, and the genrō take it as hers." Decide in the character bible whether she guesses the reason by 1921.

**S2. Ledger rows left unchanged, and a loan without a row.**
*Passage:* ledger `C-1921-MUKDEN-PROTOCOL` (owes "the same for every shipper" only); `C-1928-NANJING` (no rate clause); no row for the wheat credit (line 1688).
*Problem:* the check passes only because the old quotes are still in the text. The new terms (every destination, the crop as any freight, Siberian grain at the same rates; no rate that favours a shipper or destination) are not recorded. The 1931 credit is a loan, so AGENTS.md requires a row.
*Fix:* add the terms to both `owes` lists, with `more_quotes` for the new sentences. Add `C-1931-CHINA-WHEAT-CREDIT` (kind loan; CROWN to CN; from 1931; quote "The Crown's government sells the relief commission fifteen million bushels of wheat and flour on credit"; open: term, rate and security not stated). Add a row for the granary's ten-year contracts if they count as state contracts.

**S3. The soybean compact: "served first" and Nanjing's authority over the lines (1929).**
*Passage:* line 1656, "the crop travels at published rates on every Manchurian line, the Concord buys it by contract and never by land, and Japan's bean cake and feed are served first".
*Problem:* "served first" reads as priority of carriage, against "the same for every shipper and every destination" (1921) and "no rate that favours a shipper or a destination" (1928). Nanjing's hold on the Manchurian lines in 1929 is nominal: in 1921 the Harbin settlement needed Zhang Zuolin's consent, and this compact needs his son's.
*Fix:* "In 1929 the Concord and Nanjing, with Zhang Xueliang's consent for the Three Eastern Provinces, sign a soybean compact … never by land, and Japan's bean cake and feed have first call on the Concord's contracts."

**S4. The crop article (1923): who the parties are, who sets the price, and "royalty" on ammonia.**
*Passage:* line 1656, "A crop article, agreed between Mukden and the Realm … at the Customs' published Huludao price, and the Columbia's ammonia and the Polytechnic's soybean seed are sold there at the one royalty".
*Problem:* in 1923 Mukden is autonomous and Peking (Zhili) is hostile to it. A treaty-level article between the Realm and Zhang sits badly with Julian's rule that Pacifica does not choose China's ministers, and with A8.4's "no lending to factions". The Customs, under a Pacifican Inspector-General, would be setting the price for the Realm's own buyers. A royalty applies to licences, not to a product: it fits the seed but not the ammonia.
*Fix:* "A crop article in the district's charter gives the quay bonded elevators and lets buyers take long contracts at prices posted on the Huludao exchange and published by the Customs; the Columbia's ammonia at its published price and the Polytechnic's soybean seed at the one royalty are sold there to any farmer and to any buyer's agent, Japanese firms among them." Change the ledger row's parties to match: Mukden grants, and the estates sell.

**S5. The plotters' charge of 1931 and Thalassia's answer.**
*Passage:* line 1697, "their charge is that Pacifican contracts take Manchuria's harvest … Thalassia's case answers that a client state would close the open carriage on which Japan's own bean cake and feed depend."
*Problem:* the charge works as propaganda. The answer is weak from Tokyo's side, though: a Japanese client state would not close carriage to Japan. It also ignores the compact, which serves Japan first. "Among their charges" then repeats "their charge".
*Fix:* "Thalassia's case answers that the soybean compact already gives Japan's bean cake and feed first call, and that a client state would buy, with a war against the Concord's guarantee, a crop Japan already has. Among their other charges …" Optional: Koreans lease Manchurian land at Wanbaoshan while Japanese may not, and the plotters could make use of that.

**S6. Rossby's forecasts on the crop seasons are not plausible.**
*Passage:* line 1610 (§33), "They are tried first on the Manchurian and Yangtze crop seasons."
*Problem:* the forecasts of 1931–32 run on soundings flown between Oakland and Los Angeles. They are short-range. They cannot be tried on Manchuria or the Yangtze, where nobody takes soundings, and they say nothing about a season.
*Fix (either):* delete the sentence, or "The Weather Office asks the Customs for soundings at Huludao and Shanghai, so that they can be tried on the Manchurian and Yangtze harvests." The second puts a step towards the OPEN item after 1932 into canon; the author should choose.

**S7. The scholarship sentence contradicts canon on the indemnity, dates family entry wrongly, and leaves women out.**
*Passage:* line 1209, "The Realm pays for none of China's scholarships … an entrant brings his wife and children, never his lineage." Context: line 1128 (PROPOSED), Pacifica's Boxer contingent "turns its indemnity into scholarships"; line 1209 itself, the Polytechnic's "Boxer-indemnity scholarships"; line 750 (PROPOSED), the 1868 treaty's "jointly licensed family-emigration article".
*Problem:* Pacifica's remitted share does pay for scholarships, so "pays for none" is false on the page. The families clause sits in 1906, while G-11 says families come with Julian's course from 1908, and the 1868 article already licenses family emigration. "His wife" leaves out women entrants, though women have sat the examinations since 1859.
*Fix:* "A scholarship China funds, the remitted indemnity's among them, binds its holder to China and not to the Realm; the Realm binds only those whose places it pays for. … and an entrant brings a spouse and children, never a lineage." For the date of family entry, case A: amend G-11 to read the 1868 article's families as spouses and children, with Julian's course from 1908 raising the numbers (recommended). Case B: narrow the 1868 article.

**S8. The Huludao option was never part of the 1908 offer.**
*Passage:* line 1272, "What is left of the Manchurian offer is the option at Huludao."
*Problem:* the option is Victoria's, from 1898–99 (CR-042; a sufficer's instrument by G-3a). The sentence presents it as Julian's, which weakens canon's "takes up his mother's option" (line 1278) and blurs the turn.
*Fix:* "The option at Huludao, his mother's of 1899, was never part of the talks and outlives them."

**S9. The revision depends on CR-082, which is not in canon on this branch.**
*Passage:* the storyline on this branch has no sufficers, provisioners, tables of 1890–1930 or granary. The register has no CR-082 entry. The outline cites CR-082 R-3, B5, B7 and F-7 as approved.
*Problem:* merged alone, the granary (line 1672) and the "open carriage" doctrine have nothing before them in the storyline. The provisioner's turn then shows only in the cable of November 1908 and the errand of 1915. A9's three ends exist only in the outline, so the principle has to be inferred from the instruments.
*Fix:* merge CR-082 before CR-081's revision, or at the same time, and record the order in the register. Optionally add one cabinet sentence at the 1908 cable, never public: "In cabinet Julian reads the Office's table of 1910 as a target, and China's crop, its own harvest and its scholars become the Crown's business."

**S10. Figures stated more firmly than their sources allow.**
*Passage:* outline G-1 item 1 and the G-2 table; G-10; G-14 item 3.
*Problem:*
- The 5 Mt of Manchurian beans, cake and oil "at the peak of 1931" rests on CR-082 C4 "to verify". A-series §11 supports about 2.4 Mt of beans exported about 1926, and SMR consignments of 1.83–2.35 Mt in 1921–26. Consignments of beans and cake may double count, because the Dairen mills crush consigned beans. MODEL §8 gives 5.0 Mt "about 1930", not 1931.
- The G-2 table credits "Huludao and the crop article" with 4.8 million mouths. That is all of Manchuria's export, and Japan has first call on it.
- G-10's "about 3.3 million tonnes of grain abroad in 1931" is MODEL's 1930 figure, and the unit is million mouths. 1931 lies between 3.3 (1930) and 2.7 (1932), about 3.0–3.1 Mt.
- The "450,000 tons" of the 1931 credit are short tons, about 0.41 Mt; *Hist.*, general knowledge, to verify (15 million bushels × 60 lb). The credit is therefore about an eighth of sales, not a seventh.
- G-14's "the Realm passes its own soil and sea about 1940" belongs to §3's crossing. The table of 1930 says 1938 (provisioning §7).
*Fix:* mark the G-2 entry "Manchuria's whole export, MODEL, to verify; the Realm's share not computed". Replace "3.3 million tonnes" with "about 3 million tonnes (MODEL)" and "a seventh" with "about an eighth". In G-14, cite "the table of 1930 (1938)".

## The principle

- **Self-interest, not charity.** The 1908 offer (feeder lines, land offices, elevators), the 1915 leases, the rate clauses, the crop article, the compact, the 1931 credit (seller, creditor, relief giver) and the scholarship door all read as a buyer's and an examiner's interest. Still reading the old way: the opening of the AGENTS paragraph ("aimed at a strong, solvent China …"), which T-1 A keeps as the means; A8.10's column "floods and famine relief that show what the alignment does"; and line 1128 (PROPOSED), whose indemnity scholarships read as gifts until S7's wording is in.
- **The provisioner's turn** shows in two places: the cable of November 1908 with its works offer, and the errand of 1915. G-3a labels the regency's instruments in the outline only. S8 and S9 would make the turn readable in canon.
- **Japan, land against crop, 1915–1931:**
  - 1915: farm leases struck;
  - 1921: running rights at one rate, no management;
  - 1923: Japanese firms admitted at Huludao;
  - 1928: joint management, with no differential rate;
  - 1929: first call on the crop;
  - 1931: the plotters want land, and food stays outside the threat.

  The line holds once S3 and S5 are fixed.

## Literary effect

- **Victoria's errand** is the best of the new scenes: an agent used against her own school, a strand of the drift, and Kiyoshi already beside her. It needs S1 to be playable.
- **Giannini and the granary** is exposition as integrated: no decider, no vote, no cost. B2's wording gives each school a true argument and leaves the farmers' loss in view.
- **The plotters' charge** gives the 1931 officers a motive of their own beyond the breach of 1928. With S5 it becomes an argument Tokyo can lose on the facts.
- **The wheat credit** is one sentence of transaction. M5 of the outline leaves it as a candidate for Julian's test (OPEN), which is the author's decision.
- **Tang in 1908** gains a real offer and a real loss.

No new historical figures are needed: Wang Yongjiang, Giannini, Calkins, Zhang Xueliang and Yamagata are already there.

## Protected intent

Nothing in the revision breaches it. Noe Ward's death is not touched. Julian is never President. Nothing creates a Hawaiian throne abeyance, though B1's wording must not bar Hawaiian crown officers. Pacifica wages no campaign of extermination, and keeping food out of the threat of 1931 reinforces that.
