Reviewer in a fresh context, 22 November 2026. Read: `AGENTS.md`; `CR-081-outline.md` (the guiding principle, A0–A8, B0–B10 sampled in full, Part C, C10, the canon tensions, the author's decisions after integration, fact changes); the storyline diff against `origin/main` and the surrounding paragraphs (§14, §16, §25–§35; lines cited are this branch's); the CR-081 register entry; CR-081's ledger entries (lines 6560–6810). From `drafts/cr082-provisioning`, read only: `population/outputs/provisioning.md` §§3, 4 and 8, and `CR-082.md` (request, Parts B, D, E, F, I, L, canon tensions and decisions table). Labels follow the documents: APPROVED, PROPOSED, OPEN, MODEL. Figures are MODEL unless sourced.

## Verdict

**Not yet.** As integrated, CR-081 is a well-built policy *for China*. Its stated aim (A8, approved 20 November; AGENTS.md: "a strong, solvent China aligned with Pacifica at minimal cost to Japan") names no Pacifican interest beyond credit repaid, a larger customer and alignment. Pacifica's columns in A8's table read "a bank's return", "the alignment's carriers", "floods and famine relief that show what the alignment does". Grain appears only by accident: "grain carriage" sits in *China's* column (A8.4), soybeans and bean cake in *Japan's* (A8.15), and Huludao is "commerce, and it is commerce" (§27, line 1278). Talent flows one way: Pacifica trains China's cadres for China (A8.7, P-1), and nothing brings an exceptional Chinese mind into the Realm's examinations or institutions.

The material and social layers (Parts A and B) can mostly be re-read as grain and talent policy without changing a fact: railways, the conservancy, the farm bank, the market report, Huludao's quay, Tacoma's fertilizer, the register, the Customs' returns. Part C cannot. It has no motive layer and no year in which Julian's China policy turns from his mother's. Its two most strategic moments, Tang Shaoyi's request for Manchurian credit (November 1908) and the Manchurian crisis of 1931, are written without the crop. There is also a contradiction the CR does not see. The century plan needs about 70% of the world's grain and soybean trade (provisioning.md §8, MODEL), and a strong, industrialising China of 500 million is the largest rival buyer in that pool. CR-081 has to make China self-feeding as a matter of Pacifican interest, not of charity.

Four blockers. None needs a linter-tracked fact change. Two need an approved decision amended (canon tensions T-1 and T-6 below).

## Blockers

**B1. The aim states no Pacifican end.**
- *Passage:* A8 "The author's aim" and "The target in 1932"; C10; the integration summary (storyline line 113); AGENTS.md's CR-081 paragraph.
- *Problem:* The principle replaces the question CR-082 opened with ("what is the point of Pacifica's China policy?"), but the CR's frame still answers "China's benefit". No section says what Pacifica wants from China in 1932 or a century on. Every later decision is measured against A8, so the measure is wrong.
- *Fix:* Add an A9, "Pacifica's target in China, 1932 and after (cabinet, never stated)". It should name three ends:
  1. Manchuria's crop moves on open carriage and published rates to a market the Concord's credit, ships and standards organise, and its land stays out of any army's hands (CR-082 R-3, approved).
  2. China feeds itself, so that its 500 million do not bid against the Realm in the pool the Office's tables say the Realm will need from about 1940 (provisioning.md §3: the margin is 115% in 1932 and is passed in 1940 in the central case, or in 1931 on canon's present nitrogen).
  3. China's exceptional engineers and scientists can enter the Realm's examination on the same terms as anyone (CR-037).

  Then keep "a strong, solvent China aligned with Pacifica" as the means to those ends (canon tension T-1, case A). Add a fourth column to A8's table, "the crop and the rolls": for each decision, the mouths it feeds or the people it brings, and by what year. This is CR-082 B5's rule of two figures (approved F-7), which CR-081's loans do not yet carry.

**B2. Manchuria R-3 is not carried out.**
- *Passage:* A8.15 and S-16 (soybeans "through Huludao (canon)"); P-9 (the Mukden protocol, 1921); storyline lines 1278 (Huludao, 1909) and 1656 (the district, 1923); CR-082 L ("Soybean compact with China — date OPEN (CR-081)").
- *Problem:* R-3 names the instruments: a soybean compact, the mills and the port at Huludao, and the Realm's seed and nitrogen sold to Manchuria's farmers. CR-081 has only the port and the crushing quay. It has no compact, no seed or nitrogen for Manchuria (Tacoma's fertilizer from 1929 goes to South China's rice), no crop contract, no storage and no ledger entry. CR-082 I says "this is the purpose CR-081 lacked", and CR-081 still lacks it.
- *Fix:* Two dated instruments, both PROPOSED for the author:
  1. *1923:* the Huludao district's charter (CR-057, Wang Yongjiang) carries a Realm–Mukden crop article. It provides bonded elevators on the quay, long purchase contracts at the Customs' published Huludao price, and Columbia ammonia and Polytechnic soybean seed at the one royalty, sold to any farmer and to any buyer's agent, Japanese firms included.
  2. *1929:* after Zhang Xueliang's change of flag, a Soybean compact between the Concord and Nanjing, a companion instrument to the Treaty of Nanjing. It gives a published-rate guarantee for crop carriage on every Manchurian line, takes the crop by contract and never by land, and sets Japan's share for bean cake and feed first (R-6).

  Add the ledger rows. Cast: Wang Yongjiang, Zhang Xueliang and Hoover (see Literary effect).

**B3. Japan's land against Pacifica's crop is not staged.**
- *Passage:* P-8 (1915, line 1299); P-9 (line rates); the Treaty of Nanjing (line 1520); P-20 and §35 (line 1697).
- *Problem:* The principle's central conflict inside the Concord is that Japan wants Manchuria's land and Pacifica wants its crop. CR-081 keeps Japan's cost low and turns its interests into contracts, but it never says that the Realm's interest is the opposite of the army's. The 1931 plot (§35) is stopped on the Nanjing guarantee and the breach of 1928, and the officers' grievance is courts and customs, not land. Three passages already serve the principle by accident and should be made deliberate:
  - P-8 gives Japan land leases "for business and mining" and none for farming. *Hist.*, general knowledge, to verify: Group II of the 1915 Demands sought leases of land for agriculture in South Manchuria.
  - P-9's running rights are at "the line's published rates, the same for every shipper".
  - The Concord's joint management from 1928 hands Japan a share of the grain artery.
- *Fix:*
  - **P-8 (spring 1915):** the agricultural clause is struck on Julian's instruction, which Victoria carries to the genrō without sharing its reason. The sufficer goes on the provisioner's errand.
  - **P-9 and the Treaty of Nanjing (1928):** the published-rate clause names the crop. Joint management may not set differential rates for any shipper or destination.
  - **§35 (1931):** the plotters' aim is settlement land, and their charge is that Pacifican contracts take Manchuria's harvest while its acres are closed to Japanese farmers. Thalassia's case in Tokyo adds that a client state would close the open carriage on which Japan's own bean cake and feed depend. Wanbaoshan (July 1931) is a quarrel over paddy water (*Hist.*, general knowledge, to verify: an irrigation channel dug by Korean settlers). It suits this exactly and is already placed before the co-guarantors (C8).
  - **Julian's threat (§35, PROPOSED):** keep it to oil, transport and convertibility. Food is not used against a partner, because CR-082 F3 refuses seed and nitrogen contracts only to a power at war with the Concord.

**B4. No turn from sufficer to provisioner.**
- *Passage:* P-4e item 1 (Julian's cable, line 1272) and item 2 (Tang's mission, November 1908 to January 1909); §27's Huludao (line 1278); C1 and C2 under the regency.
- *Problem:* Victoria is a sufficer and Julian a provisioner (CR-082 F-6, approved). Under Victoria the Office's tables are read as a limit, and under Julian from 1908 as a target (B5, B7). CR-081 runs one continuous policy from 1887 to 1932. The sharpest contradiction is P-4e item 2. Tang asks for "a currency-reform loan and Manchurian credit". Julian offers only the currency loan and lets Manchuria go, although his own works rule (P-4e, "needs no vote") would allow Manchurian works credit at once. A provisioner who has just read a table forecasting the crossing for 1920 (provisioning.md §7, the 1910 table, MODEL) does not decline Manchurian credit.
- *Fix:*
  - **November 1908:** Julian offers the currency loan on the budget rule and Manchurian works credit on the works rule: feeder lines to Huludao, the railway land offices of S-4, and elevators. Yuan's fall and Tang's recall stop the Manchurian credit too, and the Huludao option taken up in 1909 is what survives of it. Julian's cable to his mother then reads as what it is, the provisioner taking the field.
  - **Before 1908:** label the regency's China instruments as the sufficer's: the conservancy of 1891, the Liaodong settlement and the Huludao option of 1898–99, the friendship with Cixi and the Polytechnic of Formosa (1906). Victoria's figures measure famine averted and partners kept, not mouths to be bought.
  - This amends an approved decision (canon tension T-6).

## Should fix

**S1. China as rival buyer is absent.**
- *Passage:* Part B (B1, B3, B10 S-16); P-21 (line 1688).
- *Problem:* CR-081 never counts China's own food imports or their future. *Hist.*, general knowledge, to verify (CR-082's sources already list it as to verify): China's coastal cities imported rice from Indochina, Siam and Burma and wheat flour from North America and Australia in the 1920s. In our timeline today China is the largest soybean importer (to verify). The pool the century plan needs is largely the pool China buys from.
- *Fix:*
  - Add a B11, "China in the grain pool". It should give China's imports by decade (Hist., to verify) and canon's change from the conservancy, the farm bank, pumping, fertilizer and settlement (MODEL), and it should state the interest: every Chinese district made self-feeding takes nothing from the Concord's granary (CR-082 F, work 4).
  - **P-21 (September 1931):** *Hist.* (the outline's own note, FRUS): China bought 450,000 tons of American wheat and flour on credit. In canon the Realm, which sells about 2.7–3.3 million tonnes abroad (provisioning.md §4, MODEL), is the natural seller and creditor. Write it as a Pacifican wheat credit to the Flood Relief Commission. That makes Pacifica a seller, a creditor and a relief giver in one transaction.

**S2. Talent: no channel into the Realm.**

- *Problem:* The principle makes both schools intelligence maximizers. The sufficers maximize by selection, keeping the Realm rich and few. The provisioners maximize by numbers. CR-081 gives neither school anything from China: the Mission's men, the scholars and the "tens of thousands" all serve China. S-2 fixes the Chinese Pacificans at canon's 4.8 million. It does not say whether Julian's entry course, lifted from 1908 (CR-082 B7, approved), moves that figure.
- *Fix:*
  - State a rule in A8.7 and P-1. China's scholars may sit the Realm's examination; those who pass may take Realm posts, but no bond is attached to Boxer scholarships, so China keeps its cadres by choice and not by contract (CR-037: the examination, never a quota). Under Victoria the open door is selective. From 1908 Julian adds family entry under the 1868 article.
  - Ask the population model whether B7 changes S-2's 4.8 million.
  - Show the cost: Li's and later Nanjing's complaint that Pacifica skims the best.

**S4. Siberian grain on the Chinese line.**

## Canon tensions (both cases each)

**T-1. "Aimed at a strong, solvent China aligned with Pacifica at minimal cost to Japan"** (A8, APPROVED 20 November; AGENTS.md).
- *Case A, canon adapted:* the aim stands as the means, and A9 (B1) states the ends in cabinet. "Minimal cost to Japan" holds everywhere except Manchuria's farm land and the destination of its crop.
- *Case B, canon amended:* the aim is replaced by the grain and talent ends, and "minimal cost to Japan" is dropped for Manchuria.
- No tracked facts either way. *Recommendation:* case A. It keeps thirty approved decisions and reads most of them as instruments. Case B would reopen Parts A and B for no gain.

**T-2. "The Republic's further purpose in the Pacific is never stated"** (CR-075; kept by the principle, item 5; CR-082 T1 case A, decided).
- *Case A:* never stated in public. The reader sees it in the Office's public tables (CR-082 B6), in cabinet minutes carrying the two figures, and in rivals who do the arithmetic: Japan's General Staff, London, and Nanjing's ministers reading the Realm's crossing year.
- *Case B:* stated in a treaty preamble, for example the soybean compact.
- *Recommendation:* case A, already decided. It is a craft requirement, not a collision: without the minutes the CR will still read as charity.

**T-3. "The giant its neighbours need not fear"** (CR-058; CR-082 T8, OPEN).
- *Case A:* it stands. In China, "control" means being the indispensable carrier, creditor and contract-holder, never the landholder, and the fear is economic. That fear is the anti-colonial undertow canon already has (line 1651) and Formosa's and Huludao's grievances.
- *Case B:* it is retired for Asia after 1928, once Nanjing and Tokyo count the Realm's contracts.
- *Recommendation:* case A, consistent with CR-082's recommendation.

**T-4. The coequal Concord** (AGENTS.md: two coequal parties).
- *Case A:* the grain programme is the Concord's. The 1929 soybean compact is signed by the Concord, Japan's rice comes first (R-6), and the common Finance and Foreign ministers, normally Pacifican, administer it. The army reads this as subordination, its existing charge against the common ministry (P-20).
- *Case B:* the programme is the Realm's own and Japan is a customer. This thins coequality in substance.
- *Recommendation:* case A.

**T-5. The Reserve Bank refuses to print in 1931–32** (CR-057, APPROVED).
- *Case A:* the refusal stands. Slump purchases come from the Patrimony's foreign arm and reserves (S5).
- *Case B:* the Bank discounts grain warrants beyond its cover, which amends CR-057.
- *Recommendation:* case A. The foreclosed home farms beside the foreign contracts give conflict without cost to canon.

**T-6. Tang Shaoyi's mission, November 1908** (P-4e item 2, APPROVED 21 November; line 1272).
- *Case A, canon kept:* Julian offers only the currency loan, and the provisioner shows himself only at Huludao in 1909.
- *Case B, canon amended:* Julian also offers Manchurian works credit under the works rule. Tang's recall ends both offers, and Huludao is what remains (B4).
- No tracked facts. *Recommendation:* case B. Case A leaves the provisioner declining Manchuria at the one moment China offers it.

**T-7. "Pacifica lends, trains and sets the standards; China owns"** (A8 target; A8.7).
- *Case A:* China keeps its cadres. The Realm's gain comes through the open examination and family entry (S2).
- *Case B:* Boxer scholarships carry CR-038's five-year bond to work in Pacifica. That is a talent drain that would sour alignment.
- *Recommendation:* case A.

## Literary effect

- **Agency and motive.** Today Pacifica gives and China chooses. Under the principle Julian wants something, and wants it on a clock: the crossing years of the 1910, 1920 and 1930 tables (1920, 1933 and 1938, MODEL). Every refusal in Part C becomes a choice between two interests instead of a display of rectitude: Sichuan in 1911, the 1913 loan, Mukden after 1926.
- **Conflict with Victoria.** The friendship with Cixi is her China; Manchuria's crop is his. The 1908 cable, the refused Sichuan buy-out (1911), the 1915 errand carrying his farm-lease instruction and the 1921 break gain a common thread: the sufficer who wants a partner that survives, against the provisioner who wants its harvest. Kaʻiulani's 1905 assurance that Japan's army will leave Manchuria is Victoria's act, and it is the precondition of Julian's policy.
- **Conflict with Japan.** Land against crop is the strand's spine from 1915 to 1931: Katō's Demands, Tanaka's expeditions, the officers of 1928 and 1931. Shidehara and Takahashi carry Japan's civilian answer, which is food by contract. Kiyoshi's son and Grishina-Almazova's salon (CR-045) already give Thalassia ears among the officers.
- **Conflict inside China.** Wang Yongjiang sells the crop and keeps Manchuria's accounts. Zhang Xueliang must choose between Japan's land and the Concord's contract. Mao's rent limit keeps grain in tenants' hands (M4). Nanjing's ministers can read the Realm's tables and ask what the open carriage is for.
- **Viewpoint and cast, all existing.**
  - Hoover, Kaiping's engineer in 1900–01 and Pacifica's food administrator, holding the food lever at The Hague, is the natural carrier of the grain strand. He administers the 1931 credit (S1) and, as an inner-circle man under bond, answers the Senate without a false word about what the contracts are for.
  - Fong reads the bean returns.
  - Wang Yongjiang writes the crop article (B2).
  - Tang Shaoyi asks for the Manchurian credit (T-6).
  - Victoria and Kaʻiulani carry the regency's limit.
  - Takahashi carries Japan's villages.
  - No new historical figures are needed.
