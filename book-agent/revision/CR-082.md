## B. The doctrine (F-4 to F-8, F-14, D16)

**B1. Three steps.**
1. **Powell's ceiling, 1888.** Powell's office reports how many acres the domain's water can reach once it is stored: about 22 million. He is believed, because his office draws the districts. This settles canon's OPEN on when he enters the Republic's service (687): by 1888.
2. **The first table, 1890.** With the Realm's first census the Office of Public Measures prints the population's course beside Powell's acres, and the year they cross: 1914.
3. **The revision with each census** (1900, 1910, 1920, 1930), laid before the Senate. Research moves the crossing back with each table: 1915, 1920, 1933, 1938.

**B2. Where the question comes from.** It does not come from Elias as a charge. It arises in the offices under Victoria. She asks for the table expecting it to show that the domain suffices. It shows a crossing in 1914, and she draws the sufficer's conclusion: fewer people, more yield (review S3).

**B3. Two schools of one aim (author, 08:27).** Both schools maximize the number of exceptional Pacifican engineers and scientists. They differ on how.
- **The sufficers.** The talent comes from selection. The Realm should be rich and few, entry selective, and every child examined and schooled at the Realm's best. The domain carries what its water carries, and an empire of mouths would dilute the schools. Victoria, Powell's office, the Hawaiian court and the nations' councils hold this view, and later Keawe, a sufficer on land and water. Their figure is the Office's own. At six-tenths of one per cent a year the Realm's works and the first sellers feed it for a century; at Julian's rate it must buy three-quarters of what the world sells.
- **The provisioners.** The talent comes from numbers as well as selection. A larger base throws up more exceptional minds at the same examination, so the half-billion must be fed and the acres found. A fed umbrella also widens the pool the Realm can recruit from. Julian holds this view, with the Polytechnic's engineers, the Works and the Navy.
- **Names (APPROVED, D18):** for the sufficers, Powell (canon) and Eugene W. Hilgard (1833–1916), the soil scientist of alkali lands, historically of the University of California (*Hist.*, to verify). For the provisioners, Hoover (CT-HOOVER, canon's food administrator) and Elwood Mead (1858–1936), irrigation engineer, historically Wyoming's State Engineer (*Hist.*, to verify). Hilgard and Mead are registered on integration.

**B4. The Office's assumptions (story assumptions, author).** The diet is held constant. The regency's tables let the growth rate fall over a century to 1.5% a year. Julian's tables, from 1908, let it fall to 2.0%, and count the Philippine crown.

**B5. The rule of two figures.** From 1890 every treaty, loan, lease and holding abroad goes to the cabinet with two figures beside its price: the mouths it feeds or the people it brings, and by what year. Under Victoria the figures are read as a limit. Under Julian they are read as a target.

**B6. Public table, unstated purpose.** The table is public, like every table of the Office. The conclusions drawn from it are cabinet minutes. The neighbours can do the arithmetic, and some do (T1).

**B7. Entry (review S4; review 2, S5).** Each table carries an entry course, and the sufficers lose the argument on numbers in every decade: the canon run grows 4.1% a year in the 1890s and 4.8% in 1900–1910. The numbers come from the regency's own settlement programme, which Powell's districts and the Land Fund's sales carry and which the Senate will not stop, and from natural increase. Victoria shapes entry and cannot shrink it. What her tables hold is the shape of entry, not its size. Hands come under the guest-worker programme of 1890, which adds workers without adding residents, and residents come by selective entry. The provisioners answer that the guest workers, "a class of workers outside citizenship" (1038), are talent the examination never sees. ## F. The provisioners' plan for the century (P-1 to P-5, D13, D14)
