# Narrow review of the shared calendar (fresh context, 24 November 2026)

I read `CR-083-086-calendar.md`, the drafts `CR-083.md` to `CR-087.md`, `AGENTS.md` and lines 1602, 1651, 1734, 1746 and 1748 of `master_storyline.md`, and nothing else. For every row of the calendar's table and each decision A to K, I looked up each place in the four drafts that states the event: body sections, each draft's own calendar table, impact notes, set pieces, ledger lines, canon tensions, fact changes and decision rows. I then checked the sailings against the calendar's own travel times. `preflight.py --draft` reports 0 errors for each of the four drafts (warnings only: registry, ledger and citation notices unrelated to the calendar). The calendar's decisions are not reargued here.

## Verdict

Agrees except for the items below. The body sections and the four calendar tables tell one story. What remains is one ordering problem inside the calendar's own logic (item 1), one gap the calendar leaves open (item 2), and leftovers in ledger lines, one set piece, one tension, label rows and the lists of findings.

## Leftovers and contradictions

1. **The renewal's instruments are exchanged before Julian has ratified.** `CR-085.md` line 124: "He then exchanges the renewal's instruments with an authorized minister of the Realm", about 28 or 29 July; the same in line 277 ("only then does he exchange the renewal's instruments") and `CR-086.md` line 335 ("after which Takahashi exchanges the renewal's instruments"). But `CR-085.md` line 168 has "the Emperor about 16 July on the cabled votes, Julian on 1 August", and `CR-086.md` lines 342 and 347 have Julian ratify only on 1 August, after the signatures. An exchange about 29 July has no ratification of Julian's to exchange. The calendar does not date the exchange. Either Julian signs his ratification of the renewal before the exchange (and 1 August is for the two articles and the late treaties), or the exchange moves to 1 August after step 4. One sentence, the same in both drafts.

2. **The Concord's guarantee of Caledonia is signed a week or more after Proclamation.** `CR-083.md` line 198: "In the last days of July the Concord's guarantee and the **Trilateral Treaty of Thalassia** are signed there"; also line 377 ("signed in late July 1932, ratified by Julian on 1 August and by the Emperor in the autumn") and line 571. The calendar's row for 22 to 30 July does not list this signature, and its decision G gives as the reason "Caledonia is then never without its guarantee". In `CR-083` the Association Treaty "holds no defence clause" (line 245), so from about 18 July to the last days of July the Republic has a guarantee the delegations have voted and nobody has signed. Not an impossibility, but the drafts should say what covers those days (the delegations' vote of 9 to 11 July, or a signature on Proclamation Day), and the calendar's table should carry the row.

3. **Ledger lines say "ratified July 1932" for instruments Julian ratifies on 1 August.** `CR-085.md` line 360 (`C-1932-NAVY-SEAT-PROTOCOL`: "agreed at Hayama 12–15 June 1932, ratified July 1932"), line 361 (`C-1932-HAYAMA-GRADUATION`, same words) and line 363 (`C-1932-ATOMIC-POWER-BOARD`: "chartered at Hayama 12–15 June 1932, ratified July 1932"). Should say: ratified by the Emperor about 16 July and by Julian on 1 August 1932 (subject to item 1).

4. **The yen's agreement in the ledger.** `CR-085.md` line 362: "`C-1932-ADJUSTABLE-PARITY` — Thalassia Adjustable Gold-Parity Agreement (June–July 1932; `PAC`, `JP`)". Should say: agreed in principle at Hayama in June, signed about 28 or 29 July 1932, in effect on signature. The draft also names its makers three ways: "an instrument of the two finance ministries" (line 124), "he and the common Finance minister sign" (line 277), and `PAC`, `JP` here. One of them.

5. **Set piece still has the sufficers name their price in July.** `CR-086.md` line 395: "**The Senate, July 1932.** An aliʻi senator makes the food order the price of the three votes that need the aliʻi seats." By decision D the price is named in the Senate's committee in the last days of May; in July the sufficers collect by having Article II voted first. The set piece should show the collecting, or move to May.

6. **"Agent-General" for the dominion's man in London.** `CR-084.md` line 267: "with a Caledonian chapter initialled by the dominion's Agent-General (`CR-083`)". Should be High Commissioner (decision A, the author's note of 10:45; `CR-083.md` line 195 already has it). The same word stands in the drafter's summary of the approval of 10:10, printed in all four drafts: `CR-083.md` line 55, `CR-084.md` line 48, `CR-085.md` line 43, `CR-086.md` line 46 ("A, the dominion's Agent-General initials the Caledonian chapter in London"). If that summary is the drafter's and not the author's words, correct it or add "(High Commissioner, by the note of 10:45)".

7. **"Westminster ratifies" survives twice.** `CR-084.md` line 219: "the eleven annuities do not hold Westminster to ratifying the retrenchment treaty"; line 386 (`T-A2`, Case B): "London still gets nothing before Westminster ratifies". By decision K: the Crown ratifies, Parliament approves; write "the King's ratification" or "its deposit". (Line 105 has the phrase inside the author's quoted sheet and stays.)

8. **The April accord called "signed" and a "treaty".** `CR-084.md` line 181: "under a signed **preliminary Anglo–Concord agreement (April 1932)**"; line 201: "Under Britain's preliminary treaty (`April 1932`)". The calendar and the rest of the draft have a preliminary accord, initialled.

9. **Britain's and the Netherlands' treaties "agreed at Hayama".** `CR-084.md` line 119: the Concord treaties (`C-1932-UK-RETRENCHMENT`, `C-1932-LABUAN-LEASE`, `C-1932-TONGA-FRIENDSHIP`, `C-1932-BATAVIA-COMPACT`) are "agreed at Hayama". By the calendar they were initialled in London in April (the Compact in June) and Hayama agrees only "the Concord's position on the British and Dutch treaties", as the draft's own line 269 says. Should say: the Concord's position on them adopted at Hayama.

10. **Caledonia at Hayama: "agrees" where the calendar has "confirmed".** `CR-085.md` line 276: "It also agrees the Concord's position on the British, Dutch and Caledonian instruments". The guarantee was settled in March and is only confirmed at Hayama (`CR-085.md` line 156 and `CR-083.md` lines 196, 197 and 259 say so). Should say: and confirms the guarantee of Caledonia.

11. **The delegations' vote of 9 to 11 July omits the guarantee of Caledonia in two drafts.** `CR-085.md` line 277: "vote the renewal and the two articles"; `CR-086.md` line 330: "vote the renewal and the texts of the two articles". The calendar and `CR-083.md` line 198 add the guarantee of Caledonia. Add it or point to `CR-083`.

12. **Label disagreement on Takahashi carrying the instruments.** `CR-086.md` line 571 (F-15): "That Takahashi carries them is PROPOSED". `CR-085.md` line 577 (R-8) has it APPROVED (10:10, H), as the calendar does.

13. **Stale entries in the lists of findings, which still describe the old order.**
    - `CR-085.md` line 601: "the Emperor signs and Chichibu deposits". Now Takahashi carries and lays down; Chichibu "neither carries nor signs" (line 125).
    - `CR-085.md` line 613: "Whether a week's delay of the Council leaves enough time for the Privy Council before the July votes." The Privy Council now advises after the votes.
    - `CR-085.md` line 618: "Takahashi's presence at Thalassia in June against his duties in Tokyo." He is not at Thalassia in June (line 177).
    - `CR-086.md` line 614: "S5: the Emperor's ratification follows separate advice on the Act". Now one advice covers the renewal and the Act (line 342).
    - `CR-086.md` lines 628 and 631: whether the Privy Council can advise in mid-July is listed as untested; lines 342 to 344 now answer it.
    - `CR-084.md` line 630: "Not done: … where the April accord is initialled against the travel times". Done by decision A.
    - `CR-083.md` line 605: "S10, that the sufficers' win of May costs nobody anything". In `CR-083` their objection is now in March (line 246).
    - The revision notes at `CR-086.md` line 8 ("only because the sufficers make it their condition in July"; "with Chichibu depositing Japan's ratification") and `CR-083.md` line 11 ("the sufficers object to it in May") are history of earlier revisions and may stand if marked superseded.

14. **Small disagreements.**
    - `CR-083.md` line 374: the compacts are "ratified by the Republic from late July 1932"; lines 198 and 230 have Burrard and Spences Bridge ratified on Proclamation Day, about 18 July.
    - `CR-084.md` line 218: "Baldwin, Chamberlain and Cunliffe-Lister go to Ottawa in July"; line 157 and the calendar add Thomas and give 13 July.
    - `CR-084.md` lines 243, 274, 284, 363 and 593: the States General "ratifies" the Batavia Compact; the calendar and `CR-086.md` line 337 have "approve", by the same reasoning as K.
    - `CR-086.md` line 347: the dominions are named after France; the calendar's order puts them after China and before Caledonia.
    - `CR-085.md` line 129 gives Julian's mandate from the cabinet and does not mention the Senate's committee of the last days of May (calendar row D; `CR-086.md` lines 263 and 302 carry it). A cross-reference would do.

## Feasibility

No sailing is impossible. Checked: Kaʻiulani, London in the last days of April to Thalassia about 15 May (fourteen days); Simon, London about 31 May to Thalassia about 14 June; Julian, about 2 June to Hayama for 12 June and back about 25 June; Simon's messenger, about 17 June to London about 1 July; the full powers, 8 July to about 22 July; Takahashi, about 17 July to about 27 July; Pattullo, 18 July to about 25 July. Three places are tight and one is the problem already listed:

- Julian lands on the day the Council opens (ten days from about 2 June; `CR-085.md` line 130 says "nine or ten"). His bargaining with Takahashi "in the days around the sittings" (line 131) then has no days before them.
- Simon reaches Thalassia about 14 June and the Council rises on 15 June. Britain's four requests are put, cabled, considered at Hayama and answered within about a day (`CR-084.md` line 221). Possible by cable; the draft should say it is a day.
- The Realm's signer of the Association Treaty must be at Victoria on about 18 July. If he waits for the Senate's vote of 9 to 11 July he has six days' sailing and arrives on the eve. `CR-083.md` calls him "the Realm's envoy" (line 198) and "the minister Julian names" (line 245); if he is already at Victoria, say so.
- Item 1 above is the only true ordering fault.

Against canon: lines 1602 (delegations meet separately at Thalassia), 1651 (Julian presides with the common ministers, national ministers outside the sittings), 1734 (agreed at Hayama; authorized ministers sign and exchange at Thalassia; Hirohito does not travel), 1746 (conference in June and July) and 1748 (Japan attends from the standing alliance) are all met by the drafts as they stand, with the Final Act of 1 August as the listed addition (`CR-086` `T-4`).

## What agrees

- March and April: terms published about 25 March, the vote of 16 April, the accord initialled in London in the last days of April with the pledge as a separate declaration, the High Commissioner on cabled instructions (`CR-083` body and table).
- May: 15 May, 26 to 28 May, the British cabinet's decision and Simon's sailing, the request and consent, Labuan; Article II's binding form won in the last days of May (`CR-086` D1, D5, E1).
- June: Kaʻiulani at Thalassia and taking part by cable; Finance and Navy at Hayama; Hirohito does not bargain and Takahashi settles the text outside the sittings; one request won and three lost; the bare results by cable and the assessment by messenger; the Severance Act with its commencement order.
- July: 8 July by cable; four two-thirds votes about 9 to 11 July with Article II first, the fifth in late July; the annuity bill about 12 July; one Privy Council advice with its narrow scope; the commencement order; Proclamation about 18 July with the Association Treaty signed at Victoria; late signatures as signers arrive; the yen in effect on signature.
- 1 August: the order of signing, Takahashi's own signature, Kaʻiulani last and seated, what is and is not ratified on the day, Victoria at Honolulu.
- Autumn: the King's ratification deposited after Parliament's approval; tranches in the order 2, 11 and 4, the third needing both ratifications; the Emperor's second advice; "Britain acts twice".
- `CR-087` states no event of the calendar and conflicts with none.
