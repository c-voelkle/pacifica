## Verdict

One more revision. The order of events from September 1931 to 1 August 1932 is now nearly the same everywhere, the annuity share agrees with `CR-084` `B1`, and the storyline citations say what the draft claims with two exceptions (S4, S5). Three things would make integration wrong as the draft stands: the wheat contract's buyer and its relation to `L892`, `L1695` and the Final Act are told three ways, one of them contradicting `CR-086`; the guarantee sits in two instruments at once and the sufficers win in July what Hayama gave in June; and the Trilateral and Association treaties grant land and rights that the draft's own standstill rule forbids. Besides these there are about a dozen leftovers of older versions and several approval labels that say APPROVED in the table and PROPOSED in the body.

## Blockers

**B1. The prairie wheat contract: who buys, under which rule, and whether a treaty records it.**
- *Passage:* lines 110, 163, 172–178, 186, 196, 280, 294–297; line 220 of CR-086.md.
- *Problem:* (a) `T9` Case B, marked Decided, says "`L1695`'s sentence that the granary takes contracts abroad only "for beans and rice" gains the prairie wheat as a stated exception." That makes the wheat a contract of the Crown's granary. The body says the opposite: the board votes the contract "beside the Crown's granary" (line 110), the wheat is "resold to Japan and China" (line 177), and it feeds "0" mouths in the Realm (line 196). A reserve "against a bad year or a closed sea" (`L1695`) is not resold. `CR-086` `D4` (line 220) still says the granary "takes contracts abroad only for beans and rice (`L1695`)", with no exception. (b) Line 175 says "`CR-086` reads it the same way for the Pampas". It does not. `CR-086` keeps the Patrimony off the Pampas because "the Patrimony behind it lends to no government in negotiation (`L892`)", and Argentina is not even at the conference; on that reading the prairie contract is barred too. (c) Line 175 rests the contract's safety on "the treaty of 1932 neither records nor conditions the contract", and line 280 says "recorded in no treaty", yet line 186 and the same line 280 name it "in the schedule to Article II of the Final Act", an instrument the Realm and Canada both sign. (d) *Hist.*: by late 1931 the seller is an agency whose borrowings the federal government guarantees and whose stocks it sells (see History). Calling it "not the government of Canada" for `L892` is thin, and Ottawa "asks first" (line 174).
- *Fix:* decide one thing and write it in `T9`, `A11`, `A12`, the calendar and the ledger: the contract is an investment of the Patrimony's board outside the granary, and `L1695`'s "beans and rice" sentence is untouched. State the `L892` reading once, as a tension of its own (the bar applies on the day of investment and to governments as counterparties), and agree it with `CR-086` `D4`, which must either adopt it or say why the Pampas differ. Say in `A11` and in `CR-086` `D3` why naming the contract in the Realm's schedule is not "recording" it (the schedule is the Realm's unilateral list), or drop "recorded in no treaty".

**B2. The guarantee is in two instruments, and the sufficers' concession is granted a month before they ask.**
- *Passage:* lines 116, 156–157, 164–165, 283–284.
- *Problem:* `A10` lists among the "Association terms": "perpetual **Alaska rail, road, and cable transit corridor**", "Pacifican defense guarantee" and "a joint naval repair contract at Esquimalt". `A10a` puts "The guarantee of Caledonia, the Esquimalt repair contract and the Alaska corridor's naval clauses" in a separate Concord instrument, "agreed at the Hayama Council in early June", ratified by "both crowns and the Concord's delegations" and not by the Senate. Line 157 then has the sufficers object in July that "the Realm is guaranteeing the defence of a republic the Senate refused to admit" and "consent when the guarantee is written as the Concord's and external only". It was already the Concord's since 5–8 June (line 116; line 205 of CR-085.md). The calendar says "the Senate consents to the treaties" (plural), `A10a` gives the Senate only the Association Treaty, and `CR-086`'s calendar has the Senate consent "to the guarantees … by two-thirds". `A10a` cites `L1604 ("two-thirds Senate consent")` but never says what majority the Association Treaty needs; `CR-084` `T-S1` (decided Case A, two-thirds) is not referred to.
- *Fix:* strip the guarantee, the repair contract and the corridor's naval clauses from `A10`'s term list. Move the sufficers' objection to before Hayama (they object in the spring to a Realm guarantee in the draft treaty; the cabinet takes it to Hayama as the Concord's), or give them a different July price. State the two-thirds rule in `A10a` with a reference to `CR-084` `T-S1`, and make the calendar's July row name the one treaty the Senate votes.

**B3. The treaties grant what the standstill rule forbids, and the nations negotiate while the ban is still law.**
- *Passage:* lines 129, 144–152, 156, 181.
- *Problem:* `A9`: "No land is granted and no dam, mine, or railway is built on a nation's unceded country without its compact." The first-wave compacts are all interior and northern. None is with a coast nation, and line 151 says "Tahltan country on the Stikine … stays under the standstill rule". Yet the Association Treaty gives "chartered industrial districts at Burrard Inlet, Prince Rupert, and Trail/Kootenays" and a "perpetual" Alaska corridor, and the Trilateral gives Canada "a bonded Canadian quay on Burrard Inlet, with transit at Prince Rupert, entrenched so that no government at Victoria can revoke them". Those stand on Squamish, Musqueam, Tsleil-Waututh and Tsimshian ground with no compact, and the corridor crosses the country the draft has just said is closed. Separately, the repeal of the 1927 statute "barring the raising of money or hiring of counsel for land claims" is an upfront constitutional term (line 129) that takes effect on Proclamation Day in June, so from March to May the nations negotiate their land with no right to counsel or funds.
- *Fix:* make each grant "subject to the compact of the nation whose ground it is", name the coast compacts (or say the quay and districts wait for them), and have Pattullo's legislature repeal the 1927 statute before the talks open. This also gives Kelly (Haida) and Paull (Squamish) something of their own at stake.

## Literary effect

The crisis has a real shape: a prosperous dominion broken by two currencies, a premier who resigns over an oath, a sovereign who is asked, wants to say yes and is forbidden. "Foreclosure by currency" is the best thing in the draft because both sides are right.

- **Who wants and who loses.** Tolmie, Julian, the Island and London are clear. Pattullo is not: he asks for union, is refused, and becomes President of something else in one sentence, with no reaction and no price. Bennett's loss is misdescribed (S3). The sufficers win a point already conceded (B2). Giannini loses for the third time in a season with an argument that has no money in it (S10, S14). Japan gets one condition and one refusal, which is right in scale but floats free of May's coup.
- **The nations.** One refusal is good and the Sekani valley is well chosen. But the nations have no stated demand of their own beyond signing or not signing, their leaders at the table are from nations that are not signing, they bargain without counsel (B3), and the loss of the King as a court of appeal is not felt by anyone.
- **Residents of Asian origin** receive everything and do nothing. Tokyo speaks for the Japanese; nobody speaks for the Chinese or the South Asians, and the only figure is an optional "from below" candidate.
- **Scene or term sheet.** `A10a`, `A11` and `A12` are term sheets, which is proper for a CR, but the set pieces follow them into rooms. The closed session of February is the hinge of the whole CR and is one sentence. The referendum, the one event with a crowd and a narrow result, has no scene. The month in which the Republic exists with no treaty, no guarantee and bonds falling due (June to July) is drama the draft notices only from London's side.
- **Victoria Kamāmalu** works against the fourth crown in February "while gathering allies"; in `CR-084` and `CR-085` she "declines to enter the debate" in July to save her standing. Both can be true, but someone should see that she spent it once.
