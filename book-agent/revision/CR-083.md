## A. Caledonia: British Columbia and the Yukon change crowns (N-1 to N-5)

**A1. 1858.** The miners who go up the Fraser are Pacificans from San Francisco. Britain makes the mainland a colony, as in canon and in history.

**A2. 1869–71, the fork.** British Columbia does not enter Confederation. Pacifica does not encourage it to. It offers the bankrupt colony a railway south to Puget Sound, a loan, and free entry for its coal and fish. Britain keeps the colony and Esquimalt. Canada stops at the Rockies. Pacifica asks for nothing more, and Victoria Kamakea's governments hold to that for forty years. The grant of the Stikine, the Yukon and the Porcupine in the spring of 1871 is made to Britain for the colony.

**A3. The Canadian Pacific.** It reaches Burrard Inlet over the colony's soil, on running rights (T5).

**A4. 1898.** Canada has no road to the Klondike that does not cross British Columbia or Alaska. London attaches the Yukon to the colony, and it is governed from Victoria. The boundary, the railway from Skagway and the assay offices stay as in canon. In 1897 Pacifica sells the old telegraph line to British Columbia, not to Canada (T7).

**A5. 1900–27.** The colony signs no treaties with the nations. South of the line their kin lease dam sites and keep their valleys. The petitions of 1909 and 1913 go to London and also to the Pacific crown. The western waters treaty of about 1906, the Columbia compact of 1922–25 and the Halibut Treaty of 1923 are British Columbia's business (T6). At Pacifica's insistence the Columbia compact is also signed with the nations of the upper river. British Columbia becomes a dominion in 1907, with Newfoundland (date PROPOSED).

**A6. 1927–31.** Britain's retreat is complete in 1927. The slump of 1929 breaks the dominion's finances. London offers three courses: government by commission, union with Canada, or the Pacific crown. The legislature at Victoria offers the crown. In 1931 the vote confirms it: the settlers together, and each nation for itself. The nations' compacts are signed inland, at Spences Bridge.

**A7. The Crown of Caledonia.** Julian wears it with the crowns of Pacifica, Hawaii and the Philippines. The legislature stays at Victoria. The Yukon is a territory of the crown, with its council at Dawson. Alaska stays under the Northern Commission.

**A8. The Fraser clause.** It is the one entrenched term of the union. No dam is built on the main stem, and no water leaves the basin. Only the nations of the river can reopen it.

**A9. What Canada is given** (a treaty of 1931, and no share of the Columbia):
1. Its railway's running rights to tidewater, and a bonded Canadian quay on Burrard Inlet.
2. A ten-year wheat contract at a floor price. This is CR-082 R-1's contract.
3. A Mackenzie waters commission. The Peace and the Liard run east into Canada, so the northern water makes the Realm the upstream party. Canada is paid for the water it loses and sits on the commission.

The boundary is declared settled. The Realm repeats that it takes no people who have not voted. Ottawa can read that either way.

*Hist.:* about 30,000 miners on the Fraser in 1858; the annexation petition of 1869; entry into Confederation on 20 July 1871 and the threats to leave in 1874 and 1878; almost no treaties in the province, the Nisga'a petition of 1913 unheard, and land-claim funds made an offence in 1927; the Yukon's 27,219 people in 1901 and 4,157 in 1921; Western Australia's vote of 1933 refused at Westminster; Newfoundland's bankruptcy of 1932.

## B. The northern water (P-2, N-6, N-7)

- **Approved (author, 19:29):** Julian's engineers propose it and prevail on it, because it is critical to national survival.
- **Dates.** Surveyed from the 1910s. Proposed with the plebiscite of 1931. Leased from the nations of each river under the compacts of Spences Bridge (T3). Built in stages after 1932.
- **Size (author, 20:32).** The first stage is about 20 km³ a year, which keeps each river's loss near a fifth. The ceiling in law is 45 km³.
- **Source.** The Peace and the Liard, which carry no salmon. The Fraser is closed (A8). The Yukon, the Stikine and the Skeena are salmon rivers and fall under canon's rule.
- **What 45 km³ waters.** 36.5 million acre-feet: 10–12 million acres at 3.0–3.5 acre-feet an acre. That feeds 18–22 million people at the yields of 1930 and 30–35 million at the century's, about 26 million mouths at the century's yields in CR-082's century module (`population/outputs/provisioning.md` §8, MODEL), 5% of the need.
- **Energy.** Lifting 45 km³ by 300–450 m takes 4.8–7.2 GW as a year-round average, and 11–17 GW of pumps if the lifting is done in five high-water months. The water gives back 4.3–6.1 GW falling 350–500 m to the fields. The drops are built on the canal, off the salmon rivers, and the pumps are fed from a dam in the Peace's canyon.
- **Limits.** The reservoirs stop where the lessor nations set the waterline. Every irrigated acre has a drain.
- **The larger plan.** The engineers draw the plan at about 135 km³, with four more rivers. The compacts refuse it. *Hist.:* the Parsons plan of 1964.
- The river flows, lifts and elevations are from general knowledge and are verified before integration.

## Canon tensions

Numbered as in CR-082.

- **T3. The Charter of 1932 is an islands charter.** Canon: the Lands and Waters Charter is brokered between Keawe and Julian in Hawaii and "makes the islands programme law" (CR-020). The outline and the discussion of 19:29–20:32 spoke of the northern water as leased "under the Charter". That was an error of the drafter's.
  - *Case A, canon kept:* the northern water is leased under the compacts of Spences Bridge (1931), which follow the mainland rule that nations lease and do not sell (CR-071). The Charter of 1932 is their island counterpart, a year later, and Julian accepts a limit in each.
  - *Case B, canon amended:* the Charter of 1932 is widened to all the crowns, and the northern leases stand under it. This changes CR-020's scope. No tracked fact changes.
  - *Recommendation:* case A. **Open.**
- **T4. British Columbia enters Confederation.** Canon (storyline §13, CR-058): "British Columbia enters Confederation on 20 July. Pacifica has encouraged it, because a strong Canadian buffer is worth more to it than a province that would provoke Britain".
  - *Case A, canon kept:* the plebiscite of 1931 is held in a Canadian province. *Hist.:* Western Australia's vote shows this fails without the federation's consent.
  - *Case B, canon amended:* Part A.
  - **Decided B** (author, 19:45, N-1). Its fact changes are in `## Fact changes`.
- **T5. The Canadian Pacific's finance.** Canon: Pacifica's money "goes quietly, through London houses and never into the charter, into the finance of the Canadian Pacific"; the brigade of 1918 sails from Vancouver in the Canadian Pacific's liners; the Vancouver–Asia trade weighs in the election of 1911. The path the author approved at 19:45 said Pacifica "does not finance the Canadian Pacific".
  - *Case A, as approved:* no Pacifican money. The line ends at Calgary, or reaches tidewater late and on harder terms. The liners, the brigade's port and the lines on 1911 are rewritten.
  - *Case B:* the quiet finance stays, for its old reason (the traffic feeds Pacifican shipping) and a new one (a Canada with a way to the sea accepts the colony's separate course). The line reaches Burrard Inlet in the 1880s on running rights over the colony's soil. The liners, the brigade and 1911 stand. The treaty of 1931 confirms what exists (G9).
  - *Recommendation:* case B. It costs canon least, and A9 already assumes a railway at tidewater. **Open.**
- **T6. Canada's school of diplomacy.** Canon: "Canada learns its diplomacy on western waters, seals, salmon and halibut"; the seal convention of 1886 is signed by "Britain for Canada"; in 1923 "Canada signs the Pacific Halibut Treaty with Pacifica, the first treaty it signs without Britain beside it".
  - *Amended by T4:* the sealers, the sockeye, the halibut and the Columbia are British Columbia's. The dominion of British Columbia signs the Halibut Treaty of 1923 without Britain beside it. Canada's dealings with Pacifica are the prairie border, the brigade of 1918, the Great Bear contract and, in 1931, the treaty of G9, which is the first it signs with Pacifica on its own.
  - Wording PROPOSED; the direction follows from N-1.
- **T7. The Yukon and the telegraph.** Canon (CR-070): "in the spring of 1897, when the strike is public, it sells Canada the old line through British Columbia as the first half of Canada's own wire to the Yukon"; the Klondike policy offers "Canada fair transit" and takes "route income from Canadian gold".
  - *Amended by T4:* the line is sold to British Columbia; the transit and the gold are the colony's.
- **T8. "The giant its neighbours need not fear"** (storyline, CR-058 summary).
  - *Case A:* the sentence stands for the years to 1931. Pacifica asked for nothing in 1871 and waited sixty years. After 1931 Ottawa has reason to count the Realm's acres, and the storyline says so.
  - *Case B:* the sentence is retired.
  - *Recommendation:* case A. **Open.**
