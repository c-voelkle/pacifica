## A. Caledonia: From British Colony to Sovereign Republic (1858–1932)

In this file, **"the dominion at Victoria"** is British Columbia (with the Yukon) from 1907 to 1932. **"The Dominion"** alone is Canada, as in canon.

**A1. 1858.** The miners who go up the Fraser are Pacificans from San Francisco. Britain makes the mainland a colony, as in canon and in history (*Hist.:* about 30,000 miners on the Fraser in 1858).

**A2. 1869–71, the fork (`T4`, approved).** British Columbia does not enter Confederation. Pacifica does not encourage it to. It offers the cash-strapped colony a railway south to Puget Sound, a loan, and free entry for its coal, timber, and fish. Britain keeps the colony and Esquimalt. Pacifica asks for no flag and no sovereignty. Elias's governments hold to that until 1887, and the Regent's and Julian's after them.
- **Pacifica's interest (PROPOSED):** Nanaimo's coal for the fleet and San Francisco, the Fraser's trade, and no second transcontinental power with a Pacific coast. This replaces canon's earlier motive for encouraging Confederation (*"a strong Canadian buffer"*).
- **London's interest (PROPOSED):** The colony costs London money. Pacifica's loan removes the cost, and Esquimalt keeps a hinterland London does not pay for.
- **Canada's line:** Canada stops at the colony's boundary—the continental divide, and north of the 54th parallel the 120th meridian. The Peace country east of the Rockies is therefore the colony's.
- **The Yukon is settled here (N-8, approved):** When London transfers the North-Western Territory to Canada on 15 July 1870 (*Hist.*), it reserves the country that history later made the Yukon Territory (which already includes the head of the Liard) and joins it to the colony.
  - *Hist.:* the Stickeen Territories (Order of 19 July 1862) ran north to the 62nd parallel under the colony's governor until the Act of 28 July 1863 returned the part north of 60° to the North-Western Territory. London here extends the 1862 precedent northward.
  - *London's motive (PROPOSED):* in 1869 Captain Charles Raymond fixed the Hudson's Bay Company's post at Fort Yukon in Alaska (*Hist.*). With Alaska Pacifican since 1867, the Pacific coast is the Yukon's cheapest road, though the Company keeps its Mackenzie route.
  - The spring 1871 navigation grant of the Stikine, the Yukon, and the Porcupine through Alaska is made to Britain **for the colony of British Columbia**, not for Canada (`T6`, `T7`).
  - **Ottawa's reaction (PROPOSED):** Macdonald protests the reservation and does not make it a quarrel with London in the year of the *Chicora* crisis and the Treaty of Washington.

**A3. The railways and the gold-dollar debt (`T5`, approved; details PROPOSED).**
- **Canada's motive:** Canada builds the Canadian Pacific Railway to the colony's boundary for the reason canon already gives (`L705 ("Grant's war is part of a plan")`): Grant's war and the *Chicora* incident proved the Union can cut the Dominion in two at the Sault canal.
- **The mountain section:** The colony builds its own section from the divide to Burrard Inlet on **gold-dollar bonds sold in San Francisco** and grants the Canadian Pacific running rights to Burrard Inlet in the 1880s. Pacifica's quiet money stays in the Canadian Pacific's finance through London houses (`T5`), and London adds an imperial mail subsidy for the Vancouver–Hong Kong liners.
  - *Canon note (`L708 ("In the spring of 1871 two treaties are made")`):* Because Ottawa never promises a railway to British Columbia as the price of Confederation, the scandal of 1873 over Allan's syndicate is removed from `L708`.
- **The Pacific Great Eastern and war debt (*Hist.*-grounded, debt size MODEL):** In 1912 the dominion at Victoria charters and guarantees the **Pacific Great Eastern Railway** northward into the Cariboo, financed in San Francisco gold dollars alongside London; after the private builders fail, Victoria takes the incomplete line over in 1918 (*Hist.:* 1912 charter and 1918 provincial takeover). Combined with the mountain-section bonds and Great War borrowing (1914–18), the dominion enters 1930 prosperous in output and trade, yet heavily indebted in **gold dollars payable in San Francisco** (sums to be sized in the finance workbook).
- **Canada's 1911 election:** With a customs line at the Rockies, Laurier still loses narrowly to Borden in 1911; the CPR, the Montreal banks, and the bonded wheat route through Burrard Inlet still pull the Prairies away from Union reciprocity.

**A4. 1896–98, the Klondike (`T7`, approved).** Bonanza Creek lies on the colony's Yukon soil, where no Pacifican finder's compact runs. Victoria governs the rush from Dawson, and for a decade Klondike royalties enrich the colony and make a separate western dominion fiscally viable. Nothing is taken from Canada in 1898. The boundary, the White Pass railway from Skagway (under the Chilkoot compact), and the assay offices stand as in canon. In the spring of 1897 Pacifica sells its old BC telegraph line (held by the telegraph company `BCCO`) to **British Columbia**, not Canada (`T7`).
- **Pattullo's route north (PROPOSED):** Because Ottawa's Commissioner Walsh does not govern Victoria's Yukon, **Thomas Dufferin "Duff" Pattullo** (born 1873 in Ontario) comes west to Victoria as a young journalist in the mid-1890s, enters the colony's lands and gold office, and serves at Dawson City from 1897 to 1902 before moving to Prince Rupert in 1908 (mayor in 1913, MLA and Minister of Lands from 1916, Opposition Leader from 1928; *Hist.* for post-1908 career).

**A5. 1900–30, the dominion at Victoria (`T6`).**
- **Dominion status (1907, PROPOSED):** British Columbia (with the Yukon) becomes a Dominion in 1907 alongside New Zealand and Newfoundland. *Hist.:* the Royal Navy vacated Esquimalt Dockyard on 1 March 1905; the dockyard is already the dominion's own from then.
- **Western treaties (`T6`):** The fur seal convention of 1886 (Britain for British Columbia), the western waters treaty of c. 1906, the **Columbia storage compact of 1922–25** (giving British Columbia half of the extra firm power its storage makes downstream, `L1554 ("a compact gives British Columbia half")`), and the **Halibut Treaty of 1923** are British Columbia's business. At Pacifica's insistence the Columbia compact is also signed with the nations of the upper river.
  - *Canada's 1918 brigade (`L1407 ("Canada sends a brigade of 4,192 men")`):* embarks at Vancouver in CPR liners under transit rights, accompanied by a smaller volunteer contingent from the dominion at Victoria (PROPOSED).
  - *Drink (`L1024 ("until British Columbia goes dry in 1917")`):* prohibition runs from 1 October 1917 to 15 June 1921, when Victoria opens government liquor stores (*Hist.*), matching the Realm's Spirit Stores.
- **Unceded Indigenous lands:** The colony signs almost no treaties; because the Peace and Fort Nelson country lie west of Canada's boundary, Canada's Treaty 8 (1899–1900) never runs there. Petitions of 1909 and 1913 go to London and are copied to the Pacific Crown; Julian's secretary acknowledges and files them as addressed to another sovereign's ministers (PROPOSED). One petitioner of 1913 lives to sign at Spences Bridge.
  - The **Allied Indian Tribes of British Columbia** form in 1916 (with James A. Teit as initial secretary, and by 1923–27 **Rev. Peter Kelly**, Haida, as chairman and **Andrew Paull**, Squamish, as secretary; *Hist.*). In 1927 Victoria passes its own statute barring the raising of money or hiring of counsel for land claims (an invention of this timeline mirroring Canada's historical 1927 Indian Act amendment). Unlike history, where the ban dissolved the organization, the Allied Tribes survive informally under Kelly and Paull to 1932 (PROPOSED).
- **Anti-Asian laws:** The colony strips the vote from Chinese residents in 1872, Japanese residents in 1895, and South Asian residents in 1907, and Vancouver riots on 7–9 September 1907 (*Hist.*), two months before Julian accedes and marries Chiyo. Because many victims are Realm citizens or Japanese subjects, Thalassia and Tokyo obtain a commission of inquiry and compensation (*Hist.:* Mackenzie King's commission paid $26,000 to the Chinese community and $9,000 to the Japanese). After Britain signs the **1919 Treaty of The Hague** carrying Japan's racial equality clause (`L1381 ("racial equality clause")`), Thalassia has a formal treaty ground for its standing quarrel with Victoria.
- **Who the settlers are by 1930:** Sixty years of rail and free trade south have settled Pacificans across the interior (Kootenays, Okanagan, Cariboo, Peace) and the Yukon. Most naturalized as British subjects to hold mining, timber, and homestead titles (PROPOSED). **Vancouver Island** (Victoria and Esquimalt) remains predominantly British and monarchist.
- **Suspicion before the crash (`T8`):** A member of the legislature at Victoria warns in the 1920s that San Francisco's banks already hold the dominion's mortgage (PROPOSED).

**A6. September 1931 – January 1932: The Monetary Divorce and Julian's Refused Leave.**
Britain's exit from gold is a rational defensive act, and British Columbia is a thriving dominion caught between incompatible monetary systems—not a failed state.
- **Why Britain leaves gold (21 September 1931):** Britain's 1925 return to gold rested on San Francisco credit (`L1566`), while Pacifica's falling industrial prices squeezed British exports (`L1562 ("get cheaper year after year")`). When Creditanstalt (May 1931) and Germany's banks (July 1931) fail (`L1687 ("Britain leaves gold in September")`), freezing London's continental bills, the Bank of England's reserves drain. Staying on gold would require unsecured San Francisco credit that hands Thalassia control of the Bank of England. Floating sterling down on 21 September 1931 lets London cut sterling export prices (`L1689 ("sterling-priced aluminium undercuts the Hilo smelter")`: sterling-priced aluminium immediately undercuts Hilo) and assemble a defensive **Sterling Area and Imperial Preference tariff wall** (Abnormal Importations Act Nov 1931; Import Duties Act Feb 1932; Ottawa Conference July 1932).
- **The Monetary Scissors at Victoria:** Sixty years of southern integration leave roughly two-thirds to three-quarters of the dominion's trade (**MODEL**), its Columbia downstream power income (`L1554`), its clearing, and its maturing 1932 mountain-railway and PGE bonds in **Pacifica gold dollars**, while its Imperial exports (canned salmon, lumber, apples to Britain) earn **floating sterling**. When sterling drops in September 1931, Victoria's Imperial revenues fall sharply while its San Francisco debt holds par.
- **London's and Ottawa's rational refusals (British cabinet PROPOSED, grounded in *Hist.* National Government formed 24 August 1931):** Premier **Simon Fraser Tolmie** (born at Victoria, loyalist Islander Conservative, premier since 1928; local dominion career in this timeline, *Hist.* dates) asks London to guarantee a refinancing of Victoria's gold-dollar debt without forcing Victoria behind the new Imperial tariff wall. In Whitehall's National Government (MacDonald, Baldwin, and Chancellor **Neville Chamberlain**, with Bank of England Governor **Montagu Norman**; PROPOSED—canon has not yet named a 1932 British cabinet), the Treasury refuses on sound financial grounds: Britain will not spend its last gold reserves paying San Francisco bondholders for a dominion outside the Sterling Bloc. London offers sterling backing only if Victoria joins the Sterling Area and Imperial Preference, or merges with Canada. Prime Minister **R. B. Bennett** (*Hist.:* PM 1930–35), carrying Prairie relief and unsold wheat ahead of the Ottawa Conference, declines to assume Victoria's gold-dollar railway debt or import an anti-tariff Pacific electorate.
- **Why British Columbia breaks where Canada and New Zealand stay:** New Zealand sells about 80% of its exports to Britain in sterling (*Hist.*), and Australia's primary export trade likewise leans heavily on London; Ontario and Québec want Imperial Preference tariffs. British Columbia has no Ontario—an Imperial tariff wall against Pacifica would ruin its mainland economy and trigger default on its San Francisco bonds. Staying on the Pacifica gold dollar requires a full monetary, power-grid, and defense association incompatible with British Crown sovereignty while Britain builds a rival Sterling Bloc and pursues an atomic weapon (`L1614 ("fully committed from 1928")`).
- **Victoria asks first (late January – February 1932; PROPOSED).** Tolmie resigns in late January 1932 rather than break with the King. Pattullo's mainland coalition opens talks with Thalassia, and its first request, pressed by the Vancouver and interior members, is the largest one: union with the Realm under Julian's crown.
- **Julian asks for leave, and the Senate refuses him a second time (February 1932).** With Victoria's request on the table, Julian asks Pacifica's Senate in closed session for leave to offer a fourth crown, for the provisioner's reason he gave when he asked it to shorten the Gulf Republic's admission count (`L1100`). **The Senate refuses him leave.** Victoria Kamāmalu (opposing from Honolulu, where at ninety-three she has no vote, `L927 ("twelve Polytechnic seats and eight aliʻi seats")`, while gathering allies ahead of the Charter bargain of 1932, `L1738 ("one last bargain")`), the sufficers, the nations' senators and the majority that has just held the line on the Gulf's parity tests will not authorize union with a settler dominion that has anti-Asian laws and unceded Indigenous land. Hawaii's and the Philippines' delegations in Ka Lōkahi signal that they would refuse a fourth settler vote that ended their two-to-one majority over the Realm (`L1234 ("gives the Realm, Hawaii and the Philippines one vote each")`). Julian did not bid before he was asked; he is asked, wants to say yes, and is not allowed to.
- **The fallback: the Gulf Republic path.** Thalassia's answer to Pattullo is therefore the one offer Julian has authority to make, an **Association Compact on the Gulf Republic model (`C-1900-GULF-COMPACT`, `L1091–1093`)**: an independent **Republic of Caledonia** outside the crowns under hard upfront civic and Indigenous conditions and a **25-year outcome-based admission clause** (`A8`).

**A7. Spring 1932: The Referendum, Vancouver Island, and London's Bitter Exit.**
- **The Referendum (April 1932):** Taken across the dominion as a single indivisible electorate **on the dominion's own existing roll under the dominion's own law** (British subjects, including naturalized Pacifican settlers in the interior and Yukon; excluded Asian residents do not vote on the referendum itself and gain full constitutional floors on Proclamation Day, honouring the author's 16:57 decision). The ballot offers: (1) entering the Sterling/Imperial Preference system (or seeking terms from Canada), or (2) establishing the independent **Republic of Caledonia** on the published draft constitution and Association terms.
  - *Vote geography (PROPOSED):* **Vancouver Island votes decisively No**, denouncing the narrow mainland victory as a "foreigners' vote" of naturalized Pacificans. Vancouver and the Fraser canneries split nearly evenly (white labour divided over the 8-hour equal-wage article). The mainland interior (Kootenays, Okanagan, Cariboo, Peace), Prince Rupert, and the Yukon carry the Yes. Exact vote percentages are OPEN.
- **Why a No-voting Vancouver Island stays in:** (1) The referendum runs dominion-wide under one act; (2) London refuses to carry a small, isolated, indefensible island micro-colony (roughly a sixth of the dominion's population, **MODEL**, across a narrow strait from the mainland and dependent on mainland food, power, and trade); (3) Victoria's civil service and Esquimalt's dockyard live off the mainland treasury. In exchange for staying without secession, the Island extracts three entrenched guarantees: **Victoria remains the Republic's capital**, **Island provincial autonomy**, and **protected Crown pensions plus a two-year British nationality opt-in**.
- **London's bitter retrenchment (PROPOSED cabinet & Churchill opposition; one annuity schedule with `CR-084`):** Whitehall does not exit smiling. **Winston Churchill** (from the Conservative backbenches), Beaverbrook, and Empire diehards denounce the National Government's *"Pacific Liquidation"* in Westminster for abandoning loyalist Vancouver Island and Britain's last North American Pacific coast. Baldwin and Chamberlain carry the **Recognition / Severance Act (May–June 1932)** only because the Admiralty admits the coast is indefensible against an atomic Concord and the Exchequer needs **war-debt principal relief** after sterling's collapse. Under `OPEN-019-05` Pacifica cancels a share of Britain's 1923 62-year annuities. The share is part of one tranche of one schedule, cancelled also for the retrenchment and for Britain's signature under Article I; `CR-084` `B1` states the combined total once (15 to 20 of about 53, MODEL) and the split among the three purposes is OPEN for the workbook. There is one debate at Westminster and one vote in each of the Realm's chambers for the whole tranche. The annuities are (`C-1923-UK-FUNDING`, `L1385`—cancelling principal rather than a moratorium-suspended instalment, `L1693`) and guarantees a long-term commercial lease on the **Bamfield Pacific Cable station** for **Imperial and International Communications Ltd.** (*Hist.:* owner from March 1930, linking to British Fanning at `L1139 ("British Fanning as the station")`). The rest of `OPEN-019-05`'s payment in kind is `CR-084`'s.

**A8. The Republic of Caledonia, Its Constitution, and the Admission Clause (May–June 1932).**
Proclaimed in May–June 1932 under President/Premier **Duff Pattullo**, the Republic takes the fur-traders' name (*New Caledonia*, dropping *British* and *New*; Island no-voters keep the old name in speech; PROPOSED). It is sovereign, outside the crowns and Ka Lōkahi, and its people are not counted in the Realm's ~37 million.
- **Upfront constitutional terms (approved):**
  1. **Pacifica-modeled institutions:** Strong executive, **examined merit civil service**, independent **Caledonian Office of Measures**, and balanced-budget limits.
  2. **Immediate repeal of the 1927 land-claims ban & First Nations Title Recognition (A9):** Lease-not-sell title and an entrenched **Fraser River Salmon Clause** (no main-stem dam; reopenable only by the nations of the river).
  3. **Immediate 8-Hour Equal-Wage Labour Article:** Same wage for the same work and the eight-hour day on public and chartered works (`CR-071` pattern).
  4. **Floors for every resident on Proclamation Day (author, 16:57):** Abolishes the 1872, 1895, and 1907 disabilities in voting, licences, and the professions for **every person resident in Caledonia on the day of Proclamation**, including long-resident Chinese, Japanese, and South Asian residents previously barred from naturalization. *(Future entry from outside the Association is Caledonia's sovereign law, naming no race, while Realm citizens and Japanese subjects entitled under 1907 hold reciprocal treaty rights.)*
- **The Gulf-Style Admission Clause (PROPOSED, matching `C-1900-GULF-COMPACT`, `L1091–1093`):**
  - *Why 25 years of measured outcomes after Proclamation Day cures the statutes:* Repealing discriminatory statutes and the 1927 ban on paper does not undo sixty years of dispossession, exclusion, and unceded territory. As with the Gulf Republic (`L1091 ("equal civil rights")`), the Senate refuses to inherit unresolved land conflicts or a de facto underclass on paper promises alone.
  - *Four outcome tests (verified annually by the Caledonian Office of Measures on Pacifica's yardstick for twenty-five consecutive years):*
    1. **First Nations Compact Completion & Parity:** Compacts concluded and honoured across at least **90% of unceded territory** (verified by joint nation–Office audits with zero unredressed compact breaches), and Indigenous infant mortality and Defense-Law-equivalent youth health/literacy rates reaching parity (**within 95% / 1.05×** of the Republic's general rate, mirroring the Gulf's sixth test);
    2. **Resident Civic Parity in Practice:** Proclamation Day's universal floors hold measurably across every municipality—zero statistical disparity in licensing, property title, jury rolls, and examination access for Asian-origin residents;
    3. **General Social & Fiscal Floor:** Infant mortality, literacy, real wages, homicide, and typhoid meet or beat Pacifica's weakest county benchmark (`L1091`), with 90%+ civil/land registers and balanced gold-dollar accounts;
    4. **Standards & Defense:** Gold parity, AC standard, standard-gauge rail, leasehold/Crown-share concession law, and open common-navy dockyard access.
  - *What admission means (PROPOSED):* If Caledonia ever completes the 25-year count and both peoples vote to unite (decades beyond 1932), Caledonia enters **the Realm as counties under the Constitution** (drawn by survey on the 1887 pattern, seated in both Senate and Chamber of Deputies, with the Yukon joining the northern territories)—**never as a fourth crown in Ka Lōkahi**, preserving Hawaii's and the Philippines' historic three-crown council permanently.
- **Territory:** Victoria is capital; Vancouver is the commercial port; the **Yukon** is a self-governing territory with its council at Dawson; government liquor stores continue.

**A9. The First Nations Compacts of 1932 (approved).**
Negotiated and initialled from **January to May 1932** between the **Provisional Commission of the dominion at Victoria** and the signing nations—with **Rev. Peter Kelly** and **Andrew Paull** of the surviving Allied Tribes prominent at the tables—and **ratified and executed by the Republic of Caledonia upon Proclamation (May–June 1932)**:
- **Standstill rule (`CR-070`/`CR-071`):** No land is granted and no dam, mine, or railway is built on a nation's unceded country without its compact. Signing nations lease rather than sell, take annual rents/power royalties, and hold published fishery remedies.
- **First-wave signing places (PROPOSED; geographic checks noted in `## Sources`):**
  - **Spences Bridge:** Nlaka'pamux and southern interior nations (with a petitioner of 1913 signing on-page); Fraser salmon clause entrenched.
  - **Kamloops / North Thompson:** Secwépemc nations (to verify).

**A10. The Pacifica–Caledonia Association Treaty and Bond Refunding (June–July 1932, Thalassia).**
- **Financing obeys `L892` and `L1695` strictly:** Neither the Reserve Bank nor the Treasury prints a dollar or grants a tax subsidy (`L1695`). Collateral is hard, already-flowing revenue: Caledonia's half of Columbia downstream power (`L1554`, converted cleanly into a bilateral Pacifica–Caledonia power compact), Burrard Inlet and Prince Rupert port tolls, new hydroelectricity export contracts, and Yukon/Kootenay royalties. *(Only after the Republic is proclaimed, solvent, and its treaties ratified may the Patrimony invest commercially in private Caledonian power or rail bonds alongside syndicates.)*

**A11. The Prairie Wheat Contract (December 1931) and the Trilateral Treaty with Canada (June–July 1932) (`CR-082` R-1).**
Two strictly separated instruments so the Crown never binds the Patrimony's independent board (`L1695`) and the board never enters a sovereign treaty negotiation (`L892`):
- **Instrument 1 — The Patrimony's Prairie Wheat Contract (December 1931):**
  - Ottawa asks first (`CR-082` rule). By late 1931 the federal government has guaranteed and taken over the prairie pools' **Central Selling Agency** (*Hist.*), and Canada is neither in default nor in treaty negotiation with Pacifica in December 1931 (`L892` respected).
  - The Patrimony's board votes a **ten-harvest forward purchase (1932–41)** inside a price band (band and tonnage OPEN for workbook sizing), paid strictly on commercial terms in **(1) earmarked San Francisco gold-dollar credit** and **(2) carriage in Pacifican ships** out of Burrard Inlet. Giannini and the Industrial Board object in the Senate during the winter foreclosures; **Giannini wins an enlarged home-crop purchase** *before* the board votes the prairie contract (PROPOSED).
- **Instrument 2 — The Trilateral Treaty of Thalassia (June–July 1932):**
  Signed by the Concord Foreign Minister (for Pacifica), Pattullo (for Caledonia), and Bennett (for Canada)—Canada's first standalone treaty with Pacifica (`T6`):
  1. **Entrenched Pacific Tidewater Access:** Perpetual CPR running rights across Caledonia and a **bonded Canadian quay on Burrard Inlet** (plus Prince Rupert transit) entrenched by trilateral treaty so no Victoria government can revoke it.
  2. **Records the Prairie Wheat Contract & Canada's Machinery Tariff Quota:** Links the Patrimony's Dec 1931 contract to the bonded quay, and carries **Ottawa's sovereign tariff concession**—an agreed annual quota of Pacifican agricultural and industrial machines entering Canada outside Bennett's emergency tariff (bringing San Francisco's gold dollars back as orders to half-idled Pacifican plants without involving the Patrimony board in tariff talks; no nitrogen fertilizer, which 1931 prairie farms barely used).
  3. **50-Year Renewal of the Great Bear Lake Uranium Contract (`C-1912-GREATBEAR-OFFTAKE`, `L1265 ("cobalt bloom and copper near Echo Bay")`):** Locks in Pacifica's commercial offtake from Canada's Northwest Territories ahead of Britain's 1933–35 atomic programme (`L1614`).
  4. **Peace–Mackenzie Navigation & Boundary Accord:** Declares the boundary settled; creates a joint navigation commission with **Charles Camsell** (deputy minister of mines, born at Fort Liard, `CT-CHARLES-CAMSELL`) as Canada's commissioner—with Alberta's participation over its post-1930 natural resources—protecting river levels on the Slave and Mackenzie (the Works' Great Bear ore road, `L1265`) and hearing/publishing petitions from basin peoples without infringing Canadian sovereignty.
- **What the two instruments carry in the Final Act (`CR-086` owns the Act and its articles):**
  1. **The prairie wheat contract is scheduled to Article II, the food order.** It is one of the sellers' contracts on which the Concord's pledge rests: the Realm undertakes that grain moves in peace at published prices, and a ten-harvest purchase of the prairie crop, carried in Pacifican ships and resold in Asia, is part of what it moves. The schedule adds nothing to the Patrimony board's contract and takes nothing from it.
  2. **The Great Bear renewal is part of why the others cannot get fuel.** With the contract renewed for fifty years, the Empire's own North American uranium stays under contract to the Works for as long as any signatory could plan. `CR-086` Part A rests on it, and `CR-084`'s British cabinet has it before it when it decides to renounce.
  3. **Canada signs Article I for itself** (author, 23 November 2026, 19:55). It has no programme to give up. Its signature means that London cannot ask Ottawa a second time, as it did in 1927, to divert the ore.
  4. **The Association Treaty and the Trilateral Treaty are annexed to the Final Act** of 1 August 1932, with the other instruments of the conference.
- **Canada's cost and the foreclosure charge (`T8`):** Bennett does not go home triumphant. Weeks before hosting the Ottawa Imperial Economic Conference, he has accepted a sister British dominion's permanent exit from the Empire and Canada's permanent loss of a sovereign Pacific coast; critics in Ottawa, London, and Victoria say **"foreclosure by currency"** aloud—San Francisco held Victoria's gold-dollar railway bonds, the Reserve Bank refused to print while sterling fell, and Pacifica's dollar peeled the coast off the Empire and wrote its constitution.
