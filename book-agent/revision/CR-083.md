## A. Caledonia: British Columbia and the Yukon change crowns (N-1 to N-5, N-8 to N-12)

**A1. 1858.** The miners who go up the Fraser are Pacificans from San Francisco. Britain makes the mainland a colony, as in canon and in history.

**A2. 1869–71, the fork (N-1, N-8).** British Columbia does not enter Confederation. Pacifica does not encourage it to. It offers the bankrupt colony a railway south to Puget Sound, a loan, and free entry for its coal and fish. Britain keeps the colony and Esquimalt. Canada stops at the Rockies. Pacifica asks for nothing more. Elias's governments hold to that until 1887, and the Regent's and Julian's after them.
- **The Yukon is settled here (N-8, approved).** When London transfers the North-Western Territory to Canada in July 1870, it keeps back the country west of the continental divide and north of the sixtieth parallel, which is the basin of the Yukon and the Porcupine, and joins it to the colony. *Hist.:* the Stickeen Territories of 1862 ran to the sixty-second parallel under the colony's governor, and the Act of 28 July 1863 gave the colony only the part south of the sixtieth. London here restores and extends the arrangement of 1862. The instrument and its date are PROPOSED.
- The grant of the Stikine, the Yukon and the Porcupine in the spring of 1871 is then made to Britain for the colony, which holds all three rivers above the Alaskan line.
- **Ottawa's decision.** Macdonald protests the reservation and does not make it a quarrel with London in the year of the Washington treaty (PROPOSED).

**A3. The Canadian Pacific (T5, case B, approved).** Pacifica's quiet money stays in the line, as in canon. The line reaches Burrard Inlet in the 1880s on running rights over the colony's soil.
- **Canada's motive.** London wants an imperial route to Esquimalt and Hong Kong and pays a mail subsidy for it. Pacifica's money rides on that route (PROPOSED). "From 1887" in the storyline is checked on integration and may slip a few years.
- British Columbia outside Confederation puts a customs line at the Rockies. The election of 1911 has to account for it.

**A4. 1896–98.** The strike on Bonanza Creek is on the colony's soil. The colony governs the rush from Victoria, and for a decade the royalty makes it rich. Nothing is taken from Canada in 1898. The boundary, the railway from Skagway and the assay offices stay as in canon. In 1897 Pacifica sells the old telegraph line to British Columbia, not to Canada (T7).

**A5. 1900–27.** The colony signs no treaties with the nations. South of the line their kin lease dam sites and keep their valleys.
- **The petitions of 1909 and 1913** go to London and also to the Pacific crown. Julian's secretary acknowledges each one, answers that it is addressed to another sovereign's ministers, and files it. Nothing is done on them before 1931 (PROPOSED).
- The western waters treaty of about 1906, the Columbia compact of 1922–25 and the Halibut Treaty of 1923 are British Columbia's business (T6). At Pacifica's insistence the Columbia compact is also signed with the nations of the upper river.
- British Columbia becomes a dominion in 1907, with Newfoundland (date PROPOSED).
- **The colony's laws against Asians.** The colony takes the vote from its Chinese residents in the 1870s, from its Japanese residents in 1895 and from its South Asian residents in 1907, and Vancouver riots against them on 7 September 1907. *Hist.:* all four. From 1907 these laws are the dominion's standing quarrel with Thalassia. Julian accedes and marries Chiyo two months after the riot.
- **Who the settlers are by 1931.** Sixty years of the railway south and free entry have made a large part of them Pacifican by birth or descent, Asian Pacificans among them (PROPOSED; to be modelled in Part E).
- **T8.** A member of the legislature at Victoria says in the 1920s that the Realm counts the dominion's acres already. The storyline gives him the sentence (PROPOSED).

**A6. 1927–31: the offer.** Britain's retreat is complete in 1927. The slump of 1929 breaks the dominion's finances.
- **London's decision.** London offers two courses: government by commission, or union with Canada. The legislature at Victoria adds a third and offers the Pacific crown.
- London gives way. The dominion's bonds are payable in gold dollars. Britain leaves gold in September 1931 and cannot carry them (canon: Britain's own debt to Pacifica "keeps its gold-dollar value"). Parliament passes an Act releasing the dominion. The navy's dockyard at Esquimalt and the imperial cable's landing pass to Caledonia under the Act. The Act, the dockyard and the cable are PROPOSED; *Hist.* for the dockyard and the cable is to verify.

**A7. 1931: the terms, the compacts and the vote (N-2, N-10).** The order is terms first, compacts second, vote third, as it was for the Philippine crown in 1907.
- **The terms of union** are signed before the vote:
  1. Caledonia keeps its own rule on entry, as Hawaii does.
  2. The Realm's floors hold for every person resident on the day of union. The dominion's disabilities of race in voting, licences and the professions end on that day (the scope is PROPOSED).
  3. The legislature stays at Victoria.
  4. The Fraser clause (A9).
- **The compacts** are signed next, nation by nation (A8).
- **The settlers' vote (N-10, approved).** It is taken on the dominion's own roll, under the dominion's own law. Nobody can say afterwards that the union was carried by people the settlers had kept from voting. The settlers vote for the union narrowly.
  - *Their reasons are their own interest:* the dominion is bankrupt, the other courses are rule by commission or rule from Ottawa, sixty years of rail and trade run south, and the entry rule stays in their hands.
  - *Vancouver Island votes no.* It has the capital and the naval town. The mainland and the interior carry the vote.
  - The figures are not set (OPEN).
- **Each nation votes for itself**, after its compact is signed.

**A8. The compacts (N-3; T3, case A, approved).** They follow the mainland rule of CR-071: a nation leases and does not sell. The Lands and Waters Charter of 1932 is their island counterpart a year later, and Julian accepts a limit in each.
- **Places.** The southern nations sign inland, at Spences Bridge. The nations of the Peace and the Liard, who are the lessors of the northern water, sign in their own country (PROPOSED: at Fort St John for the Dane-zaa and the Sekani and at Lower Post for the Kaska; names and places to verify).
- **A nation that votes no** keeps its compact, which binds the Crown whichever way it votes. Its land is not Crown land of Caledonia, and no work is built on it (PROPOSED).
- The dominion has its own law of 1927 against raising money for land claims. The terms of union repeal it. *Hist.:* the law of 1927 was Canada's.

**A9. The Fraser clause (N-4).** It is the one entrenched term of the union. No dam is built on the main stem, and no water leaves the basin. Only the nations of the river can reopen it.

**A10. Who accepts the crown (N-9, approved).** The legislature at Victoria offers it and the vote confirms it. Three bodies accept it.
- **The Senate** passes an Act of Union. The vote is narrow, and it is the sufficers' last stand: they argue that the crown is taken for the water. The count is PROPOSED and not set.
- **Ka Lōkahi** holds an extraordinary sitting between its reviews of 1927 and 1937 and admits a fourth delegation. PROPOSED: Hawaii and the Philippines both vote to admit; with four votes, the succession and a dispute between crowns need three, and a tie leaves things as they are.
- **Japan** consents at the Hayama Council of 1931, for a price (Part H).

**A11. The Crown of Caledonia (N-3, N-12).** Julian wears it with the crowns of Pacifica, Hawaii and the Philippines. His four crowns are one party in the Concord.
- **Status (N-12, approved).** Caledonia follows the Hawaiian model, not the Philippine one. Its people are citizens of the Realm and are counted in the Realm's population from 1931.
- **The name.** The legislature takes the fur traders' name for the country, New Caledonia, and drops "British" and "New" (PROPOSED). *Hist.:* New Caledonia was the North West Company's district west of the Rockies.
- The Yukon is a territory of the crown, with its council at Dawson. Alaska stays under the Northern Commission.
- The drink law Caledonia keeps after 1931 is OPEN (canon: British Columbia goes dry in 1917).

**A12. What Canada is given** (a treaty of 1931, and no share of the Columbia):
1. Its railway's running rights to tidewater, and a bonded Canadian quay on Burrard Inlet. This confirms what exists.
2. The wheat contract (A13).
3. A Mackenzie waters commission. The Peace and the Liard run east into Canada, so the northern water makes the Realm the upstream party. Canada is paid for the water it loses and sits on the commission. The peoples of the lower Peace, the Slave and the Mackenzie have seats of their own on it (PROPOSED). Canon's practice at Great Bear was a treaty with the Sahtú Dene.

The boundary is declared settled. The Realm repeats that it takes no people who have not voted.
- **Ottawa's decision.** Ottawa signs the treaty and does not contest the vote at Westminster (PROPOSED). It is the first treaty Canada signs with Pacifica on its own (T6).
- **T8, case A, approved.** "The giant its neighbours need not fear" stands for the years to 1931. After 1931 Ottawa has reason to count the Realm's acres, and the storyline says so.

**A13. The wheat contract (N-11, approved; CR-082 R-1).** It is a forward purchase by the Patrimony's board, not Treasury spending and not printed money.
- **Form.** The board buys prairie wheat forward for ten harvests from 1932, at a floor set near the slump's price. The price, the tonnage and the credit are sized in the workbook and are OPEN until then.
- **What becomes of the wheat.** The Realm grows more than it eats until about 1941–43 (CR-082, MODEL). Until then the wheat is stored or resold to Japan and China, beside the Crown's granary and the wheat credit of 1931 (CR-081 G-14).
- **The argument in the Senate.** Giannini and the Industrial Board object that the Realm's own foreclosed farmers get no floor in the winter the Bank will not print. The answer has three parts:
  1. The board's money is rent, not tax, and the Patrimony has invested abroad since 1919.
  2. The home crop is bought first, under CR-081 G-14's rule.
  3. Wheat bought at the bottom of the slump is the cheapest the Realm will ever buy.
- The cabinet's further reason is in its minute and in no speech: the contract makes the Realm the prairie's buyer and carrier, a step toward the aim of CR-082.
- **The dominion's debt.** It stays Caledonia's. The board refunds it in gold dollars against the rents of Caledonia's Crown lands (PROPOSED; to be sized).

*Hist.:* about 30,000 miners on the Fraser in 1858; the annexation petition of 1869; entry into Confederation on 20 July 1871 and the threats to leave in 1874 and 1878; the transfer of the North-Western Territory on 15 July 1870; almost no treaties in the province, the Nisga'a petition of 1913 unheard, and land-claim funds made an offence in 1927; the Yukon's 27,219 people in 1901 and 4,157 in 1921; Western Australia's vote of 1933 refused at Westminster; Newfoundland's bankruptcy of 1932.

## B. The northern water (P-2, N-6, N-7)

- **Approved (author, 19:29):** Julian's engineers propose it and prevail on it, because it is critical to national survival.
- **Dates.** Surveyed from the 1910s. Proposed with the plebiscite of 1931. Leased from the nations of each river under the compacts of 1931 (A8; T3). Built in stages after 1932.
- **Who knows, and when (PROPOSED).**
  - The surveys of the 1910s are made by the joint commission of the western waters treaty, which canon gives "powers to regulate as well as to investigate". London and Ottawa read them as surveys for the dominion's own power.
  - In 1931 the water is proposed as Caledonia's own power and irrigation. The canal south is in the compacts' text and in nobody's speech.
  - The canal crosses the upper Fraser's basin and takes nothing from it (A9). The water enters the Columbia above every compact reach of CR-055.
  - The fields the water serves are not yet named (OPEN; Part E).
- **Size (author, 20:32).** The first stage is about 20 km³ a year, which keeps each river's loss near a fifth. The ceiling in law is 45 km³.
- **Source.** The Peace and the Liard, which carry no salmon. The Fraser is closed (A8). The Yukon, the Stikine and the Skeena are salmon rivers and fall under canon's rule.
- **What 45 km³ waters.** 36.5 million acre-feet: 10–12 million acres at 3.0–3.5 acre-feet an acre. That feeds 18–22 million people at the yields of 1930 and 30–35 million at the century's, about 26 million mouths at the century's yields in CR-082's century module (`population/outputs/provisioning.md` §8, MODEL), 5% of the need.
- **Energy.** Lifting 45 km³ by 300–450 m takes 4.8–7.2 GW as a year-round average, and 11–17 GW of pumps if the lifting is done in five high-water months. The water gives back 4.3–6.1 GW falling 350–500 m to the fields. The drops are built on the canal, off the salmon rivers, and the pumps are fed from a dam in the Peace's canyon.
- **Limits.** The reservoirs stop where the lessor nations set the waterline. Every irrigated acre has a drain.
- **The larger plan.** The engineers draw the plan at about 135 km³, with four more rivers. The compacts refuse it. *Hist.:* the Parsons plan of 1964.
- The river flows, lifts and elevations are from general knowledge and are verified before integration.

## H. The Hayama bargain of 1931 (J-1 to J-6)

This part follows B. Its letter is H because Parts C to G keep the letters other files cite.

- **J-1 (author, 15:43).** Japan is not merely told of the fourth crown. The crown adds to the weight of Julian's party in the Concord, so it is discussed with Japan, and the bargain is symmetrical: the Realm goes north, Japan goes south.
- **J-2 (author, 16:40).** At the Hayama Council of 1931 Japan consents to the Crown of Caledonia. In return it receives:
  1. a leased naval station on the Davao Gulf;
  2. the Realm's undertaking not to oppose Japan in the Netherlands Indies.
- **J-3. The lease.** The Philippine crown is the lessor, on the rule that land is leased and never sold. The rent goes to Manila, and Philippine law runs outside the station's fence.
  - PROPOSED: the Philippine delegation's price in Ka Lōkahi is that no Japanese land title in Davao is added to those registered on the day of the lease.
  - The consent of the Davao Gulf's own peoples is OPEN, for the partners' CR. *Hist.:* at least a hundred Japanese were killed in land conflicts there in 1918–21.
- **J-4. Common control.** Canon's renewal of 1932 puts Japan's overseas commands under common control. The station at Davao is one of them from the day the renewal takes effect.
- **J-5. The licence in the Indies.** It is bound to treaty and concession. Japan may press the Dutch with its fleet in sight; what it takes, it takes by a treaty the Dutch sign. Whatever oil it wins is sold through a Concord oil pool, so Japan's dependence on Pacifican oil products (CR-054) becomes a dependence that runs both ways. The pool is new and PROPOSED, and it is to be checked against CR-053 and CR-054 on integration.
- **J-6. Zamboanga (author, 16:40; footing PROPOSED).** Zamboanga is the second anchorage. It is not leased. It is the Philippine crown's own station on the Basilan Strait, and Japan's ships receive the right to berth, coal and repair there. No Japanese garrison stands at Zamboanga. *Hist.:* Spain's fort there dates from 1635 and the town was the seat of government for Mindanao and Sulu under the Americans. Canon has Zamboanga in the cholera of 1882 and nowhere else; whether the Realm kept a naval station there after 1878 is to be settled on integration.

**Why Davao.** *Hist.:* Japanese settlers came to Davao from 1903 to grow abaca. They were more than ten thousand by about 1930 and 17,888 in 1939, the largest Japanese community in Southeast Asia. Canon's Philippines have been Pacifican since 1876–78, so the colony's history under the dependency and the crown is OPEN for the partners' CR; the bargain assumes it exists.

**What the bargain replaces.** CR-082 carried a PROPOSED line for the partners' CR: Japan renounces any southern advance below the Philippine crown. It is withdrawn. The storyline never stated it.

**The dominions (author, 16:40).** Japan in this history is strong and successful, where history's Japan was brittle and desperate. A Japanese or Concord station nearby does not carry the dread it carried in history. Canon agrees: Australia and New Zealand sign a defence treaty with Thalassia in 1932, and its guarantee is that no hostile base stands on the dominions' own soil. Davao does not touch it. Australia's crop guarantee waits on the Ottawa preference, as in CR-082, and not on fear of Japan.

**What stays out of this CR.** What Japan does in the Indies falls after 1932 and belongs to the partners' CR, with the Dutch reply, Britain's view from Singapore and the Borneo company's neighbours.
- PROPOSED reading for that CR: the bargain favours the navy, whose minister sits in the common cabinet, over the army, which was denied Manchuria's land in the same year. Whether the bargain comes before or after the plot of 1931 is stopped is OPEN.

## Canon tensions

Numbered as in CR-082.

- **T3. The Charter of 1932 is an islands charter.** Canon: the Lands and Waters Charter is brokered between Keawe and Julian in Hawaii and "makes the islands programme law" (CR-020). The outline and the discussion of 19:29–20:32 spoke of the northern water as leased "under the Charter". That was an error of the drafter's.
  - *Case A, canon kept:* the northern water is leased under the compacts of Spences Bridge (1931), which follow the mainland rule that nations lease and do not sell (CR-071). The Charter of 1932 is their island counterpart, a year later, and Julian accepts a limit in each.
  - *Case B, canon amended:* the Charter of 1932 is widened to all the crowns, and the northern leases stand under it. This changes CR-020's scope. No tracked fact changes.
  - **Decided A** (author, 22 November 2026, 15:43).
- **T4. British Columbia enters Confederation.** Canon (storyline §13, CR-058): "British Columbia enters Confederation on 20 July. Pacifica has encouraged it, because a strong Canadian buffer is worth more to it than a province that would provoke Britain".
