## Verdict

One more revision. The design holds and agrees with the sibling drafts on the large points: the order of events from April to 1 August, Tizard's ore, the three tranches, the Senate's two-thirds, the four straits. It is not ready because the decision record contradicts itself on what is approved, the 10° S protocol is drawn on geography that does not hold against a map or against L1381, and a layer of older text still tells an earlier version in a dozen places. None of this needs new invention; it needs one careful pass and two short questions to the author.

## Blockers

**B1. The decision record says both APPROVED and PROPOSED for the same things.**
*Passage:* lines 5, 93, 100, 496–511.
*Problem:* the table has M-0 "APPROVED" (496), and line 503 lists under "PROPOSED by the drafter (for author sign-off)": "Approve `M-0`"; line 93 still reads "Premise (`M-0` / `T-F1`, PROPOSED)". Line 100 has "One schedule, three dated tranches (approved; … item 84-8", and line 511 lists the same schedule as PROPOSED item 8. M-1 is "APPROVED, with … the Labuan roll and the Fanning buyout" (497), and line 504 lists the roll and the buyout as PROPOSED. M-4 approves "the bar on fortifying the piers" (500), and line 507 lists "Canberra winning non-fortification" as PROPOSED. Line 5 says labels "are downgraded to PROPOSED until he confirms them", which the 23:14 approval has overtaken. M-1 approves "the cap east of Ceylon", an amendment of `C-1919-NAVAL-LIMITS`, while the M-0 row says that commitment "is not covered and stays PROPOSED". An integrator cannot tell which label to carry into the storyline.
*Fix:* rewrite the PROPOSED list so that it holds only what no approval covers (I count: `A2a`, `B5`, `A3`'s cast, Brooke's refusal, the Gulf undertaking, the British cabinet list, the moratorium's dates, the instalment dates, the New Zealand rebate, Tonga's details, NKPM, the straits' scheduling); correct lines 5 and 93; decide whether the cap east of Ceylon can be APPROVED while the limits it amends are PROPOSED.

**B2. The 10° S protocol does not fit the map or L1381.**
*Passage:* `C3`, line 188; impact note, line 221; M-4, line 500.
*Problem:* "south of 10° S between Timor and 160° W, which takes in Fiji, Tonga and the Cook Islands". Rarotonga lies at about 21° 14′ S, 159° 46′ W, and Aitutaki, Atiu and Mangaia lie east of it; the main Cook Islands are outside a zone that ends at 160° W. Second, L1381 says "Pacifica and Japan retain Germany's Pacific islands" and L1316 that they "take Germany's Pacific islands". On those words the Concord holds German Samoa (Upolu and Savaiʻi, about 13° S, 172° W), which lies inside the zone and is not among the exceptions ("Tutuila's existing wireless and coaling station and Swains"). The draft raises the question only for Rabaul. Third, line 221 says "Former German Pacific territories unaffected by the 10° S line", while line 188 says that if the Concord holds Rabaul "the line is worth less to Canberra, and this is left **OPEN**". The two cannot both go into the storyline. M-4 is labelled APPROVED with this geography.
*Fix:* move the eastern limit (150° W takes in all the Cooks and leaves Tahiti out); ask the author who holds German Samoa, New Guinea and Nauru after 1919, or state the protocol so that it works either way (an exception for "stations existing on 1 January 1932", listed in an annex); rewrite line 221 to match.

## Should fix

**S1. Who consents to the guarantees is told two ways.** `T-S1` (decided, line 310), `A1` (59) and line 88 give the guarantees to the Senate at two-thirds. Line 85 still sends all three items "before **Pacifica's Senate and Chamber of Deputies** for ratification"; line 126 has the Gulf undertaking "countersigned by the Realm (voted by both Realm chambers)"; line 215 says "both Senate and Chamber vote the Realm money/guarantee acts"; set piece 1 is "The Senate & Chamber". `A1` also puts `C-1932-ANZ-DEFENSE` in the first row, and `A3` and the calendar never vote it. `A1` names a "Realm … Foreign Minister" as maker, but L1597 gives Foreign Affairs to the Concord; say who signs for the Realm alone.

**S2. The suspended instalment and the "end of the schedule".** Line 94 and the ledger line (270) say the suspended instalment is "folded into the cancelled tranche". Lines 103, 149, 157 and 203 say it is "written off on signature, on 1 August", apart from the tranche, and line 104 says the cancelled annuities "are taken from the end of the schedule". An instalment of 1931–32 is at the front. The moratorium year holds two instalments (15 December 1931 and 15 June 1932; line 106 gives both dates), and the draft says "instalment" in the singular throughout. Line 106 has them fall due on 30 June "unless the conference disposes of them", and the conference disposes of them on 1 August; say what Britain owes in July.

**S3. Britain's motive and Britain's relief do not meet.** Line 96: Britain deals because "the gold-dollar annuities against floating sterling crush the Exchequer". Line 104: the cancelled annuities come from the end of a schedule running to 1985, and "Britain goes on paying each year". On those terms Britain's yearly burden is unchanged until about 1966–70, and its whole relief in 1932 is one year's payment. That is a thin price for Vancouver Island, Sembawang, Fanning, Labuan and the weapon, and Eyres-Monsell's proposal to default (148) becomes the stronger argument. Either take part of the band from the front, or let Chamberlain say why a smaller book is worth it to him (the draft's "against which sterling can be steadied" is one clause and needs to be an argument). The same choice makes "~28–38% of remaining principal" (99) and "loses a third of the book" (149) wrong as figures: the last 15–20 of 53 annuities are a much smaller share of principal than of the count. In line 149 Chamberlain also gives "a third of the book" as the price of the signature alone, which is the combined total for three tranches.

**S4. The cabinet's minute is dated before what it discusses.** Line 76: the minute is written "Before the Foreign Minister opens the talks". Lines 95 and 197 open the talks in "late November 1931"; lines 75 and 196 date the minute "winter 1931–32". At that sitting the oil trust already objects to the Batavia Compact's terms and to the Gulf undertaking (82), which the Gulf demands only on "Learning of Seria and Bintan" (126; calendar January–April), and the aluminium president speaks of "the first review of 1937 (`CR-085` `B2`)", a term that exists only after Japan's proposal of late May. Date the minute to the spring, before the preliminary accord, or cut the hearing down to what could be known.

**S5. The straits.** (a) Malacca's western shore is Dutch Sumatra. Line 113 binds "Britain and the Concord" on Malacca, and the Compact (173) covers "Sunda, Lombok, and Makassar" only, so the Netherlands is bound on three straits and not on the fourth, though line 77 speaks of "the two powers on their shores". (b) Line 77: the Realm's "oil from the concessions of the 1920s … already come[s] through four straits". L1620's concessions are Maracaibo, Colombia, Peru, Bahrain, Kuwait and Mesopotamia, and L1693 puts "Bahrain's first oil" in June 1932. No oil of the Realm passes these straits in the winter of 1931–32. Line 67 repeats the claim. The draft admits it has no routing table; until it has one, the minute should argue from the future (Bahrain, India's and Burma's grain, which canon does not yet name as sellers) and from rubber and tin.

**S6. `B5` departs from canon in three places and lists none under `## Canon tensions`.** The reckoning of "about 1933–1935" (L1614) is overtaken; the draft points to `CR-086`'s first tension, but `CR-084` merges two steps earlier and the storyline would carry Britain's decision while L1614 stands. The character bible says of Hoare "Character use: Off the page"; line 151 brings him on and says so, but there is no tension and line 239 omits him from the character-bible list. Line 69 raises `CR-077` D-6 (a partner is never read) against the dominions' cable and leaves it OPEN without a tension. Add three short entries, the first as a cross-reference.

**S7. `B5`: arithmetic and knowledge.** Line 144 has Britain begin in 1928; line 152 says the refusal of 1926 "cost Britain the years it now lacks" and set piece 6 says "six years" (248). On the draft's own dates it cost two. Churchill was Chancellor until June 1929, so the programme of 1928 was funded by his Treasury; the scene should know that he knows the plant. "On the money voted since 1931" (146) is unexplained. "since 1927 London assumes that its Pacific cables are read" (153) has no canon behind it (L1534 dates the reading from 1925 and does not say London knows); mark it PROPOSED. Line 147 cites L1572 for "called the separation factory impossible"; that is L1575. Chamberlain's answer to default (148) leans on the gold credit of L1566, which backed a convertible pound; say what it still does after September 1931. Line 153 does not say who at Thalassia answers the three requests, and Julian is at Hayama on 5–8 June (`CR-085` `A4`).

**S8. The calendar's cast and the Fanning leftover.** Line 197 opens the talks with "Simon, Chamberlain, Cunliffe-Lister, Eyres-Monsell, Hoare"; line 95 with "Simon, Chamberlain, and Cunliffe-Lister". Line 198 still has Ka Lōkahi negotiating "with Rougier's syndicate and Burns Philp", against line 133 and against the author's instruction quoted at line 44.

**S9. Ledger identifiers.** `CR-083` defines `IIC` and says `CR-084` "should use this ID"; the draft does not list or use it. Concord treaties are `PAC`, `JP` in lines 286–290 and "`CROWN` and `JP`" in line 188; canon uses `CROWN`, `JP` for the Concord's treaties (`C-1928-NANJING`, `C-1932-CONCORD-RENEWAL`), and `CR-086`'s Final Act does too. Tonga's treaty names Julian "as Mōʻī of Hawaii" and has no `HI`. `SHELL` is a party to the Batavia Compact and to the Labuan lease without a clause that makes it one. `C-1932-MARIVELES-DOCK` lists `PAC` and `JP` as parties to a Cortes appropriation.

**S10. The reverter after `CR-085`.** Lines 97 and 271 trigger the reverter "if the Concord lapses" and cite "Japan may refuse in 1937". `CR-085` strikes the term and forbids that phrase. The header (8) admits this. The draft should give the wording that survives (dissolution by agreement of the crowns) and should show London's reaction: Britain agreed in April to a halt with an exit in 1937 and learns in June that the exit is gone. Line 189 gives Canberra that grievance and gives London none.

**S11. Release before ratification.** Tonga's treaty and the Labuan lease are "executed *after* Britain's release takes effect" in July (201), but Westminster ratifies the treaty that contains the release in the autumn (204). Say that the release is by prerogative and provisional, or move execution to the autumn. Retrocession of colonial territory probably needs an Act; I am not certain of the law of 1932.

**S12. Bintan and London.** A Japanese port for fifty years on Bintan stands across the Singapore Strait from the base Britain has just halted. Eyres-Monsell fights "Sembawang and … Fanning" (97) and nobody in London speaks about Bintan. Line 188 answers Canberra only.

**S13. `## Sources` overstates.** Line 530: "All web pages verified on 23 November 2026". Line 524: "History: checked on the web only in part". Keep the second.

## Literary effect

`B5` is the best part of the draft and is close to a scene: Tizard's "figure that matters", Lindemann right at last and losing, a default proposed and answered, three requests with three different outcomes. What it lacks: Baldwin, who was Prime Minister when the programme began, says nothing; MacDonald chairs and wants nothing; Churchill "raises no revolt" without a reason, in a season when he is in revolt over India; and Lindemann carries a cabinet secret to a backbencher the same evening with no cost to anyone. `B3.3` (Brooke's refusal, Brunei left cut in two, the pipeline through Lutong) has real cost. `A2a` gives the oil trust a case and two demands, which is right.

Elsewhere the draft is still a term sheet. Labuan's vote changes little: on a No the Concord gets "a direct commercial harbour lease" anyway (122), so the voters decide their flag and not the outcome. De Jonge "fights the Compact hard" (165), but of his four lines only KPM is shown being demanded by anyone; The Hague, which signs, has no named minister. Canberra's "win" is thin on inspection: the line bars the Concord from places it does not hold, excepts the two it does, and leaves Rabaul, Timor and the leased piers north of it. That may be the point, and if so Canberra should be shown knowing it. Tonga's treaty of "Maritime Security" is made with a Concord that pledges no warship in Tongan waters. Sālote wants something and gets it; the chiefs who lose are one clause.
