Fresh-context review of CR-084, revision 2, 23 November 2026. The reviewer read the draft, AGENTS.md and current canon, and not the writer's notes or earlier reviews.

Line numbers are raw lines of `book-agent/revision/master_storyline.md` as it stands in the working tree. The CR-083 draft (revision 7) was read only to check what CR-084 says about it. History was checked on the web where a URL is given; anything marked "from memory" is not verified.

## Verdict

Not ready to integrate; ready after a substantial revision. The approved shape (M-1 to M-4) can stand, but the draft gives powers to Ka Lōkahi that canon gives to the Concord, moves the peoples of Fiji, the Solomons, the Gilbert and Ellice Islands and Labuan between sovereigns without the compact or vote canon requires, pledges the Concord to guarantee British Hong Kong and Dutch colonial rule against canon's China alliance, and has no vote in any Pacifican, Philippine, Hawaiian or Japanese body. Pacifica's own gain is not stated, Japan's is deferred to a later CR, and nobody refuses anything that matters. The claim that it merges independently of CR-083 is not true of the text as written.

## Blockers

**B1. Peoples and land change hands with no compact and no vote.**
*Passage:* A3.1 (Labuan), A4.1 (Fiji, Gilbert and Ellice, Solomons, Line and Phoenix), new party `WPAC`, `C-1932-UK-RETRENCHMENT`.
*Problem:* Britain "cedes sovereign administration" of Labuan and "transfers administration" of Fiji and the island groups to Ka Lōkahi as the price of a debt. No islander, chief, council or electorate is asked. The draft sees the rule for rulers ("Britain cannot 'sell' protectorates over their rulers' heads", A3.2; Tonga is "not transfer[red] as property", A4.2) and then sells the colonies. Canon's rule is general. The war objects of 1861: "no people taken by decree" (L445). The Philippine crown is "offered by the Cortes and confirmed by vote, equal with the Hawaiian crown whose people also vote" (L35). In Borneo itself: "The company buys that land by compact from the people of the bay before it grants it, as it must for any land beyond its stations" (L1133). CR-074 M1 states the fault the draft repeats: "a Pacifican chartered company ruling peoples who signed nothing." The CR-083 draft, for the same season, gives British Columbia a referendum and executed First Nations compacts; CR-084 gives Fiji nothing. `WPAC` is listed as a party to the treaty that disposes of it, which shows the gap rather than closing it.
Fiji is also not what the draft says it is. It was a Crown colony in its own right from 1874, with a Great Council of Chiefs (1876), not a High Commission territory like the atolls (see History). Whether the author's approval of "WPHC islands" (M-1) meant a colony of that size is a question for the author, not something to assume.
*Fix:* Keep M-1 but make Britain cede only its own rights, each transfer taking effect on that people's act: for Labuan a vote of its residents (about 7,000 to 9,000) or a compact with its headmen; for Fiji a compact with the Great Council of Chiefs plus a vote, with the Indian community's standing settled before, not after; for the Gilberts, Ellice and Solomons compacts island by island on the Micronesian pattern, with 1932 as the start and not the end. Let at least one say no (see Literary effect). Ask the author directly whether Fiji is in M-1. Replace `WPAC` by parties that can consent (for example `FIJI`, `GEIC`, `BSIP`, `LABUAN`).

**B2. Ka Lōkahi is given powers that canon gives to the Concord, and the instruments' parties do not match the actors.**
*Passage:* A3.1, A4, C, calendar, new commitments.
*Problem:* Canon: "Ka Lōkahi, the council of the crowns, gives the Realm, Hawaii and the Philippines one vote each over the succession, the agreed matters renewed every ten years and disputes between them" (L1234), with reviews in 1917 and 1927. "Foreign Affairs, the common Navy and their Finance belong to the Concord" (L1597). The draft has Ka Lōkahi receive a sovereign cession, sign a "Treaty of Friendship and Defense" with Tonga (`TO`, `LOKAHI`, `HI`, no Concord), run a colony with a legislature (Fiji) and host "the Concord's common Navy" at Labuan. Japan has no vote in Ka Lōkahi, so the common fleet's southern anchorage would stand on ground in which one of the two coequal parties has no title or voice; the draft does not notice. Julian treats with Sālote "as Mōʻī of Hawaii", but a defence treaty is a Concord matter under L1597, and the Hawaiian crown has no foreign office in canon. The debt is the Realm's (`C-1923-UK-FUNDING`, parties `UK`, `PAC`), yet the retrenchment treaty is negotiated by "Thalassia's common Foreign Minister" and its parties are `UK`, `PAC`, `JP`, `LOKAHI`, `WPAC`. The ANZ treaty is `ANZ`–`PAC` in the ledger and CR-082 says "The guarantee is Pacifica's" (CR-082.md, R-4 row); the draft has "the Concord covenant". The existing trust's terms are unknown ("Terms of the trust not stated", ledger, `C-1927-MICRONESIA-TRUST`), so "the same trust footing" defines nothing.
*Fix:* Add a short part stating, for each instrument, who signs and under which head: the Realm's Treasury and Senate for the debt; the common Foreign minister for guarantees, with Japan's countersignature; an extraordinary session of Ka Lōkahi's three delegations to accept each trust (the next ordinary review is 1937), with the Hawaiian and Philippine votes shown; a Concord lease from Ka Lōkahi for the Labuan anchorage, on the model of "the navy keeps Guam's harbour on lease" (L1593). Make the Tonga treaty a Concord treaty of friendship that Julian signs in person, or a Hawaiian act of friendship without the defence article. Define the trust's terms here, since this CR multiplies its population many times over.

**B3. Nobody in Pacifica, the Philippines, Hawaii or Japan votes, and Pacifica's own gain is not stated.**
*Passage:* whole draft; A1, A2.3, B2.1, F.
*Problem:* The Realm gives up principal on its largest foreign claim and takes on guarantees of Malaya, Singapore's shipping, Hong Kong, "the sea approaches to India", Dutch sovereignty over the whole Indies, Brunei, Sarawak and Tonga. Canon's whole constitutional crisis of 1927–28 is about exactly this: "Julian claims provisional application under the treaty prerogative pending consent, though the Senate has already refused. This is illegal in substance" (L1593); "In 1928 she wins two-thirds Senate consent and an Act of Indemnity" (L1604). The draft has no Senate, no Cortes, no Hawaiian legislature, no Hayama Council, no Diet. The CR-083 draft has the sufficers object in the Senate to cancelling British principal for Caledonia; CR-084 cancels more and they are silent.
CR-082's rule is not applied: "every treaty, loan, lease and holding abroad goes to the cabinet with two figures beside its price: the mouths it feeds or the people it brings, and by what year" (L1020). Neither figure appears, and the sufficers (Victoria, the Hawaiian court, Keawe) and provisioners (the Navy, Julian) never divide over islands that bring mouths and no acres.
What Pacifica gets for its money is a small island held by a council, atolls that cost money, a "higher royalty" for Brunei and concessions for "Concord/Japanese consortia". The trust that buys "with money, partners and published terms, and no flag" (L1620) gets an offtake of crude the draft then promises not to sell where the Gulf sells. The reader cannot say what Julian wanted.
*Fix:* State Pacifica's interest in its own terms and put it to a vote. Candidates that fit canon: the end of the only capital-ship base west of Hawaii not under the Concord; the Malacca and Sunda passages for the trade the provisioners' plan depends on ("holds only while the sellers sell and the sea lanes are open", L1672); the debt, which sterling's fall has made hard to collect, turned into assets; the "slow fuse of the Borneo question" (CR-074 M1) put out. Give the two figures. Stage the Senate debate with the sufficers against, and say by what margin it passes and what they extract. Give the Cortes its vote in Ka Lōkahi and its price.

**B4. The Concord's guarantees collide with the China alliance and with what the guarantees could mean.**
*Passage:* A2.3, B1, B2.1.
*Problem:* The Concord would guarantee Hong Kong "against third-party aggression". China is a treaty ally whose frontiers the Concord guaranteed at Nanjing (ledger `C-1928-NANJING`: "the Concord guarantees China's frontiers, Manchuria included"); canon has just had the Canton–Hong Kong strike "aimed at Britain" (L1534), the end of the Concord's own consular courts, and "Britain holds no Weihaiwei" (L1706). The only state with a claim on Hong Kong is the ally. The draft does not list L1534, L1706 or the Nanjing treaty, and Nanjing is not consulted, though "Japan and China attend from the standing alliance" (L1748).
For the Indies the draft says "no European navy can defend the Indies" and the Concord guarantees them "against any third-party seizure". With Japan inside the Concord there is no third party in sight; the guarantee is either empty or, read as the Dutch would wish, a guarantee of colonial order against the Indies' own people. That sits badly with a state whose Companies Act forbids service "in a European colonial army" (AGENTS.md, CR-074) and whose first object was "no people taken by decree". "The sea approaches to India" has the same problem.
*Fix:* Define each guarantee narrowly: against attack from the sea by a state, never internal order, never a question between Britain and China, which goes to the arbitration court. Drop Hong Kong from the guarantee or show Nanjing's protest and the answer given. For the Indies, make the guarantee a non-aggression and non-acquisition pledge by both Concord crowns (which is what The Hague actually fears) and say so plainly; that is also where Japan gives something up.

**B5. "Merges independently of CR-083" is not true of this text, and core terms are deferred.**
*Passage:* header (Standalone merge), Depends on, calendar, D (L1385, L1861), Fact changes, C.
*Problem:* The calendar has the retrenchment "pass alongside Caledonia's Severance Act (`CR-083`)". The L1861 note marks `OPEN-019-05` "resolved by CR-083 (Caledonia tranche) and CR-084". The scope says it "Replaces CR-083's retired Davao/Indies swap": Davao has no hit in the storyline, so the forbidden pattern and M-2 retire a thing that was never canon and exists only in earlier CR-083 drafts. A1's cabinet and Churchill's opposition are the same PROPOSED item as in CR-083 A6–A7. C cites `CR-083-review-2.md` S8, a review file, inside a decision record. D says the line numbers were "verified against `drafts/cr083-caledonia`", not `main`. The two tranches come out of one schedule (about 53 of the 62 annuities remain after 1932, by count from 1923) and neither CR says how they divide. Beyond CR-083: Japan's side is sent to "a separate later CR" (header), although CR-082 reserved "any southern renunciation (the partners' CR)" (L1885) and this draft disposes of it; and the price itself is OPEN ("exact number of Britain's 1923 annuities cancelled").
*Fix:* Either declare the order (after CR-083) and keep the cross-references, or write the L1385, L1861 and calendar text so that it is true without CR-083 and add the Caledonia clause when CR-083 merges. Remove the review-file citation. Reword M-2 and the Davao pattern as a guard, not a retirement. State how the annuities are shared between the two CRs. Bring Japan's terms in the south into this CR (S6); they are half of the bargain, not a restructuring detail.

## Should fix

**S1. Hoover's moratorium is Pacifica's own, and its terms are open.** *Passage:* A1, D (L1693). *Problem:* Hoover is a Pacifican in canon; the ledger has `C-1931-HOOVER-MORATORIUM` as owed by `PAC` to the Entente, "Duration, debtors covered and restructured terms not stated", and L1693 says "restrains forced selling, and debt is restructured". The draft imports history's one-year term as fact and ignores "debt is restructured". *Fix:* mark the one-year term PROPOSED, say what was restructured in 1931, and add the commitment to the amended list.

**S2. Britain's reasons do not hold as given.** *Passage:* A1. *Problem:* (a) The 1933–35 date is "[MODEL — CR-051] The Works reckons" (L1614), Pacifica's estimate; the draft has "the Admiralty knows" it. A Britain two years from its own device would lease or suspend, not cede for ever. (b) "Germany's recovery" as a naval reason in autumn 1931 contradicts L1693 ("As Germany and Latin America stop paying their bondholders") and is hindsight (R4). (c) Chamberlain is not Chancellor until 5 November 1931 (History). (d) The Concord is renewed only to 1937 and "Japan may refuse in 1937" (L1734); London is asked to make a permanent cession against a guarantee with five years to run. (e) Britain refuses nothing it was asked for: Malaya, Hong Kong and India were not on the table. *Fix:* rest the motive on money (the gold-dollar annuity against sterling) and on the 1919 parity, give London a real refusal (Fiji; or a reverter if the Concord lapses; or the Realm's own signature beside the Concord's), and use canon's levers: Hoare, "whose station kept the account" (L1609), is in the registry and sat in the National Government.

**S3. The 1923 funding is itself PROPOSED.** *Passage:* A1, F. *Problem:* L1385 is "[PROPOSED] Britain funds its gold-dollar debt over sixty-two years in 1923" and the ledger entry is PROPOSED. An approved treaty cannot amend an unapproved loan. *Fix:* add a decision approving the funding, or say the draft rests on it.

**S4. The Hague signs without a reason and de Jonge wins nothing.** *Passage:* B1, B2. *Problem:* The guarantee is offered by the only bloc that could take the Indies. The clauses de Jonge "wins" (existing Shell holdings, no foreign troops) are already the Compact's first two terms, so his stand costs the Concord nothing. Shell is treated as passive; its head and Colijn (who, per Wikipedia, put de Jonge on the Bataafse board) are absent. The stabilization credit "to steady the guilder" is odd for a country that stayed on gold to 1936 by choice (from memory: the Netherlands held large gold reserves; the Indies treasury, not the guilder, was short). The credit is "post-ratification" yet extended in June–July 1932, the month of signature. *Fix:* let The Hague take the commercial half and decline or dilute the sovereignty guarantee as incompatible with neutrality; give de Jonge a concession the Concord wanted and does not get (the inter-island carrying trade, or New Guinea); date the credit after the States General vote.

**S5. The commercial rescue hurts the Concord's own producers.** *Passage:* B2.3–4, A3.3. *Problem:* Java sugar competes with "Hawaiʻi's and the Philippines' sugar" (L1560) and with "Formosa's sugar and rice go to Japan on long contracts" (L1541); the Cortes and Hawaii's planters would fight it. Bintan bauxite competes with "Arkansas, in the Gulf Republic, through the Gulf compact, and the Republic gets the alumina refinery and the royalty" (L1560) and touches the annex: Japan "builds no aluminium or heavy electrical industry against Pacifica's before 1937" (L1600). On rubber the draft is out of date: "From 1929 Tacoma's carbide turns from rubber to acetylene chemicals ... one rubber line stands ready as the reserve" (L1699). *Fix:* say who loses and what they get; list L1541, L1560, L1600, L1699 and `C-1928-HAYAMA-ANNEX`.

**S6. Japan's gain is prospective and its price is missing.** *Passage:* header, A3, B2.4, C. *Problem:* Japan receives no title (Labuan is Ka Lōkahi's), no named concession, and its arrangements are "a separate later CR". It also gives nothing, since the renunciation is withdrawn. Meanwhile Julian's lever of 1931 was "oil from Pacifica and the Gulf Republic" (L1720) and the annex has Pacifica supply "oil products" (L1600); Japanese-owned crude in Borneo weakens both, and the draft does not weigh it. The coup's manifesto "attacks the Thalassia bakufu and the merchant houses that share in its champions" (L1732); trading-house contracts in Java are that charge made flesh. The renewal already has "Japan's overseas commands passing to common control at the price of renewal" (L1734); the south is the obvious return, and the Hayama Council of May 1932 the obvious place, and neither is used. *Fix:* decide here what Japan gets (a stated share of the new blocks; yard work; the Navy ministry's command at Labuan) and what it signs (non-acquisition south of the Philippine crown, which also answers Canberra), and show the navy for and the army against.

**S7. Brunei, Sarawak and Shell.** *Passage:* A3.2–3. *Problem:* (a) "Higher published oil royalty" and Shell's concessions "honoured in full" cannot both be true of Seria; the royalty is a term of the lease. (b) "London retains a sterling royalty share": the royalty is Brunei's; unexplained. (c) New blocks go "to Concord/Japanese consortia at published auction royalties": an auction or an allotment? Canon: "concessions are auctioned" (AGENTS.md, CR-057). (d) The Sultan is eighteen or nineteen and by the same source timid before the Resident (History); who advises him when the Resident leaves, and why does he prefer Thalassia? (e) Shell loses the flag that protects its title and gains an offtake; its consent is assumed. (f) That Brunei and Sarawak are British-protected in this world rests only on CR-074 M1 ("Britain keeps Labuan, Sarawak and Brunei"), not on the storyline; Labuan was run by the North Borneo company in 1890–1904 (History), which here is Pacifican. *Fix:* choose between honouring and raising; name the Sultan's wazirs or the Brooke brothers as the people who bargain; state the 1888 protectorates as a PROPOSED premise; let one ruler hold out.

**S8. Australia and New Zealand.** *Passage:* C. *Problem:* The line "through Timor, New Guinea, and the northern Coral Sea" has no eastern end, so it does not say whether Fiji, Tonga or Tutuila lie south of it. "Neither Pacifica nor Japan annexes anything below the Philippines" is contradicted by A3–A4, where Ka Lōkahi takes Labuan, the Solomons and Fiji. Canberra refuses "the racial equality clause" (L1752) yet is said to welcome Hawaiian and Filipino crowns as sovereigns of Fiji and the Solomons, on its sea road, without asking for them itself; Australian sugar capital in Fiji and the dominions' phosphate at Ocean Island (from memory) are unmentioned. Japan's renunciation, which is what Canberra would want most, is withdrawn in the same section that claims to show Canberra's agency. New Zealand has no voice. "Mineral surveys" is a term without content. *Fix:* fix the line by coordinates to the date line; let Canberra and Wellington oppose the Fiji transfer or demand a share of the trusts; keep a Japanese non-acquisition pledge (S6).

**S9. The Gulf gets consultation and loses purchases.** *Passage:* A3.3, B2.4. *Problem:* The floor holds because "its purchases of the Republic's crude help hold the floor" (L1697). Any Borneo or Indies barrel taken by the Concord pool replaces a Gulf barrel in the one market the draft says it protects (East Asia, supplied by Pacifica under the annex). "Phased" and "consulted" do not change the arithmetic. *Fix:* say whose barrels are displaced and give Houston a term (a volume guarantee to 1937), or have Houston object on the page.

**S10. Manila and the south.** *Passage:* A3.3, `C-1932-PH-YARD-CONTRACTS`. *Problem:* "Manila ... voluntarily contracts" inverts buyer and seller: the common Navy places work; Mariveles is a chartered district, and "those who would separate the Philippine crown from Julian's call it an enclave" (L1681), so fleet work feeds the opposition rather than proving "zero infringement". Zamboanga lies in Moroland, "administered" (L1133) and under the Sulu compacts whose "Tausug text is authoritative, and the Sultan alone can consent to undo them" (L764); a yard there needs that consent. The Sultan of Sulu is also the lessor of North Borneo beside Labuan. *Fix:* show the Cortes debate and the Sultan's consent, or keep the yard at Mariveles only.

**S11. Citations that point at the right line and misstate it.** (a) L1885: canon has "any southern renunciation (the partners' CR)" among open items, and CR-082 has it "PROPOSED, for the partners' CR"; the draft's "Withdrawal of CR-082's proposed southern renunciation" decides an item reserved for another CR without saying so. (b) L1738 is cited for "labour and franchise floors modeled on Hawaii's Lands and Waters principles"; the Charter is "watershed reserves and minimum stream flows, two leeward industrial districts, restoration of mined land, leasehold for public land". (c) L1374 is cited for "Queen Wilhelmina hosted the 1919 Peace"; the storyline never names Wilhelmina. (d) L1612 is CR-049 and CR-050, not CR-051; L1133's Borneo sentences are CR-074, not CR-029; L1752 and its guarantee are Pacifica's to the dominions ("the guarantee that no hostile base stands on the dominions' soil"), not a limit on Concord basing. (e) L1385 speaks of "base rights at Singapore and Hong Kong" as payment in kind, that is, rights for Pacifica; the draft resolves it with none, which is a choice to state, not a fulfilment. (f) L892 binds the Patrimony only; the draft writes as if it bound champions and banks.

**S12. Fact changes.** The Davao pattern guards something canon never held; keep it only as a guard and say so. The Japanese-troops pattern is narrow (misses "marines", "naval guard", "garrisons", Sumatra, Borneo, Palembang, Bintan) and will fire on the sentence integration is most likely to write ("no Japanese troops in Java"); check how the linter treats negation. The Tonga event is keyed to the Queen's name, so "In spring 1918 Queen Sālote Tupou III ..." (her accession) would be flagged as a wrong year; key it to the treaty. There is no event for the Labuan cession or the Brunei and Sarawak compacts. If Chamberlain, MacDonald and Baldwin act on the page they need registry entries; Churchill (`CT-WINSTON-CHURCHILL`) and Hoare (`CT-SAMUEL-HOARE`) exist.

**S13. M-1 says more than A2 delivers.** The decisions table and D call Singapore's "permanent demilitarization"; A2.1 forbids only a "capital-ship naval fortress or heavy dry-dock expansion" and leaves cruisers "unrestricted". Use one description.

## The PROPOSED items

1. **Separate ruler compacts for Brunei and Sarawak, Shell honoured, new blocks, posted-price offtake, Houston consulted.** Keep the separate compacts; change the rest: resolve the royalty contradiction, make the new blocks an auction, give Houston a term and not a consultation, and state the 1888 protectorates as a premise (S7, S9).
2. **Sālote treats directly with Julian and Ka Lōkahi; Fiji's land protections; Fanning leased back.** Keep the meeting of the two crowns; change the instrument to a Concord treaty or drop the defence article (B2). Fiji's land rule is the least of what Fiji needs (B1). Keep the Fanning lease, with the owner's date corrected or left out (History).
3. **De Jonge overridden by The Hague; Churchill's "surrender of the East".** Keep both people. Change de Jonge's stand so that he wins something the Concord wanted (S4). Keep Churchill, and use what canon gives him: he was shown the sheet in 1927 and "cannot know what else Thalassia holds" (L1609), which is a reason his attack stops short. Correct the Chancellor for October 1931.
4. **Floating dock towed west or made commercial; Australian mineral survey clause.** Keep the dock as a detail, without the year 1929 unless verified. Drop the mineral clause or give it content (S8).

## Literary effect

**What works.** The distinction between a colony, a protected sultanate, a white Rajah and a Polynesian kingdom is the draft's best idea, and Tonga's "not transferred as property" is right. De Jonge as an oil man told to open the Indies, and Churchill attacking a retreat he knows the reason for, are scenes. Using the pause at Sembawang, which history supplies, is economical. The driller at Seria and the clerk in Surabaya are the right kind of view from below.

**Too convenient.** Britain, two rulers, a queen, The Hague, Batavia, Shell, Canberra, Wellington, Houston, Manila and Tokyo all say yes between October 1931 and July 1932, in the same months as the coup, the renewal, the Union's settlement, Caledonia, the Gulf compact and the Lands and Waters Charter. Opposition is noise (Churchill, de Jonge) that changes no term. Nobody walks out, nothing is left unsigned, and nothing costs Pacifica a thing it wanted. Suggested: one refusal that stands to the end of the book. The strongest candidates are Fiji's chiefs, who ceded to the British Crown by their own deed and can say it is not Britain's to assign (from memory: the Deed of Cession of 1874); Canberra, refusing a non-white sovereign in the Solomons; or the Rajah, who has no debt to anyone.

**Agency missing.** Julian has no aim and Victoria no opinion. She is the head of the sufficers, she broke with him in 1921 over a people who were not asked, and she is alive in Honolulu while Pacific islanders are assigned to a council she founded; a spectator's seat at a signing wastes her. The Pacifican Senate's two schools, the Cortes, the Hawaiian court, Keawe, Chichibu, Takahashi, the Navy minister, Kiyoshi's son and the Hayama Council are all absent. The transferred peoples have no person, name or voice; the "from below" candidates are both outside the transferred islands. Shell acts through no one. Japan is a noun.

**Pacifica's interest.** As written Pacifica pays (principal, guarantees, crude it must not sell, sugar it does not need) so that Britain can concentrate at home, the Dutch keep their colony, Brunei gets a better royalty and Japan's houses get contracts. This is the pattern the author has said he dislikes. The fix is in B3: say what Julian is buying and let the Senate price it.

**Japan.** Prospective only (S6).

**Viewpoint.** The draft reads as a term sheet. For the novel it yields four scenes at most (Nukuʻalofa, Batavia, Westminster, one from below) and none of them contains a decision by a principal character of the book.

**Wrong or doubtful**
