## Verdict

One more revision. Part A now reads as a sequence of decisions with losers, and the suite's calendar agrees on 15 May, 5–8 June, July and 1 August. It is not ready to integrate because the status labels contradict one another in four places, `T-R3`'s list of passages misses canon text that its own linter pattern cannot catch, A5 gives the Navy minister an undertaking that A3 and `CR-086` `T-5` deny him, and three ledger lines still date the Council to May. ## Should fix

**S1. Who makes the offer, and by what route (A3, line 66–68).** Four problems.
(a) Sequence. "The Privy Council advises ratification on two conditions … On that advice Japan proposes." Under the Meiji Constitution the Privy Council deliberates only when the Emperor consults it and advises on treaties laid before it; it does not shape a proposal before negotiation (see History). In canon's own 1927 the order is the right one: "Japan's Emperor ratifies on the Privy Council's conditional advice" (`L1593`). Here its advice precedes the Council of 5–8 June. Put Saionji and the inner court in May, a cabinet proposal carried by Chichibu, and the Privy Council's two conditions at ratification in July.
(b) Speed and government. The proposal is to reach Thalassia "by 25 May", ten days after the coup. The draft's own source has Takahashi acting prime minister from 15 to 26 May. A perpetual union proposed by an interim government, with formal Privy Council advice, inside ten days is hard to believe, and the draft does not say which cabinet proposes it.
(c) Countersignature. "whoever heads the government countersigns what follows, as `L1597` requires" (60). `L1597` requires the prime minister's countersignature only when "Julian appoints the three common ministers". `CR-084.md` line 55 reads the same line the other way: "no prime-ministerial countersignature on treaties (`L1597` … reserves that countersignature to appointing the three common ministers)". The two drafts must agree.
(d) The court acting ahead of the cabinet sits badly with `L1782`: "Hirohito's 1932 stand defends his constitutional choice." A court that proposes the surrender of exit on a genrō's advice is the personal rule the officers wanted, turned the other way. The draft should let a cabinet own the offer.

**S2. Chiyo's letters do not fit the dates (68, 204, 243).** Chiyo "carries Julian's answer and Takahashi's terms by her own letters in the household's bag". The proposal arrives by 25 May and Julian sails "at the end of May". A bag between Thalassia and Tokyo takes about a week each way by sea; no exchange of letters fits in five days. Set piece 2 then says "Chiyo has already had it from Takahashi", which would need Takahashi's letter to have left Tokyo about the day of the coup, and which makes her the receiver of the offer where A3 makes her the carrier of the answer. Either she sails with Julian and carries the answer in person, or the canon cable is used (`L1625`: "state business still goes by cable").

**S3. What the reviews are worth to Japan (76, 83, 87).** "If a review fails to agree, the terms in force continue." The terms in force are the 65/35 quota, the refused alternating executive and an executive that `C-1927-CONCORD-EXEC`'s note makes permanent ("the executive's five-year terms become five-year reviews", 273). Pacifica can therefore decline every change at every review for ever, and the Privy Council's second condition ("every term of the union except its existence is open at each review") is satisfied in words only. The draft says "Japan gives more than it gets" and calls that the turn; it should also say why the court accepts a default that favours the other side, or give Japan something at a failed review (arbitration by the Common Tribunal, a quota that follows the audit automatically, a sunset on the refusal of alternation). The stated motive, "the Concord should give its enemies no more dates", is also weaker than it looks: a review every five years, the first in summer 1937, is a date.

**S4. `T-R3`: the two cases are not put evenly.** Case A is described by its worst reading ("five years from a possible dissolution, on the day it guarantees the world's bread"). Case B lists no cost. Its costs are real and belong in the tension: the executive is entrusted to the Crown of Pacifica without term, which strains "joins co-equal crowns" (`L1595`) and AGENTS.md's "two coequal parties"; the Restoration charge of a "new bakufu" (`L1651`) becomes true in law; CR-021's inducement to Japan, an exit at each renewal, is withdrawn; Hirohito's "desire" in the character bible ("Japan's freedom within a viable constitutional order") changes. The author has decided Case B and this review does not reopen it, but the costs should be on the page so that the storyline carries them.

**S5. Japan's own navy and navy minister are a departure not listed (64, 66).** "The home naval establishment in Tokyo is Japan's own" and "the navy minister of Japan's own cabinet". `L1597`: "Foreign Affairs, the common Navy and their Finance belong to the Concord … Japan keeps crown, Diet, cabinet, railways, posts and home army." Canon names no Japanese navy outside the common Navy after 1927; CR-021's record speaks of "Tokyo's navy ministry" only before the compromise of 1927. I am not certain canon excludes a home establishment, but the draft asserts one without a citation and builds "two navies stand against the officers" on it. Cite it or list it under `## Canon tensions`.

**S6. King's paper is dated before the thing it objects to (77, 202).** King objects "in the General Staff's paper to the cabinet in the winter" to a seat "fixed by law to one nation", and the row calls the seat "recognition of May". The seat by law is agreed in June. Either the Navy seat by law was already Japan's known demand in the winter (say so, with `L1734`'s "making the Navy ministry part of the bargain"), or the paper is of late May. `CR-086` `T-5` has him "heard by the Council of the Seal", this draft has a paper "to the cabinet"; pick one forum or name both. Line 72's "The Pacificans who object do so before Julian sails" is also untrue of Tate, who objects in the Senate in July.

**S7. The fleet-building share (78).** "This is the one thing of substance in the table that Japan did not already have or was not already promised." `L1600`: "Japan's share includes shipbuilding below capital ships" (PROPOSED, CR-054). The draft must say what is new: capital ships, or a fixed 35% in place of an unstated share. A third of the common fleet's new building moved out of Pacifican yards in a slump has no named objector ("the naval constructor's estate" is an institution, not a voice), no figure for the tonnage or money, and no workbook task in section G.

**S8. Wigner loses nothing (55, 155).** He asks that Kansai "wait until the first has run five years" and wins that "Kansai's pile is not loaded until Tokyo's has a full year's operating record". Tokyo's first power is 1931 (`L1634`), so the year's record exists before ground is broken, and Kansai, "built in 1932–1937", is loaded about six years after Tokyo's first power. He gets his five years by the building calendar and the concession costs nobody anything. Give him a condition that binds (a full year at rated power after the opening of 29 April 1932 before Kansai's design is frozen; a separate earthquake review of the Kansai site; a right of the Board's safety co-chair to stop loading).

**S9. The Senate's ground for a two-thirds vote is thinner than A6 says (76, 101).** `L1604`: "Julian accepts a five-year first term instead of ten". The condition of 1928 is about the first term, which has run. Tate's opponents could say the condition is spent and the delegations suffice (`L1734`: "ratified by both crowns and delegations"). The draft's reading is defensible under `CR-084` `T-S1` Case A, but it should make the argument in the chamber, since it is the best argument against holding the vote at all, and say who insists on the vote. It should also say what happens at Hayama if the Senate later refuses: Julian agrees in June what the Senate must consent to in July, which is nearer to 1927 than "in 1932 he asks" admits.

## Literary effect

What works. May has a shape: a winter in which Julian has no answer, a station lit two weeks before the coup, an offer he "did not expect and could not have asked for", and a Council at which he is himself refused. Julian asking for the home army and being told that the army "is to be mastered by Japan, as it was in May, or it has not been mastered" is the best exchange in the draft. The common Navy minister "silent between two loyalties" is a scene. The Diet deputy and "the bakufu made permanent" give the other side a line.

What does not yet work.
- The turn is asserted more than motivated. Japan gives up exit, in ten days, on the ground of denying its enemies a date, and receives a yard share, a seat it already held by convention and a promise for 1937. A reader will ask what the court fears enough to do this; the draft needs one concrete fear (a second coup timed to 1937, named by someone) and one man who argues against it inside the court and loses. At present every Japanese institution agrees.
- The Japanese who lose are institutions: "the army ministry objects", "a deputy says". The Pacificans who lose have names. Name one Japanese loser.
- Tate, Giannini, Ryan and King each lose once here, and Giannini and Tate also lose in `CR-084` and `CR-086` in the same season, as the draft admits. Four objectors who all lose and then obtain a consolation each is a pattern the reader will see. Let one of them win something that costs Julian.
- Wigner's loss is free (S8), and King's is dated oddly (S6).
- A4 is a table and A7 a list of eight costs. The table suits a decision record, but "what Japan is refused" and "what Julian is refused" are where the scene is. The yen has no scene in the Council; it is settled by ministers in June–July.
- Viewpoint: the "from below" candidates are good and unchosen. The petty officer "on the morning his carrier does not sail" belongs with A3.
- Parts B to D are term sheets and say so.

## History checked

- **15 May 1932: confirmed in part.** The incident is of that date; naval officers with army cadets shot Inukai; courts martial followed with very light sentences. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_15_incident . The page gives no date for the trials opening; the draft's "The courts martial open within the week" is a canon choice and is not marked *Hist.*, correctly. In history the assassins were navy officers, so "the navy stand against them" is canon's divergence, not history.
- **Saionji: confirmed** that he recommended Saitō, who took office on 26 May 1932. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sait%C5%8D_Makoto . His advice on a treaty's term is the draft's invention; a genrō's recognised function in 1932 was recommending the prime minister. Doubtful as constitutional practice, not wrong as fiction.
- **Privy Council and treaties: confirmed in part, and against the draft's sequence.** It advised the Emperor on treaties and international agreements, deliberated only when consulted, and could not initiate. Its president in 1932 was Kuratomi Yūzaburō. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_of_Japan . See S1(a).
- **Takahashi: confirmed** as finance minister from 13 December 1931 and acting prime minister 15–26 May 1932. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takahashi_Korekiyo . **Gold embargo of 13 December 1931: doubtful as dated.** The page gives December 1931 without the day. **Yen "~60% drop": doubtful.** An NBER paper gives about 30% within a month and about another 30% against the dollar in 1932, roughly a half in all. https://nber.org/papers/w2683 . The draft's Sources also list this figure as unverified while the body marks it *Hist.*
- **Noguchi: confirmed** (26 July 1873 – 15 January 1944; Nihon Chisso Hiryo formed 1908; Casale process bought 1921). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shitagau_Noguchi . The same page has his Korean power and fertiliser companies founded in 1926, which bears on S12.
- **Mori and Showa Fertiliser: confirmed in part.** Founded October 1928; ammonium sulphate April 1931; first domestic aluminium January 1934 at Ōmachi by Nihon Yodo, renamed Nihon Denki Kōgyō in March 1934. https://the-shashi.com/en/tse/4004/ . The page does not name Mori as Showa Fertiliser's founder (it has him founding Nihon Yodo in 1926). His dates, 1884–1941: not checked (the page could not be fetched).
