## Verdict

**One more revision.** The Pacifican half of Part A now holds together: the objectors are named, the Senate's vote is honestly argued, and the costs of `T-R3` are stated. The Japanese half does not yet stand. The draft does not say which cabinet makes the offer in the week after 15 May, nobody who can bind Japan sits at the Hayama table, and the text gives two different answers to what exactly has no term. These three are at the hinge of the suite's ending and are worth one more pass before integration.

## Blockers

**B1. The offer has no determinate author.**
*Passage.* Line 78: "Canon leaves Inukai's fate open, and so does this draft. Whoever heads the government after 15 May, the proposal that follows is his cabinet's." Line 79: "The cabinet's proposal is cabled to Chichibu about 22 May." Line 84: "The offer itself is the cabinet's, and **Takahashi** carries it there."
*Problem.* Revision 5 moved the offer from the court to the cabinet for a good constitutional reason, and then left the cabinet undefined. If Inukai is dead, the cabinet of 16 to 22 May is a caretaker under an acting premier (in history Takahashi himself, as the draft's own `## Sources` line 530 records: "acting Prime Minister 15–26 May 1932"), and Saionji's business that week is choosing the next premier, not the Concord. A caretaker that surrenders the right to leave within seven days of a coup is the weakest possible author for the largest concession in the book, and the Restoration's successors will say so. If Inukai lives, the offer is his, and a prime minister who has just survived the officers has a motive the draft never uses. The two cases give different scenes, different motives and a different Takahashi ("carries it there" means nothing if he presides). The draft cannot keep the question open and also rest the ending on its answer.
*Fix.* Either decide Inukai's fate here as a listed tension (the cleanest), or write the route so that it holds in both cases: the cabinet in being resolves the offer under its acting head, the successor cabinet confirms it before the Council opens, and the cable to Chichibu follows the confirmation. If the dates then no longer reach 5 June, move the Council by a week and change `CR-083` line 140–141, `CR-084` lines 167 and 224 and `CR-086` line 286 with it. State Takahashi's office in 1932 once (canon has only "From Tokyo Takahashi asks", `L1695`).

**B2. Nobody who can bind Japan is in the room at Hayama, and the cabinet's second condition is dropped without anyone dropping it.**
*Passage.* Line 91: "Julian presides with the three common ministers, without the national ministers (`L1651`)." Line 88: "The common Navy minister does not speak for Japan." Line 84: a court that proposed the surrender "would be the personal rule the officers wanted". Line 105: "Japan refuses, on the Privy Council's first condition … The court's reason is that the army is to be mastered by Japan". Line 95: "its cabinet's second condition is met in full only for the quota." Line 85: the Privy Council "advises ratification in late June, on the same two conditions".
*Problem.* Three things. (a) The table of line 93 is a negotiation with refusals on both sides ("What Japan is refused", "What Julian is refused"), yet by the draft's own rules the Emperor cannot bargain, the national ministers are excluded, and the one Japanese at the table does not speak for Japan. The draft does not say who says no to Julian on the home army, or who accepts for Japan that every default but the quota favours the holder of the executive. Takahashi is at Hayama only to talk with Chiyo (line 87). (b) Line 105 still gives the refusal to "the Privy Council's first condition" and "the court's reason". Under revision 5 the conditions are the cabinet's (line 84), the Privy Council "has no part in shaping the proposal" (line 85) and does not meet on it until three weeks after the Council. (c) The cabinet's second condition, "every term of the union except its existence is open at each review", is not met: the executive is refused (line 103), and failing agreement everything but the quota stays as it is (line 95). The draft admits this in a subordinate clause and then has the Privy Council advise ratification "on the same two conditions". A condition attached in May, unmet in June and repeated in late June is a contradiction, and it is also the scene the Hayama Council lacks: a named Japanese minister who gives the condition up, and what he takes for it.
*Fix.* Say how Japan's terms reach the table: for example, the cabinet's envoy (Takahashi, or the premier of B1) and the common ministers settle the text in the days before and beside the Council, and the Council of the Two Crowns adopts it as resolutions "dated at Hayama" (`L1651`). Give the home-army refusal and the concession on defaults to that person by name. Rewrite line 105 without the Privy Council and without "the court's reason". Then either reword the second condition to what Japan in fact obtains (every term may be raised at each review; the quota alone has a default in Japan's favour) or let the Privy Council's advice of June record that the second condition was met in part, with a councillor who says so.

**B3. The draft gives two answers to what is without term.**
*Passage.* Line 95: "The union ends only by the agreement of both crowns." Line 84: every term "except its existence is open at each review". Line 103: the alternating executive "is refused in 1932; Japan may raise it again at a review." Line 340 (`T-R3`, costs): "The executive is entrusted to the Crown of Pacifica without term". Line 325 (`T-R3`, Case B): "the alternating executive stays refused." Line 245 and line 294: "`L1595`'s 'five-year terms' becomes five-year reviews".
*Problem.* Canon renews a thing narrower than the union: "The five-year executive trust is renewed to 1937" (`L1734`), and `L1595` entrusts "the Concord's executive … for five-year terms". The ledger says so in terms: `C-1927-CONCORD`, `open`: "The Concord's own duration is not stated; only the executive trust has terms"; and `C-1932-CONCORD-RENEWAL`, `open`: "'Japan may refuse in 1937' follows the betrothal's marriage; whether it refers to the marriage or the next renewal is ambiguous." `T-R3` reads canon as a union that could dissolve in 1937 and does not mention either note. More important for integration, Part A says the executive is one of the terms open at each review, and `T-R3` says it is entrusted without term. Those are different constitutions. Under the first, Pacifica's chair is a term Japan can reopen every five years with no default in its favour; under the second, the "new bakufu" of `L1651` is permanent law. The reader, the Diet deputy of line 83 and the Privy Council all need to know which. The replacement sentence for `L1734` is drafted (line 339); the replacement for `L1595` is not.
*Fix.* State in one sentence in `A4` and in `T-R3` what has no term (the union) and what the executive's tenure is (held by the Crown of Pacifica until both crowns agree otherwise at a review, or held without term and outside the reviews: choose one). Draft the new wording of `L1595` beside that of `L1734`. Add to `T-R3` that canon's Concord had no stated duration and that the ledger records the sentence of `L1734` as ambiguous, so that the author's decision of 19:32 rests on canon as it stands; and say whether Japan keeps a right to decline the marriage, since the new last sentence of `L1734` removes "refuse" altogether.

## History checked

Checked on the web for this review:
- **The May 15 incident.** The date, 15 May 1932, and that naval officers carried it out with army cadets and civilians, and that Inukai was shot (Wikipedia, "May 15 incident"). That naval officers led it matters for S3: history's coup and canon's both have a naval arm.
- **The Privy Council and treaties.** The London Naval Treaty was signed on 22 April 1930 and ratified on 2 October 1930 after the cabinet obliged the Privy Council to deliberate, with Saionji, "the surviving Genro", backing the cabinet (National Diet Library, "London Naval Conference"). This supports the draft's route (cabinet proposes, Privy Council advises before ratification) and is the basis of S4's doubt about three weeks.
- **The yen after December 1931.** An NBER paper on the yen in 1931–33 has the yen fall about 30% in the month after Japan left gold in December 1931 and about another 30% during 1932 (abstract of NBER working paper 2683). Compounded that is about half, and from memory the low against the dollar late in 1932 was near 60% below the old parity. "~60%" (line 131) is defensible only as the trough against the dollar; "about half within a year, more at the worst" is safer. The draft's own doubt at line 508 can be narrowed to that.

- Inukai's cabinet formed on 13 December 1931 and re-imposed the gold embargo the same day, with Takahashi at Finance (the day, which line 508 doubts, is right as far as memory serves; the ordinance ending convertibility followed within days).
