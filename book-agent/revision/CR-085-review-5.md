## Verdict

One more revision. The story of May to June is now coherent in outline and the canon tension `T-R3` is honestly argued, but the decisions record is broken in one place, the draft states in two incompatible ways what Japan gives up, and the Emperor's signed instruments cannot reach Thalassia by 1 August on the dates the suite gives. None of the three needs new invention; each needs one statement made once and carried through.

## Blockers

All three examples belong to other drafts. Nothing quoted says which additions in this draft were before him. **B2. What Japan gives up is stated two ways.**
*Passage.* Line 105: "Canon never gave the union a term; what had terms was the executive trust." `T-R3`, line 338: "what this case changes in law is the trust's expiry and Japan's right to refuse, and nothing about the union's length." Against these: line 74, "a partner who can walk away"; line 93, "Pacifica too could have declined to renew, and everything Japan is about to be given … would have been a favour revocable on that day. A partner that cannot leave is a partner that cannot be left"; line 120, "cannot be five years from dissolving when it signs"; line 337, "five years from a possible dissolution".
*Problem.* The ledger supports line 105 (`C-1927-CONCORD`, `open`: "The Concord's own duration is not stated; only the executive trust has terms"), and the ledger's note on `C-1932-CONCORD-RENEWAL` even doubts whether "Japan may refuse in 1937" refers to the renewal at all. If the union never had a term, then a refusal in 1937 would have ended only Pacifica's hold on the executive, and neither the stations' lease, the piers, the loan nor the yen would have been "revocable on that day". Takahashi's creditor argument, the sufficers' warning, Julian's fifth reason and Case A's cost all assume that refusal dissolves the Concord. The draft never says what a refusal would have done. Until it does, the reader cannot weigh the sacrifice on which the whole CR turns, and the rewritten `L1734` cannot be drafted.
*Fix.* State once, in `A1` or at the head of `A3`, what both capitals in 1932 take a refusal to mean (the natural reading: a union without an agreed executive cannot act, so refusal is exit in fact though not in law; or that the treaty of 1927 lapses with the trust). Then make lines 93, 105, 118, 120 and `T-R3` use that one account. If the answer is "exit in fact", say so in `T-R3` and drop "nothing about the union's length".

**B3. The Emperor's instruments cannot be at Thalassia on 1 August as dated, and the two drafts disagree on what the Privy Council advises and when.**
*Passage.* Line 91: the Privy Council "advises ratification in mid-July". Line 126: "The Emperor signs the countersignature at Tokyo with Japan's ratification, and Prince Chichibu deposits both at Thalassia on 1 August; he carries and does not sign (`CR-086` `E1`, `C2a`)". `CR-086` `E1` (its line 310): the Emperor "signs Japan's ratification at Tokyo in the last days of July, on Privy Council advice given on the Act itself and separate from its advice on the renewal, and Prince Chichibu deposits it in the hall."
*Problem.* (a) Canon has no air crossing of the Pacific (state business "still goes by cable", `L1625`), and a ship needs about nine or ten days. A paper signed in Tokyo in the last days of July is at sea on 1 August. (b) This draft knows one advice, in mid-July, on the renewal; `CR-086` adds a second, on the Final Act, a fortnight later. The body that needed a month and a visit from Saionji to each councillor for the first, with Hiranuma dissenting on the prerogative, is not shown giving the second at all. (c) The pledge's wording is still being fought at Thalassia in July (`CR-084` `B5`, request 1; `CR-086` `T-5`), so the Emperor countersigns in Tokyo a text not yet final. (d) Chichibu lives at Thalassia (`L1651`); he cannot "carry" anything from Tokyo. (e) Canon's own procedure is different and is not mentioned: "authorized ministers sign and exchange its instruments at Thalassia" (`L1734`; character_bible.md 1064: "In 1932 ministers sign and exchange the renewal instruments in Thalassia … his presence does not substitute for his brother's constitutional consent"). The draft replaces it without a tension or an impact note.
*Fix.* Use what the draft already has. Takahashi leaves Tokyo in mid-July and is at Thalassia "from about 25 July" (line 142). Let the Emperor sign the renewal's ratification after the mid-July advice and Takahashi carry it, as the authorized minister canon expects. For the Final Act and the countersignature, either the Privy Council's mid-July advice covers the Council's position on both articles as agreed at Hayama and the Emperor gives full powers to sign within that text, the instrument following by sea in August; or 1 August is signature and promulgation, with Japan's ratification notified by cable and deposited later. Whichever is chosen, `CR-086` `E1` must say the same, and `A3` should name both advices or say why one suffices.

## History checked

Checked on the web on 24 November 2026 unless marked otherwise.

- **The May 15 incident: confirmed, with one correction.** 15 May 1932; Inukai shot at his residence by young naval officers, aided by army cadets and civilian rightists; the officers were later court-martialled (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_15_incident). The draft's "the cadets are before a naval court martial" (line 106) is wrong on two counts: the cadets were the army's, and, from my own knowledge and not checked, the trials were held in 1933.
- **Inukai's cabinet: confirmed.** 13 December 1931 to 26 May 1932 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inukai_cabinet). Inukai born 1855, from my own knowledge.
- **Takahashi as acting prime minister: confirmed.** 15 to 26 May 1932, keeping Finance; born 27 July 1854 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takahashi_Korekiyo and the Inukai cabinet page).
- **Saitō Makoto: confirmed.** Born 27 October 1858; cabinet from 26 May 1932 to 8 July 1934; Takahashi acting premier before him (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sait%C5%8D_Makoto). Takahashi at Finance under Saitō is from my own knowledge and the draft's cited page; not separately fetched.
- **Araki Sadao as army minister in both: confirmed.** Born 26 May 1877; minister from December 1931 to 23 January 1934 under Inukai and Saitō (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadao_Araki; the Inukai cabinet page gives 13 December 1931).
- **Hiranuma Kiichirō: confirmed.** Vice-president of the Privy Council from 12 April 1926 to 13 March 1936; he strongly opposed ratifying the London Naval Treaty; born 1867 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiranuma_Kiichir%C5%8D). His dissent in the draft is in character.
- **Mori Kaku: confirmed as a Seiyūkai deputy, and more than the draft says.** Born 28 December 1882, died 11 December 1932; elected to the House of Representatives from 1920; secretary-general of the Seiyūkai in 1929; chief secretary of Inukai's cabinet for its whole term, to 26 May 1932 (https://www.ndl.go.jp/portrait/e/datas/568 and the Inukai cabinet page). See S3. His illness from the summer of 1932 is from my own knowledge and not checked; if he is to speak in June he can.
- **The Privy Council and the London Naval Treaty: partly wrong as worded.** Confirmed: signed 22 April 1930, ratified by Japan on 2 October 1930, ratifications exchanged in London on 27 October (https://ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha3/description17.html and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Naval_Treaty). Not confirmed on the web, and from my own knowledge different from the draft: the treaty was referred to the Privy Council only in late July 1930, and the Council approved it on 1 October, so it "kept" the treaty about ten weeks, not "from April to October". The point that a month is fast survives; the sentence should read "from late July to October" once the date of referral is verified. Note also that the London instrument took twenty-five days from signature in Tokyo to deposit in London, which bears on B3.
- **The yen after December 1931: confirmed, with a caution.** Gold exports suspended on 13 December 1931, the day the new cabinet took office; the yen at about 50 dollars per 100 yen on 1 December 1931, about 35 within a month, below 25 in the summer of 1932 and about 20 in November 1932, a fall of about 60 percent over the twelve months (https://www.nber.org/papers/w2683.pdf). So "~60%" is right for the year's low and not for July 1932, when the fall was about half. Line 143 should say "about 60 percent by the end of 1932", and line 568's doubt can be closed.
- **Unchecked.** The 62nd Diet's dates (from my own knowledge an extraordinary session of 1 to 14 June 1932, which would end before the Council does; the draft may keep the Diet sitting longer but should know it departs); the Seiyūkai's majority after the election of February 1932; that Privy Council advice was required for treaty ratification (from my own knowledge, yes); every *Hist.* claim in Parts B to D, which this review did not re-check.
