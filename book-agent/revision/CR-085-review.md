Fresh-context review of CR-085, revision 1, 23 November 2026. The reviewer read the draft, AGENTS.md and current canon, and not the writer's notes or earlier reviews.

Line numbers are `master_storyline.md` on the reviewed checkout. Web checks were made on 23 November 2026; anything I could not open is labelled "from memory" or "not checked".

## Verdict

Not ready to integrate; one further revision should make it ready. The draft is a term sheet: it lists what Japan receives and almost nothing that anyone refuses, loses or pays. Its central device for 1937 (the aluminium "graduation") does not work as written, its rice mechanisms cannot produce the approved 1910 and 1920 figures, it leaves unspent the one bargaining item canon itself names for 1932 (the Navy seat), and its author's-request section is in the drafter's words. The smaller dossiers (Tokyo station financing, the refinery site, the aircraft line, the Kamchatka charter) are sound in outline and can stay.

## Blockers

**B1. The 1937 graduation gives Japan nothing it would not have by leaving, and takes effect before the renewal it depends on.**
- *Passage:* B2, T-H1, P-2, `C-1932-HAYAMA-GRADUATION`, the calendar's last row.
- *Problem:* Canon already ends the bar in 1937: L1600 "builds no aluminium or heavy electrical industry against Pacifica's before 1937". The ledger says of `C-1928-HAYAMA-ANNEX` that 1937 is "the end of the Concord's second term, not a stated term of the annex". If Japan refuses renewal, the Concord and its annex lapse and the bar lapses with them. So "the bar expires on 1 January 1937, contingent on renewal" offers the zaibatsu the same freedom whether Tokyo renews or walks out; it is not "an industrial prize that Japan's own zaibatsu lobby Tokyo to secure by renewing". Second, the first term runs "from the provisional application of 1927 to 1932" (L1595) and the 1932 instruments are exchanged in June–July, so the second term ends in mid-1937. A graduation on 1 January 1937 "contingent on renewal of the Concord for its third term" turns on an event about six months later.
- *Fix:* Make the prize something only renewal gives: entry of Japanese smelters into the Concord pool at the pool's price (L1600 "keeps the Concord pool's price inside"), a stated share of the fleet's and grid's orders, alumina and power equipment at pool terms, and the 49% joint ventures converting to majority. Leaving then means building a smelter industry outside the pool against the low-cost producer. Date it to the day the third term begins. If the author wants the bar itself to be the lever, that needs a tension of its own: the bar extended past 1937 in 1932 and released early on renewal, which amends L1600.

**B2. The rice mechanisms do not reach the approved figures for 1910 and 1920.**
- *Passage:* D1, P-5, `C-1895-SAPPORO-EXCHANGE`.
- *Problem:* CR-082 (line 203 of CR-082.md) asks for a crop "5% more by 1910, 8% more by 1920, 13% more by 1932". Of the draft's three mechanisms, (2) is dated by its own people: Noguchi founds Nichitsu in 1908 and buys the Casale process in 1921; Mori founds Showa Fertilizer in October 1928 and makes ammonium sulphate in 1931 (sources below). "From the 1910s onward" cannot lift the crop of 1910. Mechanism (3), no colonial-rice price collapse, is by the draft's own account an effect of the 1920s; the Korean programme begins in 1920. That leaves Hokkaido alone to supply 5% of Japan's crop by 1910, about 2.5 million koku on CR-082's 50.1 million. From memory, not verified, Hokkaido's whole historical crop in those years was of that order or less; the draft gives no acreage (it lists it as OPEN). The breeding dates also break the rule the draft cites: CR-082 line 152 gives "twelve to fifteen years from first cross to release", which winter nurseries "can halve". A compact of 1895 therefore releases cross-bred varieties about 1901–1903, not "by the late 1890s". Also CR-082 speaks of the crop, the draft of "yields"; acreage in Hokkaido raises the crop, not the yield. And "easily clears" overstates CR-082, which says a Japan that keeps its old pace "is within 1% of its need in 1932".
- *Fix:* Keep R-6 and carry 1910 and 1920 on mechanisms that exist by then. Candidates inside canon: Chile's nitrate on Pacifica's long contracts from 1891 (CR-082) sold to Japan before the annex of 1928 reserves Japan's fertiliser market; Manchurian bean cake on open carriage (CR-081); selection and seed exchange for Tōhoku as well as Hokkaido; Hokkaido acreage with a stated MODEL figure. Say "crop", give the acreage, and move the cross-bred releases to after 1901.

**B3. The Navy seat, which canon makes part of the 1932 bargain, is not mentioned.**
- *Passage:* A1, A2, T-M4, P-1.
- *Problem:* L1734: "The naval party loses its reserved squadron arrangement as command is consolidated, making the Navy ministry part of the bargain." CR-021 E3 (line 434, 438 of CR-021.md): the Navy seat is Japanese "by convention ... while the western squadron's command is reserved", and "the Navy seat becomes part of the renewal bargain". The draft counts "two Japanese ministers (Common Navy; Trade, Shipping, and Materials)" as if the Navy seat were settled, raises the legal floor from one to two without saying which seats the law names, and never says what was bargained over the seat. It invents a fourth ministry while the compensation canon itself seeded goes unused.
- *Fix:* Decide the Navy seat first: for example, Japanese by law from 1932 (Japan's gain), or thrown open with the squadron (Japan's loss, and the reason it needs a second seat). Then judge whether a fourth ministry is still needed (see P-1 below).

**B4. The author's request is not the author's words, and one tension rests on words that appear only in an unmerged draft.**
- *Passage:* "The author's request"; T-R1; P-5 and P-6 in `## Decisions`.
- *Problem:* CR-TEMPLATE.md asks for "The author's words, verbatim, with date and time", and CR-082 does this. This section states "two strategic briefs" in the drafter's prose with no quotation and no date. T-R1 and P-6 cite "the author's 22–23 November 2026 preference"; the words are quoted only in the CR-084 draft ("On 22 November I chose a Japan that goes south with leased ports ...", line 29 of that file), which is not canon. AGENTS.md: "Do not mistake an editorial proposal, assistant suggestion or historical illustration for author-approved canon."
- *Fix:* Quote the author's requests for this CR with dates, or say plainly that there are none and the briefs are the drafter's reading of open items. Quote the 22–23 November words in this file if T-R1 relies on them.

## Should fix

**S1. The Atomic Power Board's audit reaches inside the Works.** C2 has the board "audit and publish" a formula "indexed to audited uranium-mining and fabrication costs". The Works' rules sit "in the Works' charter and the Polytechnic estate's statutes" (CR-050, AGENTS.md), not in a treaty, and the second part of the record is "sealed for twenty years from 1927" (L1612), covering among other things "the contest and the feed, the halls ... the chemistry of the bred elements". Nishina re-signed his bond in 1927 ("Every member of the inner circle signs a new bond for the second part, to 1947", L1612); as co-chair he would audit for Japan what he is bound not to tell Japan. I am not certain mining and fabrication costs fall under the seal, but the draft does not ask. *Fix:* index the tariff to something outside the wall (Tokyo's delivered coal cost, which C1 already uses), publish the formula and not its cost components, and make Nishina's divided duty a stated cost rather than a convenience. Say who in Pacifica consents: Langmuir's seat and the Council of the Seal, not the Hayama Council.

**S2. Who speaks for Japan at Hayama.** A1 has "Japan's common Navy Minister" present "Japan's renewal terms". He is Julian's appointee (L1597 "Julian appoints the three common ministers with Japan's prime minister countersigning"), a Concord officer whose own ministry is in the bargain. L1651 excludes the national ministers; L1734 says "The crowns agree the renewal". *Fix:* have Japan's terms settled by its cabinet and carried in by the Emperor's side (Chichibu between Councils, the court at Hayama), with the common Navy minister as the man in the room whose loyalty both sides doubt. That is a better scene than a spokesman.

**S3. The legal route for a treaty amendment is asserted, not shown.** A4 says "Julian's ministers and the Concord's delegates carry ratification", citing L1604, which is the consent of 1928. The route canon gives is L1602 ("Sixty delegates from each legislature meet separately ... After three disagreements they vote together without debate") and both crowns (CR-021 line 386 calls the 1932 amendment legal by that route). A fourth ministry and a change to "At least one must be Japanese by law" are larger than the amendment canon records. Tate's bloc refused the treaty in 1927 over the 65% (L1593). *Fix:* show the delegations' vote, say whether the Senate is asked, and let the 1928 conditions (L1604 "quota review at renewal ... a Polytechnic audit reported to the Senate") be used against it.

**S4. The fourth ministry's competence is wider than "no intrusion".** L1597: "Foreign Affairs, the common Navy and their Finance belong to the Concord"; customs are "agreed matters", and Japan "keeps crown, Diet, cabinet, railways, posts". A common ministry for merchant shipping, marine insurance and "grain-carrier charters" is a new common competence over both parties' merchant marines. It also puts a Japanese-by-convention minister over part of what CR-082 calls the cabinet's never-stated aim, "to be indispensable at every step of the world's grain trade, in seed, nitrogen, machines, credit, ships, ports, storage" (L1672). That may be a real price for Julian, which the draft needs; but the draft does not notice it, and the provisioners would.

**S5. The yen reset has costs the draft does not count.** (a) The quota is in gold dollars; a 20–25% lower parity raises its yen cost by a quarter to a third in the same paragraph that says Tokyo "cannot bear a higher gold-dollar share". (b) The same applies to the last rescue instalments, which the rebuilt banks owe, not "Japan" (L1589 "a stabilization agency lends it on to the rebuilt banks, which repay it"). (c) Pool aluminium and oil are priced at gold parity (L1691), so the navy's metal becomes dearer. (d) Bank of Japan underwriting inside a ±5% band drains gold; history's version worked on a floating yen. (e) "lifting Nagano silk and farm incomes immediately" is too easy: silk is priced in the Union's collapsed market. (f) China's bound duties on Japanese cotton goods run to May 1933 (L1706); a cheaper yen lands on Shanghai's mills. (g) L1720 lists "the yen's convertibility through common finance" among Julian's levers of 1931; the draft loosens it without an impact note.

**S6. Self-sufficiency and the rice contracts contradict each other.** A1 says the renewal keeps Japan "self-sufficient in staple rice"; D1 and D4 keep L1541 ("Formosa's sugar and rice go to Japan on long contracts") and L1706 ("Korea's rice goes to Japan by contract on Japanese credit"). On CR-082's table a 13% larger crop closes almost all of the 8.1 million koku gap. Then what do the contracts carry, and who buys Korea's rice? Say so, and list both lines in the impact notes.

**S7. The warnings compact runs the wrong way for typhoons.** D2 has Ōmori, a seismologist, "delivering day-ahead typhoon warnings to Guam, Manila, and the Visayas", and set piece 4 has Tokyo "wiring a typhoon track to Manila and Guam". Typhoons reach the Philippines and Guam before Japan; L1131 says only that "typhoon warnings reach Guam, Manila and the Visayas a day ahead", and L1631 that the Tokyo machine "computes typhoon tracks for Japan's coast". Japan's side is earthquake and tsunami reports outward and typhoon warnings inward. From memory, the Manila Observatory was the region's typhoon service from the 1880s; it would be the Realm's half.

**S8. The Tokyo station's financing is undated.** Ground is broken in 1928 (L1634), so the split between Tokyo Dentō and the Works must be agreed in 1927–28, in the year of Japan's bank crisis. The draft hangs it on `C-1931-TOKYO-FUEL-LEASE` and gives no date or signatories. A twenty-year lease to 1951 also outlives a refusal in 1937 and the seal's opening in 1947; say what happens to the fuel if Japan leaves. This is where the dependence canon names would bite, and the draft is silent.

**S9. The Manila–Shanghai cable ignores who held China's landings.** The historical 1906 cable belonged to a company half owned by the British Eastern group and a quarter by Great Northern (Wikipedia, below). From memory, not verified, those two companies held exclusive landing rights in China at the time, which is why. A state cable of the Realm landing at Wusong in 1906 needs that bought out, shared or broken by the Qing; the draft's point is to avoid British wires. Also "Robert Hart/Fong's Customs network": in canon "Hart takes the British ministership" (L790, PROPOSED) and Fong heads the Inspectorate.

**S10. Fact changes.** The four event patterns match any year within 120 characters of the name and fire nine times on the draft's own text (preview: "concord-atomic-power-board: canon year is 1932, not 1937", and so on). Anchor each to a verb ("is chartered in", "lands in"). The forbidden patterns are so literal they will catch nothing ("Japan (?:builds|opens|starts|pours) domestic ..."). Nothing tracks the things most likely to drift: the number of common ministers after 1932, the Sapporo compact's year, the Kansai station.

## Standalone merge

CR-085 does not merge cleanly on its own in three places.
1. T-R1 closes L1885's "any southern renunciation" as "not enacted" and points to CR-084 for what replaces it. Alone, CR-085 leaves Japan's southern question answered by nothing, on authority quoted only in CR-084's draft. Either leave the item OPEN here or merge after CR-084.
2. Both drafts amend `C-1932-CONCORD-RENEWAL`, both rewrite L1734 and L1885, and both stage the same May Hayama Council with the common Navy Minister speaking for Japan. Whichever merges second must be rebased on the first.
3. The Bintan cross-link is properly conditional. CR-054's own record already expects "Bintan and the Indies ... through the Concord, in the 1930s" (line 24 of CR-054.md), so nothing rests on CR-084 there.

Contradictions among the three: CR-084 has the common Foreign Minister negotiate Indies concessions, bunkering and shipping, which CR-085 gives to the new ministry; CR-084 says the annex is "eroded in Asian oil products" while CR-085 B1.4 restates the annex without it. CR-083 does not touch CR-085 except that its prairie wheat is resold "to Japan and China", which sits oddly beside a Japan made self-sufficient.

Japan's bargain across the three: in one summer Japan gains leased Indies ports and its first oil outside Pacifica and the Gulf (CR-084), a second ministry, a lower yen, a second station and the end of the industrial bar (CR-085). Against that it gives up only what canon already took. L1720 names Julian's levers of 1931 as "oil from Pacifica and the Gulf Republic, transport by the common navy and the yen's convertibility through common finance"; the two drafts loosen two of the three, weeks after Japanese-crewed carriers made "the danger to Oʻahu concrete" (L1732). Japan in 1932 is in a slump, on a rescue loan, its coup failed. The sum is more than its position can extract, and no draft says why Julian pays it in the year Britain retrenches and the Union comes in.

## Literary effect

What works: the Kansai station as a stake; Takahashi between Thalassia's refusal and his villages; Nishina's position, if his bond is used; the Hokkaido family and the Nagano household in the set pieces; the oil-and-aluminium champions resisting graduation (one clause in A4, worth a scene).

What is flat or too convenient:
- Nobody refuses anything except what canon already refused. The Reserve Bank, the Works, the Senate, the Diet, the army and the aluminium champion each object in a clause and then agree. No refusal costs anyone.
- Japan's gains are all certain and dated. Pacifica's price is not real: a seat Julian fills, a bar that was ending anyway, a parity canon had already conceded.
- Pacifica's own interest is invisible, which the author dislikes. The draft could state it: the bred elements returning from two Japanese stations; a rice-secure Japan that stops looking at Manchuria's land (CR-081's "land against crop"); Japanese yards and capital inside the pool instead of outside it; the fleet kept whole.
- "Japan may refuse in 1937" is asserted to survive while every party named is given a reason to renew. CR-021 leaves 1937 "open at the novel's end". The draft should raise the stakes of 1937 on both sides, not lower the odds.
- Missing actors: Chiyo, whom the manifesto names and who is canon's channel to Takahashi (L1722); Victoria and the sufficers; Hirohito beyond hosting; Chichibu beyond "channel"; the Diet; the army after May; the oil trust; Korea, China and federal Russia, though the title says "the Partners". Korea loses a rice market and China meets a cheaper yen, and neither is asked.
- Hokkaido: paddy pushed "a full generation ahead" onto the Ishikari and Kamikawa plains is Ainu land, and canon has already had Sitka say that "Sapporo settles its soldiers on Ainu land without asking" (L729). A Pacifican compact that speeds it has a moral cost the book's own rule on land would see.
