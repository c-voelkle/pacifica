## A. The 1932 Concord Renewal: The Navy Seat, the Quota, and What Both Sides Pay

**A1. The Bargain Canon Seeds: Consolidating Command and the Navy Seat (`L1597`, `L1734`; `T-M4`).**
Canon already plants the centrepiece of the 1932 renewal at `L1734 ("Japan may refuse in 1937")`: *"The naval party loses its reserved squadron arrangement as command is consolidated, making the Navy ministry part of the bargain. Japan's demand for an alternating executive is refused."*

In 1927 (`L1597 ("Foreign Affairs, the common Navy")`), at least one of the three common ministers must be Japanese by law, while the Navy portfolio belongs to Japan only *by convention* while the western squadron is reserved. In the summer of 1932, weeks after Japanese-crewed carriers made *"the danger to Oʻahu concrete"* (`L1732 ("merchant houses that share in its champions")`), Julian's hawks and the Realm's fleet provisioners press to throw the Navy portfolio open alongside the abolished reserved squadron. Tokyo's naval treaty faction refuses to surrender the western squadron unless its institutional compensation is written into law.

- **Recommended settlement (`T-M4` Case A; `C-1932-NAVY-SEAT-PROTOCOL`, PROPOSED):** Keep **strictly three common ministries** (preserving `L1597` without inventing a new common competence over domestic shipping or grain charters, `L1672 ("holds only while the sellers sell")`). As direct payment for surrendering the reserved western squadron and bringing overseas commands under unified control, the **Common Navy Ministry is upgraded from revocable convention to Japanese by treaty law** for the 1932–1937 term. To give Japan a real commercial voice without handing over a fourth cabinet ministry, the renewal establishes a subordinate **Joint Concord Materials Board** (equal Pacifican and Japanese delegates reporting to Common Finance and the Council of the Two Crowns) to oversee Hayama Annex pool allocations and mid-1937 pool-entry preparations. *(Adding a fourth Common Ministry of Trade and Materials with binding shortage-allocation powers—and the Senate treaty-amendment fight it provokes—is weighed in `T-M4` Case B.)*
- **Why Julian pays anything in 1932—and what Pacifica actually wins:** Japan enters summer 1932 weakened by the slump, repaying its 1927 bank rescue loan, its May coup defeated. Julian yields neither the alternating executive nor a fourth ministry, and he pays only where the price buys Pacifica four concrete returns:
  1. **Unified command of the common fleet** without a semi-autonomous western squadron (`L1734`);
  2. **Bred nuclear elements returning to the Works from two 80 MW Japanese power reactors** (`C2`, `L1634 ("the bred elements never stay on its soil")`);
  3. **A rice-secure Japan inside the Concord pool**, removing the hunger argument for seizing Manchurian land (`CR-081`, `L1720 ("oil from Pacifica and the Gulf Republic")`) and keeping Japanese yards and smelters inside pool price and standards discipline rather than competing outside it;
  4. **A credible stake in 1937** that binds Kansai industrialists and Tokyo's treaty faction to renewal while leaving Japan's real right to refuse in 1937 genuinely open (`L1734`).

**A2. Who Speaks at Hayama, Who Ratifies at Thalassia, and the 1937 Quota Trap (`L1602`, `L1604`, `L1651`, `L1734`).**
- **At Hayama (summer 1932, matching `CR-021`, `L1651 ("presides with the common ministers")`):** Hirohito hosts as reigning Tennō while Julian presides with the three common ministers, without national ministers in the room. Japan's common Navy Minister—Julian's appointee under `L1597`, whose own portfolio is on the table—cannot speak for Tokyo's cabinet; he sits in the room as the man whose loyalty both capitals test and doubt. Japan's cabinet settles its instructions in Tokyo and carries them in through the Emperor's side (**Prince Chichibu** between Councils, `L1653 ("Chichibu studies at Carquinez")`, and Hirohito's court at Hayama, `L1734`), while **Chiyo** maintains her personal channel to Takahashi and Empress Nagako (`L1722 ("Chiyo carries Julian")`). Because Inukai's fate after the failed May coup is left undecided in canon (`L1732`), either Inukai or his acting/successor Prime Minister (*Hist.:* Takahashi served as acting Prime Minister 15–26 May 1932) countersigns the summer ministerial appointments under `L1597`.
- **At Thalassia (June–July 1932, `L1602 ("Sixty delegates from each legislature")`):** Under canon's renewal route, sixty delegates from Pacifica's chambers (chosen half by each chamber, `L1604 ("two-thirds Senate consent")`) and sixty from Japan's Diet meet separately at Thalassia, exchanging written messages and voting together without debate after three disagreements, followed by ratification by both crowns (`L1734`). Under Case A (three ministries kept), the renewal stays inside `L1602`'s renewal procedure; under Case B (adding a fourth ministry), changing the 1927 charter's ministerial count forces a fresh Senate treaty-amendment vote.
- **The 1928 Senate safeguards in action (`L1604 ("quota review at renewal")`):** Pacifica's Senate invokes its 1928 consent conditions—quota review at renewal and the **Polytechnic audit reported to the Senate**—to challenge the industrial pool terms and currency relief. Interior county senators (whose predecessors opposed the 65% share in 1927, `L1593 ("Diet votes its quota")`) and the Polytechnic's aluminium and electrical champions fight the mid-1937 pool-entry clause on the floor; Giannini's allies protest granting Japan currency reflation after Pacifican county farms foreclosed in winter 1931–32 (`L1695 ("Takahashi asks the common Finance minister")`); Victoria and the sufficers accept keeping zero new mouths out of the Realm while refusing any dilution of the Realm's own standards or the Senate's audit power.
- **The 65/35 quota and the 1937 executive trap (`L1595`, `L1604`):** Both sides hold the **65/35 common-cost quota** unchanged for 1932–1937 (delegations remain sixty each under `L1602`). Tokyo demands—and wins only in qualified form—a joint Polytechnic and Japanese Statistics Bureau **audited-output review** before 1937. Thalassia's negotiators object sharply because `L1595` entrusts the Concord executive to Pacifica *"because Pacifica pays the larger quota and commands the fleet"*; moving Japan's quota upward in 1937 hands Tokyo fresh legal ammunition to renew its demand for an alternating executive. Pacifica refuses any automatic link between quota share and the executive chair, sharpening the 1937 confrontation rather than settling it.
- **Cabinet's two figures (`L1020 ("two figures beside its price")`):**
  - *People brought into the Realm:* **0** (co-equal crowns, `L1595`).
  - *Mouths fed and by what year:* **0 new Realm mouths**, while securing Japan's **62.2 million residents (1932, `CR-082` MODEL)** against rice shortage through 1937 and beyond 1940–42 (`L1672`). The provisioners read those zeros as a bargain that removes Japan's hunger motive in Manchuria without adding mouths to Pacifica's soil; the sufficers answer that the bargain still costs Pacifica real industrial and monetary leverage while speeding Hokkaido's seizure of Ainu land (`L729`).

**A3. Adjustable Yen Parity at Thalassia and Its Real Costs (`L1562`, `L1589`, `L1693`, `L1695`, `L1720`; `C-1932-ADJUSTABLE-PARITY`).**
Canon already records the monetary climax: because Pacifica's falling prices drag gold-pegged Japanese prices down while rural debts stay fixed (`L1562 ("At gold parity Japan's prices must follow Pacifica's down")`), Japan suffers through winter 1931–32 on gold under common finance (`L1693 ("Japan remains on gold through common finance")`) while the Reserve Bank refuses to print (`L1695`), feeding May's coup manifesto (`L1732`) until Tokyo wins *"the adjustable parity it wins at Thalassia"* (`L1695 ("adjustable parity it wins at Thalassia")`, `L1746 ("adjustable gold-dollar order")`).

- **The instrument (June–July 1932, negotiated with the common Finance minister; Reserve Bank governor John U. Calkins and Russian-department head Kashevarov, `L1607`, advising on gold cover):** Rather than history's unilateral 13 December 1931 gold embargo and chaotic ~60% yen drop (*Hist.*), Takahashi secures an orderly **20–25% downward yen parity reset (`C-1932-ADJUSTABLE-PARITY`, MODEL)** inside a **±5% consultation band** under Common Finance. `C-1927-CONCORD-RESERVES` (`L1597 ("Japan's gold in San Francisco is earmarked")`) stays inviolate: Japan's earmarked gold remains withdrawable without notice, and its San Francisco dollar balances keep 50% gold backing.
- **Seven real costs nobody escapes (PROPOSED):**
  1. *Higher yen cost of the Concord quota:* Because Japan's 35% quota is payable in gold dollars, a 20–25% lower parity raises the Diet's yen defence-and-Concord appropriation by roughly **25–33%**—forcing a bitter Diet battle between rural relief and naval taxes;
  2. *Heavier bank debt service (`C-1927-JP-RESCUE`):* Under `L1589 ("five instalments from 1929 to 1933")`, it is the **rebuilt Japanese banks** (via the stabilization agency, not the sovereign budget directly) that owe the fourth (1932) and fifth (1933) gold-dollar instalments. The reset raises their yen repayment load by a quarter to a third, forcing the stabilization agency and Bank of Japan to extend yen liquidity support so the banks pay San Francisco on schedule without a fresh bank run;
  3. *Dearer pool inputs:* Concord-pool aluminium and oil remain priced at gold parity (`L1691 ("on Arkansas bauxite at gold parity")`), raising yen costs for Japanese shipyards and the Nagoya/Ōta airframe lines;
  4. *Gold-drain ceiling on Bank of Japan reflation:* Unlike history's floating yen, Bank of Japan domestic rural-relief bond underwriting inside a ±5% gold-exchange band will drain Japan's earmarked gold in San Francisco if Takahashi over-issues, imposing a strict monetary ceiling monitored by Common Finance;
  5. *Incomplete rural relief:* Nagano's silk districts get partial relief, not instant prosperity, because the Union's depression has collapsed American raw-silk demand (`L1689 ("Japan's silk districts lose income")`);
  6. *Friction with China (`C-1930-TARIFF-NOTES`):* Because China's 1930 exchange of notes binds Chinese duties on Japanese cotton goods until May 1933 (`L1706`), a 20–25% cheaper yen undercuts Shanghai's Chinese cotton mills—provoking immediate protests from Nanjing and Shanghai mill-owners and ensuring China will refuse any tariff renewal in May 1933;
  7. *Explicit impact note on Julian's 1931 levers (`L1720`):* Making parities adjustable deliberately loosens one of the three levers Julian threatened Tokyo with in 1931 (*"the yen's convertibility through common finance"*). Julian concedes it because rigid gold deflation was handing recruits to Tokyo's Restoration officers, while keeping common-navy transport and nuclear/oil supply leverage firmly in hand.

## B. Closing `CR-054`'s Open Items and the Mid-1937 Pool-Entry Bargain

**B1. Resolving All Four Open Items of `CR-054` (`L85`, `L1560`, `L1600`, `L1742`, `L1877`).**
1. **Form and date (`L85`, `L1877`):** The industrial division of labour is drafted in 1927 alongside the provisional Concord and formally adopted as a **binding annex to the Concord's economic articles at the first Council of the Two Crowns at Hayama in 1928** (`C-1928-HAYAMA-ANNEX`, reconciling `CR-054`'s 1927 note with the 1928 first Council).
2. **Alumina refinery site (`L1560`, `C-1900-GULF-COMPACT`):** Confirmed at **Baton Rouge** inside the Gulf Republic on the lower Mississippi, serving Arkansas bauxite downriver and deepwater shipments to Pacifica's smelters (Mobile Bay is not chosen).
3. **Licensed all-metal aircraft line in Japan (`L1742 ("a licensed line builds them in Japan too")`; `C-1928-JP-AIRCRAFT-LINE`):** Operated from 1928 in **Nagoya and Ōta** (Mitsubishi and Nakajima airframe works, *Hist.* industrial centres) under Pacifica's published open-royalty licence (`L863 ("Concord partners from 1928 under the Hayama annex")`) using Concord-pool Pacifican aluminium sheet, building Concord-standard fighters, carrier/patrol aircraft, and civil transports beside Japan's shipbuilding share below capital ships (`L1600`).
4. **What Japan withholds in return (`L1600`, `L1877`):** Through the second term's end in mid-1937, Japan withholds domestic primary aluminium smelting and heavy hydro-turbine/generator manufacture against Pacifica's champions and maintains unified outward single-party discipline in the European nitrogen convention (`L1600 ("European nitrogen convention")`, `C-1930-NITROGEN-CONVENTION`).

**B2. The Mid-1937 Pool-Entry Protocol (`T-H1`, `C-1932-HAYAMA-GRADUATION`, PROPOSED).**
Canon's bar at `L1600` (*"builds no aluminium or heavy electrical industry against Pacifica's before 1937"*) runs only to the end of the second term in **mid-1937** (`L1595`). If Japan refuses renewal in mid-1937, the Concord and the bar lapse together—meaning simply letting the bar expire gives Japanese industrialists nothing they could not already take by walking out.

`C-1932-HAYAMA-GRADUATION` therefore builds a prize that **only third-term renewal unlocks**, taking effect on the **opening day of the third Concord term in mid-1937**:
- **During 1932–1937 (Joint Transition inside the Bar):** Domestic primary aluminium smelting in Japan stays barred through the second term (`L1600` preserved verbatim). Japanese electrochemical firms—notably **Noguchi Shitagau's** Nippon Chisso and **Mori Nobuteru's** Showa group (whose historical predecessor Nihon Iodine / Nihon Denki Kōgyō poured Japan's first domestic aluminium ingots at Ōmachi in January 1934, *Hist.*)—are admitted up to **49% equity in offshore Pacific-basin joint smelting and refining ventures** outside Japan, and Japanese electrical works receive open-royalty sub-assembly licences (`L863`). *(CR-054 already anticipates Bintan and Indies bauxite entering the Concord in the 1930s without touching the Gulf's Baton Rouge Arkansas royalty; nothing here depends on `CR-084` merging first.)*
- **On the opening day of the third term (mid-1937, contingent on renewal):**
  1. Japanese primary aluminium smelters and heavy-electrical works are **admitted directly into the Concord Pool at the pool's inside price (`L1600`)**, receiving a negotiated quota of common-fleet and Concord-grid procurement orders alongside pool alumina contracts;
  2. Japanese 49% stakes in the 1932–37 offshore joint smelting ventures **convert into majority Japanese-managed plants** inside the pool.
- **What walking out in mid-1937 costs Japan—and what staying costs Pacifica's champions:** Walking out frees Tokyo from the bar, but leaves Japan's fledgling smelters **outside the pool**, stripped of pool alumina, fleet orders, and tariff discipline, forced to compete head-to-head against Pacifica's low-cost 150,000-tonne Columbia/Sierra hydropower giant (`L1560`). Inside Pacifica, the aluminium and electrical champions fight `C-1932-HAYAMA-GRADUATION` bitterly because admitting Japanese smelters to fleet and grid orders surrenders part of their protected Concord order book; Julian backs it to keep Japanese capital inside the pool rather than driving it outside.

## C. The Tokyo Atomic Station (1927–28 Financing), Nishina's Bond, and the Kansai Stake

**C1. Closing `OPEN`: How the 80 MW Tokyo Atomic Station Is Paid For (`L75`, `L1634`, `L1867`; `C-1928-TOKYO-STATION-FINANCING`, `C-1931-TOKYO-FUEL-LEASE`).**
Because ground is broken in **1928** (`L1634 ("station is opened on 29 April 1932")`), financing is settled in **late 1927–early 1928** (`C-1928-TOKYO-STATION-FINANCING`, PROPOSED), in the immediate wake of Japan's 1927 banking panic (`L1589`)—when Japanese banks have no spare gold dollars for an upfront nuclear purchase:
1. **Japanese domestic yen capital (`TOKYO-DENTO`):** **Tokyo Electric Light Company (*Tokyo Dentō*)** and a Japanese utility bond syndicate finance and own the inland rock site (above tsunami reach, built to Polytechnic earthquake code, `L1634`), civil containment, cooling works, steam turbines, alternators, and grid ties in **domestic yen**, with zero upfront gold drain.
2. **Works nuclear island and 20-year fuel-and-core lease (`C-1931-TOKYO-FUEL-LEASE`, 1931–1951):** Pacifica's Light and Matter Works supplies the reactor pile, purified graphite, control gear, and leased uranium charge, recovering its outlay through a **20-year per-kilowatt-hour fuel-and-core lease** beginning at first power in 1931.
3. **The 1937 exit bite:** The fuel-and-core lease is expressly conditioned on **continued Concord membership**. If Japan refuses renewal in mid-1937 (`L1734`), Pacifica's obligation to deliver fresh fuel cores terminates, while all irradiated fuel remains bound to mandatory return to the Works (`L1634 ("the bred elements never stay on its soil")`)—keeping the real dependence canon describes acute in 1937, even a decade before the second record opens in autumn 1947 (`L1612`).

**C2. The Concord Atomic Power Board, Nishina's Divided Duty, and the Kansai 80 MW Station (`T-A1`, `C-1932-ATOMIC-POWER-BOARD`, PROPOSED).**
- **Authority inside Pacifica:** Because the Works' rules sit in the Works' charter and Polytechnic statutes rather than in a treaty (`CR-050`), Pacifican consent to the Board comes from **Langmuir's seat and the Council of the Seal** (`L1643 ("Council of the Seal")`), not from Hayama overriding the Works.
- **No audit inside the 1947 seal (`L1612`):** The Board never sees uranium-mining, enrichment, or fabrication costs inside the walled Works. Instead, it publishes an **external benchmark tariff formula** indexed strictly to **Tokyo's published delivered thermal-coal generation cost** minus an agreed Concord discount—answering Japanese Restoration charges of arbitrary fuel pricing without touching a single secret behind the 1947 seal (`T-A1`).
- **Nishina Yoshio's divided duty (`CT-NISHINA-YOSHIO`):** Co-chairing the Board for Japan beside a Works engineer of Eugene Wigner's reactor-safety school (`L1637 ("Wigner becomes the master of the reactors")`), Nishina has re-signed his 20-year bond to autumn 1947 (`L1612`). He sits as Tokyo's trusted civilian lead while bound by oath never to tell his own government what lies inside the walled college or what Pacifica recovers from Japan's returned fuel.
- **The Kansai 80 MW Station (`KANSAI-UTILITIES`) — a high-stakes lever for both sides:** The Board authorizes a **second 80 MW civilian atomic station for Osaka–Kobe** for the 1932–1937 term, financed on the same split model (Kansai electric-utility syndicate owns civil works and turbines in yen; Works leases the nuclear island and fuel under the Works' radiation-safety and Polytechnic earthquake codes, `L1634`, `L1869`).
  - *For Japan:* Kansai's industrialists gain cheap, coal-free power—and because the station will stand **half-built in mid-1937** on a Concord-conditioned fuel lease, walking out in 1937 means stranding Kansai's capital in a dead shell.
  - *For Pacifica:* Every Japanese station returns its spent fuel to Pacifica (`L1634`), so **two 80 MW Japanese power reactors breed elements that go back into the Works' hands** while drawing on the Realm's existing 4,600-tonne uranium reserve (`L1646 ("lie in reserve in 1932")`).

## D. The Partners' Deferred Dossiers: Rice, Hokkaido, Warnings, Cable, Korea, and Kamchatka

**D1. Japan's Rice Crop (1910, 1920, 1932), Hokkaido Early, and the Ainu Land Cost (`CR-082` `R-6`, `L729`, `L735`, `L1541`, `L1706`).**
`CR-082` (`L1885`) asks the partners' CR to raise Japan's domestic rice **crop** above history's by **+5% in 1910** (~2.5 million koku above 50.1M), **+8% in 1920** (~4.6 million koku above 57.5M), and **+13% in 1932** (~7.8 million koku above 60.3M, keeping Japan within ~1% of its 68.4M koku requirement for 62.2M residents without annexed colonies), and to carry the point that **no 1918 Rice Riots erupt from shortage**.

Those three dates require mechanisms that actually exist in each decade:
1. **1891–1910 (+5% crop, ~2.5 million koku):**
   - *Pre-1910 nitrogen and bean cake:* Long before Pacifica's 1915 Columbia ammonia or the 1928 Hayama Annex's reservation of Japan's fertilizer market, **Chilean nitrate** carried on Pacifica's 1891 Iquique contracts (`CR-082` §D) and **Manchurian soybean cake** moving at open rates (`CR-081`) raise Honshū and Tōhoku paddy yields from the 1890s.
   - *Hokkaido's advisory turn and early breeding (`C-1893-HOKKAIDO-RICE`, PROPOSED):* Canon already links Pacifican advisers to Kuroda's Hokkaido (`L735 ("Kuroda Kiyotaka's Hokkaido development brings Pacifican advisers north")`) and ties Sapporo's agronomists to Sitka (`L729 ("Sapporo's agronomists and Sitka's assayers trade their methods")`). Historically, Hokkaido's development agency opposed rice on American advisers' counsel until opening a test station in 1893, after pioneer **Nakayama Kyūzō** proved *Akage* rice near Kitahiroshima in 1873 (*Hist.*), and Hokkaido local breeding started only in 1915 (*Hist.*). In canon, Pacifica's **1890 food table (`L1020`)** reverses its Hokkaido advisers' skepticism by **1893**: Hokkaido's test station and Sapporo Agricultural College (acting head **Satō Shōsuke**, 1856–1939, agricultural economist and later first president of Hokkaido Imperial University 1918–30, *Hist.*, succeeding Zusho Hirotake) partner with Pacifica's stations to use **Hawaiʻi winter nurseries**—halving `CR-082`'s 12–15 year breeding cycle to 6–8 years. Pure-line *Bōzu* selections and cross-bred Hokkaido cultivars equivalent to *Hashiri-bōzu* and *Fukoku* (*Hist.*) therefore reach Hokkaido and Tōhoku fields **from 1901–1903 onward** (not before 1901). Hokkaido paddy expands early to roughly **100,000–130,000 chō (~1.5–1.8 million koku by 1910, MODEL)**, with Tōhoku selection, Chilean nitrate, and Manchurian bean cake supplying the remaining ~0.7–1.0 million koku to clear +5%.
   - *The Ainu land cost (`L729`):* Pushing paddy across the Ishikari and Kamikawa plains a generation ahead of history accelerates Japanese settlement on **Ainu land**—deepening the exact quarrel Sitka's assayers already name at `L729` (*"Sapporo settles its soldiers on Ainu land without asking"*). Pacifica's sufficers point to the contradiction between the Realm's own compact-land rule (`L445 ("no people taken by decree")`) and subsidizing Hokkaido's expansion on unceded Ainu ground.
2. **1910–1920 (+8% crop, ~4.6 million koku; no 1918 Rice Riots from shortage):**
   - **Noguchi Shitagau** (1873–1944, *Hist.*) founds Nippon Chisso (*Nichitsu*) in **1908** (*Hist.*) making calcium cyanamide and ammonium sulfate on mountain hydropower (purchasing the **Casale ammonia process in 1921**, *Hist.*), joined from 1915 onward by Japanese firms buying Pacifican hydro-turbines (`L1560`) and open-royalty ammonia technology (`L863`). Together with Hokkaido paddy (~2.5–3.0 million koku by 1920, MODEL) and Manchurian bean cake, Japan reaches +8% by 1920—preventing physical rice shortage in **July–September 1918** (so no 1918 Rice Riots from shortage topple Tokyo's cabinet).
3. **1920–1932 (+13% crop, ~7.8 million koku — and reconciling Formosa and Korea):**
   - **Mori Nobuteru** (1884–1941, *Hist.*) founds **Showa Fertiliser** in **October 1928**, producing ammonium sulfate by **1931** (*Hist.*) on Pacifican hydro-turbines (`L1560`). Because Korea is never annexed (`L1196 ("Korea remains an independent guaranteed state")`), Japan never launches history's post-1920 colonial Korean rice-requisition drive (*Hist.*) that crushed Japanese farm prices and caused the 1920s stagnation identified by Hayami and Ruttan (*Hist.*). Keeping Japan's ~1.38%/year Meiji growth pace lifts the 1932 domestic crop to ~68.1 million koku (with Hokkaido contributing ~3.5–4.0 million koku, MODEL)—within ~1% of canon Japan's 68.4 million koku requirement (`CR-082`).
   - *Reconciling `L1541` (Formosa) and `L1706` (Korea):* With Japan's home crop covering baseline table rice in normal years, **Formosa's long contracts (`L1541`)** carry primarily **sugar** plus specialty and reserve rice, while **Korea's commercial rice (`L1706`)** is far smaller than history's colonial drain—filling Japanese urban, sake-brewing, and cold-summer buffer demand (critical in chill years like 1931) while Korean merchants sell remaining surpluses into North China and Manchuria. **Korean landlords and Seoul's treasury still feel the narrower Japanese market**, adding a real economic grievance to Korean–Japanese relations.
4. **Pacific Kelp and Kombu Research Compact (`C-1932-KELP-RESEARCH`, `L1672`, PROPOSED):** In July 1932, Hokkaido Imperial University and Hokkaido's *kombu* growers sign a joint marine-agronomy compact with Pacifica's marine stations (building on mechanical kelp-harvesting lessons from Hercules Powder's wartime San Diego potash-and-acetone plant, *Hist.*), launching `CR-082` Work 5 without altering 1932 diet tables.

**D2. The 1897 Warnings Compact: Typhoon Warnings Northward, Tsunami Warnings Outward (`C-1897-WARNINGS`, `L1131`, `L1631`, `L1659`).**
Typhoons form in the western Pacific and strike **Guam, Manila, and the Visayas first** before recurving north toward **Japan**, while **Japan and Kamchatka** sit on the Pacific trench that sends tsunamis across the ocean toward Hawaiʻi:
- **Typhoons (Realm and Philippines → Japan):** The **Manila Observatory (`MANILA-OBS`, *Hist.:* founded 1865 by Jesuits; first recorded Far East typhoon warning by Father Federico Faura on 7 July 1879; official decree 1884; directed from 1897 by Father José Algué, inventor of the barocyclonometer)** anchors the Realm's western typhoon network alongside Guam and Chichijima (`C-1892-TOKYO-CABLE`), delivering day-ahead warnings to Guam, Manila, and the Visayas (`L1131`) and wiring storm tracks **northward** to Japan. From 1923, **Okada Takematsu** (1874–1956, *Hist.*, head of Japan's **Central Meteorological Observatory**, `CMO-JP`) combines Manila and ship wireless reports with the **1931 Tokyo electronic calculator** (`L1631 ("one at Tokyo")`) to compute typhoon landfall tracks for **Japan's coast**.

**D3. Manila's Direct Submarine Wire to Shanghai (1906; `C-1880-HK-MANILA-CABLE`, `C-1906-MANILA-SHANGHAI-CABLE`, PROPOSED).**
