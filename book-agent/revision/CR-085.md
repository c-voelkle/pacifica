## A. May 1932: the Turn, and a Renewal Without Term

**A1. The winter's warning (1931–32; PROPOSED).**
In 1931 Julian stopped the Manchurian plot by threatening three things Tokyo could not easily replace: "oil from Pacifica and the Gulf Republic, transport by the common navy and the yen's convertibility through common finance" (`L1720 ("oil from Pacifica and the Gulf Republic")`). In the winter that follows, the sufficers in the Senate's aliʻi seats count what is left of them. Their reason for counting is their own doctrine: a Realm that means to buy its bread abroad must not also depend on a partner who can walk away. The Batavia talks are offering Japanese houses oil of their own in East Borneo (`CR-084`; the Compact is not initialled until June). Takahashi, refused dollars that winter, is asking for an adjustable yen (`A7`). If both are granted only the transports remain. Their warning to the cabinet is that a Japan which needs neither Pacifica's oil nor its gold, and which "may refuse in 1937" (`L1734 ("Japan may refuse in 1937")`), will refuse. Julian has no answer that winter except that a partner held only by levers is not a partner. May gives the answer.

**A2. The Kansai station: decided in the winter, announced on 29 April 1932 (PROPOSED).**
The Tokyo station "is opened on 29 April 1932, the Emperor's birthday" (`L1634 ("station is opened on 29 April 1932")`). A second station for Osaka and Kobe is Julian's to give, as Tokyo was his to announce at the demonstration of 1927, and he decides it in the winter with the consent of Langmuir's seat and the Council of the Seal (`C2`).
- **Who objects, and loses.** **Eugene Wigner** (`CT-EUGENE-WIGNER`) holds that a second station in an earthquake country should wait until the first has run five years. He loses on the decision and wins one thing: Kansai's pile is not loaded until Tokyo's has a full year's operating record.
- **The announcement.** At Tokyo's opening the Emperor's household and Julian announce the second station, by radiotelephone, on the link Thalassia has had to Tokyo since 1928 (`L1625 ("from 1928 Thalassia telephones Tokyo")`). The world sees what the atom gives a partner two weeks before it sees whether the partnership holds. The Restoration officers see it too: a second station tied to a lease from Thalassia is, to them, a second chain.
- Kansai is therefore no part of what is traded at Hayama. The Council only charters the Board that will build it.

**A3. May 1932: the coup fails, and Japan proposes a renewal without term (PROPOSED).**
The officers strike "to stop the Concord's first renewal", and "Emperor Shōwa and the navy stand against them, and the coup fails" (`L1732 ("merchant houses that share in its champions")`). Canon leaves Inukai's fate open, and so does this draft; whoever heads the government countersigns what follows, as `L1597` requires.
- **The dates (PROPOSED).** The coup is on 15 May (*Hist.* for the day; canon gives the month). The courts martial open within the week. The court's proposal reaches Thalassia by 25 May. Julian sails at the end of May and the Council sits at Hayama from 5 to 8 June. Canon's proposal that Vance is killed in May by headquarters guards (`L1736 ("Vance dies in May")`, PROPOSED and open) would fall in these weeks; if it stands, Julian leaves Thalassia days after burying his foster brother, and the draft does not decide it.
- **Three institutions of Japan carry the renewal.**
  - *The sovereign.* The Emperor's stand in May is his own. He is not asked by Thalassia, and Julian sends no ship.
  - *The navy.* Two navies stand against the officers. The home naval establishment in Tokyo is Japan's own. The Japanese units of the western squadron belong to the common Navy, and Kiyoshi's son holds them loyal (`L1732`). The naval party that is about to lose its reserved squadron is the party that saves the Concord, and its claim to the Navy seat rests on that (`A4`).
  - *The Diet.* In June it votes its quota (`L1593 ("Diet votes its quota")`) and chooses its sixty delegates. It does not do so in silence: a deputy says from the floor that a Concord without term is the bakufu made permanent, and is voted down (PROPOSED).
- **Who makes the offer.** The officers had a date to strike at, the renewal of 1932, and their successors would have the next one. **Saionji**, who counselled acceptance in 1927 (`L1782 ("Saionji counsels acceptance in 1927")`), advises the Emperor that the Concord should give its enemies no more dates. Takahashi and the navy minister of Japan's own cabinet agree. The Privy Council advises ratification on two conditions: the home army stays national, and every term of the union except its existence is open at each review. On that advice Japan proposes that the renewal of 1932 carry no term.
- **What the offer is.** Japan's court buys security against its own army and pays for it in sovereignty: it gives up the right to leave. The draft says so plainly because the Restoration officers will.
- **Who carries it.** **Prince Chichibu**, the Emperor's representative at Thalassia between Councils (`L1651 ("presides with the common ministers")`, `L1653 ("Chichibu studies at Carquinez")`), receives it by cipher cable and takes it to Julian in person. **Chiyo**, who in 1931 carried Julian's threat to Takahashi (`L1722 ("Chiyo carries Julian")`), carries Julian's answer and Takahashi's terms by her own letters in the household's bag; the voice link is assumed to be overheard and is not used.
- The common Navy minister does not speak for Japan. He is Julian's appointee (`L1597 ("Foreign Affairs, the common Navy")`), his own portfolio is on the table, and at Hayama he is the man in the room whose loyalty both capitals test.

**A4. The Hayama Council, 5–8 June 1932: what is agreed, and who loses (dates PROPOSED).**
"The crowns agree the renewal at Hayama" (`L1734`). Hirohito receives as host; Julian presides with the three common ministers, without the national ministers (`L1651`). The Pacificans who object do so before Julian sails, in the cabinet and the Senate; none of them is in the room at Hayama.

**What Japan is refused.** The alternating executive: "Japan's demand for an alternating executive is refused" (`L1734`). It is refused in 1932; Japan may raise it again at a review. Fortified piers, and any base south of 10° S (`CR-084`). A fourth common ministry (`T-M4`, Case B).

**What Julian is refused.** He asks that the home army come under common command in peace, since it was the home army's officers who struck in 1931 and in May. Japan refuses, on the Privy Council's first condition, and "the home army stays national except in a Concord war" (`L1734`). The court's reason is that the army is to be mastered by Japan, as it was in May, or it has not been mastered.

**What Japan pays.** Its overseas commands pass to common control and the reserved squadron ends, as canon has it (`L1734`). Beyond canon, Japan gives up the right to refuse in 1937. Set against the table, Japan gives more than it gets, and it offers before it is asked. That is the turn.

**Why Julian accepts.** One command of the common fleet; the bred elements of two Japanese stations returning to the Works (`L1634 ("the bred elements never stay on its soil")`); a Japan that grows its own rice and has no hunger argument for Manchuria; Japanese industry inside the pool's price and standards. The fifth reason outweighs them. A Concord that is about to guarantee the world's bread and that holds the world's only atomic weapons cannot be five years from dissolving when it signs.

**What else the Council agrees.** The Concord's position on the British and Dutch treaties and the 10° S protocol (`CR-084`), its guarantee of Caledonia (`CR-083`), and what it will bring to the two articles (`A5`).

**A5. Japan's place in the two articles (PROPOSED; the text of both articles is `CR-086`'s).**
- **Article I, the renunciation.** Japan is the first power to live under the rule the article makes general. It has the atom's power by lease, its stations' fuel "is leased from the Works and returned to Pacifica" (`L1634`), and it builds no separating plant, by the terms of the fuel lease and of the Board's charter (`C2`). Canon's word for this is "an instrument of dependence". Japan is therefore not asked to renounce: it has already given what Article I asks. It is not told what the Works holds. Nishina's bond runs to 1947 (`L1612 ("sealed for twenty years from 1927")`), and only the Emperor knows more (`L1634`).
- **The pledge.** Custody of the devices is stated in `CR-086` `T-5`: they are no part of the common Navy's establishment, and the common fleet carries one only on an order under the archive's two keys. Japan countersigns the Realm's pledge as the partner whose fleet would be ordered to carry. The promise is real: a Japanese Navy minister undertakes that the common fleet will not carry the weapon against a signatory.
- **Article II, the food order: Japan is co-guarantor.** The Realm and Japan undertake together, as the Concord, what `CR-086` `D1` sets out. Japan can stand behind it because its own rice crop keeps pace with its need (`D1`), so it signs as a guarantor and not as a claimant. It brings its merchant marine, its share of the common Navy that keeps the lanes, and the contracts it already holds (`L1541 ("sugar and rice go to Japan on long contracts")`, `L1706 ("Korea's rice goes to Japan by contract")`).
- **What it costs Japan.** The pledge binds Japan as well as the Realm. Japan may embargo no signatory in peace, China included, and the army's argument of 1931, that Manchuria's crop should be taken as land, is closed by Japan's own ratified word. The army ministry objects in Tokyo and loses to the court, the navy and Takahashi.
- **In the Realm.** The provisioners object to making the rule law, and the sufficers make it their condition; that argument is `CR-086` `D5`.

**A6. The Senate's vote, ratification and the first review (`L1602`, `L1604`).**
- **The Senate (July 1932).** In 1928 the Senate consented on conditions: "a five-year first term instead of ten, quota review at renewal, Pacifica's delegates chosen half by each chamber and a Polytechnic audit reported to the Senate" (`L1604 ("quota review at renewal")`). Striking the term removes one of them, so it goes back to the Senate as an amendment of those conditions and needs two-thirds, as in 1928 (`CR-084` `T-S1` states the rule). The other conditions are kept and attach to each five-year review. Tate argues that 1927 is being repeated. It is not: in 1927 Julian imposed the Concord against a refusal, and in 1932 he asks. Tate loses the vote. The aliʻi seats make the two-thirds and name their price in `CR-086` `D5`.
- **Victoria.** She cured the illegality of 1927 on these conditions and is alive in Hawaii, ninety-three or ninety-four. Tate's men ask her to speak against striking the term. She declines, and lets it be known through the aliʻi seats that her condition in 1928 answered an unlawful act and that a lawful vote may remove it (PROPOSED).
- **The delegations (July 1932, `L1602 ("Sixty delegates from each legislature")`):** sixty delegates from Pacifica's chambers, chosen half by each chamber, and sixty from the Diet meet separately at Thalassia, exchange written messages and, after three disagreements, vote together without debate. Both crowns then ratify (`L1734`). Three ministries are kept, so the rest of the renewal stays inside this route. Interior senators and the Polytechnic's champions fight pool entry, and Giannini's allies the yen reset (`L1695 ("Takahashi asks the common Finance minister")`), in the choice and instruction of the Senate's thirty delegates.
- **The quota:** 65/35 is kept to the first review (`L1595 ("joins co-equal crowns")`). Tokyo wins a joint audit of both economies' output by the Polytechnic and Japan's Statistics Bureau before 1937. Thalassia refuses any automatic link between quota and the executive chair, since `L1595` entrusts the executive to Pacifica "because Pacifica pays the larger quota and commands the fleet". The argument returns at the first review; the union does not depend on its outcome.
- **The Final Act.** The renewal without term is annexed to the Final Act of 1 August (`CR-086`).
- **The cabinet's two figures (`L1020 ("two figures beside its price")`):** people brought into the Realm, **0**. Mouths fed, **0** new in the Realm; Japan's 62.2 million residents of 1932 (`CR-082`, MODEL) grow their own rice. **A7. Adjustable Yen Parity at Thalassia and Its Real Costs (`L1562`, `L1589`, `L1693`, `L1695`, `L1720`; `C-1932-ADJUSTABLE-PARITY`).**
Canon already records the monetary climax: because Pacifica's falling prices drag gold-pegged Japanese prices down while rural debts stay fixed (`L1562 ("At gold parity Japan's prices must follow Pacifica's down")`), Japan suffers through winter 1931–32 on gold under common finance (`L1693 ("Japan remains on gold through common finance")`) while the Reserve Bank refuses to print (`L1695`), feeding May's coup manifesto (`L1732`) until Tokyo wins *"the adjustable parity it wins at Thalassia"* (`L1695 ("adjustable parity it wins at Thalassia")`, the conference's adjustable gold-dollar order, which canon states for the Union's currency).

- **The instrument (June–July 1932, negotiated with the common Finance minister; Reserve Bank governor John U. Calkins and Russian-department head Kashevarov, `L1607`, advising on gold cover):** Rather than history's unilateral 13 December 1931 gold embargo and chaotic ~60% yen drop (*Hist.*), Takahashi secures an orderly **20–25% downward yen parity reset (`C-1932-ADJUSTABLE-PARITY`; the range is the drafter's estimate, PROPOSED, until the workbook sizes it)** inside a **±5% consultation band** (PROPOSED) under Common Finance. `C-1927-CONCORD-RESERVES` (`L1597 ("Japan's gold in San Francisco is earmarked")`) stays inviolate: Japan's earmarked gold remains withdrawable without notice, and its San Francisco dollar balances keep 50% gold backing.
- **Seven real costs nobody escapes (PROPOSED):**
  1. *Higher yen cost of the Concord quota:* If Japan's 35% quota is payable in gold dollars (PROPOSED; canon does not say in what money it is paid), a 20–25% lower parity raises the Diet's yen defence-and-Concord appropriation by roughly **25–33%**—forcing a bitter Diet battle between rural relief and naval taxes;
  2. *Heavier bank debt service (`C-1927-JP-RESCUE`):* Under `L1589 ("five instalments from 1929 to 1933")`, it is the **rebuilt Japanese banks** (via the stabilization agency, not the sovereign budget directly) that owe the fourth (1932) and fifth (1933) gold-dollar instalments. The reset raises their yen repayment load by a quarter to a third, forcing the stabilization agency and Bank of Japan to extend yen liquidity support so the banks pay San Francisco on schedule without a fresh bank run;
  3. *Dearer pool inputs:* Concord-pool aluminium and oil remain priced at gold parity (`L1691 ("on Arkansas bauxite at gold parity")`), raising yen costs for Japanese shipyards and the Nagoya/Ōta airframe lines;
  4. *Gold-drain ceiling on Bank of Japan reflation:* Unlike history's floating yen, Bank of Japan domestic rural-relief bond underwriting inside a ±5% gold-exchange band will drain Japan's earmarked gold in San Francisco if Takahashi over-issues, imposing a strict monetary ceiling monitored by Common Finance;
  5. *Incomplete rural relief:* Nagano's silk districts get partial relief, not instant prosperity, because the Union's depression has collapsed American raw-silk demand (`L1689 ("Japan's silk districts lose income")`);
  6. *Friction with China (`C-1930-TARIFF-NOTES`):* Because China's 1930 exchange of notes binds Chinese duties on Japanese cotton goods until May 1933 (`L1706`), a 20–25% cheaper yen undercuts Shanghai's Chinese cotton mills—provoking immediate protests from Nanjing and Shanghai mill-owners and making it likely in 1932 that China will not renew the notes in May 1933 (canon says only that no renewal is promised);
  7. *Explicit impact note on Julian's 1931 levers (`L1720`):* Making parities adjustable deliberately loosens one of the three levers Julian threatened Tokyo with in 1931 (*"the yen's convertibility through common finance"*). With the oil lever spent knowingly in `CR-084` `A2a`, only the common Navy's transports and the fuel lease remain. Julian concedes it because rigid gold deflation was handing recruits to Tokyo's Restoration officers.
  8. *Costs in the Realm:* Japanese cloth and consumer goods become a fifth cheaper in the Realm's and the Concord's markets, Pacifican machinery a quarter dearer in Japan, and the Union and Britain have a precedent weeks before the conference.
- **The yen and the conference's rule.** `CR-086` `B1` states the conference's rule for every member: it names its parity and consults before changing it. The yen's band is the Concord's stricter case of that rule, and `C-1932-ADJUSTABLE-PARITY` is the only parity instrument between the two crowns.

## B. Closing `CR-054`'s Open Items and the Pool-Entry Protocol

**B1. Resolving All Four Open Items of `CR-054` (`L85`, `L1560`, `L1600`, `L1742`, `L1877`).**
1. **Form and date (`L85`, `L1877`):** The industrial division of labour is drafted in 1927 alongside the provisional Concord and formally adopted as a **binding annex to the Concord's economic articles at the first Council of the Two Crowns at Hayama in 1928** (`C-1928-HAYAMA-ANNEX`, reconciling `CR-054`'s 1927 note with the 1928 first Council).
2. **Alumina refinery site (`L1560`, `C-1900-GULF-COMPACT`):** Confirmed at **Baton Rouge** inside the Gulf Republic on the lower Mississippi, serving Arkansas bauxite downriver and deepwater shipments to Pacifica's smelters (Mobile Bay is not chosen).
3. **Licensed all-metal aircraft line in Japan (`L1742 ("a licensed line builds them in Japan too")`; `C-1928-JP-AIRCRAFT-LINE`):** Operated from 1928 in **Nagoya and Ōta** (Mitsubishi and Nakajima airframe works, *Hist.* industrial centres) under Pacifica's published open-royalty licence (`L863 ("Concord partners from 1928 under the Hayama annex")`) using Concord-pool Pacifican aluminium sheet, building Concord-standard fighters, carrier/patrol aircraft, and civil transports beside Japan's shipbuilding share below capital ships (`L1600`).
4. **What Japan withholds in return (`L1600`, `L1877`):** To the first review in mid-1937, Japan withholds domestic primary aluminium smelting and heavy hydro-turbine/generator manufacture against Pacifica's champions and maintains unified outward single-party discipline in the European nitrogen convention (`L1600 ("European nitrogen convention")`, `C-1930-NITROGEN-CONVENTION`).

**B2. The Pool-Entry Protocol (`T-H1`, `C-1932-HAYAMA-GRADUATION`, PROPOSED).**
Canon's bar (`L1600`: Japan "builds no aluminium or heavy electrical industry against Pacifica's before 1937") was written for a Concord with a second term ending in 1937. The bar lapses in 1937 whatever is agreed, so lifting it is no gift. Under `T-R3`, Case B, there is also no renewal on which a gift could hang. What the protocol gives is the pool's inside price and a share of the Concord's orders on a fixed day.
- **1932 to the first review (mid-1937):** primary aluminium smelting in Japan stays barred, as `L1600` says. ## C. The Tokyo Atomic Station (1927–28 Financing), Nishina's Bond, and the Kansai Stake

**C1. Closing `OPEN`: How the 80 MW Tokyo Atomic Station Is Paid For (`L75`, `L1634`, `L1867`; `C-1928-TOKYO-STATION-FINANCING`, `C-1931-TOKYO-FUEL-LEASE`).**
Because ground is broken in **1928** (`L1634 ("station is opened on 29 April 1932")`), financing is settled in **late 1927–early 1928** (`C-1928-TOKYO-STATION-FINANCING`, PROPOSED), in the immediate wake of Japan's 1927 banking panic (`L1589`)—when Japanese banks have no spare gold dollars for an upfront nuclear purchase:
1. **Japanese domestic yen capital (`TOKYO-DENTO`):** **Tokyo Electric Light Company (*Tokyo Dentō*)** and a Japanese utility bond syndicate finance and own the inland rock site (above tsunami reach, built to Polytechnic earthquake code, `L1634`), civil containment, cooling works, steam turbines, alternators, and grid ties in **domestic yen**, with zero upfront gold drain.
2. **Works nuclear island and 20-year fuel-and-core lease (`C-1931-TOKYO-FUEL-LEASE`, 1931–1951):** Pacifica's Light and Matter Works supplies the reactor pile, purified graphite, control gear, and leased uranium charge, recovering its outlay through a **20-year per-kilowatt-hour fuel-and-core lease** beginning at first power in 1931.
3. **What the lease is conditioned on:** the fuel-and-core lease runs only while Japan is a member of the Concord. If the union were ever dissolved, the Works' duty to deliver fresh cores would end, and all irradiated fuel would still return to the Works (`L1634 ("the bred elements never stay on its soil")`). Under a renewal without term this is a description of the dependence canon names and no longer a cliff in 1937. It stays in force after the second record opens in autumn 1947 (`L1612`).

**C2. The Concord Atomic Power Board, Nishina's Divided Duty, and the Kansai 80 MW Station (`T-A1`, `C-1932-ATOMIC-POWER-BOARD`, PROPOSED).**
- **Authority inside Pacifica:** Because the Works' rules sit in the Works' charter and Polytechnic statutes rather than in a treaty (`CR-050`), Pacifican consent to the Board comes from **Langmuir's seat and the Council of the Seal** (`L1643 ("Council of the Seal")`), not from Hayama overriding the Works.

Instead, it publishes an **external benchmark tariff formula** indexed strictly to **Tokyo's published delivered thermal-coal generation cost** minus an agreed Concord discount—answering Japanese Restoration charges of arbitrary fuel pricing without touching a single secret behind the 1947 seal (`T-A1`).
- **Nishina Yoshio's divided duty (`CT-NISHINA-YOSHIO`):** Co-chairing the Board for Japan beside a Works engineer of Eugene Wigner's reactor-safety school (`L1637 ("Wigner becomes the master of the reactors")`), Nishina has re-signed his 20-year bond to autumn 1947 (`L1612`). ## D. The Partners' Deferred Dossiers: Rice, Hokkaido, Warnings, Cable, Korea, and Kamchatka

**D1. Japan's Rice Crop (1910, 1920, 1932), Hokkaido Early, and the Ainu Land Cost (`CR-082` `R-6`, `L729`, `L735`, `L1541`, `L1706`).**
`CR-082` (`L1885`) asks the partners' CR to raise Japan's domestic rice **crop** above history's by **+5% in 1910** (~2.5 million koku above 50.1M), **+8% in 1920** (~4.6 million koku above 57.5M), and **+13% in 1932** (~7.8 million koku above 60.3M, keeping Japan within ~1% of its 68.4M koku requirement for 62.2M residents without annexed colonies), and to carry the point that **no 1918 Rice Riots erupt from shortage**.

Those three dates require mechanisms that actually exist in each decade:
1. **1891–1910 (+5% crop, ~2.5 million koku):**
   - *Pre-1910 nitrogen and bean cake:* Long before Pacifica's 1915 Columbia ammonia or the 1928 Hayama Annex's reservation of Japan's fertilizer market, **Chilean nitrate** carried on Pacifica's 1891 Iquique contracts (`CR-082` §D) and **Manchurian soybean cake** moving at open rates (`CR-081`) raise crops from the 1890s: the bean cake and fish meal go on the paddy, and the nitrate goes on upland crops and mulberry, since nitrate is lost in flooded fields. What carries the cake to Japan before the rate rules of the 1920s is ordinary trade through the treaty ports.
   - *Hokkaido's advisory turn and early breeding (`C-1893-HOKKAIDO-RICE`, PROPOSED):* Canon already links Pacifican advisers to Kuroda's Hokkaido (`L735 ("Kuroda Kiyotaka's Hokkaido development brings Pacifican advisers north")`) and ties Sapporo's agronomists to Sitka (`L729 ("Sapporo's agronomists and Sitka's assayers trade their methods")`). Historically, Hokkaido's development agency opposed rice on American advisers' counsel until opening a test station in 1893, after pioneer **Nakayama Kyūzō** proved *Akage* rice near Kitahiroshima in 1873 (*Hist.*), and Hokkaido local breeding started only in 1915 (*Hist.*). In canon, Pacifica's **1890 food table (`L1020`)** reverses its Hokkaido advisers' skepticism by **1893**: Hokkaido's test station and Sapporo Agricultural College (acting head **Satō Shōsuke**, 1856–1939, agricultural economist and later first president of Hokkaido Imperial University 1918–30, *Hist.*, succeeding Zusho Hirotake) partner with Pacifica's stations to use **Hawaiʻi winter nurseries**—halving `CR-082`'s 12–15 year breeding cycle to 6–8 years. Pure-line *Bōzu* selections and cross-bred Hokkaido cultivars equivalent to *Hashiri-bōzu* and *Fukoku* (*Hist.*) therefore reach Hokkaido and Tōhoku fields **from 1901–1903 onward** (not before 1901). Hokkaido paddy expands early to roughly **100,000–130,000 chō (~1.5–1.8 million koku by 1910, MODEL)**, with Tōhoku selection and bean cake supplying the rest. Only Hokkaido's excess over its historical crop counts toward the +5%, because history's figure already contains Hokkaido's historical crop; the split between Hokkaido and the rest is **OPEN** until the series is in `population/provisioning.py`. Canon says only that Pacifican advisers went north; that they first opposed rice, as Capron did in history, is new here and PROPOSED.
   - *The Ainu land cost (`L729`):* Pushing paddy across the Ishikari and Kamikawa plains a generation ahead of history accelerates Japanese settlement on **Ainu land**—deepening the exact quarrel Sitka's assayers already name at `L729` (*"Sapporo settles its soldiers on Ainu land without asking"*). Pacifica's sufficers point to the contradiction between the Realm's own compact-land rule (`L445 ("no people taken by decree")`) and subsidizing Hokkaido's expansion on unceded Ainu ground.
2. **1910–1920 (+8% crop, ~4.6 million koku; no 1918 Rice Riots from shortage):**
   - **Noguchi Shitagau** (1873–1944, *Hist.*) founds Nippon Chisso (*Nichitsu*) in **1908** (*Hist.*) making calcium cyanamide and ammonium sulfate on mountain hydropower (purchasing the **Casale ammonia process in 1921**, *Hist.*), joined from 1915 onward by Japanese firms buying Pacifican hydro-turbines (`L1560`) and open-royalty ammonia technology (`L863`). Together with Hokkaido paddy (~2.5–3.0 million koku by 1920, MODEL) and Manchurian bean cake, Japan reaches +8% by 1920—preventing physical rice shortage in **July–September 1918** (so no 1918 Rice Riots from shortage topple Tokyo's cabinet).
3. **1920–1932 (+13% crop, ~7.8 million koku — and reconciling Formosa and Korea):**
   - **Mori Nobuteru** (1884–1941, *Hist.*) founds **Showa Fertiliser** in **October 1928**, producing ammonium sulfate by **1931** (*Hist.*) on Pacifican hydro-turbines (`L1560`). Because Korea is never annexed (`L1196 ("Korea remains an independent guaranteed state")`), Japan never launches history's post-1920 colonial Korean rice-requisition drive (*Hist.*) that crushed Japanese farm prices and caused the 1920s stagnation identified by Hayami and Ruttan (*Hist.*). A crop 13% above history's in 1932 is ~68.1 million koku, which from this draft's crop of 1920 is growth of about 0.8% a year, (with Hokkaido contributing ~3.5–4.0 million koku, MODEL)—within ~1% of canon Japan's 68.4 million koku requirement (`CR-082`).
   - *Reconciling `L1541` (Formosa) and `L1706` (Korea):* With Japan's home crop covering baseline table rice in normal years, **Formosa's long contracts (`L1541`)** carry primarily **sugar** plus specialty and reserve rice, while **Korea's commercial rice (`L1706`)** is far smaller than history's colonial drain—filling Japanese urban, sake-brewing, and cold-summer buffer demand (critical in chill years like 1931) while Korean merchants sell remaining surpluses into North China and Manchuria. **Korean landlords and Seoul's treasury still feel the narrower Japanese market**, adding a real economic grievance to Korean–Japanese relations.
4. **Pacific Kelp and Kombu Research Compact (`C-1932-KELP-RESEARCH`, `L1672`, PROPOSED):** In July 1932, Hokkaido Imperial University and Hokkaido's *kombu* growers sign a joint marine-agronomy compact with Pacifica's marine stations (building on mechanical kelp-harvesting lessons from Hercules Powder's wartime San Diego potash-and-acetone plant, *Hist.*), launching `CR-082` Work 5 without altering 1932 diet tables.

**D2. The 1897 Warnings Compact: Typhoon Warnings Northward, Tsunami Warnings Outward (`C-1897-WARNINGS`, `L1131`, `L1631`, `L1659`).**
Typhoons form in the western Pacific and strike **Guam, Manila, and the Visayas first** before recurving north toward **Japan**, while **Japan and Kamchatka** sit on the Pacific trench that sends tsunamis across the ocean toward Hawaiʻi:
- **Typhoons (Realm and Philippines → Japan):** The **Manila Observatory (`MANILA-OBS`, *Hist.:* founded 1865 by Jesuits; first recorded Far East typhoon warning by Father Federico Faura on 7 July 1879; official decree 1884; directed from 1897 by Father José Algué, inventor of the barocyclonometer)** anchors the Realm's western typhoon network alongside Guam and Chichijima (`C-1892-TOKYO-CABLE`), delivering day-ahead warnings to Guam, Manila, and the Visayas (`L1131`) and wiring storm tracks **northward** to Japan. From 1923, **Okada Takematsu** (1874–1956, *Hist.*, head of Japan's **Central Meteorological Observatory**, `CMO-JP`) combines Manila and ship wireless reports with the **1931 Tokyo electronic calculator** (`L1631 ("one at Tokyo")`) to compute typhoon landfall tracks for **Japan's coast**.
- **Earthquakes and tsunamis (Japan and Kamchatka → Pacific):** After the **15 June 1896 Sanriku tsunami** struck Hawaiʻi unannounced (`L1131`), **Ōmori Fusakichi** (1868–1923, *Hist.*, Omori's Law and Bosch–Omori seismograph) and `CMO-JP` wire offshore earthquake and tide-gauge warnings **outward** over both `C-1892-TOKYO-CABLE` and `C-1903-KURIL-CABLE` (including Petropavlovsk seismic wires, `L1659`), feeding Honolulu's Volcano Observatory and Hawaii's 1913 Harbour Act (`C-1913-HARBOUR-ACT`).

**D3. Manila's Direct Submarine Wire to Shanghai (1906; `C-1880-HK-MANILA-CABLE`, `C-1906-MANILA-SHANGHAI-CABLE`, PROPOSED).**
Historically, the 1906 Manila–Shanghai cable (*CS Silvertown*, *Hist.*) belonged to the Commercial Pacific Cable Company (50% British Eastern Telegraph, 25% Danish Great Northern, 25% Commercial Cable) precisely because Eastern Extension and Great Northern held exclusive coastal landing concessions in China (*Hist.* 1899–1900 agreements).

In canon, where **Edmund Fong** heads the Chinese Maritime Customs Inspectorate (`L790 ("Hart takes the British ministership")`) and China leans toward Pacifica, the Qing Imperial Telegraph Administration refuses to extend foreign coastal exclusivity against a Chinese-terminating line. Under **`C-1906-MANILA-SHANGHAI-CABLE`** (signed and landed in **1906**), the Realm's telegraph estate and the Philippine administration lay a Carquinez gutta-percha sea cable (`L1131`) from Manila to the **three-mile line off Wusong**, while China's Imperial Telegraph Administration owns and operates the shore end and Shanghai office. Traffic between Manila, Thalassia, and China's treaty ports bypasses British Hong Kong without carving a new foreign cable enclave out of Shanghai, while Britain's 1880 Bolinao cable stays open as a commercial circuit (`L1131`).

**D4. Korea, Kamchatka, and the Deferred Southern Question (`L1196`, `L1674`, `L1885`).**
- **Kamchatka Company (`C-1927-KAMCHATKA-CO`, remains PROPOSED):** Annotates a 20-year renewable charter (1927–1947, PROPOSED) with the consent of the Republic of Kamchatka (`KAM`), one-third Pacifican equity, directly managed paid crews (`CR-031-R1`), and Tokyo–Seoul–Thalassia oversight of Korean contract workers (`L1674`).
- **The southern question (`L1885 ("Japan's yields, Hokkaido and any southern renunciation")`):** settled by `CR-084`, which merges first. Japan signs no renunciation of the south; it has leased piers and concessions under the Batavia Compact, unfortified, and no base south of 10° S. This CR recites that and decides none of it.
