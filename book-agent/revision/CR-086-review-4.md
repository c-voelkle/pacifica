## Verdict

One more revision. The two articles, the checks of `C5`, France's reservation and Szilárd's dissent are sound and agree with canon and, in the main, with the siblings. What does not yet hold is the mechanics of the last month: Article II has two authors and two dates, the signatures and ratifications of 1 August contradict the image the author chose for Julian, and several approval labels say different things in the body, the tension and the decision table.

## Blockers

**B1. Article II is proposed by the cabinet in the winter and also forced on the cabinet by the sufficers in July.**
- *Passage.* Calendar, line 281: "Winter 1931–32 | Julian's cabinet proposes the two articles; the Council of the Seal consents; Japan consents in principle". Line 286: Hayama agrees "the Concord's position on the articles" on 5–8 June. Against these, `D5` line 258: "The eight aliʻi seats and their allies make Article II their condition for all three", "It is Victoria's school that writes the limit into her son's triumph"; line 259: "The provisioners in the cabinet would keep the rule as a practice and not as law"; line 223: "This is the text as the Senate passes it. The sufficers' first draft said…"; and line 76: "The fights, refusals and prices happen before the conference… Thalassia seals what is already won."
- *Problem.* If Julian's cabinet proposed Article II in the winter, Japan agreed it in June and the conference negotiated it with a dozen powers, the sufficers cannot extort it in July, and a cabinet that proposed it cannot be the party that "would keep the rule as a practice". Julian is himself a provisioner in canon (`L1020`, `L1672`), so the reader cannot tell whether he wants the article or is made to take it. The text is also "narrowed" after a "first draft" at an unstated date, yet Japan's Privy Council advises in late June (`E1`, line 294) and the fourth undertaking is one Mead fights in what reads as July. A multilateral text cannot be reworded by one Senate in the month it is signed. `CR-084` `B5` has only "the article" (the first) settled in the winter, and `CR-084` `A3` makes Article II the sufficers' fifth condition; the siblings therefore support the July version, not the calendar's.
- *Fix.* Choose one history and state it once. The stronger one for the story: the winter proposal is Article I only (correct line 281 and `C2`'s "the two articles" echo in the calendar); the cabinet intends the food rule as a declaration or practice; the sufficers state their condition in the winter or spring, when they also warn on Batavia (`CR-085` `A1`) and object to the Caledonian guarantee in May (`CR-083`), so that the text with its four undertakings exists before Hayama, Mead is heard before Hayama, and July is only the vote. Then line 76 is true, and `CR-085` `A5` and the Privy Council have a settled text to consent to. Say in one sentence what Julian himself wanted.

**B2. On 1 August Julian signs "one paper", yet both crowns ratify the Act that day, and nobody signs for the Realm's own undertakings or for Japan's crown.**
- *Passage.* `E3`, line 313: "signs the declaration of custody; it is his only signature that day (`T-9`)". Calendar, line 290: "both crowns ratify it that day for the Concord". Line 294: "The common Foreign minister signs on 1 August, and both crowns ratify the same day". Ledger, line 382: the pledge is given by `PAC`, "countersigned by `JP` as an act of the Concord"; line 383: Article II "with `PAC` as guarantor". `CR-085` `A5`: "Japan's crown countersigns the Realm's pledge".
- *Problem.* A crown's ratification is the sovereign's act and signature; Julian cannot ratify the Final Act for the Concord on the day and also have signed only the custody declaration. Case C of `T-9` (he ratifies and hands the Act to Chichibu) was the option the author did not choose, and the calendar still carries it. The pledge and the guarantee are undertakings of the Realm, not of the Concord, and the draft names no one who signs them for the Realm; the common Foreign minister is a Concord officer. Japan's crown is to countersign the pledge and ratify the same day, but canon has "Hirohito does not travel to Pacifica" (`L1734`), and `E1`'s "Who signs" names no one for Japan. The decided image (the pledge read, one signature) is therefore not yet consistent with the instrument around it.
- *Fix.* Either move the crowns' ratification of the Act off the day (instruments of ratification prepared on the delegations' vote in July and exchanged on 1 August by the ministers, as `L1734` already describes for the renewal: "authorized ministers sign and exchange its instruments at Thalassia, where they are promulgated"), or say that the custody declaration is the instrument that carries the pledge, so that Julian's one signature is the Realm's signature of its own undertaking. Name who holds Japan's full powers in the hall (Chichibu is the natural choice; this draft must say so, and `CR-085` `A5` must agree). Correct line 298, "the Concord has ratified it", to match.

**B3. The same items carry APPROVED in the decision table and PROPOSED in the body.**
- *Passage.* `F-5` (line 522): "The ore sweep and its refusals (`T-U2` Case B) | APPROVED"; but `A2`'s heading (line 86) is "(PROPOSED)" and the PROPOSED list, item 2 (line 533), lists "Urgeiriça's dumps… the single Radium Hill purchase; the refusals in Cornwall, Saxony and Madagascar". `F-8` (line 525): the dual track APPROVED; but `B6`'s heading (line 132) reads "`T-C1`; PROPOSED" without distinction. `F-2` (line 516): "carried free, nothing paid" APPROVED under item 86-3; but `C6` (line 209) marks "charges nothing and pays nothing (PROPOSED)". `C2a`'s heading (line 160) reads "APPROVED, items 86-4 and 86-5" over a section that contains the annexed declaration read by every signatory and "Who decides the pledge", which item 12 of the PROPOSED list (line 543) gives as the drafter's.
- *Problem.* `AGENTS.md` requires the APPROVED, PROPOSED, OPEN and MODEL distinctions to be preserved, and the storyline's local labels are copied from these on integration. As written an integrator cannot tell which label a sentence gets.
- *Fix.* Make each heading carry the label of its row in `## Decisions`, split where the row splits (the siblings do this). For `F-5`, quote the question the author answered at 23:21 and label only the events he approved; remove them from the PROPOSED list. For `C2a`, label the custody rule APPROVED and the annexing, the reading by signatories and the route of decision PROPOSED.

## Should fix

**S1. Line 76 claims more than the draft delivers.** "The fights, refusals and prices happen before the conference, each with a loser who is named." Several happen at the conference or in July: France's reservation and the "price list" exchange (`C4`), the German delegate (`C5`), Britain's Board of Trade (`D4`), the Senate's vote and Mead (`D5`). Several losers are not named: "the provisioners in the cabinet" (line 259), the "French delegate", the "German delegate", the "common Foreign minister" who answers both. After B1 is fixed, either date these earlier or soften the sentence to what is true: the decisions of the powers are taken before, and the conference records objections that are already lost.

**S2. How the pledge ends is not in the article.** `C1` (line 149) gives only "a signatory that keeps the article". The rule that the pledge "ends only on the court's finding" is in the header (line 4), in `C5` (line 202: "It cannot declare the pledge lapsed; only the court can (`C2a`)") and in the ledger (line 382), but `C2a`, which `C5` cites, does not state it; it says only that King "obtains the limiting words". Put the sentence in `C1`. State also whether the pledge holds in war: Article II is "in peace", the pledge has no such words, and King's paper is the place where a reader expects the question.

**S3. The third undertaking of Article II is worded three ways and collides with what the Act schedules.** `D1` (line 221): "neither food, its means, nor credit for them conditional on any concession"; the header (line 4): "against making food or credit conditional"; the author's question as quoted (line 27): "neither food, its means nor credit is made conditional". Credit at large and credit for food are different promises, and the whole of Part B is credit and debt relief given for a concession. "In peace" governs all four undertakings in `D1`, only the embargo in the ledger line (383), and `T-8` says only that the words are "kept". The draft should also say what a "concession" is, since ordinary security and price are conditions too. Finally, the schedule lists instruments that pair food with political terms: New Zealand's crop guarantee is carried by a defence treaty "with the guarantee that no hostile base stands on the dominions' soil" (`L1752`), Australia's "waits until after 1932" on Ottawa, and the Gulf's compact stands beside its admission tests. Say that the undertaking is prospective, or that it does not reach instruments already made.

**S4. "Any signatory may buy at the prices the Realm's own houses pay" (`D1`, `D4` line 254) does not mean anything a grain house could honour.** A house's buying price is not a price at which it sells, and read literally the sentence abolishes the houses' margin. If it means access to the same sellers on the same published terms, say that. It is the only thing Britain gets from Article II ("that sentence and nothing more"), so it has to be exact.

**S5. Japan's consent to the text.** Line 294: "Japan's ratification follows the Privy Council's advice of late June (`CR-085` `A3`)". In `CR-085` `A3` the Privy Council advises on the renewal laid before it, on two conditions; nothing there says it had the two articles before it. `CR-085` `A5` counts Japan's cost under Article II as "Japan may embargo no signatory in peace" and does not mention the fourth undertaking decided at 23:52, which binds Japan's credit and carriage toward China and Korea as well. One sentence here and one in `CR-085` are needed.

**S6. The pledge's vote in the Senate has no story.** `E1` makes the pledge one of five two-thirds votes, and `D5` makes Article II the sufficers' condition for three others. Nothing says who votes against the pledge, whether King's argument is made in the chamber, or what the senators are told of the weapon they pledge (line 158 covers only the third clause). The General Staff's provisioners oppose in `CR-084` `A3`; they are the natural opposition here. Note also that `CR-084` puts three instruments (the Malaya counter-signature, the Gulf countersignature, `C-1932-ANZ-DEFENSE`) under what this draft counts as one of the "five".

**S7. The common Foreign minister has no name.** He hands Simon the draft in April (`CR-084` `B5`), delivers the two best lines of the conference (lines 192 and 204) and signs the book's last act. The draft lists the name as OPEN. It should be settled, or the author asked, before integration; `CR-084` leaves open in the same way "the minister Julian names" for the Realm's own counter-signatures.

## Literary effect

The ending lands as a design. The pairing in line 76 is the strongest idea in the suite, `D2`'s use of 1919 gives Article II a reason to be believed, and Julian reading the pledge on the day the founder's voice is broadcast is a true close for him. Three things weaken it. First, B1: a triumph the cabinet proposed is a different last chapter from one the Regent's school forced on her son, and the draft tells both. Second, the day itself is thin on the page: the Act is "signed and promulgated", the minister who signs is nameless, Japan's hand is absent, and Victoria's set left on (`G5`) is the only image with feeling in it. Third, the cost. `E4` keeps the instruction: no sentence states the cost or calls it settled, and the Altai, the cylinders, Szilárd's roll and France carry it. But commentary inside the draft does name the irony ("The cabinet's aim is reached in the act of renouncing its abuse, and no one says so in the chamber", line 261; "lying with true sentences" made law, `C7`). That is right for a change request and must not be carried into the storyline as narrator's judgment; the impact notes should say so.

## History checked

Checked on the web for this review:
- The Ottawa conference opened on 21 July 1932; Bennett chaired, Baldwin led for Britain, Bruce represented Australia and Coates New Zealand. The page gives the close as 18 August in one place and 20 August in another (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire_Economic_Conference). Simon is not listed among those attending, which fits his being at Thalassia. The draft's "that week" for 1 August holds.
- Urgeiriça: idle from about 1926 to 1929; bought privately in August 1929 by two British nationals, who founded the Companhia Portuguesa de Radium. Confirmed in the paper the draft cites (https://literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/History%20of%20the%20Evaluation%20and%20Exploitation%20of%20a%20Group%20of%20Small%20Uranium%20Mines%20in%20Portugal.pdf), which dates first working to 1912; the Wikipedia page says 1913 and has neither the idle years nor the sale.
- Radium Hill: a second phase of mining by the Radium and Rare Earth Treatment Company ran from 1923 to 1931, and both its sites had stopped by 1932 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radium_Hill). So in 1926–27 it was a working mine, not only dumps; the purchase is plausible, and "closed to the Works since 1927" is the draft's invention and should stay PROPOSED.
- Pani: born 1878, died 1955; Treasury Secretary in 1932. The page consulted gives no day (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_J._Pani), so "26 February" in `## Sources` is unverified.
