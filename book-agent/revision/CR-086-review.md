Fresh-context review of CR-086, revision 1, 23 November 2026. The reviewer read the draft, AGENTS.md and current canon, and not the writer's notes or earlier reviews.

Line numbers `L…` are lines of `book-agent/revision/master_storyline.md` as it stands on `main`. The three unmerged drafts (CR-083, CR-084, CR-085) were read only to test the standalone-merge claim.

## Verdict

Not ready. The draft contradicts approved canon in at least six places (Guaymas, the Crown's granary, Hoover's statement of 1926, Europe's graphite error and the 1947 seal, what foreigners know of the stockpile, Britain's own entry into the monetary settlement), and it gives the atomic convention no Pacifican decision-maker at all. It is a list of fourteen instruments with signers; the refusals it advertises are mostly refusals of things Pacifica never asked for, so they cost nobody anything. It adds eight to ten more signatures to a June–July 1932 that canon and the three other drafts have already filled. The uranium and thorium part (A1, A2) is the nearest to usable and could be made a sound, smaller CR on its own.

## Blockers

**B1. Guaymas: the draft "opens" what canon has had since 1867 and does not deliver what canon is waiting for.**
- *Passage:* C1; ledger `C-1867-GUAYMAS` ("Activated in 1932"), `C-1932-GUAYMAS-PROTOCOL`; set piece 3.
- *Problem:* The draft reads L1681 ("Guaymas, the Mexican free port with a Pacifican railway, waits on Mexico") as if the port and railway were dormant. In canon they are not. L687: "A Guaymas railway concession and free port go with it" (1867, PROPOSED). L1342: the Sonorans' "state runs on Pacifican copper, the Guaymas railway and the Sonora customs" (by 1913, APPROVED). L1024: "In the 1920s a casino resort on the Sonoran shore at Guaymas, under Mexico's law". L1681 stands in the paragraph on chartered districts, and what waits is the district: CR-057.md line 258 defines it as "a Mexican charter for an industrial district beside the free port of 1867; timing OPEN until the Mexico–Pacifica timeline is settled". The draft's protocol creates "a bonded free-port transit terminal linking Pacifica's Arizona–Sonora railway to the Gulf of California", which already exists, and has Mexico refuse "any extraterritorial Pacifican port enclave", which no chartered district in canon is (Huludao, Birmingham and Houston are chartered by their own governments, L1681). So the item neither closes CR-057's open date nor adds anything.
- *Fix:* Decide which it is. Either (a) Mexico charters the industrial district in 1932 by its own act, on Mexico's terms (Mexican courts, customs and labour law; what Mexico gets: smelting and canning wages, port bonds; what Pacifica gets: Sonoran copper smelted beside its railway), amend `C-1920-CHARTERED-DISTRICTS` and add a district commitment, leaving `C-1867-GUAYMAS` alone; or (b) Mexico still declines and Guaymas goes on waiting, in which case drop C1. Add L687, L1024 and L1342 to the impact notes either way.

**B2. The Crown's granary buys Argentine wheat and maize, against L1695 and L892, in a year when the Realm is foreclosing its own wheat farmers.**
- *Passage:* C2; C3 (rice options); ledger `C-1932-GRANARY-CONTRACTS` cross-links; opening paragraph of Part C.
- *Problem:* L1695: the granary "buys the home counties' wheat and feed first, at the slump's price, and takes ten-year contracts abroad for beans and rice, which the Realm's fields do not grow in the quantities the Office's tables ask". Wheat, maize and linseed from the Pampas are not beans or rice, and "Farms are foreclosed and county banks fail that winter" (same line). Giannini, who "answers in the Senate that the money should go to the mortgages", would not let Argentine wheat pass in silence; the draft gives him no line. The granary is the Patrimony's investment (ledger: parties `ESTATES`, `COUNTIES`), and L892 says the Patrimony invests abroad "never in a government in default or in treaty negotiation with Pacifica". The draft has "Buenos Aires" sign with the granary, and has Siam's rice options ride on a protocol under negotiation with Bangkok. Economically the item is also unexplained: L1672 has the margin closing "about 1940–42" (the draft writes "about 1941–43"), and in 1932 the Realm sells wheat. What does it do with Argentine grain in 1932?
- *Fix:* Take the granary out of C2. Under CR-082 P-4 ("The Realm buys there by contract through its own houses, never through Chile") the buyers are private houses, and the canon-consistent reason to be on the Bolsa in 1932 is the cabinet's aim at L1672, "to be indispensable at every step of the world's grain trade": Pacifican houses as merchants, carriers, lenders and nitrogen sellers of Argentina's crop to third markets, not as importers into the Realm. Say that, and say who objects at home. Keep the granary to beans and rice from sellers who are not governments and not in negotiation. Correct "1941–43" to canon's "1940–42".

**B3. Tier 2 of the convention would hand Europe what canon keeps from it, and nobody in Pacifica is shown deciding it.**
- *Passage:* A3, Tier 2; `C-1932-ATOMIC-CONVENTION`; tension T-N1; set piece 1.
- *Problem:* L1614: "Europe's graphite, made without Pacifica's purification, absorbs too many neutrons and blocks the other road, and Pacifica does not correct the error". L1640 lists "graphite from petroleum coke tested batch by batch" and beryllium among the bottlenecks and says "Exports of these goods are restricted as the commercial property of a state champion". L1612 seals to 1947 "the chemistry of the bred elements". A Works power core installed in any "non-enriching state" puts purified graphite, the reactor's proportions and its control gear on foreign soil under foreign engineers; it corrects Europe's error by delivery. The Tokyo station is not a precedent for strangers: Japan is the co-party of the Concord, Nishina is bonded and "Japanese engineers trained by Nishina's group" run it (L1634). The convention's stated purpose ("multiplying Pacifica's own pool of bred elements") would, if it appears in any published text or delegate's speech, tell Europe that irradiated fuel is valuable, when L1614 says that by 1932 "the element such piles breed is an idea in Europe, with no production pile, no chemistry and no weapon route". The offer is also premature: the first Pacifican stations are "begun in 1929", "two are loaded in 1932" (L1634). T-N1 presents the choice as convention against no convention and never mentions this cost. Last, the rules of the programme live in "the Works' charter and the Polytechnic estate's statutes, no ordinance or law" (AGENTS.md, CR-050), under "a Council of the Seal" with the director in the chair (L1362). An international convention with foreign inspection is a new kind of instrument for the Works. The draft names no director (Langmuir), no Council, no Julian, no Senate, and no successor to the Chief who "retires after the demonstration" (L1612). An arbitration court (L1381, PROPOSED, terms unstated in the ledger) is given an inspectorate without comment.
- *Fix:* Three workable shapes, in order of preference. (1) Confine fuel leasing in 1932 to the Concord and treaty allies with bonded operators, which is what CR-051.md line 733 called "the Concord's atomic terms", and state openly that leasing to third states is argued inside the Council of the Seal (Szilárd for it as the only lasting dependence, Wigner and Langmuir against it because of the graphite) and refused or deferred beyond 1932. That is a scene and a cost. (2) If the author wants the offer made, present it as a canon tension against L1614 and L1640 with its price stated: Europe learns the graphite. (3) Drop Tier 2 and let the dependence strategy be the Concord's terms plus ore and heavy water. In every case name who consents and keep the "bred elements" purpose in the cabinet minute, off the published page.

**B4. Foreign delegates cite a stockpile figure that is the Works' own reckoning, and the draft turns "material for" into "devices".**
- *Passage:* A3 ("pointing out that Pacifica already holds material for roughly fifteen devices (`L1640`)"); set piece 1 ("Pacifica keeps its fifteen devices behind the 1947 seal").
- *Problem:* L1640 is a MODEL sentence in the narrator's voice: "by 1932 the Works has made material for some fifteen devices in all". Nothing in canon publishes it; the production figures are the heart of what is sealed. Britain, Germany, France and the Union cannot "point it out". And unresolved item 29 (L1867) keeps open "how many devices are assembled after 1927". The set piece's "fifteen devices" decides it in passing.
- *Fix:* Let the delegates argue from what they do know: three devices admitted or inferred in 1927, five years of published power tables, a second record sealed to 1947. Write "an arsenal whose size it will not state". Remove the number from the set piece.

**B5. The secondary ore sweep contradicts L1580 and L1617 and is not believable after 1927.**
- *Passage:* A1 items 2 and 3; calendar rows 1919–1927 and 1926–1929; `C-1925-URGEIRICA-ORE`, `C-1926-RADIUM-HILL-ORE`; tension T-U2.
- *Problem:* (a) L1580, about 1926: "the Chief tells the keys that with Joachimsthal, Shinkolobwe and Fergana in hand no one will build a device for twenty years. Szilárd answers five, once they know it works, and Hoover, who has found ore on four continents and knows of it in Saxony, Cornwall and Portugal, backs him". Hoover names Portugal as ore outside the Works' hand; that is why he backs Szilárd. A long-term Urgeiriça contract dated 1925 makes his sentence false. The draft cites L1580 as support. (b) L1617: in 1927 "London, needing uranium, asks [Ottawa] to set aside the Works' Great Bear contract for imperial priority". A London that asks Ottawa will ask Lisbon, its oldest ally, and Adelaide. The draft has Britain lock Cornwall and Travancore and then leaves Portugal selling to the Works "through 1932" and South Australia likewise (the ledger entry runs 1926–1932, while the text says "until Australian export scrutiny tightens after 1928"). History points the other way: Urgeiriça stood idle from 1926 to 1929 and was bought in August 1929 by two British subjects, who founded the Companhia Portuguesa de Radium (see History checked). (c) Radium Hill's whole output before 1932 was about 350 milligrams of radium bromide and 150 kilograms of uranium. "50–100 tonnes of uranium" from "davidite concentrates and dormant tailings" is several hundred times that and would need a new mine and mill on British soil, in two years. (d) "Pacifica's commercial front" at Urgeiriça is not canon's way: purchases are made "under the Works' name" as "a ruthless trust in the salt" (L1262), "negotiated by the Works through the estates" (L1503). A front that hides the buyer sits badly beside "never a false paper"; at the least it needs the same sentence canon uses each time, that nothing false is stated. (e) The arithmetic: 5,800 + (200–350) + (80–150) + (50–100) is 6,130–6,400, not 6,150–6,400; the reserve is 4,930–5,200, not 4,950–5,200.
- *Fix:* Build the sweep around what the canon lines already imply. Great Bear carries the increase (it is canon and only its volumes are open). Portugal: the Works moves after Hoover's remark, in 1926–27, buys the idle mine's dumps and a short contract, and loses the mine to British buyers in 1929; that is a refusal with a date and a cost, and it matches history. Radium Hill: one purchase of dumps and stock in 1926–27, a few tonnes at most, closed by the Commonwealth after the demonstration. Cornwall, Saxony and Madagascar as drafted. Recompute the totals. Say in one sentence what the sweep buys, in the terms of L1503 ("The ore is time and leverage, not a guarantee"): how many months of British delay, set against the "tens of tonnes" in Cornwall that the draft itself concedes are enough to feed a first British device.

**B6. The monetary section omits Britain's terms, and Canada's "trap" depends on them.**
- *Passage:* B1, B3; tension T-M1; `C-1932-CA-MONETARY-ASSOC`; set piece 2.
- *Problem:* L1748: "Germany, Britain and France enter the monetary settlement". The draft gives France a reservation and Germany a haircut and says nothing of Britain: at what parity sterling enters, what happens to "its debt to Pacifica [which] keeps its gold-dollar value" (L1687), or how entry squares with the Ottawa preference of L1752. B3 then rests on Canada being "caught between Britain's September 1931 gold exit … and San Francisco's gold dollar". If Britain enters the adjustable order in the same weeks, the gap Canada is supposed to straddle is being closed at the same table, and Canada needs no instrument of its own. Further: canon's list of who comes to Thalassia (L1748) has no Canada; "Canada remains British" (L1752) is PROPOSED text that this would qualify; "Canadian borrowers' gold-dollar bond service" is an unanchored premise (canon has Pacifica financing the Canadian Pacific "through London", AGENTS.md CR-058); Canada had no central bank before 1935 and had stopped redeeming its notes in gold in January 1929, so "clinging to pre-1931 gold parity" is not its historical position; and set piece 2 puts Bennett in person at Thalassia in the week before he chairs a conference in Ottawa that opens on 21 July.
- *Fix:* Add a paragraph on Britain to B1 or state plainly that Britain's terms are left to CR-084 and mark B1 incomplete without it. Then either drop B3 (recommended; see The PROPOSED items) or reduce it to something Ottawa asks for and can get without a treaty: a Treasury-to-Treasury credit to carry Canadian gold-dollar coupons, signed by a finance minister or a delegate, with Bennett in Ottawa. If kept, show where the Canadian gold-dollar debt comes from.

Julian, Victoria, Langmuir, the Council of the Seal, the common Foreign and Finance ministers, Calkins, the Senate, Giannini and the two schools do not appear. *Fix:* for each instrument say who proposed it, who signed for whom (the Concord's common minister or the Realm's; CR-084's draft has a table for this and it is the right model), and who at home objected.

The stabilization facilities have no lender.** B1 backs the "clearing band" with "San Francisco stabilization facilities" months after "The Bank's board … refuses to issue beyond its cover" and "Farms are foreclosed" (L1695). **S5. China's protocol lacks China's motive and misdescribes two things.** L1714: "Silver is the money of Pacifican loans for work in China, so silver's fall is Pacifica's loss". The Shanghai bankers have just taken "a cut in their bonds' service" (L1711); the draft could use that. *Fix:* state the conversion rate and who bears the loss, give Soong a gain (a reserve credit, the customs surtax freed, the works share), make Japan a party or an objector, and consider dating it earlier (see S13).

**S6. Shinkolobwe: the wrong line, the wrong counterparty and an unclear concession.** The 1919 terms are at L1379 ("The Comité Spécial transfers the Shinkolobwe concession out of Union Minière into a new Belgian-registered company three-quarters owned by the Light and Matter Works … with all its output going to Pacifica. Belgium keeps sovereignty, royalties, seats on the board and a free allowance … Belgium understands what it signed in 1927"), which the draft never cites; it cites L1359, the 1915 find. "An expanded … civilian-power quota" is undefined: if it is uranium, it breaches "all its output going to Pacifica" and the draft's own "exclusively"; if it is leased power, it is Tier 2 by another name. Belgium "understands … in 1927" and then waits five years; it is not at Thalassia in canon. *Fix:* cite L1379, make Belgium the party, date the demand 1928–29, define the quota, and size the royalty as a cost.

**S7. Tier 1's test-notice article "mirrors" a notice Pacifica did not give.** L1575: in May 1924 the Works "tests its first device in secret"; the public sentence comes "The same morning", and "The measurements of what the winds carry north-east go into the sealed record". ## The PROPOSED items

**G-1, the uranium and thorium sweep. Keep, changed.** This is what canon asked for (L1262, L1646, L1867, L1869, L1881) and the draft's instinct, that 1927 turns sellers into refusers, is right. Change as in B5: Great Bear as the main addition; Portugal bought late and lost to British buyers in 1929; Radium Hill a single small purchase; totals recomputed. Sub-items:
- Great Bear's company, route and volumes: keep as PROPOSED. 200–350 t over about twenty years is modest for the deposit. Check the 1910s railhead. Reconcile with CR-083's fifty-year renewal if both merge.
- Cornwall, Saxony, Madagascar refused: keep. Correct "Weimar".
- Shinkolobwe revision: keep the idea, since it is the one place Pacifica pays; change per S6.
- Brazilian monazite: keep. It fits canon's pattern (uranium bought "for glazes, glass and alloy steel", L1259; heavy water "as research chemicals", L1643) provided the cerium is in fact used and the text says nothing false is stated. Make the buyer the Works through the estates, or say why private houses. The tonnage is unverified by the draft's own admission.
- Travancore restricted "on London's quiet advice": keep as PROPOSED, fix the state's status, and note that thorium's value is not public in 1928, so London's motive needs a sentence (suspicion of anything the Works buys is enough).

**G-2, the convention. Change heavily.** Tier 1 can stay as a short safety and test-notice convention if it is dated honestly (1927 as its model, not 1924), if someone in Pacifica is shown wanting it (it gives Thalassia notice of Britain's first test, which is its real value and should be said in cabinet), and if Britain is shown weighing that. The "dependence strategy" row should then say honestly that outside the Concord dependence in 1932 rests on ore, heavy water and the restricted goods, and that leasing abroad is argued and deferred.

**G-3, the monetary order, the court, China, Canada. Split into four.**

- China: change (S5) and consider 1930–31 with ratification at Thalassia. The dual track is a good idea and matches the stakes canon gives the Shanghai bankers.

## Standalone merge and the four drafts together

**What CR-086 rests on.** Technically nothing: no passage needs a fact from an unmerged draft, and the one explicit link (Caledonia's monetary articles) is conditional. In substance it leans on two of them. Its "global settlement" has no British terms, which CR-084's draft supplies (the retrenchment treaty and the annuities), and no yen, though L1695 says "Tokyo's answer is the adjustable parity it wins at Thalassia", which CR-085's draft supplies. Merged alone, CR-086 would give canon a monetary section that details France, Germany, China and Canada and is silent on the two powers canon names first.

**Duplicates.**
- `CT-RB-BENNETT`: added by CR-083 and CR-086 with the same aliases. Whichever merges second must drop its `add_person`; CR-086 notes the identity but not the consequence.
- `C-1912-GREATBEAR-OFFTAKE`: CR-083 renews it for fifty years inside a Trilateral Treaty; CR-086 fills in company, route and volumes. Compatible, but one ledger entry must carry both, and CR-086's Canada section never mentions a renewal signed at the same conference by the same prime minister.
- `C-1932-THALASSIA`: annotated by CR-084, CR-085 and CR-086, each with different additions.
- `C-1927-CONCORD-RESERVES`: CR-085 "confirmed intact"; CR-086 "extended" to China. The second wording is wrong on its own terms (S5).
- The adjustable parity: CR-085 creates `C-1932-ADJUSTABLE-PARITY` with a ±5% consultation band and a 20–25% yen reset; CR-086 describes an "adjustable gold-dollar clearing band … requiring consultation" with no figures. One mechanism, two names.
- Atomic institutions: CR-085 charters a Concord Atomic Power Board at Hayama in May (safety codes, a cost-plus lease formula, a second Japanese station); CR-086 puts safety standards and fuel-lease inspection under the arbitration court in June–July. Two bodies for the same lease in one summer, and Japan gets a published price where third states get none. Between them they divide L1871's open item without either saying so.

**Contradictions.**
- Canada: in CR-083 Bennett at Thalassia accepts the loss of a Pacific coast, a machinery quota and a fifty-year uranium renewal, and "does not go home triumphant"; in CR-086, the same weeks, he also puts the Canadian dollar in San Francisco's band. Together that is no longer "Canada remains British" in any sense a reader would recognise, and neither draft shows the sum.
- Britain: CR-084 has Britain bargaining annuities for retrenchment; CR-086 has it lock Cornwall and Travancore and sign a test-notice article. Neither mentions the other's subject, though uranium and the debt are two of canon's three prongs (L1612).
- Authority: CR-084 distinguishes Concord treaties (common Foreign Minister, Hayama, the delegations) from Realm acts (both chambers). CR-086 does not, and its ledger entries would not match CR-084's.

**Is the summer feasible.** Canon already puts into 1932: the Union's settlement (recognitions, claims, naval ratio, the court), the monetary settlement with Germany, Britain and France, the German debt, the Concord's renewal instruments, the dominions' defence treaty, the Gulf's oil compact and the Lands and Waters Charter. The three other drafts add, at or around the conference: the Caledonia treaty and a Trilateral Treaty; the British retrenchment treaty, Labuan, Tonga, Fanning, the Batavia Compact initialled and two chambers' votes in July–August; a fourth common ministry, the graduation protocol, the parity agreement, the Atomic Power Board and a kelp compact. CR-086 adds the atomic convention, the court's reservation, China's protocol, Canada's band, Shinkolobwe and, the same summer, Guaymas, with the Pampas and Siam in the autumn. That is some twenty-five instruments in about eight weeks, most signed by one unnamed common Foreign Minister and voted by one Senate. History's nearest conference, Lausanne, sat from 16 June to 9 July 1932, the same weeks, and settled one question; London in 1933 sat six weeks on currencies and settled none. It is not believable as drafted, and it flattens the ending: when everyone signs everything in one place, no signature matters. CR-086 is the easiest of the four to thin, because most of its items have a better date elsewhere (S13). Thalassia should keep from this draft only what must be there: France's reservation, Germany's haircut and, if kept, the court's reservation.

## Literary effect

**What works.** The premise that the demonstration closes the ore market is sound and dramatically useful. Belgium raising its price is the right kind of consequence. Soong signing while warning that Shanghai and Canton will not give up silver is a real scene with two interests in it. The Sahtú barge hand, the silver-shop apprentice and the Bangkok clerk are good choices of viewpoint. France's and Germany's partial entries give Thalassia some texture.

**A list of instruments.** The draft has no protagonist and no opponent. Each section runs mechanism, signer, "hard refusal". The refusals are mostly of things nobody proposed: Argentina refuses "any political association, naval coaling right, or Pacifican arbitration over South Atlantic waters"; Siam refuses "any security treaty, foreign garrison, or base rights"; Mexico refuses an "extraterritorial Pacifican port enclave"; Canada refuses "political association, customs union". CR-082 and canon already say Pacifica wants none of these. A refusal costs something only if the other side wanted the thing. The real refusals (Cornwall, Saxony, Madagascar, Travancore, the separation ban) are stated in a line each and cost Pacifica nothing the draft measures.

**Agency by party.**
- The Works and its chiefs: absent. No director, no Council, no successor to the Chief, no ore-office head. The one debate the material offers (lease reactors abroad or not) is not staged.
- The scientists: Wigner and Szilárd appear only as a citation. Szilárd, who "calls the signposts lying with true sentences" (L1580) and designed the opening, is the natural critic of a convention that asks others for candour.
- Julian, Victoria: neither appears. Victoria is "conscious of … the price of its victory" (L1754); this draft adds nothing to the price she counts and gives her no view of a convention built on her son's sealed record.
- The Senate's two schools: absent. Part C is provisioners' policy from end to end, and no sufficer objects.
- The Reserve Bank, Giannini, the champions: absent, in a draft that lends abroad after the refusal to print.
- Britain: locks two sources, signs Tier 1. Its interest is plain; its choices (buy Urgeiriça, press Canberra, bargain uranium against debt) are not shown.
- France, Germany: act from interest, briefly. Good.
- Belgium: acts from interest; the best foreign party in the draft, once S6 is fixed.
- Portugal, South Australia, Brazil: sellers without a voice.
- The Union under Dawes and Young: restated from canon with an invented narrow vote.
- China: Soong has a constraint but no gain (S5). Wang, who holds the civil budget, and Chiang, who has "the Shanghai bankers" (L1537), are named in the registry list and do nothing.
- Canada under Bennett: signs against his own stated policy for a benefit Britain's entry would give him anyway.
- Mexico: Pani crosses out lines nobody wrote.
- Argentina: the Bolsa scene is good; the government's role is unclear.
- Siam: a date and a clerk.
- Japan: co-party to the Concord, present only to "protest and accept" the dilution of its own clause.

**What Pacifica wants, gets and pays.** The draft's implicit answer is: more ore, a legal frame for leasing, creditor protection in China, footholds with two grain sellers. Its payments are a Shinkolobwe royalty of unstated size and unstated credit lines. The author's concern that Pacifica's own gain be visible is met in A1 and nowhere else stated as a gain; the concern that it be a real bargain is not met, because almost nothing is paid. The moat as drafted is nearly costless and has one honest weak point, Cornwall's "tens of tonnes", which the draft mentions and does not follow: if that is enough for Britain's first device, the sweep buys delay only against later, larger programmes, and the text should say so in the Works' own voice.

**Too convenient.** Everyone the Realm needs is in one building in July (Soong, Bennett, the Belgians), Mexico signs the same summer, and the two grain sellers follow in the autumn, one of them a government four months old. The Siamese revolution falling "in the very weeks the Thalassia Conference sits" is used as a coincidence, not a cause.

**The title.** "Global settlement" is not earned: Britain and Japan are missing from the money, Russia and the Gulf from everything, and the "Rim" is three unrelated contracts. "Moat" in canon is the industry ("After 1927 the lead is defended by the next plant, not the old secret", L1640; CR-051's own title); the draft uses it for ore and a treaty.

**The price of victory.** The draft removes price. The materials for adding to it are in its own subject: a convention that asks the world for notice while the 1924 record stays sealed; gold lent abroad in a foreclosure winter; a clause given to Japan in 1919 and trimmed in 1932; an ore trust that has made itself the reason every chancery now counts tonnes.
