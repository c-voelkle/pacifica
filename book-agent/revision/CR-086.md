## A. The Atomic Moat: Uranium Sweep, Thorium, and the Post-1927 Dependence Strategy (1919–1932)

**A1. Beyond the Four Sources: What Pacifica Secures—and Where Europe Refuses (`L1262`, `L1265`, `L1580`, `L1646`, `L1867`; `T-U2`).**
Canon records **~5,800 tonnes of uranium** mined from 1864 to 1932 across the Western Slope (2,760 t), Shinkolobwe (2,270 t), Joachimsthal (390 t), pre-1909 by-product (300 t), and Fergana (84 t), leaving ~4,600 t in reserve (`L1646`), while leaving Great Bear Lake's volumes uncounted (`L1881 ("Great Bear's year, company, volumes and route")`) and flagging *"Mining as much as Pacifica can get its hands on"* for this CR (`L1646`).

Pacifica's ore office (run under Herbert Hoover until he enters public office in 1925, `L1580 ("knows of it in Saxony, Cornwall and Portugal")`) extends its quiet radium-trust buying model (`L1262`) from 1919 to 1932—**and hits hard sovereign walls once the November 1927 sea demonstration wakes Europe up (`L1612`, `L1614`)**:

- **What Pacifica adds to the 1932 ledger (MODEL, raising total mined uranium to ~6,150–6,400 t and reserve to ~4,950–5,200 t):**
  1. **Great Bear Lake (`C-1912-GREATBEAR-OFFTAKE`, closing `L1881`):** Worked from the 1910s by a Canadian-chartered operator (the **Mackenzie Radium and Silver Company**, PROPOSED) financed by the Works on Crown land under the Sahtú Dene treaty and the Works' ore-handling code (`L1265 ("Délı̨nę does not become a village of widows")`). High-grade Echo Bay pitchblende travels south by summer Mackenzie River barge to Waterways and winter tractor/aircraft freight, contributing roughly **200–350 tonnes of uranium through 1932 (MODEL)**—the exact supply Ottawa refused to divert to London in 1927 (`L1617 ("Ottawa keeps the contract")`).
  2. **Portugal — Urgeiriça and Beira Alta (`C-1925-URGEIRICA-ORE`, PROPOSED):** *Hist.* Urgeiriça was Portugal's premier radium-uranium mine from 1913 (consolidated into *Companhia Portuguesa de Radium* in 1929). Using gold-dollar finance and hospital radium gifts (`L1262`), Pacifica's commercial front takes long-term concentrate and tailings purchase contracts through 1932, securing roughly **80–150 tonnes of uranium (MODEL)** and denying Europe its cleanest western pitchblende source.
  3. **South Australia — Radium Hill and Mount Painter (`C-1926-RADIUM-HILL-ORE`, `SA-RADIUM`, PROPOSED):** *Hist.* Discovered in 1906, Radium Hill was Australia's first radium/uranium mine, worked sporadically into the early 1930s. Pacifica's ore buyers contract for davidite concentrates and dormant tailings before Canberra or London wake to uranium's military value, adding roughly **50–100 tonnes of uranium (MODEL)** until Australian export scrutiny tightens after 1928.
- **Where Pacifica is refused—and what it has to pay to hold what it has:**
  1. **Britain locks down Cornwall (`L1580`):** *Hist.* South Terras near St Stephen-in-Brannel was Britain's only mine extracting radium on-site in the 1920s. Immediately after the 1927 demonstration (`L1614`), Whitehall bars foreign purchase of Cornish pitchblende and tailings dumps (modest, low-grade ore, roughly tens of tonnes recoverable), feeding Britain's own post-1928 separation effort (`L1614 ("first device in five to seven years")`).
  2. **Germany holds Saxony (`L1580`):** Weimar Germany refuses foreign concessions on the Saxon Erzgebirge cobalt-uranium districts (Johanngeorgenstadt and Schneeberg), reserving domestic ore for Europe's heavy-water power piles (`L1614`).
  3. **France holds Madagascar:** Paris keeps Madagascar's uranium-niobium ores (betafite) inside French colonial control.
  4. **Belgium extracts a price at Shinkolobwe (`C-1919-SHINKOLOBWE-COMPANY`, `L1359`):** Once the 1927 sea test reveals why the Works financed Katanga in 1915, Brussels and Union Minière (`UMHK`) demand revision of the 1919 concession on the sidelines of Thalassia in 1932. Rather than risk Belgian nationalization or a London–Brussels breach, Pacifica accepts **higher Belgian Congo royalties and an expanded Belgian hospital-radium and civilian-power quota** (`C-1932-SHINKOLOBWE-REVISION`, PROPOSED) to keep Shinkolobwe's ore flowing exclusively to the Works.

**A2. Thorium: Quiet Monazite Insurance (`L1637`, `L1869`; `C-1928-BRAZIL-MONAZITE`, PROPOSED).**
Wigner and Szilárd's 1925–26 breeder proposal notes that *"one day thorium, can be turned into fuel"* (`L1637 ("one day thorium, can be turned into fuel")`), and canon leaves *"thorium (a later CR)"* open (`L1869 ("thorium (a later CR)")`).
- **Mechanism:** *Hist.* In the 1920s, monazite beach sands rich in thorium—mined mainly in **Brazil (Bahia and Espírito Santo)** and **Travancore (British India)**—had fallen into a quiet commercial slump as electric bulbs replaced Welsbach incandescent gas mantles. Obeying `L1262`'s rule (*buy what nobody values; say nothing false*), Pacifican chemical houses from **1926 to 1932** sign ordinary commercial monazite purchase contracts in **Brazil** (`BR`, `C-1928-BRAZIL-MONAZITE`, PROPOSED) for rare-earth cerium, optical glass, and lighting-alloy uses, accumulating a multi-hundred-tonne thorium-oxide reserve (MODEL) at slump prices.
- **Refusal and technical limit:** Commercial purchases from **Travancore** end after 1928–29 when British India's Travancore administration restricts bulk foreign monazite exports on London's quiet advice. Thorium breeding remains **strictly experimental beyond 1932** (`L1637`); the stockpile is a 1940s insurance reserve, never a 1932 power fuel.

**A3. The Post-1927 Dependence Strategy and the Thalassia Atomic Materials and Safety Convention (`L1640`, `L1643`, `L1871`; `T-N1`, `C-1932-ATOMIC-CONVENTION`).**
Canon marks *"the dependence strategy after 1927 (a separate CR)"* open (`L1871 ("the dependence strategy after 1927")`). Once fission and the 1927 demonstration are public (`L1612`), Pacifica defends its lead on two tracks:
1. **Hard Bottleneck Denials (already canon):** Export restrictions on high-power/high-vacuum valves, Langmuir condensation pumps, petroleum-coke graphite, and beryllium (`L1640 ("Exports of these goods are restricted")`), plus the 1930 Norsk Hydro heavy-water contract (`L1643 ("contracts for Norsk Hydro's heavy water")`) delaying Europe's heavy-water piles without preventing them.
2. **Soft Standard-Setting — The Thalassia Atomic Materials and Safety Convention (`C-1932-ATOMIC-CONVENTION`, signed June–July 1932, PROPOSED; `T-N1`):** Anchored to the renewed Permanent Arbitration Court (`C-1919-ARBITRATION-COURT`, `L1746`), the Convention splits cleanly into **two tiers**:
   - *Tier 1 — Ore Safety, Hospital Salts, and Maritime Test Notice (signed widely, including Britain, France, Germany, the Union, Japan, China):* Codifies the Works' published radiation-medicine standards, mandatory shielding and medical registers for uranium miners (generalizing the Sahtú Dene code of `L1265`), and mandatory advance maritime/air closure notices and fallout reporting for high-energy open-air or ocean tests (mirroring Pacifica's own 1924 Black Rock and 1927 sea-demonstration notices, `L1575`, `L1612`).
   - *Tier 2 — Civilian Fuel-Lease Access (the dependence hook):* Any non-enriching state may contract with Pacifica's Works (and allied utilities) for civilian power-reactor cores on Tokyo's fuel-return model (`L1634 ("the bred elements never stay on its soil")`)—cheap power without spending half a percent of GDP on separation halls—**strictly on condition** that it accepts Arbitration-Court radiation inspection of its reactor hall and returns 100% of irradiated fuel to Pacifica (multiplying Pacifica's own pool of bred elements while removing smaller powers' incentive to build national isotope plants).
   - **What the great powers refuse:** **Britain, Germany, France, and the Union all flatly refuse any treaty cap on national isotope separation or atomic weapons testing**, pointing out that Pacifica already holds material for roughly fifteen devices (`L1640`) and refuses to disarm or open its second record before autumn 1947 (`L1612`). They sign Tier 1 only—keeping Britain's 1933–35 device race (`L1614`) and Europe's heavy-water power piles genuinely alive beyond 1932.

## B. The Thalassia Global Settlement: Monetary Order, Arbitration Court, China, and Canada (June–July 1932)

**B1. The Adjustable Gold-Dollar Order and Renewed Arbitration Court (`C-1932-THALASSIA`, `C-1919-ARBITRATION-COURT`, `L1746–L1750`).**
Canon places the **Thalassia Conference in June–July 1932** (`L1746 ("At the Thalassia Conference in June–July 1932")`), where the Union's gold rescue (`C-1931-UNION-RESCUE`) secures Washington's acceptance of the continental settlement (Union Senate recognition of the Confederacy and Gulf Republic, renunciation of surviving 1865 and 1871 claims, naval ratio below the common fleet and Britain, adjustable gold-dollar order, and a founding seat in the renewed arbitration court, `L1746–L1748`), while Germany, Britain, and France enter the monetary settlement and Japan and China attend (`L1748 ("Germany, Britain and France enter the monetary settlement")`).
- **How the monetary order works (and where Europe balks):** Pacifica's gold dollar remains fixed on full Reserve Bank cover (`L1695 ("refuses to issue beyond its cover")`) as the anchor. Partner currencies join an **adjustable gold-dollar clearing band** backed by San Francisco stabilization facilities (`C-1932-THALASSIA`), requiring consultation before parity adjustments. **France**, sitting on large gold stocks in 1932 (*Hist.*), refuses to place the Bank of France under San Francisco clearing rules and signs only a narrow bilateral gold-clearing protocol. **Germany** enters the monetary order and settles Union bank claims (`C-1932-GERMAN-DEBT`, `L1748`) only by forcing Wall Street lenders to absorb a deep principal haircut—giving the Union *"partial recovery"* (`L1748`), not whole repayment.
- **What the Union's consent costs in Washington:** President Dawes faces banking collapse (`L1750`), and Owen D. Young wins the November 1932 election backed by Union savers holding Pacifican gold paper (`L1750 ("Owen D. Young wins the Union election")`), yet Senate ratification of the 1865/1871 renunciation passes narrowly over fierce **revanchist opposition** that treats Thalassia as a national humiliation (`L1752`). Pacifica takes on **no** common Union budget, citizenship, free movement, or political guarantee (`L1748`). **Pensacola (`OPEN-019-11`, `L1746 ("Pensacola lease remains")`)** and **Franklin Roosevelt's post-1932 office (`OPEN-019-12`, `L1750 ("Navy Secretary role after his New York governorship")`)** remain explicitly **OPEN**.
- **The Renewed Arbitration Court (`C-1919-ARBITRATION-COURT`, `L1381`, `L1746–L1748`):** Grants the Union a **founding seat** beside Pacifica, Japan, Britain, France, Germany, and China, and houses Tier 1/Tier 2 administration of `C-1932-ATOMIC-CONVENTION`. To reconcile Japan's 1919 racial-equality principle (`L1381`) with Union and Dominion participation (`L1752`), the renewed Court statute preserves racial equality in international commercial and arbitral standing while adding an explicit **domestic-immigration reservation**—a bitter compromise Japan's delegates protest and accept only to keep the Union inside the Court.

**B2. China's Dual-Track Gold-Exchange Standard (`L1714`, `C-1932-CHINA-GOLD-STANDARD`, PROPOSED; `T-C1`).**
Canon notes that *"Silver is the money of Pacifican loans for work in China, so silver's fall is Pacifica's loss, and in 1929 a Pacifican commission proposes a gold-exchange standard linked to Pacifica's dollar, the money the soybean compact's contracts are written in"* (`L1714 ("a Pacifican commission proposes a gold-exchange standard")`).
- *Hist.* parallel: In 1929 Nanjing's Finance Minister **T. V. Soong** (Song Ziwen, 1894–1971, Minister of Finance 1928–33, *Hist.*) invited Edwin W. Kemmerer's Commission of Financial Experts, which drafted a gradual gold-exchange standard (*Sun* unit) that stalled historically for want of foreign gold reserves and because of civil war.
- **The Thalassia Protocol (July 1932, `C-1932-CHINA-GOLD-STANDARD`):** Unlike history, Nanjing holds recovered customs (`C-1928-NANJING`), reunified civil-budget backing under Wang Jingwei and Soong, and Pacifican credit. At Thalassia, **T. V. Soong** signs a **dual-track Gold-Exchange Protocol**:
  1. Maritime Customs assessments, foreign railway bond service (`C-1896-CN-RAILWAY-BONDS`), and Manchurian soybean-compact contracts (`C-1929-SOYBEAN-COMPACT`) transition to a **Pacifica-dollar-linked Chinese gold-exchange unit** backed by earmarked Chinese gold and dollar balances in San Francisco on `C-1927-CONCORD-RESERVES` terms (100% earmarked gold withdrawable without notice; 50% gold cover on dollar balances, `L1597`);
  2. **Where China refuses full conversion:** **Shanghai's traditional native banks (*qianzhuang*), silver merchants, and semi-autonomous Canton** reject abolishing domestic silver circulation. Soong therefore keeps **interior provinces on silver** for ordinary domestic trade—protecting Pacifica's foreign loan service and customs without provoking a nationwide interior currency revolt (`T-C1`).

**B3. Canada's Monetary Association (`L1617`, `L1752`, `C-1932-CA-MONETARY-ASSOC`, PROPOSED; `T-M1`).**
Caught between Britain's **September 1931 gold exit** (`L1687 ("Britain leaves gold in September")`) and San Francisco's gold dollar—with Western Canada and Québec already in Pacifica's orbit (`L1617`)—Prime Minister **R. B. Bennett** (1870–1947, PM 1930–35, *Hist.*) faces a trap ahead of the **Ottawa Imperial Economic Conference (21 July – 18 August 1932, *Hist.*)**: following sterling down breaks Canadian borrowers' gold-dollar bond service, while clinging to pre-1931 gold parity crushes Prairie wheat exports.
- **The Thalassia Clearing Band (July 1932, `C-1932-CA-MONETARY-ASSOC`):** Ottawa enters Thalassia's **adjustable gold-dollar clearing band** with a San Francisco stabilization line, allowing the Canadian dollar to sit midway between sterling and Pacifica's gold dollar while keeping Great Bear Lake's uranium contract (`C-1912-GREATBEAR-OFFTAKE`) intact.
- **Ottawa's hard refusal:** Bennett refuses any political association, customs union, or impairment of British Crown allegiance (`L1752 ("Canada remains British")`), and immediately hosts the Ottawa Imperial Economic Conference in late July–August 1932 to preserve Empire trade preferences (`L1752 ("imperial preference agreed at Ottawa")`). *(Standalone-mergeable onto `main`; if `CR-083` merges separately, Caledonia's monetary articles govern BC/Yukon while Ottawa's clearing band remains valid for Canada.)*

## C. The Rim Contracts: Mexico (Guaymas), Argentina (the Pampas), and Siam (1932)

Under `L1020 ("two figures beside its price")`, Pacifica's cabinet minute attaches two figures to the Rim commercial instruments: **0 people brought into the Realm** (no annexations, `L445 ("no people taken by decree")`), while securing commercial export access to **Argentina's Pampas grain pool and Siam's Chao Phraya rice pool**—two of the nine century sellers in `CR-082` Work 3 needed from **about 1941–43** when the Realm's home food margin closes (`L1672 ("holds only while the sellers sell")`; exact tonnage shares left `OPEN` for `provisioning.md` §8).

**C1. Mexico: The Guaymas Free Port and Railway Protocol (`C-1867-GUAYMAS`, `L1617`, `L1681`; `C-1932-GUAYMAS-PROTOCOL`, PROPOSED).**
Canon notes that Pacifica defused Union intervention in Mexico in 1927–28 around Mexico's December 1927 oil-law amendment (`L1617 ("Mexico's oil-law amendment of December 1927")`) while *"Guaymas, the Mexican free port with a Pacifican railway, waits on Mexico"* (`L1681 ("Guaymas, the Mexican free port with a Pacifican railway")`).
- **The 1932 bargain:** Facing collapsing silver and oil revenues in 1932 under President **Pascual Ortiz Rubio** (1877–1963; President 5 Feb 1930 – 2 Sept 1932, *Hist.*) and Secretary of Treasury **Alberto J. Pani** (1878–1955; took office 26 Feb 1932, *Hist.*), Mexico activates `C-1867-GUAYMAS` through **`C-1932-GUAYMAS-PROTOCOL`** (signed summer 1932): Guaymas opens as a bonded free-port transit terminal linking Pacifica's Arizona–Sonora railway to the Gulf of California (handling Arizona/Sonora copper, northwestern Mexican winter produce, and coastal Pacific shipping) in exchange for San Francisco commercial port-modernization bonds.
- **Mexico's hard refusal:** Pani and Calles's circle **refuse** any extraterritorial Pacifican port enclave, foreign customs guards, or weakening of Article 27 subsoil sovereignty. Mexican customs officers, Mexican courts, and Mexican federal labour law govern every quay inside Guaymas; Pacifica gets commercial bonded transit and nothing else.

**C2. Argentina: Pampas Grain Clearing Contracts Against Ottawa Preference (`CR-082` §D P-4 & Work 3, `L1560`, `L1752`; `C-1932-PAMPAS-GRAIN`, PROPOSED).**
*Hist.* President **Agustín Pedro Justo** (1876–1943; took office **20 February 1932**) watched Britain's July–August 1932 Ottawa Imperial Economic Conference threaten Argentina's vital British beef and grain exports (which historically forced Buenos Aires into the one-sided May 1933 Roca–Runciman Pact).
- **The 1932 commercial hedge:** Seeing Ottawa's preference wall rise (`L1752 ("imperial preference agreed at Ottawa")`), Buenos Aires signs commercial grain, maize, and linseed clearing contracts (**`C-1932-PAMPAS-GRAIN`**, autumn 1932) directly with **Pacifica's private grain houses and the Crown's Granary (`C-1932-GRANARY-CONTRACTS`)**—strictly bilateral San Francisco–Buenos Aires trade (**never** routed through Chile, obeying `CR-082` §D P-4, because Pacifica is rival Chile's 1900 guarantor, `C-1900-CHILE-GUARANTEE`, `L1560 ("write the loan down as the price of the Strait")`). Shipments are paid in Pacifica gold dollars, Polytechnic agricultural machinery, and Columbia ammonia.
- **Argentina's hard refusal and real limit:** Buenos Aires **refuses** any political association, naval coaling right, or Pacifican arbitration over South Atlantic waters, and Britain remains Argentina's dominant chilled-beef buyer—so Pacifica secures an early commercial foothold in Work 3's Pampas grain pool without displacing London on the Río de la Plata.

**C3. Siam: Watching the Rice Bowl After the 24 June 1932 Revolution (`CR-082` §E R-5, `L1681`; `C-1932-SIAM-COMMERCE`, PROPOSED).**
*Hist.* On **24 June 1932**—in the very weeks the Thalassia Conference sits—the bloodless **Siamese Revolution** (*Khana Ratsadon*, with civilian leader **Pridi Banomyong**, 1900–1983) ends absolute monarchy under **King Prajadhipok (Rama VII)** (1893–1941) and establishes a constitutional regime.
- **The autumn 1932 protocol (`C-1932-SIAM-COMMERCE`):** Facing depressed rice prices and wary of British Malayan or French Indochinese financial tutoring, Bangkok's new constitutional government signs a modest commercial and technical protocol with Pacifica and the Philippine crown: equal port access for Mariveles trucks (`L1681 ("assembles trucks for Saigon, Bangkok and Batavia")`), Polytechnic irrigation advisers on Chinese/Philippine open-contract terms, and commercial Chao Phraya rice purchase options for Pacifica's grain houses.
- **Siam's hard refusal:** Bangkok **refuses** any security treaty, foreign garrison, or base rights—preserving Siam's buffer neutrality between British Burma/Malaya and French Indochina, matching `CR-082` §E R-5 (*"Siam is watched"*).

## Canon tensions

- **T-U2. Secondary uranium and thorium sweep vs. `L1646`'s 5,800-tonne total (`L1646 ("Mining as much as Pacifica can get its hands on")`, `L1867`, `L1869`).**
  - *Case A (four sources + Great Bear only):* Add only Great Bear Lake (`L1265`) and leave Portugal, South Australia, and Brazilian monazite uncontracted. Minimizes workbook edits, but leaves `L1646` and `L1869`'s explicit next-CR promises unfilled and hands clean secondary pitchblende to Britain's post-1928 programme.
  - *Case B (targeted commercial sweep with European refusals):* Add Great Bear (200–350 t U), Urgeiriça (80–150 t U), Radium Hill (50–100 t U), and Brazilian monazite thorium insurance—raising `L1646` modestly to ~6,150–6,400 t U (MODEL)—while **Britain locks Cornwall and Travancore, Germany holds Saxony, France holds Madagascar, and Belgium raises Shinkolobwe royalties**.
  - **Recommended: Case B.**
- **T-N1. Two-tier Thalassia Atomic Materials and Safety Convention vs. pure bottleneck unilateralism (`L1640`, `L1871 ("the dependence strategy after 1927")`).**
  - *Case A (export controls only, no convention):* Defend Pacifica's lead purely through equipment restrictions (`L1640`) and Norsk Hydro heavy water (`L1643`). Avoids multilateral diplomatic friction, but leaves Pacifica with no legal standard-setting framework for civilian fuel leases or test safety.
  - *Case B (two-tier Convention anchored to the Arbitration Court):* Pair hard bottlenecks with Tier 1 universal safety/notice norms and Tier 2 voluntary civilian fuel-lease dependence—while **Britain, Germany, France, and the Union explicitly refuse any weapons or enrichment ban**, preserving the post-1932 European nuclear race (`L1614`).
  - **Recommended: Case B.**
- **T-C1. China's dual-track gold-exchange protocol vs. silver-only deferral (`L1714 ("a Pacifican commission proposes a gold-exchange standard")`).**
  - *Case A (deferred indefinitely, as in history's Kemmerer Commission):* Leave China wholly on silver through 1932. Avoids banking friction in Shanghai, but leaves Pacifica's silver-denominated Chinese loans exposed to silver's slump.
  - *Case B (dual-track customs/railway/soybean protocol):* T. V. Soong links Maritime Customs, foreign railway bonds, and soybean contracts to a Pacifica-dollar gold-exchange unit backed by earmarked San Francisco reserves, while **interior provinces stay on silver** in deference to Shanghai's *qianzhuang* and Canton.
  - **Recommended: Case B.**
- **T-M1. Canada's Thalassia clearing association vs. leaving Canada wholly to Ottawa (`L1617`, `L1752 ("Canada remains British")`).**
  - *Case A (no Thalassia monetary link for Canada):* Keep Ottawa wholly outside Thalassia's monetary settlement. Emphasizes imperial distance, but leaves Canadian gold-bond service and Prairie wheat squeezed between sterling's fall and San Francisco's dollar.
  - *Case B (adjustable clearing band under strict British Crown allegiance):* R. B. Bennett joins Thalassia's adjustable gold-dollar clearing band in July 1932 while refusing any political association and immediately hosting the Ottawa Imperial Economic Conference.
  - **Recommended: Case B.**
