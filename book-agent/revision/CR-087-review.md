## Verdict

One more revision. The line itself is sound and the geography the draft states from memory holds up: the divide, the trough, Mount Cleveland and the ten miles are all confirmed. But the draft has the county map backwards: the historical Missoula County of 1887 already reaches the 49th parallel far east of the Lewis crest, so the approved line takes land off the drawn county and does not add any, and `## D`, the impact notes and `T-1` all need rewriting on that fact, which the author should be told.

## Blockers

**B1. The map as drawn already holds the trough and much more; the draft assumes the opposite.**

- *Passage:* `## D`: "taking the trough from historical Chouteau County if the historical line lay west of it"; "Missoula's 19,488 square miles and its population do not change in any figure the storyline uses". `T-1` Case A: "the county map's edge is wrong and so is `CR-083` `A3`". `A1`: "East of the Lewis crest everything is the Union's: the St Mary and Belly valleys, Chief Mountain and the plains."
- *Problem:* In the atlas data the script uses (Missoula, version of 20 November 1867, valid to 1889, 19,488 square miles), Missoula's eastern edge north of 48°27′ N is a straight meridian at about 113°28′ W, running due north to the 49th parallel. It leaves the Continental Divide near Two Medicine and Dawson Pass and runs east of it all the way. South of 48°27′ N the Deer Lodge and Chouteau line is the divide, through Marias Pass, so the map already agrees with `A1` there. North of it the drawn county holds everything between the divide and 113°28′ W: about 510 square miles (my measurement from the atlas edges, MODEL), including the trough, the Belly valley, Many Glacier, Chief Mountain and the upper St Mary. Its border with Alberta as drawn is about 27 miles, from the tripoint (114°04′ W) to about six miles east of Chief Mountain, not a tiny one at Waterton Lake. Chouteau County holds none of the trough; the atlas confirms that Teton County in 1893 was made "from CHOTEAU and MISSOULA", the Missoula part being this strip. So:
  - the map task is the reverse of what `## D` says: Missoula must lose the country between the new line and the meridian, about 420 square miles, and keeps the trough, which I measure at about 90 square miles and not "150 to 200";
  - the map's edge is wrong under Case A and under Case B alike, so that clause of `T-1` does not tell the cases apart;
  - `CR-071` Part I, line 409, "Missoula (unchanged)", is approved canon, and against it the draft moves the St Mary and Belly valleys and Chief Mountain from the Realm to the Union. That is a second tension and it is not listed;
  - the author chose at 07:19 among three lines "the drafter had set out". The line the map actually draws was not one of them. On that line a road to Alberta is easy (the Belly and St Mary valleys cross the parallel inside it), which bears on his request of 07:02 to test "the possibility of a road border with Alberta".
- *Fix:* Rewrite `## D` and the `CR-071` impact note on the facts above. Add a tension `T-2` (the canon map's Missoula against the line: Case A, keep the drawn meridian, 27 miles of Alberta and an easy road; Case B, the Lewis crest, with the east side going to the Union). The author's words of 07:02 ("incongruous with the mountainous terrain") and 07:19 point to Case B, but put it to him in one line with the square miles and the 27 against 10 miles before it is labelled decided. Correct the trough's area and state Missoula's loss as MODEL.

**S2. "Two crests, one phrase" needs one more leg to stand on.** The Union's reason as given ("want the two descriptions to agree so that no strip is left between them") argues for one line, not for the eastern one; it is met equally by reading both as the divide, which is how our timeline read the Blackfeet treaty. The Realm's argument is stronger than the draft makes it, and canon supplies it: `L632 ("not the water")` says "In the Rockies the line follows what each side can hold, not the water." Give the Union something for conceding, or say plainly that the commission's instructions were to close the survey that season. As drafted, "a commission that wants to go home" carries the whole concession.

**S3. If both governments now read "main range" as the Lewis crest, the trough is taken out of the Blackfoot territory of 1855 by two surveyors, without the nation. That is the real content of "The Blackfeet are not asked", and it is stronger than "The Piikani hunt it from the east". State it in `A3`; it is what `L629`'s "Federal cession does not settle Native title or consent" is for. It also gives the Union's commissioners a motive that is discreditable and believable, which S2 wants.

Reword to: the line chosen does not rest on a compact; the question stays OPEN, as the last bullet already says.

**S5. "The standstill rule of `CR-070` and `CR-071`" is not a canon name.** The phrase "standstill rule" occurs in no canon file I was allowed to read; it is `CR-083`'s. ## Geography and history checked

- **Continental Divide: Lewis Range from Marias Pass to Flattop, then west to the Livingston Range and into Canada.** Confirmed. NPS, `nps.gov/glac/learn/education/continental_divide.htm`: "follows the crest of the Lewis Range from Marias Pass to Flattop Mountain", then "swings west to the crest of the Livingston Range, which it follows into Canada". USGS PP 294-K: the divide "swings from the Livingstone Range to the Lewis Range at the north end of Flattop Mountain".
- **The seam between the ranges through Waterton Lake, the valley south of it, Flattop and McDonald Creek.** Confirmed. PP 294-K and PP 296 (npshistory.com mirrors): "through Waterton Lake, up the Waterton Valley, over or around Flattop Mountain ... thence down McDonald Creek".
- **Upper Waterton Lake crossing the parallel, Goat Haunt at its head, Boundary Bay.** Unchecked on a fetched page; not in doubt for the lake and Goat Haunt. Boundary Bay I could not confirm; from memory it is on the west shore just north of the line, so "across the lake at Boundary Bay" should read "just south of Boundary Bay".
- **The Lewis crest north of Flattop and where it meets the parallel.** Confirmed in substance from peak coordinates (Wikipedia's list of the park's mountains), not from a topographic sheet: Mount Kipp, Cathedral Peak, Stoney Indian Peaks, Mount Cleveland (48°55′30″ N, 113°50′53″ W, 10,466 feet, the park's highest), Goat Haunt Mountain, Miche Wabun Peak (48°58′11″ N, 113°49′52″ W), reaching the parallel at about 113°50′ W on the ridge between the lake and the North Fork of the Belly. "Past the head of the Belly River" is right in order (Ahern Pass, then Stoney Indian Pass). The topographic check the draft lists as OPEN is still owed for the last two miles.
- **The tripoint.** Confirmed: Boundary Monument 272, 49°00′ N, 114°04′05.65″ W (`highpointers.org`, tripoints page).
- **Length of the border.** Confirmed: 114°04′ W to about 113°50′ W at 49° N is about 10.3 miles (16.6 km). "About ten miles" stands as MODEL.
- **Flattop about 6,500 feet.** Not confirmed; PP 296 says "about 6,800 feet" for the tops of Flattop and West Flattop.
- **A road from Lake McDonald up McDonald Creek over Flattop to the head of Waterton Lake.** Physically plausible (it is the seam the geologists describe, with a low, broad top); that any such road was proposed in our timeline is unchecked, and the draft does not claim it.
- **Akamina, 1915.** Confirmed with the corrections under Minor (Bigfork Eagle, 5 June 2015). Akamina Parkway begun 1921, open to Cameron Lake 1927: confirmed (Parks Canada).
- **The 49th-parallel survey reached the divide in 1874.** Confirmed: the advance party found the earlier commission's monument "on the summit of the Rocky Mountains" on 18 August 1874 (Manitoba Historical Society, "Markers on the Forty-Ninth").
- **Missoula County in 1887.** The draft's assumption is wrong. Eastern line north of 48°27′ N a meridian at about 113°28′ W to the parallel; the upper Waterton valley, and the whole east side of what is now the park, lay in Missoula, not Chouteau (Atlas of Historical County Boundaries, Montana chronologies and geometry; see B1).
- **Treaty of 1855, "main range of the Rocky Mountains".** Confirmed, Article 4: "thence due west on said parallel to the main range of the Rocky Mountains, and thence southerly along said range to the place of beginning" (text at `cdsc.libraries.wsu.edu/ccrh/blackfeet-treaty-of-fort-benton-1855/`). Article 3 uses the same phrase for the common hunting ground.
- **Ktunaxa use.** Confirmed: the Tobacco Plains band "historically traversed the mountains on annual bison hunts into the Waterton area" (NPS, `glac/learn/education/american_indians.htm`). Whether they used the trough itself or the passes north of the parallel (South Kootenay, Akamina) is unchecked; the draft's "cross it by the passes" may be too specific.
- **Piikani use.** Confirmed in general (the same NPS page: mountains and eastern foothills used by the Blackfeet); for the Waterton valley specifically, unchecked. Note S3: by the treaty's words the trough was Blackfoot territory, which is firmer than a hunting claim.
- **Marias, 1870.** Confirmed: 23 January 1870, Baker's column on Heavy Runner's camp; 173 dead by the army's count, 217 by the Blackfeet timeline (Montana OPI, Blackfeet timeline).
- **Starvation winter, 1883–84.** Confirmed: over 500 dead (same source).
- **Alberta in 1874.** Unchecked on the web; from memory the North-West Territories (plural), with the District of Alberta from 1882.
