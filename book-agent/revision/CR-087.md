## A. The change

**A1. The line (APPROVED; author, 24 November 2026, 07:19).**
Canon says of the Western line that "the detailed boundary follows the mountain front, divide crests" (`L629 ("divide crests")`) and leaves its surveys PROPOSED. In the far north this CR fixes it.
- From Marias Pass north the line follows the Continental Divide, which there is the crest of the Lewis Range (*Hist.*).
- At the north end of Flattop Mountain the divide leaves the Lewis Range and crosses west to the Livingston Range, which it follows into Canada (*Hist.*). The line does not go with it.
- The line stays on the crest of the Lewis Range, past the head of the Belly River and Mount Cleveland, and meets the 49th parallel on the ridge east of Upper Waterton Lake (the peaks and the point are from memory and are to be read from a topographic map).
- The Realm therefore holds the trough between the two ranges north of Flattop: the upper Waterton valley and the head of Upper Waterton Lake. Geologists draw the seam between the ranges through that lake and valley (*Hist.*).
- On the 49th parallel the Realm runs from the tripoint with British Columbia and Alberta, on the divide, east to the Lewis crest: about ten miles (MODEL; to be measured), across the lake at Boundary Bay. That stretch is the Realm's only border with Alberta, and with Canada once British Columbia is out.
- East of the Lewis crest everything is the Union's: the St Mary and Belly valleys, Chief Mountain and the plains.

**A2. Why the line runs there (PROPOSED).**
- **The words.** The peace of 1865 describes this part of the line as "the crest of the main range of the Rocky Mountains". The Union's own treaty of 1855 with the Blackfeet uses the main range as that nation's western limit (*Hist.*, from memory; to verify), and the Union's commissioners want the two descriptions to agree so that no strip is left between them.
- **The survey.** The joint survey reaches the north end in 1874, the season in which the survey of the 49th parallel closes at the divide (*Hist.* for the latter, from memory). North of Flattop it finds two crests and one phrase. The Realm's surveyor argues for the eastern crest, which is the higher and carries the range's highest summits; the Union's argues for the western, where the waters part. The Union gives way. The trough is uninhabited by settlers, has no pass a wagon can use and no mineral anyone has found, and conceding it keeps the Blackfeet line and the Western line on the same words.
- **What nobody intends.** Neither side is thinking of Canada. That the Realm now touches the North-West Territory, later Alberta, on a mountain lake is an accident of the phrase, noticed in Ottawa before it is noticed in Washington.
- **The loser.** The Union's surveyor, who files a protest that the divide was the natural line and is overruled by a commission that wants to go home. The protest is the Union's only paper on the matter, and the revanchist press finds it in the 1890s.

**A3. The Blackfeet are not asked, and the valley is not settled (PROPOSED).**
- The author's question of 07:09 is answered by his decision of 07:19: the line stays on the crest and no compact moves it to the mountain front. The Piegan remain across the line, on the Union's side, as `CR-060` N5 leaves them.
- The trough is not empty country. The Piikani hunt it from the east, and the Ktunaxa cross it by the passes on their way to the buffalo (*Hist.*, from memory). "Federal cession does not settle Native title or consent" (`L629`), and the standstill rule of `CR-070` and `CR-071` holds: nothing is granted or built there without a compact. None is made by 1932. Whose country the valley is, and with whom a compact would be made, is OPEN.
- Whether Pacifica ever treats with the Piegan, after the killings on the Marias in 1870 or the starving winter of 1883–84, is not decided here. The drafter's view is that it is worth a CR of its own.

**A4. Roads: surveys only (APPROVED as to "surveys only"; author, 24 November 2026, 07:19; the routes and dates are PROPOSED).**
- **The trough.** The one route to the border that lies wholly in the Realm runs from Lake McDonald up McDonald Creek, over Flattop at about 6,500 feet and down to the head of Waterton Lake. The Realm's road office surveys it in the 1920s. It is snowbound most of the year, it would carry nothing but visitors, and the lake's steep shore stops it short of anything in Alberta. It is not built.
- **Akamina.** Canada's parks branch proposes in 1915 a road from Waterton over Akamina Pass, through a corner of British Columbia and down the North Fork of the Flathead (*Hist.*); Alberta builds its side to Cameron Lake by 1927 (*Hist.*). The corner belongs to the dominion and from 1932 to Caledonia, so the road needs three governments. It is surveyed and not built, as in history.
- **No railway** is surveyed; there is no grade.
- The border on the lake is marked by a cut line and monuments. Whether a boat landing and a customs officer stand at Boundary Bay in summer is OPEN.

**A5. What follows for 1932.**
- Caledonia's southern line, from the Strait of Georgia to the divide, runs against the Realm throughout. The Union touches Caledonia nowhere; its border with Canada begins at the Lewis crest.
- Canada has no usable land contact with the Realm. This is `CR-083`'s point about Bennett and the quay at Burrard, and it rests on this CR.

## D. Model and workbook tasks

- **The county map is not redrawn.** `population/draw_map_1887.py` builds each county from our timeline's counties of 1 August 1887, whose geometry is not in the repository, and its cuts are straight lines by bounding box. Missoula is the historical county whole (`population/data/counties/map_1887.toml`), which is where the straight edge comes from. To draw this line the script needs a crest polyline (a small file beside `rivers_colville.geojson`, digitised from a topographic source) and a way to cut a county along it, taking the trough from historical Chouteau County if the historical line lay west of it.
- **Area and people.** The trough is of the order of 150 to 200 square miles (MODEL; to be measured) and has no residents in 1887. Missoula's 19,488 square miles and its population do not change in any figure the storyline uses.
- No workbook change.
