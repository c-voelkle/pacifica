## A. The change

**A1. The line (APPROVED; author, 24 November 2026, 07:19 and 08:22; its first bullet is PROPOSED).**
Canon says of the Western line that "the detailed boundary follows the mountain front, divide crests" (`L629 ("divide crests")`) and leaves its surveys PROPOSED. In the far north this CR fixes it.
- From Marias Pass north the line follows the Continental Divide, which there is the crest of the Lewis Range (*Hist.*). This is the drafter's statement: canon gives "the mountain front" in Montana and does not say where the line passes from the front to the divide, which is OPEN. The map as drawn agrees with it from Marias Pass to about 48°27′ N.
- **What the map draws now.** `CR-071`'s Missoula County is our timeline's county of 1887 whole. North of about 48°27′ N its eastern edge leaves the divide and runs due north along a meridian at about 113°28′ W to the 49th parallel. As drawn, the Realm therefore holds everything between the divide and that meridian: the trough, and also the Belly valley, Many Glacier, Chief Mountain and the upper St Mary, about 510 square miles, with about 27 miles of border with Alberta (the review's measurements from the atlas; MODEL). The meridian is a legislature's line of 1867 and follows nothing on the ground.
- At the north end of Flattop Mountain the divide leaves the Lewis Range and crosses west to the Livingston Range, which it follows into Canada (*Hist.*). The line does not go with it.
- The line stays on the crest of the Lewis Range, past the head of the Belly River and Mount Cleveland, the range's highest summit, and meets the 49th parallel on the ridge east of Upper Waterton Lake (*Hist.* for the crest and the summit; the exact point is to be read from a topographic map).
- The Realm keeps the trough between the two ranges north of Flattop, about 90 square miles (MODEL): the upper Waterton valley and the head of Upper Waterton Lake. Geologists draw the seam between the ranges through that lake and valley (*Hist.*).
- On the 49th parallel the Realm runs from the tripoint with British Columbia and Alberta, on the divide, at 114°04′ W, east to the Lewis crest: about ten miles (the review measures 10.3), across the lake at Boundary Bay. That stretch is the Realm's only border with Alberta, and, once `CR-083` takes British Columbia out, with Canada.
- East of the Lewis crest everything is the Union's: the St Mary and Belly valleys, Many Glacier, Chief Mountain and the plains. Against the map as drawn, that is about 420 square miles passing from the Realm to the Union, and with it the easy road into Alberta by the Belly and St Mary valleys (`T-2`).

**A2. Why the line runs there (PROPOSED).**
- **The words.** The peace of 1865 describes this part of the line as "the crest of the main range of the Rocky Mountains". The Union's own treaty of 1855 with the Blackfeet runs that nation's limit "to the main range of the Rocky Mountains, and thence southerly along said range" (*Hist.*, article 4), and the Union's commissioners want the two descriptions to agree so that no strip is left between them. That argues for one line and not for the eastern one. The Realm's surveyor supplies the rest from the peace itself: "In the Rockies the line follows what each side can hold, not the water" (`L632 ("not the water")`), and a peace that everywhere else ignores watersheds does not suddenly follow one for its last thirty miles.
- **The survey.** The joint survey reaches the north end in 1874, the season in which the survey of the 49th parallel closes at the divide, on 18 August (*Hist.*). The British commissioners, coming west along the parallel, do not know until the crests are settled whose surveyors they will meet over the last ten miles. North of Flattop it finds two crests and one phrase. The Realm's surveyor argues for the eastern crest, which is the higher and carries the range's highest summits; the Union's argues for the western, where the waters part. The Union gives way, and gets something for it: the meridian strip. The territorial line of 1867 had run the Realm's county east to a meridian across the St Mary country, on the Blackfeet's side of the mountains, and the Union had protested it since. The Realm gives up the strip east of the crest, some 420 square miles, for the crest itself. The trough is uninhabited by settlers, has no pass a wagon can use and no mineral anyone has found, and conceding it keeps the Blackfeet line and the Western line on the same words.
- **What nobody intends.** Neither side is thinking of Canada. That the Realm now touches the North-West Territory, later Alberta, on a mountain lake is an accident of the phrase, noticed in Ottawa before it is noticed in Washington.
- **The loser.** The Union's surveyor, who files a protest that the divide was the natural line and is overruled by a commission that wants to go home. The protest is the Union's only paper on the matter, and the revanchist press finds it in the 1890s.

**A3. The Blackfeet are not asked, and the valley is not settled (PROPOSED).**
- The author asked at 07:09 why the Blackfeet are not asked, and at 07:19 chose the crest. He did not say that they would have refused, or that they are never asked. The crest line forecloses nothing: the Piegan remain across the line, on the Union's side, as `CR-060` N5 leaves them, and a later compact could still be made.
- **What the survey does to them, unasked.** If both governments read "main range" as the Lewis crest, two surveyors have taken the trough out of the Blackfoot country of 1855 without the nation, and have at the same time put the St Mary country, which the meridian had cut off, back beside it. Nobody tells the Piegan either thing in 1874.
- The trough is not empty country. The Piikani hunt it from the east, and the Ktunaxa cross it by the passes on their way to the buffalo (*Hist.*, from memory). "Federal cession does not settle Native title or consent" (`L629`), and the rule canon states for Alaska, that "the land office grants nothing and the claims courts register nothing on a nation's country without that nation's compact" (line 727 of master_storyline.md; `CR-070`), is here extended to the trough (PROPOSED): nothing is granted or built there without a compact. None is made by 1932. Whose country the valley is, and with whom a compact would be made, is OPEN.
- Whether Pacifica ever treats with the Piegan, after the killings on the Marias in 1870 or the starving winter of 1883–84, is not decided here. The drafter's view is that it is worth a CR of its own.

**A4. Roads: surveys only (APPROVED as to "surveys only"; author, 24 November 2026, 07:19; the routes and dates are PROPOSED).**
- **The trough.** The one route to the border that lies wholly in the Realm runs from Lake McDonald up McDonald Creek, over Flattop at about 6,500 to 6,800 feet and down to the head of Waterton Lake. The Realm's road office surveys it in the 1920s. It is snowbound most of the year, it would carry nothing but visitors, and the lake's steep shore stops it short of anything in Alberta. It is not built.
- **Akamina.** Canada's parks branch advocates in 1915 a road from Waterton over Akamina Pass, through a corner of British Columbia and down the North Fork of the Flathead (*Hist.*), and itself builds the road as far as Cameron Lake by 1927 (*Hist.*). The corner is British Columbia's, and with `CR-083` Caledonia's from 1932, so the road needs more than one government. It is advocated, partly surveyed and not built, as in history.
- **What the crest line gives up.** On the map as drawn the Belly and St Mary valleys cross the parallel inside the Realm, and a road to Alberta there would have been easy; the Union builds one at Chief Mountain in our history. The author chose the crest knowing this (08:22).
- **No railway** is surveyed; there is no grade.
- The border on the lake is marked by a cut line and monuments. Whether a boat landing and a customs officer stand at Boundary Bay in summer is OPEN.

**A5. What follows for 1932 (takes effect with `CR-083`; its first sentence rests on the author's words of 06:55).**
- Caledonia's southern line, from the Strait of Georgia to the divide, runs against the Realm throughout. The Union touches Caledonia nowhere; its border with Canada begins at the Lewis crest.
- Canada has no usable land contact with the Realm. This is `CR-083`'s point about Bennett and the quay at Burrard, and it rests on this CR.
