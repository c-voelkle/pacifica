### A1. Work 4 Resolved (`CR-082` `T11`): The Realm's Diet and Dual-Column Office Tables from 1930 (`APPROVED`)

`CR-082` held the Office of Public Measures' tables to a constant `2.1` acres of average Union crop land per head (crop land for food, fibre, and tobacco, excluding work stock and exports, *Hist.*), explicitly leaving the nature of Pacifica's own table — Work 4 — to a culinary CR (`L2039 ("the Realm's diet and the farmed sea")`; `T11` Case A, line 414 of `CR-082.md`).

**Why Pacifica's table takes less crop land than the Atlantic Union's (`APPROVED` principle; specifics `PROPOSED`):**
1. **Six converging pantries, the wok, and coastal ecology.** Unlike the Midwest-fed Atlantic Union — where grain-finished beef and slab-of-meat dining drive high crop-land ratios — Pacifica's everyday table draws heavily on Pacific fish and shellfish, pork and poultry, dry beans, Manchurian and local soybeans (*tofu*, *doujiang* soy milk, *miso*, *shōyu*, *douchi* fermented black beans; `L113 ("China's other century")`, `C-1929-SOYBEAN-COMPACT`), Sacramento Valley short-grain *japonica* rice, orchard fruit, and market vegetables. Equally decisive is **Cantonese and Hakka wok technique** (`APPROVED`, 23:05): slicing ounces of pork, poultry, tofu, or shrimp thin and flash-cooking them over cheap urban gas or hydro-electric heat with crisp California greens (*bok choy*, *gai lan*, snow peas, asparagus, bitter melon), ginger, scallion, and **oyster sauce** (*Hist.*: invented by Lee Kum Sheung in Guangdong in 1888, and brewed locally in Pacifica from San Francisco Bay and Puget Sound oysters). By migrating out of Chinatowns into mixed working-class kitchens and boarding houses, the wok becomes the single biggest domestic culinary engine behind `diet_saving = 0.22`. Beef remains plentiful on arid western rangeland (which `CR-082` already leaves outside the cropland account), rather than competing for irrigated feedlot grain.
2. **Alkali-soil *japonica* rice, Delta reclamation, and Chinese orchards (`PROPOSED`; Ah Bing `APPROVED`).** In our timeline, William W. Mackie (Bureau of Soils) and Japanese pioneer Kenju Ikuta proved between 1908 and 1912 at Biggs (Butte County, California) that Japanese short-grain rice (*Wataribune* and *Kiushu*) thrived on heavy Sacramento Valley adobe and alkali hardpan too poor for wheat (*Hist.*). Earlier still, Kurume-born **George Shima (Ushijima Kinji)** (`CT-GEORGE-SHIMA`, 1864–1926, *Hist.*) immigrated to San Francisco in 1889, diked and drained Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta peat islands dismissed as worthless swampland, and became California's "Potato King" (*Hist.*). Beside them, Chinese market gardeners and orchardists pioneer California's commercial asparagus, celery, strawberries, and pears — exemplified in Oregon's Willamette Valley by **Ah Bing** (`CT-AH-BING`, born c. 1848, *Hist.*), the Manchurian-born orchard foreman for Seth Lewelling in Milwaukie who co-developed the dark sweet **Bing cherry** in **1875** (*Hist.*). In our timeline, Ah Bing visited China in 1889 and was barred from ever returning by the Chinese Exclusion Act (*Hist.*); in Pacifica, with no Exclusion Act, Ah Bing sails freely back to Oregon, continues breeding stone fruit and pears, and anchors Pacifica's renowned Pacific-coast orchard belt. Together with **Eugene W. Hilgard**'s (`CT-EUGENE-HILGARD`, 1833–1916) alkali-soil agronomy and early Hakka and Japanese family settlements (`L783 ("Wakamatsu colony")`), short-grain rice, Delta potatoes, and intensive truck/orchard crops scale a decade or two ahead of our timeline (1880s–1900s), quietly raising caloric and horticultural yield per irrigated acre on ground wheat farmers bypassed.

### A2. Work 5 Resolved (line 311 of CR-082.md): The Farmed Sea's Four Pillars and Dual-Line Accounting (`APPROVED`)

`CR-082` added Work 5 — a Pacific marine-agronomy industry anchored in California's kelp works and Japan's *kombu* growers (`L1702`, `L1704 ("Japan's rice keeps pace")`) — noting that counted in whole-diet calories it looks small (`14.3 million` mouths in the `50,000 km²` central case at `0.3` grain-equivalent), while in crude protein (~`5 million` tonnes/year, `MODEL`) it is far larger, and left the choice of measure to this CR (line 311 of `CR-082.md`).

**The Four Marine Pillars (`PROPOSED`):**
1. **California Giant Kelp (*Macrocystis*) & Hokkaido *Kombu* (*Laminaria*):** Built on Hercules Powder's wartime San Diego mechanical kelp harvesters (1916–19, *Hist.*) and Hokkaido's centuries-old *kombu* beds, joined formally in July 1932 under `C-1932-KELP-RESEARCH` (`L1704`). Yields three distinct streams: (a) direct human table food (*dashi* stock, dried *kombu*, *wakame*); (b) **dried kelp meal for coastal and Rockies dairy/swine/poultry feed**, replacing irrigated feed grain and naturally supplying iodine; and (c) **potash and alginate fertilizers** for California and Hawaiian orchards.
2. **Integrated Shellfish, Chinese Bay/Squid Cooperatives, and Monterey Red-Abalone Ranching:** Pacific oysters, mussels, clams, bay shrimp, squid, and red abalone (*Haliotis rufescens*) form a multi-ethnic coastal guild. Long before Japanese deep-water divers arrived, Guangdong fishermen built California's first commercial shellfish and inshore fisheries: **Point Alones** at Monterey (founded early 1850s — famous for night-torch "fire-basket" squid fishing and shallow-water abalone, *Hist.*) and **China Camp** on San Pablo Bay (founded c. 1870 — bay-shrimp netting and drying platforms, *Hist.*). In our timeline, discriminatory fishing laws and the suspicious May 1906 fire wiped out Point Alones (*Hist.*); in Pacifica, neither the bans nor the arson occur, and both settlements survive as licensed marine cooperatives supplying dried bay shrimp, dried squid, and dried oysters as everyday Realm pantry staples. In 1897, Chiba Prefecture university-trained marine biologist **Gennosuke Kodani** (`CT-GENNOSUKE-KODANI`, 1867–1930, *Hist.*) arrived at Monterey after Otosaburō Noda spotted dense abalone beds, partnered with landowner Alexander M. Allan at **Point Lobos (Whalers Cove)** to found the Point Lobos Canning Company, and brought Japanese *ama* free-divers and copper-helmet hard-hat divers to launch Monterey's global abalone trade (*Hist.*). In Pacifica, Kodani's deep-water divers complement rather than replace Point Alones's Chinese inshore fleets; both work alongside the Realm's marine stations (`C-1932-KELP-RESEARCH` amended), regulating kelp canopies, shellfish beds, and abalone seeding together as coastal polyculture.
3. **Hawaiian *Loko Iʻa* (Walled Coastal Fishponds) Scaled Across the Pacific:** Canon already places Thalassia on windward Oʻahu beside the ancient Hawaiian fishponds of **Kāneʻohe Bay** and Heʻeia (`L832 ("Kāneʻohe Bay")`, `L1344 ("old Hawaiian fishponds")`). Traditional *loko iʻa kuapā* — lava-rock seawalls fitted with wooden *mākāhā* sluice gates that trap juvenile *ʻamaʻama* (striped mullet) and *awa* (milkfish) to fatten on natural pond algae with **zero grain feed** (*Hist.*) — are protected by the Hawaiian Crown and restored as working protein infrastructure rather than filled for real estate. By the 1920s, Hawaiian *kiaʻi loko* (pond keepers) exchange engineering methods with Filipino *bangus* (milkfish) pond builders around Manila Bay (*Hist.* for centuries-old Philippine milkfish *palaisdaan*) and trial sheltered-lagoon ponds along Baja California and the outer islands.
4. **Conserved Wild Fisheries as a Capped Baseline:** Alaska and Columbia salmon and Pacific halibut (`C-1921-FISHERIES`, `C-1923-HALIBUT`, protected by the Realm's dam rule keeping salmon rivers open) are held explicitly as a **capped, non-expanding baseline**. Learning early from California sardine fluctuations (`provisioning.toml` `[sea]` note), the Office never projects wild catches upward to close a century gap.

- **The Dual-Line Office Measure from 1932 (`APPROVED`, resolves line 311 of CR-082.md):**
Starting with the 1932 marine-agronomy annex tied to `C-1932-KELP-RESEARCH`, the Office of Public Measures reports Work 5 on **two separate lines**:
- **Line 1 — Whole-Diet Grain-Equivalent Line (`0.3` factor, unchanged from `CR-082`):** Low `20,000 km²` = `5.7M` mouths; Central `50,000 km²` (`50M` dry tonnes/yr) = `14.3M` mouths; High `100,000 km²` = `28.6M` mouths (`MODEL`, `provisioning.md` §8). Prevents wishful thinking: the sea does *not* grow bread or rice calories, and changes nothing in the 1932 grain-balance tables (`L1704`).
- **Line 2 — Protein & Feed-Displacement Line (`APPROVED`, narrative-level):** Reports marine animal and algal protein separately — led by the central century target's **~5 million tonnes of crude protein/year** (`MODEL`, line 311 of CR-082.md) alongside shellfish, Point Lobos abalone, and zero-grain *loko iʻa*/*bangus* pond harvests — plus the **tonnage of livestock feed-grain and orchard potash displaced** by kelp meal. This line explains why Julian and the marine stations invest in sea farming decades before the grain margin closes: marine protein breaks the feedlot bottleneck and keeps meat and dairy cheap without burning irrigated acres.

### A3. The Pacifican Table (1850s–1932): Six Pantries and Everyday Institutions (`PROPOSED`)

Pacifica's cuisine does not grow out of gourmet salons; it forms in mining camps, railway bunkhouses, port boarding houses, Hawaiian royal tables, and Carquinez factory canteens where **six founding pantries** meet without exclusion laws:
1. **Californio & Mexican:** nixtamalized corn *masa*, pinto and black *frijoles*, chiles, limes, olive oil, range beef.
2. **Hawaiian:** *kalo* (taro) and *poi*, *limu* seaweeds, raw reef fish, sea salt, *kalua* pork, coconut.
3. **Cantonese & Hakka (10%+ of the Realm's population, `L783`):** wok technique, soy sauce, oyster sauce, fermented black beans (*douchi*), ginger, scallion, *siu mei* roast pork (*char siu*) and duck, Hakka mountain home cooking (*niang doufu* stuffed tofu, pork belly braised with preserved mustard greens, salt-baked chicken), *doujiang* soy milk and firm spiced tofu (*dougan*), rice noodles, tea, and dried shrimp/squid.
4. **Japanese:** *kombu* and *bonito* *dashi*, *miso*, *shōyu*, *tofu*, short-grain *japonica* rice, pickles (*tsukemono*), buckwheat *soba*, raw-fish knife craft.
5. **Filipino:** cane vinegar and calamansi/citrus *kinilaw*, garlic, *bangus*, highland coffee, tropical fruit.
6. **Euro-American & Native Northwest/Southwest:** wheat sourdough, butter and milk, smoked Columbia salmon, mountain trout, piñon, orchard stone fruit (including Oregon's Bing cherries), California wines.

Because Chinese Pacificans hold full citizenship, sit in legislatures, and own orchards and fisheries without Exclusion laws, **there is no defensive "chop suey" ghettoization**: authentic Cantonese, Hakka, Shanghainese, and Beiyang restaurants thrive openly in every port city alongside deep cross-pollination with Pacifica's other five pantries. Out of that daily contact grow **eight signature Pacifican culinary institutions** by 1932:
- **The Three-Coast Raw Fish Triad:** Hawaiian *poke* (cubed tuna or reef fish dressed with sea salt, *limu*, toasted kukui nut, and — once Japanese families settle — *shōyu* and sesame oil), Baja/Californio *ceviche* and Filipino *kinilaw* (citrus- or vinegar-cured whitefish and shellfish with chile), and Japanese *sashimi* / hand-pressed *nigiri* (carried inland on Pacifica's refrigerated electric railcars). By the 1920s, raw cured fish is an ordinary workday lunch from Honolulu to San Francisco and Monterey, half a century before it reaches our timeline's Atlantic America.
- ***Yum Cha* (Dim Sum) Tea Houses — Midday Mirror of `CR-076`'s Dry Halls:** While `CR-076` gives Pacifica morning coffee rooms (`L1036`) and dry community halls on ordinary nights (`L1032`), Cantonese tea houses (*chálou*) anchor Sunday mornings and midday civic life across San Francisco, Honolulu, Sacramento, Portland, and Manila — rolling trays of San Pablo bay-shrimp *har gow*, pork-and-mushroom *siu mai*, *char siu bao*, and *cheung fun* rice-noodle rolls beside pots of jasmine, oolong, and *pu-erh* tea. Senators, Polytechnic faculty, dock foremen, and merchants close deals over tea and dumplings as naturally as over coffee.
- **Hakka Mountain Cooking, *Siu Mei* / *Manapua*, and Morning Soy Stalls:** Because Hakka families lead early Chinese migration (`L783`), Hakka preservation and highland cookery (*niang doufu*, salt-baked chicken, preserved mustard greens with pork) take root naturally in the Sierra, Rockies, and Hawaiian plantation towns. Cantonese *siu mei* roast-pork windows feed Hawaiian ***manapua*** steamed buns (sold on station platforms beside *ekiben*), while Manchurian soybeans (`C-1929-SOYBEAN-COMPACT`) fuel morning street stalls serving hot ***doujiang* (fresh soy milk) and *youtiao* crullers** alongside sourdough and coffee, plus dense five-spice tofu (*dougan*) packed in miners' and rail workers' kits.
- **The Wok, *Dashi*–Chile–Citrus–Soy, and the Borderland Kitchen:** Everyday household cooking marries the high-heat Cantonese/Hakka wok, oyster sauce, kelp *dashi*, *shōyu*, and *miso* with California citrus, Mexican chiles, and either sourdough bread or Sacramento short-grain rice. In California's valleys, Baja, and the Southwest, Chinese–Californian and Chinese–Mexican contact produces a beloved everyday ranch-and-railroad dish — **wok-seared range beef or pork with California tomatoes, onions, green chiles, soy sauce, and vinegar over Delta potatoes or rice** (Pacifica's homegrown twin of Peru's *lomo saltado*) — sitting beside citrus-soy grilled salmon (*Pacific teriyaki*), pan-seared Monterey abalone steaks, and boarding-house breakfasts pairing sourdough toast or rice with miso-kelp soup.
- **Pacific Noodle Houses (*Men-ya*) and *Saimin*:** Steam-fogged counter shops along San Francisco's Embarcadero, Carquinez's workshops, Honolulu's docks, and railway junctions serving wheat alkaline noodles (early *ramen*/*shina-soba*), Cantonese *wonton* soups, buckwheat *soba*, and Honolulu's quintessential multicultural bowl — ***saimin*** (Japanese *dashi* broth, Chinese egg noodles and *char siu*, green onion, and local toppings) — hot, fast, cheap, and radically land-thrifty.
- ***Ekiben* and *Manapua* on the Electric Railways:** Adopting Japan's station boxed-lunch craft (*ekiben*, originated at Utsunomiya station in 1885, *Hist.*) alongside Hawaiian-Chinese steamed *manapua* buns, Pacifica's trans-Sierra, Shasta, Rockies, and Deseret electric lines sell regional cedar- and redwood-veneer lunch boxes on station platforms: smoked Columbia salmon with short-grain rice and pickled plum at Portland; citrus-roast pork or Monterey abalone in California; green-chile braised beans and smoked trout in the Rockies; taro-leaf steamed fish, *manapua*, and Kona coffee flasks on Oʻahu.
- **Food-First Eating Halls Under the 1888 Spirits Act (`CR-076`):** Under `L1032 ("Spirits Act")` and `L887 ("the earned cup")`, hard spirits live behind state Spirit Store counters, community halls stay dry on ordinary nights with coffee counters (`L1036 ("Coffee is the working day")`), and beer and wine remain licensed in private hands. Starved of the American saloon's cheap-whiskey tab, Pacifica's urban evening life settles into **food-first *izakaya*–*cantina*–*dai pai dong* eating halls** — pouring California wine, Pacific lager, or Sacramento-brewed *sake* alongside wok-fried clams in black-bean sauce, grilled skewers, dumplings, *poke*, tacos, and pickles — and late-night **coffee rooms** serving Folger's, Kona, and **Satori Kato**'s (`CT-SATORI-KATO`) soluble coffee.
- **Pacifica League & Polytechnic Dining Halls:** Boarding academies, Polytechnic commons, and Carquinez/San Francisco technical dormitories feed students from every province and partner nation on standardized Pacifican trays (rice or sourdough, wok greens, fish/tofu/beans, orchard fruit, kelp soup, milk), turning six regional pantries into one shared national palate inside two generations.

### A4. Reciprocal Table Traffic Back to Japan (1870s–1932) (`PROPOSED`)

Just as Japanese craft transforms Pacifica's table, Pacifican harvests reshape everyday urban Japan — complementing `CR-085`'s rice self-sufficiency (`L1704`) without breaking the Japanese rice farmer's price:
- **California and Oregon orchard and vineyard exports:** Navel oranges, lemons, raisins, walnuts, almonds, dried apricots, Willamette Valley Bing cherries and pears, and light table wines move into Yokohama, Kobe, Tokyo, and Shanghai on refrigerated Concord steamers.
- **Conserved Northwest fish:** Canned and frozen Alaska/Columbia salmon and Pacific halibut supplement Japanese coastal catches.
- **Hawaiian coffee, pineapple, and sugar:** Kona coffee (`L1036 ("Japanese students who meet in the coffee rooms")`), canned pineapple, and cane sugar fuel Tokyo and Osaka *kissaten* coffee houses.
- **Hokkaido dairy and *yōshoku*:** Sapporo's western-trained stations under **Satō Shōsuke** (`CT-SATO-SHOSUKE`) supply butter, condensed milk, and cheese alongside Pacifican wheat flour, feeding Japan's everyday *yōshoku* (Western-Japanese cooking: *karē raisu*, *tonkatsu*, *korokke*, *anpan*, and wheat *ramen*).

### A5. Public Health Dividends: Beriberi, Goitre, and the Pellagra Contrast (`APPROVED` historical anchors; institutional mechanics `PROPOSED`)

1. **Early Eradication of Naval Beriberi via Takaki Kanehiro (`CT-TAKAKI-KANEHIRO`, 1849–1920, *Hist.*):** In 1884, Imperial Japanese Navy Surgeon-General **Takaki Kanehiro** proved through the comparative training-ship voyage of *Tsukuba* (repeating *Ryūjō*'s 1882–83 Pacific route, where 169 of 376 crew fell ill and 25 died of beriberi) that supplementing polished white rice with barley, meat, fish, condensed milk, and vegetables cut beriberi to near zero — decades before thiamine (Vitamin B1) was chemically isolated (*Hist.*). Under the Concord's naval exchange (`C-1885-NAVAL-DIET`, `PROPOSED`), Takaki's 1884 ration tables are shared at once with the Pacifican Admiralty and merchant marine, keeping Pacifican, Japanese, and Philippine crews free of beriberi and carrying mixed barley-rice, bean, soy, and milk rations into Realm boarding schools and mining camps.
2. **No Rockies or Interior Goitre Belt (`PROPOSED`):** In our timeline, the American Great Lakes, Rockies, and Pacific Northwest formed an endemic "goitre belt" due to iodine-poor mountain and glacial soils until iodized salt was introduced in 1924 (*Hist.*). In Pacifica, routine dietary *kombu*/*dashi* stock, coastal fish carried inland by electric rail, and **kelp-meal supplements in Rockies dairy and livestock feed** prevent endemic inland goitre a generation early (reinforced later by iodized salt).
3. **Zero Pellagra — Contrast with the Rump Confederacy (`L1129`, `PROPOSED`):** Canon already notes that the rump Confederacy's cotton-mill villages "live with pellagra and hookworm" (`L1129 ("pellagra and hookworm")`) — pellagra being the niacin (Vitamin B3) deficiency caused by a monotonous un-nixtamalized cornmeal-and-fatback diet (*Hist.*). Pacifica's combined pantry — lime-treated Mexican *masa* (which unlocks bound niacin, *Hist.*), Pacific fish, poultry, beans, soy, rice bran, and universal coffee — eliminates pellagra completely. The Office of Public Measures prints continental morbidity tables without commentary; the numbers speak for themselves.
