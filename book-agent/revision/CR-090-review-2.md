## Findings

1. **Hamaguchi missing (`A2.4`, D-13).** The B4 fix seated Wakatsuki from July 1929 on the ground that no London Naval Treaty means no shooting of Hamaguchi; without the shooting Hamaguchi, Minseitō president since 1927, stays in office. Canon needs Wakatsuki only by 1931 (`L1800`); `L1650` reads as 1927. *Revision 4:* Hamaguchi July 1929 – April 1931, resigning ill and dying in August 1931 (reason `PROPOSED`); Wakatsuki April–December 1931.
2. **Saitō on the air on 15 May (`A5`).** Saitō takes office on 26 May; Takahashi is acting prime minister (`L1816`). *Revision 4:* Takahashi alone.
3. **Territorial limit in the Yokosuka bridge (`A5`, D-17).** "Offences on Japanese territory and waters" may not reach Kashiwagi's carriers, which threaten Oʻahu (`L1814`). *Revision 4:* jurisdiction by commission wherever the officers serve, fixed on the day of the offence; the court sits at a common-Navy station under Japanese law.
4. **The Peers vote of March 1931 (`A4.3`, D-16).** The historical defeat was 184–62, so a narrow passage needs a positive cause; the absence of Manchurian war fever cannot explain a vote taken six months before the Incident; the historical bill covered towns and villages only, so the prefectural extension is a further divergence. *Open:* awaiting the author's choice.
