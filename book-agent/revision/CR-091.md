## A. The change

### A1. The Structural Principle — Downward Mobility and the Small Butterfly Effect (`APPROVED` shape, D-1)

1. **One ruling dynasty, not two.** The magic of Pacifica's divergence rests on a small initial butterfly effect: one gifted Missouri–Taos frontiersman (Elias Thorne) intersecting with real historical figures and constitutional institutions. Having a second invented surname climb back into the Five in 1915, command a 50,000-man army, and hold one of the Realm's two atomic keys (`L1922 ("Polytechnic chief's key in the summer of 1932")`) undermines both that minimalist divergence and `CR-037`'s anti-clique examination safeguards.
2. **The counter-trajectory.** Instead of shadowing the Thorne–Kamehameha dynasty inside Thalassia's cabinet, the Vances move **downward and outward**:
   - The **Thornes** climb from a burned frontier homestead to three crowns, the Crystal Palace, and the Council of the Seal.
   - The **Vances** begin on the exact same 1841 Santa Fe trail (`L177 ("Santa Fe Expedition")`), never join the examined ruling class, and live out the next ninety years inside **the Pacific Companies (`CR-074`) and the outer railway perimeter**—absorbing the mud, salt, snow, and moral compromises that keep Thalassia's treaties clean on paper.
3. **One thematic fuse from 1846 to 1932.** Caleb Vance's January 1863 Rio Grande standing order (*"a chief who comes in under a flag goes home under one"*, `L509 ("Mangas Coloradas, who has come in under a flag")`) and the Pacific Companies' December 1863 walkout at Suzhou (**Article 9 / the Suzhou Article** of the Companies Act of 1888, line 138 of `CR-074.md`) establish the moral standard of Pacifica's frontier service. The Vance arc tests what happens to that standard when Pacifica itself grows into a great power whose treaties demand a **Second Suzhou** on the Altai railway in 1921.

### A2. The Three-Generation Perimeter Vance Lineage (1841–1932) (`APPROVED` shape, D-1; names & particulars `PROPOSED`/`OPEN`, D-4 & D-6)

The legacy `Marcus Vance I / II / III` numbering is retired so the lineage reads as distinct people shaped by distinct frontiers rather than a repeating staff archetype.

1. **Generation 1 — Caleb Vance (`CT-CALEB`, c.1816–October 1887): The Frontier Standard.**

   - Keeps Abigail Merritt's secret from 1851, brings Elias to her Texas door in 1863, and dies at Donner Pass in October 1887 stepping in front of Silas's shot aimed at the unrelated Soberanes, whispering *"Don't give it"* to preserve the Truce (`L959 ("Donner Pass in October 1887")`, `L1970 ("Caleb Vance and Marcus Vance I")`).
   - **Disposition of Marcus Vance I (`CT-MARCUS-I`, active c.1849–February 1878, `L794 ("Vladivostok in February 1878")`, D-6 `PROPOSED`/`OPEN`):** Marcus I remains Caleb's younger, **childless** brother who builds the regular General Staff, serves in the Northwest and Confederacy, and dies at Vladivostok in February 1878—ending the regular-army Vance branch cleanly in Generation 1 so no military-staff dynasty exists after 1878. *(OPEN alternative under D-6: reassign Marcus I's 1861–78 regular-army general role to a California-stationed historical officer such as Charles Stone or Edward Ord so Caleb is the sole founder of the Vance name.)*

2. **Generation 2 — Caleb's Son (`CT-VANCE-SURVEYOR`, born c.1856–60; given name `OPEN`, e.g. *Tomas* or *Daniel* Vance, D-4): The Pacific Companies Veteran.**
   - Replaces the nameless placeholder railway surveyor between Marcus I and Marcus III.
   - **No Carquinez pedestal:** Born into Caleb's frontier household (a Californio or borderland marriage is `PROPOSED`), he fails—or refuses—the Commissions Board's 1882 mathematics examination (`CR-071`) and never enters the Polytechnic estate. After witnessing Donner Pass in October 1887 (knowing Silas fired the shot that killed his father), he rejects Thalassia's patronage and joins the **Pacific Companies**, passing through Silas's inland-command school at Sacramento after the **Companies Act of 1888** (`L1003 ("labour law of 1888 fills in the article")`, `CR-074` D5).

   - **Petrograd and Kronstadt (1918–20):** Taken hostage by the Cheka in September 1918 when eleven younger advisers are shot (`L1467 ("oldest of the advisers, the surveyor Vance")`), he survives two years in captivity and is the first Pacifican brought up alive from the *Petropavlovsk*'s hostage deck after Kuroda Kiyoshi's fatal cut at Kronstadt in May 1920 (`L1516 ("first of the Pacifican advisers brought up")`). His survival is no longer a plot device for an invented father—it is the rescue of a forty-year veteran of Pacifica's outer rim.

3. **Generation 3 — Caleb's Grandson (`CT-MARCUS-III` retained as stable registry ID; numeral retired; born c.1886–90; given name `OPEN`, e.g. *Nathan* or *Jack* Vance, D-4): The Perimeter Captain.**

Patton's armoured train *Pele* (`L1502 ("a coalition armoured train from the northern wing")`), fighting beside **Anatoly Pepelyaev's** Tomsk riflemen at **Perm (December 1918, `L1485 ("Pepelyaev's corps, the bulk of the Siberian Army")`)**, and holding the **Volga bridge approaches at Zelenodolsk (June 1919, `L1490 ("holds the Volga bridge at Zelenodolsk")`)** alongside **Khamit Tokhtamyshev's** Alash cavalry while the wounded, railway families, and Czechoslovak Legion echelons clear the span.

### A3. Separating the 1915 Polytechnic Chief Seat and Summer-1932 Nuclear Key (`PROPOSED`, D-5)

Evicting the Vance surname from the Five cleanly resolves two canon tensions at once: `CR-037`'s anti-clique principle and `CR-086`'s open question of who holds the Polytechnic chief's nuclear key in summer 1932 (`L1922`, `L2093 ("Polytechnic chief's key in the summer of 1932")`).

1. **The office stays non-hereditary.** When **Rafael Soberanes** (`CT-SOBERANES`) retires in 1915 at sixty-eight (`L1366 ("Soberanes retires in 1915")`), the Polytechnic Chief / General Staff seat and overall command of the 50,000-man Pacifican Expeditionary Corps (`L1448 ("Pacifican Expeditionary Corps of 50,000")`, `L1452 ("Vance the line and the depots")`, `L1520 ("command he never wanted")`) pass to an **unrelated examined successor**—never a Vance.
2. **Who signs the overnight Altai wire:** It is this institutional Polytechnic Chief—executing Julian's Harbin mandate from headquarters—who cables the 02:00 handover order down to Captain Vance at the Altai siding, sharpening the structural contrast between **the clean desk at Thalassia/Omsk** and **the Company man standing in the ballast**.
3. **Who holds the summer-1932 key (`CR-086` `L1922`):** Because the Polytechnic Chief is not killed in May 1932, the chief's key remains continuously in lawful hands through the 1932 Final Act—closing `CR-086`'s vacancy wrinkle cleanly.
4. **Candidate identities for the 1915 Polytechnic Chief (`OPEN`, D-5):**
   - **Option 5a (Historical 1880-cohort logistics mind):** A real American logistics organizer repurposed plausibly into Pacifica's examination service—most cleanly **George C. Marshall** (*Hist.* born 31 December 1880 – 1959; exact birth-year match for the former `CT-MARCUS-III`, a methodical, blunt staff logistician famous for Philippines small-unit service and speaking unvarnished truth to power) or **Malin Craig** (*Hist.* born 1875 in Missouri – 1945; career logistics/Chief of Staff officer).
   - **Option 5b (Unrelated examined Pacifican):** A newly minted first-generation Carquinez/Vallejo College graduate (for example, Hawaiian, Californio, or Chinese-Pacifican) whose rise proves Soberanes's anonymous-script examination system (`CR-037`) produced leaders with no founding-era surname behind them.

### A4. The Second Suzhou — The Altai Siding Safe-Conduct Betrayal (Spring 1921) (`APPROVED` core, D-2; particulars `PROPOSED`, D-7)

The legacy **1921 Altai gas massacre** (`L1533 ("decisive gas massacre takes place in the Altai")`)—a vestigial plot beat from the pre-revision draft—is retired and replaced by **the Altai Siding Safe-Conduct Betrayal ("the Second Suzhou")**, fought and lost along the **Altai Railway** (*Hist.* opened 21 October 1915: Novonikolayevsk–Barnaul–**Semipalatinsk**), the single steel track linking Tomsk's Siberian regionalists directly to Alash Orda's capital.

#### 1. The Three Historical Leaders (`PROPOSED` elevation from `CR-041` mentions)
All three figures are real historical participants already named in canon under `CR-041` (`L1448`, `L1463 ("commission under Sapozhnikov closes its sittings with Bukeikhanov, Yermekov and Tokhtamyshev")`, `L1485`):
- **Anatoly Nikolayevich Pepelyaev** (`CT-ANATOLY-PEPELYAEV`; *Hist.* 15 July 1891 – 14 January 1938; dies spring 1921 in canon): Born in Tomsk; graduated from the **Siberian Cadet Corps in Omsk (1908)** and Pavlovsk Military School (1910); decorated WWI veteran of the 42nd Siberian Rifle Regiment; led the May 1918 Tomsk uprising and captured **Perm on 24 December 1918** (`L1485`). In a timeline where the failed November 1918 Omsk coup keeps Admiral Kolchak from becoming Supreme Ruler (`L1476 ("declines it in front of them")`), Pepelyaev never serves a monarchist dictatorship; he remains the beloved, plain-coated frontline champion of Tomsk's Siberian regionalism and the green-and-white Siberian flag—believing Julian's 10 September 1918 pledge (`L1470 ("promises a Siberia on the Pacifican model")`) means Siberia has won its own 1846.
- **Khamit Negmatullauly Tokhtamyshev** (`CT-KHAMIT-TOKHTAMYSHEV`; *Hist.* c.1885/1890 – December 1919; survives into spring 1921 in canon because the coalition holds the Volga and wins): Descended from the Atigay clan of the Argyn tribe in Omsk uezd; graduated from the **Siberian Cadet Corps in Omsk in 1908—the exact same graduating class as Anatoly Pepelyaev** (*Hist.*)—and Konstantinovsky Artillery School; WWI Russian infantry/artillery captain who arrived in Semipalatinsk in mid-1918 to head **Alash Orda's Military Council** and raise the **1st Alash Cavalry Regiment** (`L1463`). Unsentimental, disciplined, and fiercely protective of Kazakh land and dignity, he views Pacifica's 1851–52 Native land compacts (`CR-033`) and the printed August 1918 treaty as his people's one shield against settler dispossession.
- **Alimkhan Abeuovich Yermekov** (`CT-ALIMKHAN-YERMEKOV`; *Hist.* 25 May 1891 – 22 June 1970; survives 1921 in canon): Born in Karkaralinsk (Semipalatinsk oblast); mining-engineering and mathematics student at the **Tomsk Technological Institute** (entered 1912, befriended Siberian regionalist Grigory Potanin) and Alash Orda co-negotiator at Omsk (`L1463`). A quiet statistician and map-maker who speaks the exact technical language of **Vance Gen 2's** survey notebooks, he treats the Office of Public Measures' printed 1918 treaty as binding geometry.

#### 2. Why the Reversal Is Geopolitically Unavoidable
No villainy or panic is needed; four rational, incompatible pressures lock shut on the Altai branch line in spring 1921:
1. **The holdouts' stand:** After Pacifica's pay-chests and newsreels steer the **Tomsk Regional Duma vote** into Wrangel's federation (`L1541 ("Siberian Regional Duma at Tomsk, elected under the guarantor's eye")`) and dissolve the 1918 Siberia–Alash union **without ever putting a ballot to Alash**, ordering Tokhtamyshev's regiments broken up into Emba oil guards and a Turkestan cavalry division (`L1541`), Pepelyaev's Tomsk veterans and Tokhtamyshev's Semipalatinsk horsemen refuse to disband. They occupy an Altai Railway junction and grain elevators between Barnaul and Semipalatinsk—shooting at no one, demanding only the unbought plebiscite for Siberia and an actual vote for Alash promised in August 1918.
2. **Wrangel's constraint:** Federal Russia is starving and brittle; Kutepov and the conservative officer corps will overthrow Wrangel if he lets a mutinous Tomsk general and an armed Kazakh force hold a railway and dictate federal terms. Wrangel makes custody of the mutinous officers a non-negotiable condition for signing the **Treaty of Harbin**.
3. **Tokyo's and Julian's constraint:** After the spring 1920 Nikolayevsk massacre (`L1541`), the Japanese Army is eager for any pretext to push west of Baikal. If an armed standoff lingers on the Altai branch beside Mongolia and Japanese line-guard battalions, Harbin collapses, Japanese divisions move west unilaterally, Siberian grain stops flowing to Huludao (`CR-082`, `L1495 ("count Siberia's grain on the railway board's contracts")`), and Kiyoshi's sacrifice at Kronstadt yields a second civil war.
4. **Why only Vance can disarm them:** Federal Russian regiments will not fire on Pepelyaev's Perm veterans, and a Japanese assault would ignite steppe guerrilla war. Pepelyaev and Tokhtamyshev will only come down from the siding to talk to **Vance Gen 3**—their comrade from Perm and Zelenodolsk, wearing the Pacific Companies badge of the Suzhou Article.

#### 3. The Act, the Cover-Up, and Victoria's Rupture
- **The safe-conduct broken:** Walking down the ballast alone under a white flag, Vance signs a **Pacific Company safe-conduct** modelled verbatim on Caleb's 1863 Fort Craig and Mangas Coloradas terms: arms stacked into Pacifican custody, safe passage under Company guard to Harbin or California, no handover to Russian courts-martial. Pepelyaev and Tokhtamyshev stack their rifles because they trust *him*.
- **The 02:00 wire:** Overnight, encrypted headquarters orders arrive from the Polytechnic Chief under Julian's Harbin mandate: federal Russia asserts sovereign jurisdiction over its own officers; Japanese battalions are steaming toward the next junction; Vance's Company must stand down from the siding at 04:00, transfer custody to Wrangel's federal gendarmerie, and sign the Office of Public Measures dispatch.
- **The choice:** Invoking Article 9 (**the Suzhou Article**) and holding the boxcars at gunpoint against federal Russian troops means blowing up Harbin and risking war with Japan. Vance blinks. Before 04:00 he forces the civilian **Yermekov** into a southbound mail-car coal tender—saving one friend, who refuses to shake his hand and returns to Semipalatinsk as an embittered witness beside Bukeikhanov inside rump Alash (`L1541`). At dawn, Wrangel's gendarmerie takes custody; a drumhead field court executes **Pepelyaev**, **Tokhtamyshev**, and a dozen Siberian and Alash officers behind the water tower (echoing Novoselov's September 1918 murder in the Zagorodnaya grove, `CR-045`, `L1473 ("shoots the one who would not sign his resignation, Novoselov")`).
- **The triple violation:** The **Office of Public Measures** publishes the sanitized bulletin listing the dead as *"irregulars killed resisting disarmament in the Altai"* (`L1537`). In a single morning, Pacifica breaks **Caleb's 1863 flag-of-truce oath**, **the Pacific Companies' 1863/1888 Suzhou Article**, and **Elias's 1851 *On the Public Arts* rule against falsifying an account**.
- **Victoria's 1921 renunciation (`L1535`):** When Vance brings Caleb's grandson's broken safe-conduct, Pepelyaev's green-and-white ribbon, and the true ledger to Honolulu, Victoria's charge against Julian becomes a single, airtight moral chain: the 1918 oath, the 1919 grain-and-Moscow trade, the 1920 nuclear seal, the unasked partition of Alash, and a **Second Suzhou** buried inside the Office of Public Measures she and Noe Ward built. Haunted by her own 1887 Donner silence—which taught Julian that a clean lie is an acceptable price for peace—she renounces her executive standing without going public. Julian withholds the Altai siding record from **Hirohito** (`L1537`), keeping their personal friendship genuine while concealing what Harbin cost.

You only keep what you have men standing on"*) and tells him Caleb's 1863 Mangas Coloradas story—earning Tokhtamyshev's hard-won trust.
3. **Christmas Eve on the Frozen Kama *(Perm, 24 December 1918 — Vance Gen 3, `CT-ANATOLY-PEPELYAEV`)***: At thirty below, steam freezing off armoured train *Pele*'s cylinders, 27-year-old Pepelyaev—in a plain enlisted sheepskin coat with a green-and-white Siberian ribbon pinned inside his lapel—splits scalding tea and salt pork on the footplate with Vance's Pacific Company crew. Inside captured Perm, Pepelyaev walks alone between looting Cossacks and three captured Red lathe-fitters, pistol holstered, stripping the Cossack captain of command on the spot (*"If we hang the men who run the lathes, we aren't building Siberia—we're just changing the color of the Cheka"*). That night he asks Vance how a handful of Californios and frontiersmen held off two empires in 1846.
4. **The Signal-Box After the Bridge *(Zelenodolsk, June 1919 — Vance Gen 3, `CT-ANATOLY-PEPELYAEV`, `CT-KHAMIT-TOKHTAMYSHEV`)***: Under Frunze's shellfire, Vance's Pacific Company holds the Volga bridge approaches while the hospital cars and last Czechoslovak echelons clear the span—only holding because Pepelyaev's Tomsk riflemen refuse to pull back ahead of the wounded and Tokhtamyshev's Alash horsemen pack ammunition up through the mud on steppe ponies. Afterward in a splintered signal-box, knowing Vance's father is still a Cheka hostage in Petrograd, Tokhtamyshev presses a small steppe compass into Vance's palm (*"First we bring your old surveyor out of Petrograd; then you help Yermekov lay our rail spur to Semipalatinsk"*). A year later, after Kiyoshi's cut on the *Petropavlovsk* brings Vance's father up alive, the three drink to Kiyoshi like brothers.
5. **The Altai Siding — The Second Suzhou *(Barnaul–Semipalatinsk Line, Spring 1921 — Vance Gen 3, `CT-ANATOLY-PEPELYAEV`, `CT-KHAMIT-TOKHTAMYSHEV`, `CT-ALIMKHAN-YERMEKOV`)***: After the steered Tomsk vote dissolves the Siberia–Alash union without asking Alash, Vance walks down the frozen ballast alone with a white cloth and a Pacific Company safe-conduct modelled on Caleb's 1863 terms. Inside the heated boxcar, Yermekov lays the printed 1918 treaty beside Vance's safe-conduct; Pepelyaev says quietly, *"I don't trust Julian's cabinet, and I don't trust Wrangel's colonels. I trust you because I saw you at Zelenodolsk."* They stack their rifles. At 02:00 the headquarters wire orders Vance to turn the siding over to Wrangel's federal gendarmerie at 04:00 or blow up Harbin and bring Japanese battalions west of Baikal. Vance breaks: in the dark he shoves the civilian Yermekov into a southbound mail-car tender (Yermekov refuses his hand); at dawn Pepelyaev unpins the green-and-white Siberian ribbon and drops it beside Vance's boots, and Tokhtamyshev asks quietly, *"Did your grandfather do it this way?"* Behind the water tower, the drumhead volley fires; Vance signs the falsified Office of Public Measures dispatch, then carries the real safe-conduct and ribbon to Victoria.
6. **The Freezing Switchyard *(Huludao / Mukden Line, Autumn 1931 — Vance Gen 3)***: Ten years after stepping back from the boxcar door in the Altai, Vance—now an ageing Pacific Company captain on the Manchurian railhead—faces down armed Restoration officers demanding troop trains in violation of Julian's 1931 prohibition (`L1810`). Surrounded by his multi-ethnic Company crew (Californio, Filipino, and Chinese railwaymen), he locks the switches and refuses to step off the iron—knowing he has signed his own death warrant for May 1932, and sending the Chronicler's copper-bound parcel (the 1921 Altai safe-conduct, Pepelyaev's ribbon, and the unredacted siding ledger) south on the last coastal steamer.
