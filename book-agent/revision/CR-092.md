## A. The change

### A1. The Vocabulary of the Two Agons and the Two-Stamp Apex (`APPROVED`, D-1)

Pacifica rejects hereditary aristocracy without flattening society into one ladder of honour. Instead, the Realm runs on **two co-equal, equally unforgiving agons**—two distinct modes of public proof—with the highest offices reserved for those who survive **both**. Because the Pacific Companies' trials of body, nerve, language, and command under fire are every bit as brutal as Carquinez's examinations, calling only the Polytechnic *"the tested"* is rejected in both narration and dialogue. Equally, the divide between the two agons is **horizontal, not vertical**: entering the Companies is not downward mobility, and passing Carquinez alone does not confer complete civic authority.

| Track | Formal & Proud Self-Names | The Other Side's Sneers | Institutional Root |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Bench / Examination Agon** | **The Examined** (statutory, `L863 ("the examined take it over")`) · **Bench men** (exam bench, assay bench, laboratory bench, judge's bench) · **Script men** (anonymous numbered scripts) | **Mandarins** (Senate floor, frontier press, *Deseret News*—pointing to the Qing-inspired numbered scripts) · **Ink-men** or **Slide-rules** (Sacramento barrack slang) | `CR-037` anonymous numbered scripts (`L857 ("known to their examiners only by number")`); `CR-072` (`L863`) |
| **The Field / Companies Agon** | **Company men** (formal, `CR-074` A1) · **Roll men** or **Field men** (the Roll of Deeds read at county festivals, `CR-074` D12) · **Privateers** (saloon swagger after 1878, deplored by the Companies Office and worn with pride by the men, `CR-074` A3) | **Bounty-men** (mocking the 10-year land warrants) · **Camp-captains** or **Rifle-votes** (mocking Company commissions that carry the third vote under the 1888 Franchise Law without a civil examination) | `CR-074` (`L873 ("Pacific Companies have served abroad")`, `L1003 ("labour law of 1888 fills in the article")`); `CR-021` (`L999 ("1888 Franchise Law gives each adult citizen")`) |
| **The Apex (succeeding at *both*)** | **Two-stamp** or **Twice-proved** (drawn directly from the Carquinez State Assay Office and San Francisco Mint—gold proved by both fire and weight—and echoing Pacifica's civic usage of *mana*, `L887 ("Public provings of works")`) | *(Rarely sneered at; two-stamp leaders are the only figures both sides instinctively trust to hold the harness)* | `CR-034` Assay Office; `CR-049` civic *mana* (`L886 ("civic usage of mana")`, `L887`) |

**Canonical rule of the apex:** Pacifica's highest leadership belongs to **two-stamp** figures who earn standing in both worlds—**Elias Thorne** (surveyor/assayer and Olompali/borderlands veteran), **Rafael Soberanes** (Carquinez at fourteen and Manila/Vladivostok/Keelung sea commander), **Kuroda Kiyoshi** (examined fleet commander and Kronstadt swordsman), **Kuroda Kiyotada** (Carquinez + Etajima + western-squadron sea command), **Julian** (four-language examined ruler with personal political nerve), and **Keawe Kahananui** (examined engineer who proves himself in Hilo's mud, water, and public fights).

### A2. Pacifica's 2×2 Political Compass (`APPROVED` framework, D-2; placements `PROPOSED`, D-3)

Pacifica's domestic politics cannot be read on an Atlantic left–right line. From the 1880s through 1932, every major argument in the Realm plays out across **two genuinely orthogonal axes**:

1. **Vertical Axis — Scale & Ecology (`CR-082`, `L1032 ("two figures beside its price")`, `L1064 ("sufficers lose the argument on numbers")`):**
   - **Sufficers (North):** Measure carrying capacity first—John Wesley Powell's 22-million-acre irrigable ceiling (`L703 ("John Wesley Powell heads the land and water office")`), selection over headcount, salmon passes on the Columbia, dry-land yield, clean harbours, and living within what the land and compacts can honestly sustain.
   - **Provisioners (South):** Read the Office's tables as a target to beat—scale, 2.0% population growth after 1908, synthetic nitrogen, Siberian grain contracts, continental credit, and staying big enough in industry and ordnance never to be overwhelmed by Atlantic empires.
2. **Horizontal Axis — Mode of Proof / The Two Agons (`CR-037`, `CR-074`):**
   - **The Bench / Examined ("Mandarins") (West):** Anonymous scripts, double-entry audits, unified gauges, universal statutes, institutional continuity over personal loyalty.
   - **The Field / Company Men ("Privateers" / "Bounty-men") (East):** Deeds before witnesses, keeping one's spoken word under a white flag, the Suzhou Article, county self-rule, and local compacts.

#### Institutional Mapping — Why No Major Institution Is a Monolith

1. **The Five (`CR-027`, `CR-052`, `CR-058-R1`, `CR-079`):** The Five do not sit uniformly in one corner. The **Polytechnic/General Staff**, **Power** (Tesla → Ryan), and **Light & Matter Works** (Hillebrand → Langmuir) anchor **Provisioner × Bench**; the **Champions' seat** (Logan Turner → Doheny) deliberately pulls **Provisioner × Field** industrialists and oilmen into the Crown's unwritten arrangement; and the **Press seat** (Hearst → Lippmann) operates as the noisy public arena where all four quadrants fight for opinion.
2. **The Universities (`CR-034`–`CR-036`, `CR-050-R1`, `CR-082`):** Pacifica's higher learning splits visibly across the compass:
   - **Carquinez Polytechnic** (funded by the public gold royalty) is the flagship of **Provisioner × Bench** statecraft.
   - **Ralston College** (funded by private San Francisco merchant silver, `L881`) is its sharpest **Sufficer × Bench** check—competing with Carquinez in physics and chemistry and signing the 1910 and 1927 Hetch Hetchy renewals under written protest (`L1312 ("work moves behind a wall")`).
   - **Vallejo College at Monterey** (`L1022`, founded 1889) exists specifically to bind the Bench's county-born graduates into a common Realm officer and administrative culture—and serves as the nursery for **two-stamp** leaders.
   - **The Pacifica League colleges** (denominational colleges paid only for examined results) and the **Colegio de Santa Fe** (`L1186`, dry-land farming under Widtsoe) anchor regional and Sufficer inquiry outside the Bay Area.
   - **The Sacramento Pacific Companies School** (`L1003`, `CR-074` D5) is the proud counter-university of the **Field** agon: admission by trial of body and nerve, rank by deeds before witnesses, and working foreign languages earned for real postings abroad.
3. **The Pacific Companies — Open vs. Raised (`CR-074`):** Even inside the Field half, a sharp fault line runs between **Open Companies** (multi-ethnic units where Anglo, Californio, Chinese, Japanese, Hawaiian, and Filipino comrades serve shoulder-to-shoulder—generally outward-looking **Provisioner × Field** bridges) and **Raised Companies** (chartered to Native nations, Deseret members, or single irrigation districts—rooted in **Sufficer × Field** local loyalty, where a settler unit like the Feather River company carries real nativist volatility in 1886 and 1906, `L924 ("night mob on the Feather")`, `L1238`).
4. **The Prosperity Party (`CR-072`, `L630`, `L863`):** The Party is not a single quadrant—it is **the grand four-quadrant brokerage harness**. Founded by Broderick and Judah in 1866 (`L630`), captured by the Examined county slates around 1880 (`L863`), and incorporating **Deseret's People's Caucus** (after resisting the 1879 Collar, `L861 ("The Committee party's papers call it the Collar")`), Hawaii's **Hui**, and the interior trades and lodges, the Party survives only so long as the Bench's county boards and the Field's interior voters remain convinced the other side is playing by the published rules.
5. **The Office of Public Measures and the Reserve Bank (`CR-049`, `CR-057`, `CR-082`):** The **Office of Public Measures** is the neutral referee that makes the compass governable: Sufficers cite its tables as ceilings, Provisioners cite them as targets, and Company men trust its annual rolls of dead, wounded, refusals, and land warrants (`CR-074` D2). Falsifying an Office return breaks the one floor all four quadrants stand on.

### A3. The Bridge Vacuum and Why 1921 Threatens Julian's Balancing Act (1887–1932) (`PROPOSED`, D-4)

Julian's life task as Sovereign is holding Pacifica's four quadrants in a single harness without triggering another Donner Pass. The domestic danger of the **1921 Altai crisis** (`L1535 ("Julian approves a falsified report")`, `L1537 ("false figures are published through the Office")`) comes directly from how the compass shifts between 1887 and 1921:

1. **1887 — Donner Pass (`L959 ("Donner Pass in October 1887")`):** The first open clash between the unexamined frontier (Silas) and the Examined Bench (Soberanes). **Caleb Vance** (Field) takes Silas's bullet to save **Soberanes** (two-stamp), keeping the Truce of 1887 alive at the cost of a twenty-five-year private lie.
2. **1906–1911 — Testing the Harness (`L1238`):** Harlan Tate's nativist Sacramento board tries to split interior settler loyalties away from the Examined consensus; open-company veterans who served beside Japanese and Chinese comrades break ranks with the nativists (`CR-074` G), and Julian's 1911 secret ballot breaks the county machines—concentrating more of the brokerage burden on the Crown itself.
3. **1915–1920 — Both Two-Stamp Military Bridges Vanish:** **Rafael Soberanes** retires at sixty-eight in **1915** (`L1366 ("Soberanes retires in 1915")`), and **Kuroda Kiyoshi** dies on the *Petropavlovsk* at Kronstadt in **May 1920** (`L1516 ("dies on the quarterdeck in the morning light")`). Within five years, the Realm loses both of the towering **two-stamp** military figures whom Bench officers and Company men alike trusted instinctively.
4. **Spring 1921 — Why the Altai Betrayal Threatens to Topple It All:** When a pure **Provisioner × Bench** headquarters orders a pure **Sufficer × Field** Pacific Company captain to hand over surrendered Siberian and Alash officers in violation of **Article 9 (the Suzhou Article)**—and then covers the breach through a falsified **Office of Public Measures** dispatch—the scandal is domestic dynamite:
   - **Across the 124 counties**, 25,000+ Pacific Company veterans, Sacramento school alumni, and Harlan Tate's interior Senate bloc (`L1647 ("county boards want leverage")`) would read the truth as proof that the *Mandarins* murdered the Companies' honour code and corrupted the neutral referee to protect a cabinet treaty.
   - **Inside Carquinez, Vallejo College, and the Five**, any Company captain invoking the Suzhou Article at gunpoint against headquarters orders would be read as a Donner-style praetorian mutiny threatening to drag the Realm into war with federal Russia and Japan.
   - **Cross-quadrant moral shock:** On conscience, **Victoria Kamāmalu** (*Sufficer × Bench*) and the Company captain (*Sufficer × Field*) converge against **Julian** and the Polytechnic Chief (*Provisioner × Bench*).
   - **Why both Julian and Victoria keep the lid shut in public:** Julian falsifies the Office return not merely to save the Treaty of Harbin abroad, but because an open Bench-vs.-Companies war in 1921 would split the Prosperity Party and snap the 1887 Truce in half. Victoria condemns him in private and renounces her executive standing—yet refuses to denounce him publicly for the exact same reason she kept the Donner secret for twenty-five years.
5. **1931–1932 — Holding by Inches:** By the end of the novel, Julian's balancing act survives—not because the dual divide is healed, but because every quadrant still finds a concrete win inside the Realm: **Keawe's** Lands and Waters Charter answers the **Sufficers**, the Senate's refusal of a fourth crown checks **Provisioner** overreach, the **Reserve Bank** holds gold parity against pressure, and in autumn 1931 a Bench chief and a Company captain hold the Manchurian rail line together across ruined private trust. The sealed copper cylinders ensure that the cost of keeping the compass from tearing apart is never erased.

### A4. Educating a Two-Stamp Sovereign: Julian Thorne's Apprenticeship, "David Kahale" at Unsan, and the Tokyo Year (1886–1907) (`APPROVED` shape, D-6; closes Unresolved #13 at `L2045`)

If Pacifica reserves its highest rungs for **two-stamp** leaders (`A1`), canon must show how a twenty-one-year-old raised by Victoria Kamāmalu and Rafael Soberanes actually earned both stamps—and how a **Sufficer** regent's hānai son became a **Provisioner** Sovereign. This closes **Unresolved #13** (`L2045 ("CR-021 settles his four-language education")`) inside `CR-021`'s fixed chronology (`born 1886` · `Tokyo 1905–06` · `accedes November 1907`).

#### 1. Childhood and the Bench Stamp (1886–1903, ages 0–17)
- **Four languages, distinct sources (`L971 ("Japanese with the naval party")`):** Julian grows up speaking Hawaiian with Victoria in Honolulu (learning *aliʻi* bearing, open-ocean swimming, and Sufficer stewardship), English with tutors, Spanish among Soberanes's Monterey circle (surveying and maps), and **Satsuma/Etajima officer-register Japanese from the naval party** at Honolulu and Pearl Harbor—never court Japanese from Victoria.
- **No royal bypass of the Bench:** Remembering Donner Pass (when Julian was one), Victoria refuses to let him inherit authority on paper without surviving the examination bench. Between fourteen and seventeen, Julian sits Carquinez's **anonymous numbered scripts** (`L857`) and completes Carquinez and Vallejo College coursework in engineering, statistics, and constitutional law—earning his **Bench stamp** honestly rather than by private tutorial.

#### 2. Incognito at Unsan: "Private David Kahale" (Spring 1904 – Spring 1905, ages 18–19)
- **An airtight cover:** Neither *"Julian"* nor *"Ward"* nor Maui can hide him, since Noe Ward's identity and her son's name and age are known across the Realm. Instead, Julian enlists at eighteen under the ordinary name **David Kahale**, listed on the Companies Office roll as **twenty years old** (matching Elias's build) from **Hilo, Hawaiʻi**—whose docks and Japanese plantation families make his Hawaiian-English and Satsuma-inflected Japanese unremarkable. Officially, the court gazette announces a quiet year of private engineering and constitutional reading in Monterey and the Sierra under Soberanes's circle prior to Tokyo. Only Victoria, Soberanes, and the Companies Office commissioner hold the sealed cross-reference.
- **The Unsan detachment and elite-team culture (`L1164 ("Unsan concession granted by Korea in 1897")`, `CR-074` F4):** Under `CR-074` D4 and D8, an eighteen-year-old holds no commission. Private Kahale serves as an enlisted junior surveyor-clerk and rifleman in a **14-man outlying guard post** twelve miles up a mountain creek from Unsan's Taracol headquarters. The Company men are not boorish saloon mercenaries; their demeanour resembles an **elite sports team and its seasoned coaches**—proud, athletic, competitive, and fair, led by a composed fifty-year-old Keelung and Borneo veteran Sergeant who holds no blanket resentment toward Carquinez, simply measuring every newcomer by stamina, clean gear, and reliability under watch.
- **Everyday bunkhouse life:** Because Victoria kept him riding, shooting, and swimming open Hawaiian surf from childhood, "Kahale" quickly matches the squad's best marksmen and hauls wet mine-timbers without complaint; the men affectionately dub him **"Hilo"** or **"the Judge."** What feels quietly unusual about him is his steadiness—reading Thucydides and Korean primers by oil-lamp after watch, writing crisp shift reports, listening to older men without interrupting, and curbing a winter camp-fever outbreak in the Korean miners' bunkhouses by digging shifted latrines and boiling water barrels on Noe Ward's health-board methods without lecturing anyone. In return, twelve months around the stove—mending harness, playing cards, and listening to men weigh irrigation water shares and second five-year terms for an eighty-acre warrant—gives him an unfiltered education in what Pacifica's laws feel like from the bottom of a pay-book.
- **The trial by fire (earned chain of command, *Hist.* April 1904 anchor):**
  - *The Cossacks at the Culvert (April 1904):* When Russian Cossack cavalry push south across the Yalu into Unsan (*Hist.*) while the main Company Captain is twelve miles south at Taracol escorting civilian families toward Anju (*Hist.*), thirty Cossack raiders ride into the upper valley demanding draft mules and Korean miners as porters. The Sergeant—firmly in command—speaks English, Spanish, and Tagalog, no French or Russian, and sends Kahale onto the timber bridge strictly as his **unarmed interpreter** under fourteen sighted rifles. When the Cossack sotnik rides his horse up to Kahale's chest to rattle him, Julian stands utterly still and lays out the exact tactical arithmetic in calm, peer-register French (fourteen breech-loaders behind stone walls, a dynamite magazine, Japanese scouts six miles south at Chongju), offering a neutral voucher for four sacks of oats and zero Koreans. The Cossack takes the oats and rides north. Inside the mill-house, the Sergeant nods once—*"Good nerves, Kahale."*
  - *The Night Ambush (Delegated Authority):* Two nights later, irregulars/straggling foragers strike the outpost in the dark and fire the Korean dry-house. The Sergeant takes a rifle ball through the thigh at the culvert and the senior corporal goes down. Under fire, Julian drags the bleeding Sergeant behind an iron ore-cart and clamps a clean tourniquet. Grey with shock, the Sergeant grips his wrist and transfers command lawfully: *"Hold the culvert till daylight, Kahale. Keep 'em off the Koreans."* Julian holds the dark line for five freezing hours, taking a grazing burn on his left forearm pulling miners from the shed, until the Taracol relief column arrives at dawn.
  - *The Japanese Quartermaster (May 1904):* Weeks later at Taracol HQ, the Company Captain calls Private Kahale forward as his interpreter opposite a **Japanese First Army** quartermaster colonel attempting to requisition Unsan's carts, coal, and Korean workers for the Yalu crossing. Julian translates with cool precision in **Satsuma/Etajima officer-register Japanese** (`L971`), quoting the 1897 concession and the 1871/1887 equality treaties. When the startled Army colonel snaps where a mining-guard private learned to speak like a staff officer, Julian answers cleanly without breaking cover: *"Honolulu, Colonel. Half the instructors on the docks are retired Satsuma sailors."* He never oversteps his captain; both men simply walk away knowing they stood beside unmistakable *mana* in a private's coat.
- **Lasting friendships and the root of the Provisioner turn:** Serving out the full brutal winter of 1904–05, Julian watches passing Russian and Japanese armies strip northern Korean villages bare while Unsan's small neutral rice store runs dry because Korea lacks the railways, nitrogen, and heavy workshops to tell giant empires *no*. That winter—before he ever reaches Tokyo—converts Victoria's Sufficer pupil into a **Provisioner**. He leaves Unsan with two lifelong bonds:
  1. **The Coach-Sergeant:** Invalided from frontier duty by his culvert wound, the Sergeant becomes an instructor at the **Sacramento Pacific Companies school**. Recognizing *"David Kahale"* in the 1907 Sovereign's portrait, he tells his fellow instructors only *"He's two-stamp; let it rest"*—and years later Sovereign Julian walks straight past Sacramento's honour guard to shake the limping old coach's hand.
  2. **A Calamba Bunkhouse Comrade (`CR-074` F8):** A Filipino rifleman from Luzon (son of a tenant evicted at Calamba in 1890–91 serving for his friar-estate warrant) who shares his blanket in the April freeze and shows Julian honestly how the Philippine land board looks from below. Three years later he stands quietly in Manila's crowd when Julian swears the Philippine Crown oath (`L1278 ("Philippine Cortes offers the Philippine crown")`), and for twenty years afterward sends Julian one plain, unceremonious letter a year from his redeemed farm.

#### 3. Across the Strait: The Tokyo Year (Spring 1905 – Spring 1906, ages 19–20, `L1258`)
- **From tin plates to lacquer:** Finishing his one-year Unsan engagement in spring 1905 without breaking cover, Julian sails quietly from Chemulpo to Yokohama and steps ashore as nineteen-year-old **Julian Thorne**, attached formally to the **Pacifican Legation and Imperial Japanese Navy liaison staff in Tokyo** (`L1258 ("year in Tokyo attached to the legation and navy")`), the Unsan burn scar hidden under a starched cuff. His bunkhouse habits—listening without interrupting, checking physical supply lines rather than taking briefings at face value—set him apart immediately.
- **Kuroda Kiyoshi and the Unsan echo (May–Summer 1905):** Arriving right as his older half-brother **Kuroda Kiyoshi** wins his May 1905 cruiser victory over Russia's *Petropavlovsk* squadron (`L1272 ("Kuroda Kiyoshi claims the crown")`), Julian works around Yokosuka and Kure and sizes up Kiyoshi's effortless two-stamp command over Japanese naval officers—spotting the proud constituency that will test the Crown in early 1907. At a summer 1905 reception for returning First Army staff, Julian briefly crosses paths with the **quartermaster colonel** from Taracol; neither speaks of it, yet the colonel pauses mid-bow at the familiar Satsuma cadence and unblinking gaze, unable to prove a thing and treating the nineteen-year-old heir with sudden gravity.
- **Urajio and Korea from the ground up (Autumn 1905):** During the direct **Urajio peace** (`L1231 ("Russia negotiates directly")`, `L1236 ("Russian indemnity payments")`), when Tokyo's Army wing pushes to make Korea a protectorate, Julian feeds exact ground-level intelligence on Pyongan's roads, magistrates, and grain stores to the Pacifican envoy—reinforcing Victoria's insistence that **Korea stay an independent guaranteed state** (`L1234 ("Korea remains an independent guaranteed state")`), while seeing firsthand why Japan's Army resents Pacifica cutting its mainland wars short (`CR-090` A1).
- **Walking the yards, Saionji, and locking in the Provisioner mind (Winter 1905 – Spring 1906):** Keeping his Unsan habit of inspecting ground himself, Julian walks Yokosuka's dry-docks, Shimbashi's railway yards, Kanda's bookshops, and Kyushu coal piers in plain wool, learning that Japan is already an industrial peer (`CR-031-R1`, `CR-088`) who must be bound by equal institutions rather than patronage. After **Saionji Kinmochi** becomes Prime Minister in January 1906 (`L1258`) and Julian hears **Takahashi Korekiyo's** circle reckon war bonds, London credit, and the $300 million Russian indemnity against Theodore Roosevelt's 1903 containment drive east of the Rockies, Julian's **Provisioner** conviction becomes permanent. Winter evenings inside Tokyo's *kazoku* households also teach him how Japanese aristocratic kinship actually works—giving him the exact instinct he uses in March 1907 to settle Kiyoshi's stalled bid (`L1274 ("coalition comes apart piece by piece")`) without humiliating his half-brother by asking, in Japanese, for **Lady Chiyo** (`L1282 ("marries Lady Chiyo")`).
- **Homecoming into 1906 (`L1254`, `L1261`):** Sailing home in spring 1906 straight into the **April 1906 San Francisco earthquake** (`L1254 ("earthquake of April 1906")`) and **Harlan Tate's Sacramento school crisis** (`L1238`, `L1261 ("comes home to the school crisis")`), twenty-year-old Julian arrives already visibly **two-stamp**—strengthening Silas's December 1906 renunciation and making the later 1921 Altai betrayal cut twice as deep to the Company men, who know Julian was once *"David Kahale of Unsan,"* one of their own.

1. **Cider and Slide-Rules at the County Festival *(Interior Valley, ~1896)***: At a county festival of marksmen and riders (`CR-074` D12), retired Open-Company *"Privateers"* needle a Carquinez irrigation surveyor as a *"Mandarin"* and *"Ink-man,"* while he dryly calls their land warrants *"bounty-acres."* A visiting **two-stamp** officer—carrying both a Vallejo College ring and a Keelung/Korea scar—steps up to the target rail, shoots cleanly, then checks the ditch gradient in the surveyor's notebook, shutting both tables up without raising his voice.
2. **Gold and Silver at the Table *(Ralston College, San Francisco, 1910 or 1927)***: Inside private, silver-endowed **Ralston College** (`L881`), senior trustees and physicists argue sharply with Carquinez's **Provisioner × Bench** delegates over renewing the walled Hetch Hetchy compact (`L1312`)—proving the Bench itself is divided between Sufficer restraint and Provisioner urgency.
3. **The Quiet Caucus After Tomsk *(Sacramento, Winter 1921–22)***: In a back room of a Prosperity Party county hall, retired Sacramento-school Company captains and Harlan Tate's interior floor-men pass round a rumour out of Siberia while Examined Party organizers fight to hold the county slates and Deseret delegates together—making palpable why Julian feared an open Bench-vs.-Companies rupture would undo 1887.
4. **Reading the Floor *(Thalassia Senate Gallery, late 1920s — Julian and Kahekili)***: Julian walks his young heir **Kahekili** through the four quadrants visible on the Senate floor below—Sufficer scientists, Provisioner engineers, Field champions, and interior Company veterans—explaining why a Pacifican Sovereign must earn **two stamps** and can never let any single quadrant capture the Office of Public Measures.
5. **The Culvert and the Porch at Unsan *(Northern Korea, April–May 1904 — "Private David Kahale," the Coach-Sergeant, the Calamba rifleman, and the Taracol Captain)***: Opening in everyday bunkhouse camaraderie (marksmanship bets, boiled-water barrels against winter fever, a Calamba rifleman explaining what an evicted tenant's land warrant actually buys), the scene pivots into the April 1904 Cossack incursion (*Hist.*): Kahale walking onto the bridge unarmed strictly as the Sergeant's French interpreter under fourteen sighted rifles, dragging the thigh-shot Sergeant behind an iron ore-cart during the night ambush two days later and holding the culvert on his explicit word until dawn, and weeks later standing quietly beside the Taracol Captain in a coal-stained coat answering a startled Japanese First Army colonel in Honolulu Satsuma-register Japanese.
6. **Across the Strait — Yokosuka Slipways and Nagatachō *(Tokyo & Yokosuka, Summer 1905 – Winter 1906 — Julian Thorne, Kuroda Kiyoshi, Saionji Kinmochi)***: Weeks after leaving Unsan's mud, nineteen-year-old Julian hides his forearm burn scar beneath a starched legation cuff as he walks Yokosuka's dry-docks beside Kiyoshi's officers, exchanges one silent, weighted look with the returning First Army quartermaster colonel at a summer reception, feeds northern Korean road and grain figures into the Urajio negotiations, and walks Shimbashi's rail yards after an evening with Saionji—understanding for good why Victoria's Sufficer caution cannot protect Pacifica in a world of industrial empires.
