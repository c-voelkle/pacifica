## 1. Proposed direction

Pacifica helps a Russian movement defeat the Bolshevik central government and establishes a durable partnership with a federal Russia under Wrangel's provisional leadership. Siberia enters that federation through a negotiated regional compact. Ukraine remains independent. Crimea becomes an autonomous Russian federal land, while the main western Donbas districts belong to Ukraine. Transbaikalia remains outside the Russian federation under Japanese protection.

Julian offers Wrangel a recognizable Russian state, a functioning army, reconstruction credit, access to trade and a defensible settlement. In return, Wrangel recognizes the western states and the Asian territorial settlement, accepts enforceable federal institutions and gives up a programme of imperial reconquest. Pacifica's advantage rests on a useful Russian partner whose interests support the settlement. Russian subordination cannot depend on Wrangel's personal gratitude alone.

The working military chronology opens coalition intervention in late May 1918. In January 1920 Kiyoshi dies after securing the Ob railway crossing and the evacuation of wounded soldiers, railway families and a final Legion transport. Wrangel's Russian armies take Moscow in March 1920 after victories at Tula and the Oka approaches. The central Bolshevik regime disintegrates over the following weeks as its armies, administration and communications cease functioning together. Regional armies and underground organizations continue fighting into 1921–1922. This sequence is selected by the author; strengths, exact days and operational feasibility still require assessment beyond the existing Siberian financial model.

The Legion's cooperation rests on a separate political and military compact. Danubian federal reform begins in 1917 and establishes a credible path to Czech and Slovak self-government before the historical independence programme becomes definitive. The coalition guarantees the Legion's homecoming, recognizes its service and funds its agreed obligations without imposing an Austrian indemnity. Its withdrawal does not await Moscow's capture or the end of the residual Russian war.

## 3. Russia before the revolution

### The eastern retreat and European development

The earlier loss of the Ussuri region, Vladivostok and Sakhalin remains. Russia has no comparable sovereign Pacific terminal from which to sustain the historical imperial contest. The Far Eastern rivalry does not lead to the historical sequence of catastrophic land and naval losses in 1904–1905.

**Author-selected replacement:** retain the Manchurian confrontation and Kiyoshi's May 1905 naval victory, replacing destruction of the Baltic Fleet with defeat of a small Petropavlovsk-based cruiser squadron. Japan wins the regional contest while Russia preserves the greater part of its trained forces and naval investment. The campaign below implements the selected direction within this review draft.

Capital, military attention and infrastructure spending turn more strongly toward European Russia. Industrial growth enlarges the urban workforce and its demands; it does not resolve low wages, insecure working conditions, land pressure or exclusion from political power. Agrarian discontent and industrial strikes intensify together. Growth strengthens the state's resources while increasing the difficulty of governing through the old institutions.

The loss of Pacific access provides the counterweight. Russia lacks a supply route that it can administer and secure for itself. Japanese Vladivostok and the Chinese-owned Manchurian railway can still handle Russian imports under negotiated transit agreements, including the proposed Pacifican locomotive deliveries. Such access is conditional on foreign cooperation, available capacity and commercial terms. It is not the equivalent of possessing the port. Small northern Pacific outlets do not substitute for Vladivostok as a major wartime terminal.

The author's equal offset is the governing narrative assumption. This draft assigns no numerical growth premium, military saving or net change in Russian national strength.

### The Manchurian campaign and the cruiser victory

During the Boxer crisis in 1900, Russia enters Manchuria from Transbaikalia and occupies the Chinese railway toward Harbin. A temporary security intervention becomes an attempt to secure lasting railway privileges and regain access to a southern commercial outlet. Chinese demands for evacuation and Japanese concern over its Ussuri frontier lead to the confrontation of 1904–1905. Completion of the Circum-Baikal section improves Russia's western reinforcement route without giving it ownership of the Chinese railway, Japanese Vladivostok or a Russian Port Arthur base.

Japan advances principally from its Ussuri territory along the eastern railway, with Chinese cooperation. Harbin and the Sungari remain the principal land theatre. The Russian army withdraws toward Transbaikalia before suffering a catastrophic encirclement. The detailed battles and strengths remain OPEN; the regional defeat does not reproduce the historical sequence of massive land and naval losses.

After the loss of Vladivostok, Russia prepares a limited naval depot at Petropavlovsk in Avacha Bay, approximately 1900–1903. Coal, ammunition and provisions, loading craft, workshops, spare machinery, a repair tender and modest defences support a small cruiser force. This is an additional fictional investment, not an assumption that the historical harbour already possessed those facilities. Major hull or machinery damage exceeds the depot's repair capacity. Its isolation and lack of a railway prevent it from replacing Vladivostok as a supply gateway for European Russia.

The squadron's principal mission is commerce warfare on the Pacific approaches to Japan. It threatens Japanese military cargoes, forces escorts and diversions, and draws cruisers away from other duties while Russia fights in Manchuria. Protection of Russia's remaining northern possessions and supply vessels is a secondary task. It can disrupt the maritime system but cannot blockade Japan or routinely control the Japan–Vladivostok route. An illustrative establishment is two long-range armoured cruisers, one protected cruiser and support vessels; exact ships, availability after the 1898–1899 war and procurement costs remain OPEN or MODEL.

The core force deploys before open hostilities, around 1903, using ordinary outward provisioning arrangements. French ports can assist staging without becoming its continuing wartime operational base. During 1904, a few successful raids establish a real threat to transport. Winter provisions, imported spares and maintenance are included in the depot plan before renewed operations in spring 1905.

In May 1905 Kiyoshi defeats the renewed operation. He coordinates escorts, merchant sightings, port reports and scouting, then concentrates a superior cruiser force against the raiders during a return or replenishment movement. Shipboard wireless assists coordination without supplying perfect ocean surveillance. A battle east of Hokkaido or toward the southern Kurils is the working geographical range; the exact location and day remain OPEN. The selected result is one Russian cruiser disabled and captured, one seriously damaged and one escaping with lesser damage. Most crews survive. Kiyoshi secures the prize and survivors rather than pursuing the remaining ships indefinitely. The damaged ships reach Petropavlovsk, whose limited facilities cannot restore the squadron to sustained operations during the decisive campaign period.

The victory restores dependable movement of military cargoes, releases escorting and hunting forces, and supplies a conspicuous captured ship. It supports Kiyoshi's existing 1907 honors without attributing the destruction of Russia's battle fleet to him. Russia retains its principal battle fleets and most naval personnel. The simplest command arrangement puts Kiyoshi over a Japanese cruiser division; the precise nationality of participating ships and Pacifica's belligerent status remain OPEN. Russian treatment of neutral cargoes requires a stated wartime justification, not a blanket assumption that every Japan-bound ship is an enemy.

The regional naval setback reinforces the Manchurian and diplomatic reasons to settle later in 1905. Russia withdraws from Manchuria and recognizes the agreed frontier and Korean arrangements, within their existing approval scopes. Treaty location, exact indemnity and detailed clauses remain qualified. The earlier approximately $300m indemnity estimate is not validated by this smaller naval victory; financial pressure and the land settlement require separate assessment.

The historical Ulsan action provides a comparison for putting a cruiser squadron out of action with limited ship losses. It is not transplanted to Kamchatka as an actual historical event. The depot is a counterfactual response to the limitations of Russia's northern outlet. ### The constitutional confrontation of 1905

**Proposed immediate trigger:** in 1905 the government blocks a national zemstvo petition for representative control over public spending and represses a strike among railway and engineering workers. Railway stoppages spread into a political general strike while rural land seizures widen. The government confronts disruption of revenue, transport and local administration without relying on a disastrous Japanese war to explain its loss of authority.

War credits, transport disruption and frustration over another unsuccessful eastern venture sharpen the confrontation. The immediate political trigger remains proposed; the selected cruiser defeat contributes pressure without becoming an equivalent of Tsushima or the sole cause of revolution.

An autumn concession grants civil liberties in principle and a legislative Duma. The ensuing constitutional settlement preserves the emperor's control over ministers and strong powers to restrict or dissolve representation. The broad sequence of limited reform, renewed repression and unresolved rural grievances remains comparable to the historical 1905–1907 settlement. The proposed petition and strike are fictional replacement triggers, open to revision.

The historical October Manifesto and subsequent restrictions on the Duma supply the institutional comparison, not evidence that these alternate incidents occurred. [Presidential Library record of the first Duma](https://www.prlib.ru/history/619233); [October Manifesto collection](https://www.prlib.ru/node/2205854).

### The Great War and 1917

Russia still enters the European war in 1914. Its stronger European development does not remove the burdens of mobilization, food distribution, inflation, casualties and political distrust. The absence of secure sovereign Pacific supply access increases its dependence on northern routes and negotiated Asian transit. Better individual factories or railways do not guarantee that the whole wartime system can distribute sufficient food and equipment.

The February Revolution and the October seizure of power remain on the historical 1917 timetable: February/March and October/November respectively, depending on the calendar. The monarchy falls; a Provisional Government fails to settle the central questions; the Bolsheviks seize national power. This preserves the agreed revolutionary sequence without fixing every ministerial appointment, local clash or military episode.

Wrangel retains a broadly comparable professional and Great War career. Any decorations or personal connections dependent on the historical 1904–1905 campaign need checking. His memoir's Ussuri divisional designation also requires reconciliation with the earlier territorial loss; the Transbaikal regimental connection to Semenov and Ungern can be retained without assuming an unchanged Russian recruiting base in Japanese territory.

### Kiyoshi at the Ob crossing in January 1920

**Author-selected sequence.** The Ob victory remains in the winter of 1919–1920, with its military purpose revised. The coalition defends a vital rear crossing before the final advance on Moscow. The attackers are an organized Bolshevik force drawing on armed groups in the Altai and western Siberia; the battle does not require the main Bolshevik army to advance through the coalition's western forces to reach the Ob. Detailed strengths, commanders and the exact January day remain OPEN.

The attack threatens the crossing and its supporting depots at Novonikolayevsk. Stocks already accumulated farther west give the advancing forces endurance, but interruptions would delay fresh formations and heavy equipment, endanger local units and shake confidence in the coalition's ability to protect its own communications. Holding the crossing preserves the concentration for the final operation.

Kiyoshi is at the eastern command and transport centre coordinating reserve movements and the replacement of departing Legion units. When a setback threatens the western bridge approaches, telephone communications fail and conflicting withdrawal reports circulate. Russian and Japanese formations begin responding through separate chains of command. He moves to the forward command post and uses his authority and relationships to restore a common defence. Vance remains responsible for railway movements, bringing up reserves and keeping the eastern approaches clear.

Trains carrying wounded soldiers and railway families remain west of the threatened crossing. One of the final homebound Legion transports, including wounded legionaries, is also held there. Immediate withdrawal would preserve the headquarters and some combat units while leaving those people exposed. Kiyoshi commits the available reserve to holding and restoring the bridgehead while Vance organizes their evacuation. Russian wounded remain in the schedule alongside Japanese casualties, while the Legion transport retains its place in the protected evacuation. Engineers and railway crews perform the technical work; Kiyoshi coordinates the defence, evacuation priorities and relief attack. The returning legionaries are not compelled to resume an open-ended campaign as the price of rescue.

Artillery hits the forward command post while the operation is underway, fatally wounding him. The arrangements he has made continue to function: the relief force restores the threatened perimeter, the wounded and families cross, and the railway remains usable. Kiyoshi dies later that day knowing the crossing has been secured. His command decisions achieve the military and human purpose of the defence before his death becomes its public symbol.

The victory completes his personal trajectory from claimant to defender of the common institutions. Having relinquished his claim to the crown, he accepts responsibility for people whose survival depends on the partnership. Russian soldiers can remember the admiral who defeated their navy in 1905 as the commander who protected their wounded in 1920. Czech and Slovak survivors remember that he helped fulfil the coalition's promise to bring them home; their representatives subsequently honor him publicly. The act gives the partnerships a concrete demonstration of mutual obligation without replacing their constitutional and treaty guarantees. The exact transport, individuals, commemorative form and ceremony date remain OPEN within the selected connection.

### The final advance on Moscow and the central collapse

The concentration begins in 1919. Southern armies avoid their historical overextension and retain a viable Orel–Voronezh front. Eastern forces preserve the middle-Volga connection and move into European Russia before the January Ob battle. Kiyoshi's victory preserves the communications supporting those armies. It does not launch an army from Siberia that reaches Moscow within a few weeks.

Ukraine supplies coal, food and railway transit under agreements with the Russian leadership. Recognition of Ukrainian independence therefore has an immediate military benefit: Wrangel can concentrate against the Bolsheviks while obtaining supplies from his western neighbour. Secure cultivator tenure, protection against forcible estate restoration, purchased supplies and accompanying civilian administration reduce hostility in the Russian rear. These policies do not imply unanimous peasant or worker support.

In February the principal advance follows **Orel–Tula–Serpukhov–Moscow**. Kappel's eastern army advances toward the Ryazan approaches, threatening southeastern communications and preventing easy concentration against Kutepov. Both movements serve Wrangel's principal operation; they do not become independent races for the capital. Tula combines a key position on the southern approach with important weapons and ammunition production. Red formations counterattack and some Russian attacking units need relief. Wrangel commits his reserve to winning and consolidating this battle before continuing north. Factory damage and the restoration of production require their own assessment; capture does not instantly turn the factories into a new supply source for the attackers.

In late February and early March the Bolsheviks attempt to restore a defensive position covering the Oka and Moscow's southern approaches. Trotsky intervenes personally to rebuild discipline, bring up reserves and prevent retreat becoming dissolution. Wrangel maintains concentration while the eastern wing complicates Red reinforcement. Some formations surrender or disintegrate; committed Communist units continue resisting. Offers of service or demobilization to former imperial officers serving the Reds operate through individual decisions and records. The Red command does not collectively change sides.

In March the government prepares evacuation as the outer defence fails. Garrison commanders, municipal officials, railway authorities and party organizations receive conflicting instructions. Wrangel offers surrender terms protecting ordinary soldiers, civilians and officials who maintain essential services. Krivoshein prepares food deliveries and municipal administration. Fighting continues around military installations and defended districts, while negotiations detach portions of the garrison from the party leadership. Moscow falls through military defeat, evacuation and local capitulations; there is no assumed citywide conversion to the White cause.

During March and April, surviving central formations are pursued and principal communications secured. Yudenich's northwestern force has kept pressure on Petrograd under an agreement recognizing neighbouring states' independence. Petrograd and Kronstadt must now be taken or brought to surrender so they cannot receive a functioning replacement Bolshevik government. Their detailed military or negotiated settlement remains OPEN within that required outcome. Other potential regional centres are likewise prevented from restoring unified central rule.

The regime collapses cumulatively. Field defeats consume dependable formations; loss of Tula and disruption of communications make rebuilding them harder. Evacuation separates ministries, military headquarters and security organizations. Provincial authorities and army commanders make independent arrangements, while the successor Russian administration takes over food distribution, transport and ordinary services. The central party-security apparatus loses the means to compel obedience across the country. Capturing individual leaders follows this institutional collapse rather than causing it by itself. Regional armies and underground organizations survive into 1921–1922, providing the setting for the later Altai war.

### Vance and the Altai consequence

Vance carries out the evacuation, reserve movements and reinforcement plan under fire. His performance demonstrates why he can follow Kiyoshi in coalition coordination. He maintains continuity through the agreed emergency arrangements; the subsequent appointment requires Japanese agreement and preserves national chains of command. Kiyoshi's Japanese offices and command of Japanese national forces do not pass automatically to a Pacifican successor. Exact appointment instruments, participating senior officers and the division of responsibilities remain to be specified within OPEN-018-18.

The Ob gives Vance a standard he has personally helped to uphold. Kiyoshi preserved the military operation while bringing Russian and Japanese wounded and railway families to safety. In the 1921 Altai partisan war, Vance uses gas knowing Japanese prisoners are present, sacrificing them to secure the military objective. His competence and inherited responsibility connect the two decisions; the later act is a betrayal of an example he witnessed and helped make possible.

The Japanese dead at the Altai belong to the partner whose trust Kiyoshi sustained. A particular prisoner unit or personal link to the bridge defenders remains OPEN. The dramatic connection does not depend on inventing that identification. Julian's proposed falsification of the report then conceals a breach of the common obligation made tangible at the Ob. Having supported a Russian government that publicly prosecutes responsibility for mass killing in 1920–1921, he suppresses the evidence against his own commander. Victoria's confrontation and withdrawal retain their established place in 1921, as do the existing qualifications on the report, disclosure and private exchanges. This selection establishes the Ob-to-Altai relationship and the public-trial contrast without closing unrelated elements of OPEN-018-18.

### The Czechoslovak Legion

**Author-selected settlement.** The Legion's cooperation rests on national self-government, a defined military agreement and guaranteed homecoming. Its service is honored within the surviving Danubian federation and by Russia and the coalition. Neither financial rewards nor military decorations replace the constitutional settlement.

The political divergence begins in 1917, when Karl opens a genuine federal negotiation. By early 1918, initial constitutional guarantees and provisional implementation make the offer more substantial than a promise contingent on imperial victory. ### Lenin, Trotsky and Stalin

**Author-selected fictional outcomes for the review draft.** The three leaders are captured at different stages and prosecuted through Russian courts. The cases concern demonstrable orders and actions established in the altered timeline. Historical source documents supply comparisons and evidence to assess, not automatic proof that every historical incident recurs after the divergence. Exact charges, records, capture locations and days of proceedings remain OPEN.

**Lenin** leaves Moscow toward the northeast before its final loss, seeking to preserve the party centre and resume organized resistance. His remnant government becomes isolated as military formations and provincial authorities capitulate. He is captured during this second collapse in spring 1920. A public Russian trial later in 1920 concerns responsibility for state killings and orders established in this timeline. He remains defiant and argues revolutionary necessity. The selected outcome is conviction and execution in late 1920.

**Trotsky** remains engaged in restoring military coherence between headquarters and threatened formations. His command group is cut off during the retreat from the final Oka defensive positions in late February or early March, and he is taken alive. He is tried alongside Lenin, with conviction and execution in late 1920 as the selected outcome. His political defence is substantial and publicly heard. His capture follows the defeat and fragmentation of his military apparatus; it is not the single event that makes the Red armies collapse.

**Stalin** evades the initial collapse and attempts to preserve clandestine party networks after the loss of open state power. He is arrested later in 1920, tried separately and executed in 1921. He acts as the senior political and military organizer of 1919–1920, without the apparatus of his later historical dictatorship. The case depends on demonstrated civil-war actions and subsequent armed organization within this timeline; no later historical crime is imported as a charge for an event that has not occurred.

The different capture and trial schedules allow the centre to collapse before every clandestine network is dismantled. They also establish the later contradiction at the Altai: Julian supports public accountability for mass killing under the Russian successor government, then conceals evidence against Vance when his own commander sacrifices Japanese prisoners in 1921.

## 6. Federal Russia and the Asian settlement

### The federal compact

Federal Russia combines a common external state with substantial regional self-government. Foreign policy, the national currency, a common customs area and agreed defence belong to the federation. Regional institutions control local administration, education, land policy and ordinary policing within a constitutional allocation of powers. Federal courts protect enumerated rights; changing a region's status requires more than a central executive order.

Siberia negotiates entry through its regional assembly. Tomsk remains the proposed parliamentary centre and Irkutsk the regional executive centre. A defined revenue share funds regional government, while common transport and commercial rules make federation useful to both Siberia and European Russia. Fiscal shares, representation and the division of police powers remain to be specified.

Sakha autonomy, communal land and language rights remain within Siberia's nested constitutional arrangements. The North-East Territory retains district and indigenous representation. Cossack autonomy does not confer unlimited authority over non-Cossack residents or exempt the region from the common rights and land settlement.

## 7. Ukraine, Crimea and the Donbas

### Independent Ukraine

Ukraine remains a sovereign state outside the Russian federation. Skoropadsky is the proposed initial governing partner, with a constitutional executive, representative institutions, its own army and an agrarian settlement that can survive the withdrawal of unrestricted German coercion. A Ukrainian government dependent on restoring landlords by force would reproduce one of the historical Hetmanate's central weaknesses.

Germany's survival in the European settlement gives this state a different external foundation from the historical late-1918 collapse. It does not resolve domestic legitimacy by itself. The historical choice between independence and federation was contested, which makes a negotiated independent course possible without making it inevitable. [Yuki Murata on Ukraine's 1918 state settlement](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jarees/2018/47/2018_17/_article/-char/en).

Wrangel's recognition of independent Ukraine is a major concession. His memoir endorses Ukrainian autonomy within Russian unity, not an unambiguous permanent separation. Julian must obtain a public treaty commitment, supported by the reconstruction bargain and reciprocal guarantees. A private promise to postpone the question is insufficient.

### Donbas and the Don frontier

The working allocation puts Yuzovka, Luhansk and Mariupol in Ukraine, and Rostov, Taganrog and the main Don territory in Russia. Prewar imperial administrative districts provide the starting material for a commission, alongside local representation and practical transport links. This is a negotiating outline, not a surveyed boundary or a wholesale adoption of later Soviet or modern borders.

There is no independent Donbas buffer state. Ukrainian sovereignty operates through local elected institutions, language guarantees, ordinary property law and protection for industrial workers. Russia and Ukraine agree predictable coal, steel, railway and transit terms with arbitration. Common commercial rules do not give a foreign commission extraterritorial government over the industrial region.

### Crimea

Crimea becomes an autonomous federal land of Russia, subject to a locally ratified settlement. An elected legislature represents all residents, while a Crimean Tatar representative institution protects cultural, religious and land rights. Sevastopol remains a Russian federal naval base with a defined relationship to civilian government.

Ukraine receives Russian recognition, the main western Donbas allocation, a non-aggression commitment and reliable commercial access through the Kerch Strait. In return, it accepts Crimea's federal Russian status. Rail transit, food, fuel and coastal supply agreements address the peninsula's dependence on the mainland. Russian fleet provisions forbid blockade or intervention to change Ukraine's government.

This bargain must overcome a real conflict of aims: Skoropadsky historically pressed a Crimean claim and used an economic blockade. The draft does not assume his consent merely because he knows Wrangel. [Ukrainian historical reference on the 1918 Crimean blockade](https://ukr-revolution.history.org.ua/termin/ekonomichna_blokada_krymu_1918).

Mainland Taurida, exact Don frontier districts and minority guarantees require a boundary commission. Galicia and Bukovina remain questions for the surviving Habsburg federation; they are not automatically awarded to Ukraine. Finland, Poland and the Baltic states remain outside Russia on the working peace framework. Their detailed borders, Belarus, Bessarabia and the Caucasian settlements remain OPEN wherever existing canon does not decide them.

## 8. Germany, Japan and China

### Germany

Germany receives a recognized independent Ukrainian buffer, western frontier settlements and reliable access to trade. Grain contracts, industrial orders and predictable transit make an economically recovering Russia useful without placing Ukraine under Russian military control. Reciprocal security commitments reduce the danger of a restored Franco-Russian military encirclement.

In return, Germany recognizes federal Russia, accepts Ukrainian sovereignty beyond temporary occupation arrangements and agrees the withdrawal or strict limitation of its garrisons. These agreements follow the armistice and peace negotiations; they do not turn Germany into an Entente ally in May 1918.

The existing European peace framework remains the working setting: Germany retains Alsace-Lorraine, pays no indemnity, loses colonies and develops a parliamentary monarchy; Austria-Hungary survives as a fragile federation. The author-selected Danubian reform now begins in 1917 and provides Czech and Slovak self-government, negotiated Hungarian assent and representative ratification. Germany has an interest in a stable constitutional partner and continuing markets, while national rights give the Legion's political representatives grounds to accept the federal settlement. Austria pays no punitive indemnity and its industry is not seized as a reward for Legion service. Japan receives Russian recognition of its existing Pacific holdings, the Transbaikalian protected territory and the Kamchatka/Koryak settlement. ### China

## 10. Balance of power through 1932

Federal Russia is large enough to be a useful counterweight in Europe and Asia, while its recognized western neighbours and the Pacific settlement restrain renewed imperial expansion. Germany, Russia and the Danubian federation remain distinct continental powers. Ukraine has enough territory, industry and political agency to function as a state, rather than a permanent field of proxy conflict.

The Danubian settlement preserves the common market and the Czech industrial connection while giving Czech and Slovak institutions a stake in constitutional continuity. Returning legionaries receive a recognized place in that order, rather than an indefinite foreign deployment or exclusion as traitors. Austria and Hungary retain markets and security through equality within the federation. National disputes, minority grievances and fiscal bargaining can still make it fragile; the federal compact is not a claim that these conflicts disappear. The Legion's Russian service does not create a separate Czech–Slovak sovereign party to the 1922 Asian alliance.

France gains security from a Russia capable of resisting unilateral German dominance, but does not automatically regain the old alliance on its old terms. Britain gains a continent with several centres of power. Germany gains assurances against a coalition aimed at overturning the peace. These interests make a concert possible; they do not eliminate future rivalry.

Pacifica holds the strongest position through reconstruction finance, industrial supply, sea routes and treaty arbitration. It gains no automatic duty to fight every Russian frontier war. The 1922 alliance must define an Asian security area, consultation requirements, assistance conditions and treatment of wars of aggression. European guarantees require separate decisions and resources.

The two existing financial orders remain: Union capital is important in Berlin and Vienna, while Pacifican claims dominate its existing creditor relationships. Russian reconstruction may extend Pacifican influence without transferring every German or eastern European loan into San Francisco's books. The 1931 banking crisis, Union rescue and 1927–1932 Concord arc retain their existing causes and qualifications unless later financial work identifies a necessary change.

The federal treaty must survive ordinary changes of government. Representation, regional revenue, trade access and reciprocal security provide continuing interests beyond the life of Wrangel. His historical death in 1928 does not establish an alternate death date or cause; the succession mechanism nevertheless needs to work if he dies, retires or loses office.
