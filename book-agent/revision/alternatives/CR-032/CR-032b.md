## 1. Proposed direction and assessment

Julian concludes during summer 1916 that prolonged war threatens the survival of a functioning Russian government. He regards revolutionary disintegration as a more immediate danger to Pacifica than a limited German continental ascendancy. He restricts new Russian credit, attempts constitutional stabilization and begins negotiating a general European peace.

Pacifica remains a belligerent with Pacifican and Japanese gains to defend. Its contribution to settlement is a combination of diplomatic terms, limited continuing wartime finance, reconstruction commitments, shipping and access to supplies. Julian accepts that Germany will remain a major continental power. He seeks an arrangement in which European governments recover without obtaining the means or alignment to overturn Pacifica's position in the Pacific or North America.

The working success sequence reaches a **general armistice and signed preliminary peace in August 1917**. The agreement stops fighting across the participating theatres and specifies evacuation, security and economic obligations. Detailed peace treaties and ratification follow in late 1917 and early 1918. An invitation to a conference, an Austrian separate peace, or an unsigned ceasefire proposal does not satisfy this outcome.

The assessment is conditional. An earlier peace is a credible alternative if Germany's civilian negotiating authority survives military opposition and France and Italy accept substantial compromises. Those are major political divergences, not consequences that credit restrictions alone can produce. The best economic outcome also requires competent Russian reform and an orderly conversion of Pacifican and Japanese industry.

Success could produce greater Pacifican prosperity and reduce its security costs. Its effect on relative supremacy is less certain: Britain, France, Germany and Russia would retain more resources and independence than under CR-032. Julian trades some leverage over distressed governments for larger markets, fewer hazardous claims and a wider network of durable partnerships.

## 3. Julian's diagnosis in summer 1916

### Commercial reports and political information

Julian's assessment combines three kinds of evidence. Pacifican suppliers and railway representatives report what is paid for, unloaded and delivered through Japanese ports and Chinese transit. Financial correspondents report inflation, demands for advances and deteriorating administrative reliability. Russian conservative and constitutional contacts explain why the court is unlikely to repair the political system through ordinary appointments.

The proposed decisive event is a confidential credit review in July–August 1916. Russian representatives seek further advances while independent delivery reports show persistent failures in transport and provisioning. A private assessment conveyed through Kokovtsov's financial circle and corroborated by contacts around Guchkov challenges the official assurances. These encounters are new fictional events. Neither man becomes Julian's subordinate or an already committed member of a future cabinet.

Julian's conclusion concerns the government's capacity to coordinate the country. Improved factories and battlefield successes can coexist with deteriorating civilian administration. His analysts therefore resist interpreting each successful offensive as proof that the political crisis has passed.

Actual 1916 loan negotiations involved disputes about security and the relationship between Russian borrowing and British financing. They supply a credible setting for intensive commercial scrutiny. They do not establish Julian's fictional conference or its conclusion. [Francis's report of 20 May 1916](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1914-20v01/d152).

### Revolutionary danger

The intelligence service follows published revolutionary programmes, organizations and disagreements. Julian fears that the fall of the existing government could leave constitutional politicians without sufficient authority while more disciplined revolutionary groups exploit the resulting crisis.

He can recognize an attack on the existing political and property order without predicting the date of Lenin's success or the institutions of a later Soviet state. His assessment distinguishes parliamentary socialists, agrarian movements and revolutionary defeatists. Broad political labels cannot substitute for knowing who controls troops, transport, food and representative bodies.

The forecast is that another prolonged campaign without reform makes a constitutional rupture or revolution probable. It does not specify the eventual winner. Its accuracy remains compatible with an unsuccessful attempt to save the monarchy and a later attempt to stabilize a successor government.

## 5. The political changes the peace requires

### Germany accepts a bounded victory

German leaders receive recognition that their state and dynasty will survive as major European powers. They gain restored access to food, materials and commerce, a reasonable colonial settlement and negotiated eastern security. Germany evacuates occupied western territory and accepts that Belgian independence and other states' commercial agency cannot be merely nominal.

The December initiative becomes a genuine negotiation rather than primarily an exercise in assigning blame. Bethmann Hollweg's private explanation of the historical offer demonstrates why this change is necessary. [December 1916 memorandum](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/wilhelmine-germany-and-the-first-world-war-1890-1918/the-german-peace-offer-december-5-1916).

The submarine decision is another fork. In this branch, the Kaiser accepts a conditional postponement because a valuable settlement is available and Pacifican and Japanese maritime capacity makes rapid British collapse uncertain. Tonnage, escorts and replacement shipping must eventually substantiate that assessment. The neutral Union supplies no approaching army with which to frighten Berlin.

The harder confrontation comes after the Russian revolution makes victory seem cheaper. Karl's preference for settlement, fear of domestic dislocation, a parliamentary peace constituency and an acceptable treaty must outweigh military demands to exploit the opportunity. Historical parliamentary support for compromise provides a political opening, not authority to compel the army. [Reichstag resolution of 19 July 1917](https://germanhistorydocs.org/de/das-wilhelminische-kaiserreich-und-der-erste-weltkrieg-1890-1918/ghdi:document-987).

The working fictional divergence is that the Kaiser sustains civilian negotiating authority during the July crisis. Bethmann remains responsible for concluding the agreement. If the generals threaten resignation, the crown has a viable command succession rather than requiring the chancellor to yield. Exact replacements and command arrangements are OPEN. This is not an assertion that replacing famous generals would be politically easy. A contemporary military participant's account describes the historical use of resignation threats. [July 1917 command crisis](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/wilhelmine-germany-and-the-first-world-war-1890-1918/erich-ludendorff-vs-theobald-von-bethmann-hollweg-july-1917).

A full parliamentary monarchy does not arise automatically from an early peace. Civilian authority over the settlement is the necessary immediate change. The German constitution and succession retain separate decisions.

### Britain accepts that continental predominance is negotiable

Britain receives an independent Belgium, a western evacuation, protection of maritime access and an end to the demands of an expanding war. It retains its fleet and the means to protect its empire. Pacifican credit continues through a negotiated transition rather than disappearing at the moment imports become essential.

Britain accepts Germany's continuing continental strength, compromises on colonial questions and supports a Danubian federation. It cannot simultaneously insist on dismantling every German overseas interest and expect Berlin to treat the settlement as an acceptable compromise.

Julian does not demand that Britain transfer all the later proposed bases or convert an unaccumulated 1918 debt into equivalent concessions. Shipping, access and commercial agreements are negotiated on their own merits. British participation in any naval limitation arrangement remains a separate bargain.

### France accepts an incomplete territorial settlement

The working offer restores occupied France, finances essential reconstruction and supports a reciprocal security arrangement. The guarantee combines France's own army, British commitments and specified Pacifican economic and maritime measures. Pacifica does not promise an immediate continental army it has never built.

Germany retains Alsace-Lorraine in the proposed central case, with substantial local autonomy, language protections and negotiated commercial access. This is a real French concession. A future vote or territorial review would itself require German acceptance and is not inserted as a costless solution.

A French ministry must obtain authority to accept these terms. The spring military crisis may supply pressure, while reconstruction support and limited reciprocal military restrictions provide benefits. The cabinet, vote and exact security instruments remain OPEN. France's failure to accept this bargain is one of the main ways the scenario fails.

The alternative is a negotiated territorial adjustment acceptable to both governments, with its own industrial and strategic terms. It cannot be adopted by simply promising the same land to each side. No Rhineland annexation or punitive programme is assumed in the central case.

### Italy and the other theatres receive actual settlements

Italy's proposed package includes the Trentino and negotiated commercial, port and minority arrangements elsewhere. Trieste and the Adriatic territories require specific negotiation; a free-port arrangement is an option, not an accepted substitute for every Italian claim. Pacifying Italy by awarding all its contested claims would undermine the federal principle offered to the monarchy's peoples.

Serbia and Romania require restoration of functioning governments, evacuation arrangements and agreed security. Bulgaria needs its own territorial and economic bargain. The Ottoman settlement must address occupied areas, navigation, provincial claims and British security concerns. British and French gains achieved only in 1918 do not appear in a treaty signed in 1917.

The peace fails as a general settlement if one of these disputes keeps a major theatre active and other powers resume support. The detailed Balkan and Ottoman map is OPEN. Pacifica's preference for peace cannot supply consent on behalf of these states or their populations.

## 7. Julian's lending and the conversion to peace

### Separate borrowers and obligations

Julian directs Pacifican policy and influences syndicates; the Treasury, banks and private investors retain distinct assets and liabilities. Restricting a new issue is different from cancelling a loan already advanced.

From late summer 1916, new unsecured Russian exposure is sharply limited. Existing claims are reviewed for likely restructuring and loss. Essential transport, food and medical programmes continue through agreed purchasing and administrative arrangements. Safeguards protect deliveries without converting Russian institutions into foreign property.

Where Britain or France purchases supplies for Russia, the identity of the debtor is explicit. A British payment obligation remains British even if Russia later defaults. A Russian bond held by a Pacifican investor does not become British-guaranteed merely because London supports the alliance.

Gold or securities already placed in agreed custody can support particular advances. Collateral cannot secure several claims simultaneously at its full value. Retention of title to machinery provides a claim, not a guarantee that equipment can be repossessed during political disorder.

### Make the offer credible to both camps

Pacifica supplies a defined transition facility for agreed imports and essential services. Reconstruction finance becomes available as an armistice, evacuation and other specified obligations take effect. If a party rejects the agreed minimum basis, Julian can continue limited defensive assistance to the other camp. The policy is a conditional settlement offer, not a promise to terminate Allied resistance regardless of German terms.

The ceiling and its exceptions need political authorization. Private lenders may disagree or find alternative borrowers. Pacifican exporters and Japanese banks may resist a policy that reduces orders. Neutral Union finance can weaken Julian's leverage; Washington is neither conscripted into his policy nor treated as a Pacifican ally.

A historical financial warning late in 1916 was interpreted as having diplomatic implications. The comparison supports the potency of financial signals, not a claim that Julian can reproduce every institution of the historical United States. [New York Federal Reserve account](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2017/pot171220?hashed_user=23463b99b62a72f26ed677cc556c44e8).

### Finance civilian conversion before the armistice

The prospective order programme includes merchant shipping, railway equipment, agricultural machinery, electrical works, port repair and housing. Procurement is based on actual demand, budgets and contracts. Government guarantees and cancellation payments have costs; they are not free substitutes for lost military orders.

Japanese and Pacifican manufacturers receive advance notice, negotiated changes to contracts and opportunities to sell into several markets. Banks lengthen suitable industrial funding and recognize losses where necessary. Ending the war earlier shortens the boom but may also bring an abrupt contraction while loans remain outstanding.

German patents and seized commercial rights require a settlement. Signed licences, compensation or arbitration can preserve useful production while resolving claims. Peace does not automatically legalize every wartime appropriation.

The mechanism for Pacifican influence is participation in recovery: shipping, clearing, insurance, industrial supply and syndicated finance. Access and contracts remain valuable without requiring every country to borrow exclusively in San Francisco. Existing Union interests in Berlin and Vienna continue unless particular transactions establish otherwise.

## 8. Karl and Budapest in 1917

The proposed federal bargain begins with Karl's recognition that a general peace requires a viable monarchy afterward. Julian offers support for constitutional continuity and recovery, while national representatives require institutions and rights before accepting that continuity.

The earlier Hungarian industrial mission to Japan and Pacifica, involving Gratz in 1912–1913, is an optional new connection. The proposed winter 1916–1917 railway and provisioning crisis supplies immediate pressure. Neither event is treated as existing canon.

Tisza's departure alone does not change the governing majority. Esterházy's summer ministry reveals the limits of reform without parliamentary support. The proposed Wekerle bargain in late summer and autumn draws a substantial group of conservative deputies into a coalition supporting national accommodation. Industrial and agricultural interests receive continued markets, property protection and a credible recovery programme.

Karl's crown supports the government consistently. Hungarian institutions enact the Slovak autonomy settlement; an imperial decree does not dispose of the Hungarian constitutional problem. A provisional administration and preparations for an elected diet begin during 1917, with enforceable authority over language, education, local administration and specified revenues. A binding process takes Slovakia into the wider federal settlement.

Budapest's concession is substantial: it loses exclusive authority over Slovaks and the privileges of the old dual system. In exchange, constitutional Hungary retains government, commercial access, security and reciprocal minority guarantees. Protection of property allows ordinary law and compensated agrarian reform; it does not freeze every existing estate arrangement permanently.

Czech self-government and Slovak institutions permit shared Czech–Slovak bodies while preserving a Slovak voice. A representative federal chamber, a chamber of constituent states, a constitutional court and agreed revenue entitlements support the bargain. Changes to autonomy require the affected state's consent, while ordinary federal business cannot depend on unanimous approval of everything.

The same principle reaches Croats, Romanians, Ruthenians and the monarchy's other peoples. Their detailed constitutional and territorial arrangements remain OPEN. A Slovak exception followed by continued denial of every comparable claim would weaken the settlement.

Pacifica supplies limited, defined assistance and participation in recovery. Much of the material programme follows the armistice. It does not furnish unrestricted wartime resources to its enemies or displace all Union lending to Vienna.

## 9. Russia after the summer peace

### The central case

The monarchy falls in February/March 1917 despite Julian's warnings. This preserves the immediate revolutionary rupture while allowing the war's later course to change.

The central success case is a constitutional Russian successor that survives through peace, land reform, functioning administration and representative legitimacy. Its relationship with Pacifica is commercial and diplomatic first. A permanent alliance depends on a government and treaty acceptable to both parties; it is not awarded in advance.

The August peace removes the immediate demand for another offensive and permits controlled demobilization. It offers the government an achievement that the Bolsheviks cannot claim exclusively. Food still has to reach the cities, soldiers need credible discharge arrangements and cultivators need an enforceable land settlement.

The essential domestic programme includes secure cultivator tenure, compensation and credit where workable, predictable payments for supplies, municipal services and regional institutions. These measures draw on the practical concerns discussed in CR-032 without transferring Wrangel's historical 1920 administration bodily into 1917.

Julian's preference remains a conservative or liberal constitutional executive. That preference is difficult to reconcile with a population in which socialist and agrarian parties command extensive support. The draft does not guarantee a conservative electoral majority or install one by military decree. Limited food and transport assistance does not itself constitute an alliance with a socialist national government.

If representative politics produces a government Julian will not accept as a strategic ally, Pacifica can retain trade, relief and limited practical relations while withholding an alliance. A stable Russia outside an exclusive Pacifican alignment may still be safer than revolutionary warfare. Obtaining both constitutional legitimacy and Julian's preferred governing partner is a separate political test.

### Revolutionary movements and German policy

The Bolsheviks and other revolutionary groups remain active. Demobilization, unemployment, land disputes and food failures can still generate upheaval. The July offensive and associated events cannot be preserved unchanged if that offensive is cancelled.

Germany's facilitation of revolutionary travel and other destabilizing activity is subject to the new diplomatic setting. A promise to cease such support has value but is difficult to verify. Preventing one journey would not remove a domestic revolutionary movement; Lenin may seek another route. The success case rests on a functioning government and diminished grievances, not the disappearance of particular opponents.

The October/November seizure of national power is averted in the central case. This is an outcome being tested. A peace agreement alone does not establish it, and no numerical probability is assigned.

### Constitution and regions

The form of the Russian state remains for representative settlement. A constitutional monarchy requires an accepted sovereign and a mandate; a republic requires institutions capable of governing. Wrangel's personal appointment does not answer either question.

Federal or regional arrangements address Siberia and other regions through political negotiation, rather than reunion after conquest. The Baltic lands, Finland, Ukraine, Crimea, the Don and the Donbas require separate decisions. Ukrainian autonomy or independence cannot be settled by transplanting a map from the later civil war. Polish independence or association needs Russian, German and Polish commitments rather than merely a change of labels.

If the national government collapses despite peace, the adverse branch may involve regional governments, rival assemblies and armed conflict. It does not automatically reproduce CR-032's forces, territories or dates.

## 10. Japan China and Transbaikalia

Pacifica and Japan retain their established partnership and their jointly developed professional methods of construction and production. The change shortens the war economy; it does not restore a coercive labor-syndicate explanation for Japanese development.

Japan gains recognition of essential existing Pacific holdings, commercial access and an opportunity to redirect wartime capacity. Tokyo accepts a compromise peace because continued trade, finance and partnership offer benefits beyond further conquest. Its army, cabinet, Diet, business interests and court do not become a single actor with identical preferences.

China participates in its own railway, territorial and commercial arrangements. Foreign transit through Chinese territory remains subject to Chinese rights. Changes to the availability of Russian revolutionary support would affect China's later politics, but do not guarantee reunification or end domestic conflict.

**Transbaikalia remains the hardest retained territorial requirement.** In CR-032, central collapse and coalition victory provide a setting for Japanese protection. A functioning Russian government emerging from an earlier peace has stronger reasons to resist losing a region.

The proposed negotiating route combines local representative consent to a civilian protected territory, Russian treaty recognition, reliable transit and supply arrangements, commercial access, security assurances and a defined financial settlement. Japan receives protection rather than annexation; Transbaikalia remains outside the Russian federation, consistent with the author's selected destination. No automatic return to Russia is scheduled.

This route is unproven. The Cossack Host and other inhabitants cannot be assumed to consent, and finance alone may not secure Russian recognition. Keeping the rest of Russia united and obtaining reliable trade may be insufficient compensation. A local vote under military pressure would not by itself create a durable agreement.

The European peace can succeed while this objective remains unresolved. Full compatibility with the author's territorial requirement cannot be claimed until the regional bargain is credible. The draft neither silently abandons Japanese protection nor invents a completed cession.

Kamchatka, the Koryak coast, the Commander Islands, Arctic leases, northern indemnities and other later Harbin arrangements likewise require review of their independent basis. The Nikolayevsk massacre and a 1921 victors' conference are not recreated simply to justify the same acquisitions.

## 11. The Legion and the principal characters

### National rights and return

The Legion's political cause is addressed through real Czech and Slovak self-government beginning in 1917. Masaryk's acceptance depends on enforceable rights and ratification. He is not asked to accept unchanged imperial government in exchange for pensions.

The army's composition and organization are those achievable by 1917. Later recruitment and the size of the 1918 corps cannot be assumed. The Zborov episode and honors attached to it require reassessment if the Russian summer offensive does not take place.

The armistice and federal agreement recognize service, remove treason or desertion penalties arising merely from joining the Legion and guarantee safe return. Repatriation begins in late 1917, with completion during 1918 as the proposed working objective, supported by a binding funded schedule once numbers and routes are established. Ordinary individual offences remain subject to ordinary law.

An agreed European railway route is the preferred option after a general armistice. Sea transport through Japanese Vladivostok remains available where needed. No 1918 railway revolt, years of compulsory Russian service or 1920 Ob battle is required as a condition of homecoming.

Accrued pay, medical treatment, disability and dependants' provision, training, employment and preserved military traditions remain. Coalition service liabilities and future domestic pension responsibilities are allocated explicitly. Austria receives no punitive indemnity to fund them. The settlement recognizes service actually performed; the Legion is not credited with an unperformed liberation of Moscow.

## 12. Comparative power and prosperity through 1932

### The sources of Pacifican advantage

Pacifica enters the peace with productive capacity, trained people, maritime reach and an established Japanese partnership. The final war years enlarge these advantages in the existing scenario but are not their origin.

Earlier recovery creates potential demand for machinery, ships, electrical equipment, transport and financial services. A Russian state able to pay wages and govern regions can become a larger market than one requiring years of civil war and military reconstruction. Reduced security burdens release capacity for other purposes, subject to the costs of demobilization and conversion.

Commercial access, treaty arbitration, insurance, clearing and participation in reconstruction give Pacifica influence. These arrangements must offer benefits to counterparties. Exclusive control of European finance is neither required nor assumed.

### The limits of the advantage

Britain and France retain more wealth and bargaining power. Germany remains an industrial competitor. Russia may recover without becoming Julian's preferred ally. Neutral Union banks can compete for European business and may earn a larger share of the recovery.

The earlier peace therefore improves Pacifica's opportunity for durable prosperity more clearly than it guarantees unrivalled power. If Julian's objective is the maximum dependence of foreign governments, prolongation may offer greater leverage. If the objective is security, national wealth and a settlement capable of surviving changes of government, the early peace has a stronger potential case.

| Long-term influence | Broad recovery can support mutually useful networks. | Influence rests more heavily on indebtedness and military settlement. |
| Relative rivals | European powers and the Union remain more independent. | Pacifica may stand higher relative to more exhausted competitors. |

### The later crises

CR-032 already works with a German peace without a punitive reparations programme. CR-032b cannot claim an additional gain from abolishing reparations that the comparison case does not contain.

The 1923 Japanese earthquake can remain an external event, while the banks' condition and the scale of reconstruction finance change. The 1927 Concord may remain a preferred institutional destination, but its timing and political bargain require a new causal account.

Private foreign lending, currency pressures, property speculation and banking mistakes can still generate crises. Earlier peace does not abolish the possibility of a depression. The particular 1931 Vienna and German banking sequence, Japanese rescue and 1932 Union association need reassessment against changed balance sheets and politics. They are not automatic rewards of the new branch.

## 13. Tests of the peace and its durability

The following are qualitative decision tests. The assessments identify dependencies and failure mechanisms; they are not statistical probabilities.

| Test | What would establish a credible success | Present assessment |
|---|---|---|
| T1 German terms | Authorized acceptance of western evacuation and an eastern settlement compatible with functioning neighbours. | Major political divergence. Military opportunity after March can overturn it. |
| T2 German political authority | A crown and civilian ministry willing and able to conclude peace despite command opposition, with a viable response to resignation threats. | Essential and demanding. A Reichstag resolution alone does not pass the test. |
| T3 French consent | A ministry and parliamentary coalition accepting the actual Alsace-Lorraine and security terms. | Major unresolved obstacle. Financial leverage is insufficient as a complete explanation. |
| T4 British participation | Negotiated import finance, maritime security, reconstruction and colonial terms worth accepting. | Plausible negotiating route; exact concessions and alternatives remain open. |
| T5 Italy and other theatres | Signed settlements or accepted procedures addressing the Adriatic, Balkans and Ottoman front. | Necessary to a general peace. Several unresolved territorial conflicts remain. |
| T6 Hungarian and national consent | Legislation, representative participation, funded institutions and a binding federal transition. | Substantial parliamentary change required. Stability depends on other peoples receiving comparable treatment. |
| T7 Finance and shipping | Authorized credit limits, realistic civilian contracts and actual capacity for supplies and relief. | Commercially assessable; no completed numerical scenario exists yet. |
| T8 Russian stabilization | Food, land, discharge arrangements and legitimate institutions continue after the armistice. | Peace improves the opportunity but does not establish the outcome. |
| T9 Transbaikalia | Credible local consent and Russian recognition of continuing Japanese protection. | Particularly weak retained territorial dependency; not demonstrated by the European bargain. |
| T10 Pacifican predominance | Recovery produces sufficient commercial and institutional advantages to outweigh reduced creditor leverage and stronger rivals. | Greater prosperity is plausible. Superior relative dominance remains conditional. |

### Outcomes if conditions differ

| Branch | Immediate result | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| B1 Negotiated peace and Russian stabilization | August armistice holds; constitutional processes and recovery make progress. | Strongest case for avoiding CR-032's military costs and improving prosperity. |
| B2 Peace delayed beyond the revolutionary crisis | Negotiations continue into late 1917 while Russian institutions deteriorate. | The opportunity to avert Bolshevik national rule narrows. No unchanged March 1920 campaign follows automatically. |
| B3 Peace holds but Russian government fails | General European war ends, but Russian land, military and political disputes produce renewed upheaval. | Pacifica faces a different intervention or containment decision; some anticipated security savings disappear. |
| B4 The agreement breaks down | Evacuation, borders or ratification fail and fighting resumes. | Earlier credit restrictions and mobilization choices may leave Pacifica and its partners less prepared. |
| B5 Recovery succeeds with weaker Pacifican control | Europe and the Union obtain much of the commercial benefit; counterparts diversify suppliers and lenders. | Pacifica may be richer while falling short of Julian's preferred relative supremacy. |

The central case should be adopted only with a narrative explaining the political decisions behind T1–T6. Economic plausibility additionally requires T7. A claim that the peace solves the Russian danger requires T8. Full compatibility with the territorial requirement needs T9. A claim of greater global predominance needs T10 as well as greater wealth.

## 14. Financial assessment required for comparison

The baseline workbook remains the CR-031 file, recorded with SHA-256 29aaeaafeeef712ff6c276c71495c461a122f111726abdafacd558f9bab30091. This draft does not alter or recalculate it.

The comparison should keep identical pre-divergence assumptions and then separate commitments by date, borrower, lender and purpose. The existing aggregate advances, Japanese orders and industrial expansion cannot be reduced in proportion to the number of months of war. Goods already delivered, facilities under construction and debt service continue after an armistice.

Required scenario accounts are:

1. **War commitments:** existing contracts, new advances, deliveries, cancellation compensation, Treasury spending and private participation from summer 1916 onward.
2. **Claims and collateral:** repayment, interest, impairment, gold and securities, and ownership of liabilities. Allied paper is not all a Pacifican Treasury asset.
3. **Industrial conversion:** forgone procurement profits, new civilian demand, inventories, unemployment, training, shipping and construction already underway.
4. **Reconstruction:** grants, recoverable loans, guarantees, private equity and operating revenue, with their respective funding and risks.
5. **Russia:** surviving claims, food and transport assistance, land and administrative costs, and security costs in both the stabilization and failure branches.
6. **Japan:** industrial borrowing, foreign short-term liabilities, contract conversion and later earthquake exposure.
7. **Military and territorial obligations:** demobilization, retained readiness, any avoided Russian intervention and the costs of protecting agreed positions.
8. **National wealth and income:** domestic investment, consumption, foreign receipts and terms of trade. War-related GDP, loan principal and profit are different measures.
9. **Power and concessions:** bases, territorial recognition, commercial access, counterpart autonomy and obligations that do not appear fully in financial totals.
10. **Later crises:** revised private loan growth, currency and banking exposure, the Concord's financing and the assumptions behind 1931–1932.

Foregone lending is not a lost profit equal to the principal. Avoided military spending is not automatically an equal addition to national wealth: resources must find other uses and existing commitments still require payment. Reconstruction exports cannot be counted both as domestic sales and as additional investment income without the corresponding financing.

The economic claim to test is whether more reliable income, productive investment and avoided losses exceed forgone wartime earnings, conversion costs and the value of concessions no longer obtainable. No new growth rate, dollar saving or numerical probability is supplied here.

## 18. Assessment for the next revision

An August 1917 peace is a defensible narrative branch if the treaty has identifiable domestic supporters and authorized governments on both sides. The German command crisis, French territorial concession and participation of Italy and the other theatres are the principal immediate obstacles.

The stronger prosperity case comes from preserving customers, productive capacity and financial reliability while reducing military exposure. The stronger power case requires an additional argument: that Pacifica's useful role in recovery and its maritime and Asian position outweigh the reduced dependence of healthier European powers.

The branch has not yet demonstrated Russian stabilization, a compatible governing partner, Japanese protection of Transbaikalia or the particular 1932 endpoint. These are substantive tests to resolve before adoption. CR-032 remains available as the separately developed outcome if continued war and Russian revolutionary victory are preferred.
