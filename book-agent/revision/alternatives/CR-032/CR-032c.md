## 1. Proposed direction

Julian settles the succession and assumes the Pacifican and Hawaiian crowns in November 1907, followed by the Philippine oath and proclamation in Manila in December. Kiyoshi's confrontation and renunciation occur in February–March, before separate consent votes and the final Act of Settlement. Julian therefore begins the European tour of 1908 as an established sovereign of three coequal crowns, with the Japanese partnership and dynastic bargain already secured. He builds relationships supporting a Bosnian settlement that combines recognized sovereignty with national rights and commercial access, and an Agadir settlement that connects French–German accommodation to an expanding Pacifican–Japanese industrial economy.

His purpose is to increase Pacifica's wealth, security and influence by making its institutions and partnerships useful to more countries. He is willing to cultivate a stronger Germany, a more prosperous France, a reforming Habsburg monarchy and a capable Japan. Their growth provides markets and counterweights to the Union. Their participation also limits how freely Julian can command the system he helps build.

The working success case prevents a general European war in 1914 and establishes a continuing process for handling later crises. It permits constitutional reform, national self-government, changing commercial relationships and negotiated territorial adjustments. It does not require the permanent preservation of every dynasty, colonial arrangement or frontier.

The principal achievement is a different alignment of interests. Germany obtains attractive opportunities within the Pacifican–Japanese commercial network; France participates without surrendering its security relationships; Britain retains substantial access while accommodating Pacifica's independent leadership. Governments have practical reasons to resist a Union attempt to overturn the North American settlement by force.

The draft develops the accession, tour, Bosnia and Agadir as a connected sequence. The Balkan Wars, the immediate 1914 crisis and Russia's subsequent political course remain decisions to resolve before this alternative can replace the existing narrative.

## 3. The succession settlement from 1906 to 1908

### Preparations and the reason to settle the succession

The working sequence completes all three accessions in 1907. It replaces this draft's initial suggestion of leaving Victoria on the Hawaiian throne while Julian takes only the Realm's crown. Separate consent remains essential, but the principal political and ceremonial transition now occurs together.

The regional settlement after Kiyoshi's limited 1905 victory, the approaching 1907 compact renewal and Julian's coming of age provide a reason to act. Victoria has governed Pacifica since 1887. She wants the founder's succession resolved through law while she can still negotiate and defend the terms. Hawaiian and Filipino representatives seek enforceable institutional gains from the transition; Japanese partners seek recognition and durable rights. The proposal starts with negotiations during 1906 rather than compress every constitutional preparation into a few weeks in 1907.

Julian retains his 1905–1906 year with the Tokyo legation and navy. Court introductions in that year bring him into contact with Chiyo, allowing familiarity and her own political judgment to precede the marriage bargain. The new biographical working assumption is that she was born in 1886. She is therefore an adult at the proposed wedding in November 1907. Her exact birthday, parents and education remain unchosen; her identity as a woman from another Kuroda branch is preserved.

Julian returns to Pacifica in the latter part of 1906. The working calendar places his twenty-first birthday before the November 1907 accession; it assigns no exact birthday. His majority and the lawful instruments must both precede his assumption of personal sovereign authority.

### Silas's final year and the family wedding

The 1906 school-segregation crisis remains. Tate's board segregates Japanese pupils and revokes the mission school's licence. Silas lends the campaign his name despite his shame over the injury to his mother's institution. This creates an immediate contradiction between his nativist constituency and his daughter's marriage into the naval party.

Move the wedding of Silas's younger daughter and Tanabe Makoto from 1909 to autumn 1906, in the chapel of her grandmother's mission school. Tanabe is already attached to Kiyoshi's staff; his precise rank is to follow the revised service chronology. Kiyoshi attends as the victorious admiral, before his 1907 countship and Fleet-General appointment. The two elder sons meet for the only time. This preserves the scene's purpose and makes Tanabe Silas's son-in-law before he challenges Kiyoshi in the succession crisis.

After the wedding, Silas visits Victoria at Thalassia and confesses Donner in November 1906. She has known since 1887. His existing decline, shame and the sight of the family connection outlasting his politics motivate the confession; no new assassination or invented conspiracy removes him. He dies at Sacramento in December 1906, aged about fifty-five. The specific illness and final scene remain unchosen. An examined officer takes the inland command in early 1907, replacing the original 1913 appointment without handing it to an unqualified young favourite.

This is a proposed six-year advance of Silas's ending. The revised branch no longer preserves his 1912 death as a condition. His death leaves Kiyoshi the founder's eldest surviving son before the claim, retaining the main narrative's cause for that claim rather than inventing a different seniority argument while Silas is still alive.

### Kiyoshi's challenge in February and March 1907

The compact negotiations carry into early 1907. Kiyoshi receives his countship and Fleet-General appointment before the February confrontation; the exact days remain unspecified. The renewed partnership includes the joint war council, family immigration and civic equality for Japanese Pacificans. Domestic enactments and the final succession terms complete its implementation during 1907. Japan's institutional rights are not contingent on Kiyoshi becoming king.

Kiyoshi petitions Victoria in February as Elias's eldest surviving son, offering a lifetime reign followed by Julian. Victoria identifies the contradiction: an eldest-son principle also gives his own son a future claim. The coming Act and Julian's majority create an immediate deadline. Kiyoshi believes his recent victory, newly recognized standing and the naval party's importance allow him to deny the succession its two-thirds majority.

The western squadron anchors off Kāneʻohe Bay on a courtesy visit during February. Kiyoshi gives no order. He threatens the availability of naval-party officers for the agreed command rather than declaring war, firing on Thalassia or mounting a battle against Pearl Harbor. The squadron's presence conveys his leverage without proving that its officers will support coercion.

His coalition fails through institutions already present in 1907. The ʻAha Aliʻi supports Julian's Hawaiian succession and will not supply Kiyoshi with Hawaiian rank; Thalassia stands on Hawaiian throne land. Victoria publishes his memorial. Tate's nativist faction cannot openly join a Japanese claimant even while opposing Julian's settlement. The Five keep their estates and institutions out of the factional contest. Examined officers refuse a political refusal of duty, and Tanabe conveys that refusal to Kiyoshi personally, now as the man who married his elder brother's daughter.

Tokyo's response comes from the Meiji court and Saionji's government. They defend Japanese rights within the partnership while refusing to authorize a Japanese count's acquisition of an independent foreign crown on his personal initiative. In this proposed decision, accepting that crown would require Kiyoshi to relinquish his Japanese command and the position binding him to his branch house. He risks losing the name and standing Japan has given him. This is a fictional political decision by the existing court and cabinet, not an asserted automatic rule copied from a later crisis.

Victoria is sixty-eight during the confrontation, turning sixty-nine in November. Kiyoshi is not attempting to outlast a seventy-five-year-old regent. Nor can he rely on Uehara's resignation bringing down Saionji in December 1912, or Yamamoto's fall in March 1914. Those historical events are not moved into 1907. The immediate failures of his coalition, and Tokyo's refusal to endorse personal elevation, replace their dramatic functions. [National Diet Library on the 1912 cabinet crisis](https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha3/description01.html), [Japanese political chronology](https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/utility/chronology.html).

### The Pearl Harbor bargain and Chiyo's assent

In March 1907 Julian goes to Pearl Harbor without an escort and asks his brother in Japanese for a wife from his house. The substance of the original confrontation survives: the younger man recognizes Kiyoshi's standing and offers his house a place in the succession without surrendering the immediate crown.

Chiyo's assent and the agreement of her own branch form part of the settlement. Kiyoshi cannot convey her as property or make her his biological daughter. Her prospective position offers Japanese-origin families and officers continuing access to the court, and gives her a political undertaking of her own. The couple's prior acquaintance in Tokyo makes the bargain a marriage they can choose as well as a dynastic settlement.

Kiyoshi renounces for himself and his descendants in March, with Japanese assent to the agreed terms. His own branch and Japanese name remain; Kiyonaka retains the main Kuroda house. The marriage is contracted for later in 1907. His countship and naval command continue on their institutional terms, and a Kuroda connection will enter the next generation through Chiyo. The public recognition he wanted arrives in the final Act, which names him "Kuroda Kiyoshi, son of Elias Thorne". He never uses the Thorne name.

### Constitutional consent before the final Act

The October–March Season is retained. Preparatory legislation passes before the session ends in March 1907, after the claimant agreement. It provides lawful procedures for Hawaiian restoration, Philippine representation, published terms and the separate votes. It does not bring the crowns into union before their peoples have consented.

In Hawaii, Victoria and the competent Realm authorities remove the appointed Territorial Council and restore the legislature through the preparatory settlement. The 1876 judgment remains valid; ending the encroachment does not reverse the judgment or retrospectively legalize the earlier ordinances. The restored Hawaiian legislature and the ʻAha Aliʻi place the written union and succession terms before Hawaiian subjects. The campaign runs through spring and summer, and the union wins narrowly.

In the Philippines, the preparatory organic arrangements convene the representative Cortes using the political institutions developed through the municipal tribunals and provincial juntas. Calderón prepares the organic terms in 1906–1907. The Cortes offers the crown under a signed instrument, and a separate summer plebiscite endorses the terms broadly. The offer precedes the vote; it is not an annexation announced during a royal visit. Existing dissent and the conditional southern compacts are not erased by the result.

Retain the kingdom's single-chamber Cortes and permanent commission, its own budget and army, and the rule against posting a Gobernador to his home province. The ʻAha Aliʻi retains custody of Hawaiian crown and kuleana lands; Thalassia's land remains land of the Hawaiian throne. Each crown keeps its own legislature and language, and the alternat gives each first place in its own instruments. A Kuhina Nui and Lugarteniente General conduct the respective day-to-day governments without reviving the superseded personal veto.

The Realm accepts the agreed remaining Philippine Redemption Loan obligation at the 1907 transfer. Its balance must be valued at that date; the approximately $9.5 million quoted for the original later settlement is not copied backwards or treated as recalculated.

In October 1907, after both votes and the necessary enactments by the participating institutions, Senate and Chamber pass the final Act of Settlement by two-thirds. It creates the Crown of Pacifica in the House of Thorne-Kamehameha, records Kiyoshi's renunciation and embodies the separately approved union terms. The ʻAha Aliʻi proclaims Julian heir to the Hawaiian crown as Kamehameha VI. The succession remains eldest son, then eldest daughter, otherwise an adopted heir with Senate consent. The final instrument belongs to the next ordinary Season; it is not an out-of-session royal ordinance creating the sovereign's own title.

### November and December 1907 and the Manila ceremony

Victoria lays down the Pacifican regency and Hawaiian crown in November 1907. Julian succeeds her directly in Hawaii and assumes the Realm's sovereignty under the Act. The Hawaiian accession and coronation are distinct from the Realm's constitutional proclamation, even when held within the same sequence of festivities. Victoria remains his hānai mother and a former queen with her own Hawaiian commitments; she does not continue a separate reign until 1915 or resume the regency when he travels.

Julian and Chiyo marry in November, after his accession and before the Manila visit. Exact days and wedding ritual remain open. The sequence leaves time for the voyage to Manila and does not place the wedding, Hawaiian coronation and Philippine proclamation on a single day or in a single city. Thalassia's existing halls and public spaces can serve the constitutional events; the sequence does not require every later capital building to be finished eight years early.

In December Julian swears before the Cortes in Manila and is proclaimed under the raised standard. This is the advanced Manila accession ceremony, or the Manila coronation festivities in the broader narrative sense. Preserve its particular rite: no bishop crowns him, and Philippine kingship rests on the offer, ratification and constitutional oath. The supporting ecclesiastical arrangements provide a Filipino archbishop for the Cathedral Te Deum by this date, building on the earlier diocesan settlement; no named historical archbishop's appointment is silently backdated. Victoria sits in a pew and sings the hymn shared by her church and Rome. Chiyo attends with her own place in the new dynasty.

The three accessions are complete by the end of 1907. Ka Lōkahi begins operating as the council of the three crowns, with one vote each. Its agreed matters receive their first ten-year review in 1917 and their second in 1927. These are not the five-year executive term or two-party Council later established with Japan. The proposed combined style remains subject to each crown's own instruments; Julian is never President.

### The archive and the first years of personal rule

The regency's sealed papers pass to Julian with the November 1907 transfer. While looking for evidence about Noe, he discovers the concealed Donner record and learns that Victoria knowingly preserved the lie. He tells no one. Silas's recent private confession and Julian's documentary discovery are separate events, and the public account of 1887 remains intact. Noe's death remains unresolved; the archive establishes neither a murderer nor Victoria's guilt in it.

The marriage establishes Chiyo's household and patronage before the European tour. Julian departs with his official delegation in late spring 1908; the working sequence does not require Chiyo to accompany every visit. She remains in the Pacific during the pregnancy and Kahekili's birth in late 1908, developing her own court and Japanese connections. His names and identity remain Kahekili / Ikazuchi, the son of Julian and Chiyo and future Kamehameha VII, distinct from the lost Kalanimoku.

On returning from the main European negotiations, Julian's confrontation with the Senate over county administration and his manipulation of its voting machinery move into 1910–1911. The specific Australian petition retains its existing qualification. The incident now shows a ruler already successful abroad pressing his domestic authority too far. It is not repeated as a second accession crisis in 1915–1917. Soberanes's retirement and Vance's succession to the Polytechnic/General Staff seat can remain in 1915; an earlier crown does not make Vance ready for that office eight years sooner.

## 4. The European tour in 1908 and 1909

The proposed tour begins in late spring 1908, after the marriage and all three accessions, with follow-up visits and ministerial negotiations extending into 1909. Its itinerary is a working sequence rather than a set of historical meetings. Julian travels as a young sovereign supported by experienced ministers, commercial specialists and interpreters. His established Japanese, English, Spanish and Hawaiian do not confer unprovided fluency in French or German.

Ministers and the respective day-to-day governments continue under the settled constitutions during his absence. Victoria is available as an experienced adviser and an authorized diplomatic representative where a commission is given; travel does not restore her regency or Hawaiian reign. Chiyo's remaining in the Pacific for the late-1908 birth is part of the itinerary, rather than an implication that the entire royal household visits every European capital.

## 5. Bosnia in 1908 and 1909

### The crisis and the negotiating method

The working sequence retains the Habsburg annexation announcement in October 1908. Julian responds to an actual crisis rather than having perfect advance knowledge of private Austro-Russian discussions. He seeks agreement by spring 1909 on sovereignty, local constitutional rights, Serbian access, Ottoman compensation and reciprocal security obligations.

Franz Joseph's contemporary correspondence and proclamation already connected annexation to constitutional government and withdrawal from the Sanjak of Novi Pazar. They provide a basis on which Julian can demand concrete enactments rather than invent an entirely new public justification. Those documents also express the monarchy's own interested account of its rule. [September–October 1908 documents](https://ungarisches-institut.de/dokumente/pdf/19081005-1.pdf).

The instrument is negotiated through a limited, pre-agreed agenda. The relevant Berlin Treaty parties authorize the necessary amendments; Pacifica participates by invitation as mediator and financier. An unrestricted conference that reopens every territorial claim would be much harder for Vienna to accept.

### Terms for the principal parties

| Party | Receives | Accepts |
|---|---|---|
| Austria-Hungary | International recognition of sovereignty in Bosnia and Herzegovina; reduced Ottoman and Russian opposition; a more dependable Serbian commercial relationship | Effective local constitutional powers, a funded reform programme and reciprocal restraints on coercion |
| Bosnia and Herzegovina | A distinct common provincial position, a representative Diet with meaningful local legislative and budget authority, rights for the principal communities and a gradual agrarian settlement | Habsburg sovereignty and common imperial responsibilities within the negotiated constitution |
| Serbia | Predictable agricultural access, workable southern transit and security against invasion or regime change as the ordinary response to disputes | Recognition of the Bosnian settlement and effective action against armed conspiracies involving its officials or territory |
| Ottoman Empire | Austrian compensation, renunciation of Habsburg military privileges in Novi Pazar and protected Muslim religious, property and nationality rights | Recognition of the transfer of sovereignty through an authorized agreement |
| Russia | A public role in the settlement, enforceable Serbian and Orthodox protections and a connected Bulgarian financial settlement | Recognition of the Bosnian settlement without a Straits concession imposed on unwilling parties |

Bosnia remains outside the exclusive administration of either Austria or Hungary. It is not immediately transformed into a third South Slav kingdom. Bosnian Serb, Muslim and Croat representatives participate in the constitutional terms; no single group is permitted to speak for the entire province.

Land reform proceeds through registration, adjudication and compensated tenant purchase with affordable financing. The allocation of costs between provincial revenue, imperial support and loans requires assessment. Foreign lending cannot make an unsustainable land settlement affordable merely by extending its maturity.

Serbia receives a dependable agricultural trade arrangement, veterinary arbitration and improved use of the existing route through Skopje to Salonica. Rolling stock, refrigeration and bonded warehouses can provide gains before major new construction is completed. The proposal does not invent Serbia's first southern outlet or promise an instantly completed mountain railway. Transit rests on Ottoman consent and would require the assent of any later sovereign controlling Salonica.

Security obligations are reciprocal. Serbia investigates and prosecutes offences within its jurisdiction through agreed evidence and judicial procedures. Austria-Hungary accepts consultation, inquiry and a temporary mutual standstill before coercive action. Austrian police do not acquire executive authority inside Serbia. A standing commission handles commercial grievances, cross-border incidents and disputes over compliance.

### Compensation and Russian participation

The historical Austro-Ottoman protocol of 26 February 1909 supplies a real negotiating anchor for recognizing annexation and settling the Novi Pazar question. The proposed additional constitutional and Serbian guarantees are counterfactual changes. [Austrian State Archives record of the protocol](https://archivinformationssystem.at/detail.aspx?ID=6766872).

The Bulgarian settlement is connected diplomatically but retains its own parties and obligations. Russia can remit part of the Ottoman war-indemnity claim while Bulgaria assumes a corresponding obligation to Russia, allowing recognition of Bulgarian independence. Historical documents show that such a financial structure existed; it is not a Pacifican gift paying every claimant. No historical sum is adopted as a newly validated CR-032c model input. [Protocols reproduced in Georges Scelle's 1912 study](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-journal-of-international-law/article/abs/bulgarian-independence/E90790C0D83A67989838D6D9ABC124D8).

Opening the Straits to Russian warships is excluded from the automatic compensation package. It requires separate Ottoman and great-power agreement. Russian participation must have value without promising a concession that Julian cannot deliver.

### Why this changes the next crisis

The historical crisis also ended without a European war. The proposed difference is a settlement that avoids treating Russian and Serbian humiliation as the mechanism of peace, establishes reciprocal obligations and begins delivering constitutional and commercial benefits.

The hardest concessions remain Vienna's acceptance of enforceable restraint and Serbian acceptance of Habsburg Bosnia. Some nationalists reject the bargain. Its credibility depends on courts, provincial budgets and trade working during 1909–1911. A signed agreement followed by continued arbitrary rule would not create a different road to 1914.

## 6. Agadir in 1911

### The historical opening and the change required

France and Germany had already attempted a political and commercial accommodation over Morocco in 1909. A shared public works enterprise existed before the crisis. British parliamentary questions in February 1911 show that its national allocation of capital was itself contentious. Julian must improve delivery, incentives and access rather than present the existence of a consortium as an original solution. [Commons debate of 28 February 1911](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1911/feb/28/morocco-british-commercial-interests).

The German gunboat Panther arrives at Agadir on 1 July 1911 after French military intervention in Morocco. Kiderlen-Wächter seeks substantial African territorial compensation and a defensible German national success. His own correspondence makes the political obstacle explicit: attractive commercial prospects cannot automatically replace the territorial concession he demands. Historically the settlement of 4 November paired German acceptance of French predominance in Morocco with substantial French territorial concessions in Equatorial Africa. CR-032c instead makes a larger industrial agreement the principal prize, with a limited African frontier adjustment available if needed for agreement. ### The connected agreements

| Agreement | French gain | German gain | Pacifican interest |

The agreements are negotiated together but remain separately enforceable. An unsuccessful commercial investment does not restore a right to threaten Morocco or seize a port.

### France and Caillaux

Caillaux becomes premier on 27 June 1911 and is the principal prospective French partner. De Selves remains responsible for foreign affairs and Jules Cambon negotiates in Berlin. Julian supports an authorized negotiating channel and a parliamentary case for the agreement. He does not create a private royal arrangement that leaves French ministers unable to defend their own policy.

Caillaux's historical government fell in January 1912 amid conflict over his diplomacy. Preserving a durable French constituency for the bargain is therefore a substantive change. [French Senate biography](https://www.senat.fr/senateur-3eme-republique/caillaux_joseph1254r3.html).

France receives financing for assessed Moroccan needs and access to additional investment opportunities across Pacifica's network. French firms compete for equipment, engineering and shipping business; participation must produce a domestic industrial benefit as well as returns to investors. The French government retains its British and Russian relationships. Julian does not ask it to exchange its continental security for remote commercial promises.

Consultations and access to negotiated supplies or emergency credit can support French confidence. An unlimited Pacifican guarantee or a promised continental army would exceed the proposed bargain. Alsace-Lorraine is not settled by the Moroccan agreement and is not quietly conceded on France's behalf.

### Germany and the first commercial commitments

Germany receives a group of executable contracts rather than a prospectus promising unrestricted access to all of Asia. Candidate projects include port machinery, power equipment and manufacturing partnerships on territory within Pacifican jurisdiction. Fuel and refining arrangements involving the Gulf Republic require its government and the relevant firms to agree. Chinese projects require Chinese authority and a credible assessment of the changing political situation.

Participating German firms contribute their own engineering and chemical expertise. Pacifican firms contribute complementary production methods, intellectual property and commercial infrastructure; Japanese firms bring advanced construction, manufacturing and finance. Technical agreements are reciprocal and preserve commercially valuable rights on each side. Japan is neither a labour reserve nor a late pupil dependent on European or Pacifican instruction.

Shipping services, freight agreements and commercial bunkering support the projects. The initial package should have identified sponsors, paid subscriptions or authorized credit, tenders, delivery dates and customers. The project list, values and participants remain OPEN or MODEL; no named firm is assumed to accept an uneconomic contract.

The political offer includes reciprocal renunciation of forcible revision of Germany's existing Pacific position while the agreements are observed. German islands are not transferred to Pacifica as the price of remaining at peace. Germany's existing treaty position in China requires Chinese participation in any change; Julian does not own Chinese sovereignty to grant or confirm away.

In return, Germany recognizes Pacifica's regional standing, agrees to specified limits on new military establishments and renounces support for forcible revision of the North American settlement. German commercial access need not include a naval foothold in Pacifica's strategic core.

A limited Cameroon frontier adjustment is the preferred political fallback. A settlement with no African territorial compensation is an additional option requiring a larger German political divergence. Wilhelm and Bethmann must be willing to overrule or replace Kiderlen if his demands block an otherwise acceptable agreement. Julian cannot purchase that decision simply by promising better returns.

### Moroccan government and development

The working political arrangement preserves the Sultan and Moroccan institutions within a more limited French-led protective relationship than the historical outcome. France obtains recognized responsibilities for external security and specified assistance to administration. The limits, duration and review of those responsibilities require an agreed instrument.

Moroccan representatives participate in taxation, borrowing, concessions and the allocation of local development revenue. Merchants, religious authorities and regional representatives have channels to influence decisions and present grievances. Land acquisition requires published terms, adjudication and compensation. Workers receive wages and training, and the programme develops Moroccan administrative and technical capacity.

The development budget includes Moroccan services and productive works rather than assigning every additional receipt to foreign debt service. Borrowing must be supportable from realistic revenue. The Sultan's assent is necessary but does not establish the consent of every community or end resistance by itself.

These provisions are a genuine restraint on French unilateral authority and on concessionaires. Their acceptance depends on the fiscal and strategic benefits offered to France and the political leverage created by the wider settlement. They are not presented as an easy expression of existing colonial policy.

### Britain Spain and existing treaty rights

Britain participates early. Germany provides a clear statement against a naval base at Agadir and a route to withdrawal. British companies retain fair commercial opportunities, and Spain participates in provisions affecting its interests. The arrangement does not become an exclusive Franco-German commercial cartel.

Grey's historical statement identifies British exclusion and uncertainty over German intentions as important sources of escalation. Early consultation changes those mechanisms without assuming that every Anglo-German dispute disappears. [Grey's statement of 27 November 1911](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1911-11-27/debates/9149f840-aef6-49b7-b4d0-d15584814f0a/SirEdwardGreySStatement).

Existing commercial treaty rights must be identified separately from membership in a new investment programme. If the Union retains the historical American rights, Pacifica cannot erase them through a Franco-German agreement. The historical American response required Senate participation for adherence to a settlement modifying treaty rights. This is a relevant institutional comparison, not proof of the alternate Union's exact position at Algeciras. ## 8. Finance and commercial authority

Julian's offers distinguish Treasury commitments, regulated banking, Crown interests and private investment. The existing arrangements for chartered industries, competitive champions and independent banks remain. No Crown bank shareholding or personal ownership of every European claim is created.

Government participation takes the form of specifically authorized subscriptions, contracts, export guarantees or limited credit facilities. Private lenders and corporate boards decide whether to participate on the proposed terms. Creditworthiness, currency, collateral, construction risk and expected traffic must be assessed project by project.

Each participating government can publish the political obligations and authorized fiscal exposure while commercial negotiations retain appropriate confidentiality. Public scrutiny does not require broadcasting every negotiating position in advance. The essential purpose is to prevent contradictory private assurances and obligations that a government cannot defend before its legislature.

The first projects should be few enough to supervise and useful enough to create a constituency for continuation. No blanket investment guarantee covers all French and German activity in Africa or Asia. China’s 1911 upheaval is a reason to sequence commitments carefully, not a source of effortless new concessions.

Pacifica's return consists of industrial and transport revenue, investment income, useful standards, stronger customers and lower security exposure. Royal prestige can help convene the parties, but it cannot turn a poor project into a productive asset.

## 9. Union containment and the changing balance of power

The Union remains resource-rich, industrially capable and hostile to Pacifica's position. Its emulation programme continues under the established presidential chronology, including Taft in 1911. Pacifica's aim is to reduce the prospect that Union power can be combined with European or Japanese military support against it.

The proposed agreements seek three concrete results:

1. **Recognition:** participating governments recognize Pacifica and the Gulf Republic and accept the North American settlement as the basis of relations. They oppose its forcible revision.
2. **Limits on official military support:** governments withhold bases, state-backed arms credit and comparable assistance from a Union campaign of territorial revision. Reciprocal undertakings against assistance to aggression elsewhere make the obligation defensible beyond Pacifica's immediate interests. Definitions and enforcement require negotiation.
3. **Competitive opportunities:** European and Japanese firms receive commercially attractive alternatives to exclusive dependence on Union markets, finance or strategic partnerships. Agreements protect specified intellectual property and restrict unauthorized military sublicensing.

Ordinary Union commerce and lawful investment continue. Pacifica cannot maintain a permanent global embargo, prevent the Union from innovating or command every foreign bank's portfolio. European prosperity can also strengthen Union trade and finance. The containment case depends on relative gains, political commitments, continued deterrence and Pacifica's own capacity to keep innovating.

The main alignment changes are:

| Power | Opportunity created by Pacifica | Consequence for the traditional alignments |
|---|---|---|
| Germany | Commercial recognition and valuable access to Pacifican and Japanese development | Overseas growth need not depend on confronting Britain or aligning with the Union. Germany's independent ambitions remain. |
| France | More outlets for capital and industry, with greater security from negotiated German restraint | Participation in the Entente can coexist with meaningful cooperation with Germany and Pacifica. |
| Britain | Access to development, shipping and financial business, with clearer limits on colonial confrontations | Britain bargains with Pacifica as an independent power and has reasons to seek accommodation with Germany. |
| Austria-Hungary | A more workable Balkan position and development linked to national accommodation | Its security can rest less exclusively on demonstrations of force and German backing. Hungarian and national consent remain difficult. |
| Russia | Development finance and a Balkan settlement in which it has a public role | It has alternatives to confrontation but retains serious disagreements with Japan and domestic constitutional pressures. |
| Japan | Reciprocal industrial and financial participation with European partners | It helps shape the system and retains a direct stake in the Pacifican partnership. |

No unified European political bloc is created. Overlapping agreements permit cooperation while governments retain independent interests and suppliers. A programme that visibly subordinates every participant to Pacifica could instead encourage Britain, Germany or the Union to organize a counter-coalition. Meaningful shared gains and restraint over sovereign decisions are therefore part of Julian's security strategy.

Carnegie can advocate peace and profitable international investment as an independent figure. His Pacifican securities do not make him willing to endorse permanent Union weakness or serve as Julian's agent.

## 10. The conditions for avoiding a general war in 1914

Bosnia and Agadir establish experience and interests that can improve the response to the next crisis. They do not settle Macedonia, Albania, Ottoman security, Balkan territorial claims, colonial rivalry, continental war plans or the Anglo-German naval dispute.

The next development must address the Balkan Wars of 1912–1913. If they still occur, the parties need credible territorial and transit settlements, minority protections, limits on external sponsorship of renewed war and procedures for frontier disputes. Serbia's access through Salonica must continue by agreement with the sovereign actually controlling the route. The 1909 settlement supplies no perpetual concession over territory belonging to another state.

A naval agreement can include consultation, building information and negotiated limits tied to actual forces and strategic requirements. It must permit review when a non-participating Union expands. The model's combined Pacifican–Japanese parity with Britain in 1919 is not a statement of the fleet balance in 1908 or 1911.

Two routes to preventing general war remain available for author selection:

- **A different immediate crisis:** improved constitutional practice, security or the altered movements of the principal actors prevent the historical Sarajevo assassination. Its absence needs a specific causal scene rather than an unexplained disappearance.
- **A contained crisis after an assassination:** an attack still occurs, but the governments use judicial cooperation, inquiry and a mutual standstill. Berlin conditions support on those procedures, Russia supports verified investigation while restraining escalation, and the parties keep mobilization and railway preparations from silently deciding the political outcome.

The second route tests the institutions more severely and demonstrates a substantive change from earlier diplomacy. It is the preferred option for further development, not a selected fictional event. Austria must have a credible way to obtain accountability; Serbia must have sovereignty and a government capable of complying. Neither an unlimited German commitment nor an unconditional Russian promise to Serbia is compatible with the proposed restraint.

Franz Ferdinand's survival, the circumstances of any assassination, cabinet mandates and the exact crisis timetable remain OPEN. The working success outcome is no general European war in 1914; a claim of permanent peace would require more than these settlements can establish.

## 12. Russia Asia and the later settlement

The branch preserves Russia's stronger European development and weaker sovereign Pacific access as the earlier narrative balance, without assigning a net numerical gain. Domestic industrial and agrarian conflict continues. Avoiding a Great War removes an important historical source of military, fiscal and provisioning pressure, so a revolution at the same time cannot simply be assumed to have the same causes.

The author's earlier requirement for the 1917 revolution remains a continuity issue requiring explicit resolution. One route retains that timing through a separately developed constitutional confrontation, land conflict and administrative failure. Another revises the timing and subsequent government by a new author decision. This draft selects neither a cancellation of the revolution nor a predetermined Bolshevik victory.

Julian's concern about revolutionary movements can develop through the existing Russian unrest, political reporting and financial scrutiny. It does not require him to predict Lenin's exact success or claim knowledge of institutions not yet created. A policy of targeted assistance, constitutional stabilization or containment would follow the circumstances actually developed.

The federal Russian settlement, Ukrainian and Crimean provisions, Donbas arrangements and Japanese protection of Transbaikalia need a new negotiating basis if Russia does not undergo CR-032's defeat and civil-war sequence. Japan's protection objective cannot be awarded through an unrelated Franco-German agreement. Local authority, Russian acceptance and the costs and duration of protection remain necessary questions.

Without a European war, a Czechoslovak Legion formed from that war's prisoners and defectors does not arise in its existing form. Czech and Slovak national rights must be pursued through the monarchy's constitutional politics. The earlier federal direction and avoidance of punitive Austrian terms can remain goals, but their dates and political route must be developed independently. Karl is not given sovereign authority before his accession.

German Pacific islands and Tsingtao are likewise not acquired through a war that never begins. Chinese sovereignty and the existing Japanese partnership remain constraints. Peace may create opportunities to convert some military positions into negotiated commercial arrangements, but no such transfer is adopted here.

## 13. Power and prosperity through 1932

The strongest prosperity case is a larger and less damaged market for Pacifican and Japanese production, more reliable foreign income, useful reciprocal innovation and fewer costly military commitments. The strongest power case is a network of capable states whose interests support Pacifica's sovereignty and whose development uses routes, firms and institutions in which Pacifica has a durable stake.

|---|---|---|---|
| European conflict | Great War followed by the developed Russian campaign | War ends through an August 1917 settlement | General war in 1914 is prevented in the working success case |
| Pacifican diplomatic intervention | Wartime finance and coalition leadership | A wartime turn toward general peace | Earlier accession, crisis settlements and development agreements |

| Union containment | Military deterrence, allied interests and later financial links | Healthier European partners with a revised financing pattern | Recognition, official restraint and attractive overlapping partnerships |

CR-032 already avoids a punitive German reparations programme. Neither alternative gains an additional advantage merely by removing a Versailles burden absent from the comparison case. The Union's existing neutrality during the Great War likewise cannot be counted as a new achievement of CR-032c.

Earlier peace changes the financial geography. The later split between Union claims in Berlin and Vienna and Pacifican claims in London, Paris and Asia cannot be carried over unchanged. Nor can Britain owe the same wartime debt, transfer bases for an unaccumulated liability or become a dependent partner on the same timetable.

The 1923 Japanese earthquake can remain an external event. Japan's industrial balance sheets, banking exposure and reconstruction bargain change with its previous development. The 1927 Concord, 1931 banking crises and 1932 Union association retain their existing authority in the main narrative but require a new causal account in this alternative. Peace does not eliminate speculative lending, maturity mismatches, currency pressures or political conflict.

The advanced succession also changes the internal constitutional setting. Ka Lōkahi's reviews of agreed matters fall in 1917 and 1927. Its Hawaiian and Philippine delegations can still support the Concord in 1927, but their support does not override the Realm Senate's refusal. The three-crown review and the five-year Japanese–Pacifican executive settlement remain separate processes. Kahekili's 1932 betrothal and marriage now concern an adult heir, not a sixteen-year-old, and the 1932 plot must reflect that difference without giving him Japan's throne or an earlier Hawaiian reign.

A larger network may make Pacifica richer and safer while reducing its power to dictate any particular partner's policy. Whether it produces greater relative dominance through 1932 is a separate question from whether it produces greater prosperity.

## 17. Continuity and integration if selected

Selection would require rewriting the actual succession and diplomatic narrative, with corresponding changes to character documentation and the historical background. Section 3 supplies the proposed sequence and a crosswalk for the displaced dates. The earlier succession must be carried through the master storyline's headings, summaries, family references and 1932 scenes, rather than merely appended beside the unchanged 1914–1915 account. War-dependent Russian, colonial, financial and personal events still require their own decisions. Local review labels in CR-032c are not canonical decision identifiers.

The corresponding narrative order is:

1. **1905–1906:** limited Russian naval defeat, Julian's Tokyo formation, the segregation crisis, the family wedding and Silas's ending.
2. **1907:** compact renewal, Kiyoshi's challenge, the Pearl Harbor bargain, restored institutions, votes, the Act, accession, marriage and the sealed papers.
3. **December 1907–1909:** Manila completes the accession sequence; the European tour and Bosnian diplomacy follow, with Chiyo's household and Kahekili's late-1908 birth in the Pacific.
4. **1910–1913:** the domestic Senate confrontation, Agadir, the Qing collapse and support for Sun, and the Balkan tests. Julian acts throughout as the reigning sovereign, and no second Kiyoshi succession crisis occurs.
5. **1914 onward:** follow the war-prevention branch and retain the independent 1915 Soberanes/Vance transition. The Twenty-One Demands, Russian intervention and later personal ruptures are assessed on their own changed causes; they are not moved into 1907 with the coronations.

The three drafts describe distinct causal branches. CR-032 develops victory over a Bolshevik state after a Great War. CR-032b attempts to stop that war in 1917. CR-032c attempts to create different incentives and institutions before the war begins. Elements can be combined only where their political and material causes remain compatible.

The proposed choice is to develop Pacifica's predominance through a larger and more cooperative economy whose capable partners help sustain it. That offers Julian greater productive scope and fewer opportunities to rule through another government's distress. Its success depends on his willingness to share enough benefits and authority for those partners to continue choosing participation.
