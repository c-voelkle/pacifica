## 1. The neutrality scenario

Julian assumes all three crowns in 1907 and spends the next seven years making the Japanese partnership more dependable, widening European relationships, restricting opportunities for external support to Union aggression and preparing transport, credit and intelligence arrangements. The assassination at Sarajevo still occurs. In July 1914 he helps turn the existing Bosnian obligations into a verified settlement, while Germany supports enforceable Serbian compliance instead of an unrestricted Austrian campaign. Austria obtains a substantial security and diplomatic success without invading Serbia. Russia accepts an outcome that preserves Serbian sovereignty and its own standing. No general war follows.

From 1914 through 1932 Pacifica pursues armed neutrality and selective cooperation. In this scenario neutrality means independence from the European military blocs and readiness to discharge agreed defensive obligations. It does not require a European war to exist, nor does it cancel the Japanese compact. The working scenario assumes no later general European war and no Russian civil-war campaign. Earlier conflicts, including the limited 1904–1905 confrontation and the Balkan Wars of 1912–1913, remain part of its background.

Pacifica grows through productive investment, trade, maritime capability and the usefulness of its institutions. Japan obtains reliable defence and profitable peacetime opportunities. Britain, France and Germany remain stronger, less indebted and more independent than in the war alternatives. The Union continues to industrialize and compete, but does not receive the particular opportunities created by European wartime dependence. None of those developments proves Pacifican global supremacy without a financial and strategic comparison.

This completed version replaces CR-032d's earlier branching framework with one working path. The July settlement, Russia's constitutional transition in 1917, negotiated Transbaikalian protection, gradual Danubian accommodation and the 1927 Japanese partnership treaty are proposed mechanisms for that path. They are sufficiently specified to identify costs and consequences, while their political feasibility remains a subject for review. They are not authorial approval to alter the canon.

The earlier succession retains its dramatic costs. Julian knows Donner before the European tour, interferes with the Senate's voting machinery in 1910–1911 and increasingly uses institutions to advance personal power. Without the Russian campaign there is no Ob death, Altai atrocity or resulting murder-and-archive sequence. Their removal changes the ending rather than transferring those scenes to a substitute war.

## 3. The succession and its necessary chronological changes

### The 1904–1906 background

The small Russian squadron remains based on a prepared Petropavlovsk depot during the 1904–1905 confrontation. In May 1905 Kiyoshi captures one cruiser and compels the damaged survivors to withdraw. Russia retains its principal battle fleets. His victory is operationally important and establishes his prestige without restoring the historical destruction of the Baltic Fleet. The earlier loss of Vladivostok and useful Pacific ports strengthens Russia's European concentration while weakening its supply position; neither advantage nor disadvantage eliminates industrial, agrarian and constitutional conflict.

Julian spends 1905–1906 with the Tokyo legation and navy. He meets Chiyo, a member of another Kuroda branch, before a marriage is proposed. Retain the working birth year of 1886 for her, making her an adult at the November 1907 wedding; her exact birthday, parentage and education remain OPEN. Julian returns in the latter part of 1906. His majority precedes his personal assumption of sovereignty in November 1907; this sequence does not assign an exact birthday.

The 1906 school-segregation dispute remains a real breach of the partnership. Tate's board segregates Japanese pupils and revokes the mission school's licence. Silas associates himself with that campaign, while his daughter chooses marriage to Tanabe Makoto. The contrast supplies a domestic reason to make the partnership's civic undertakings enforceable.

Move the Tanabe wedding from 1909 to autumn 1906, in the mission-school chapel. Kiyoshi attends as the victorious admiral, before the titles he receives in 1907. The two elder sons meet for the only time. Tanabe is already attached to Kiyoshi's staff, with his exact rank to follow the revised service chronology. He is Silas's son-in-law before the succession confrontation.

Silas confesses Donner to Victoria at Thalassia in November 1906 and dies at Sacramento in December, aged about fifty-five. She has known since 1887. His decline, shame and the family wedding motivate the confession; the specific final illness remains OPEN. An examined officer succeeds him in the inland command in early 1907. This six-year advance of his ending is necessary if Kiyoshi is to claim as the founder's eldest surviving son in 1907.

### The confrontation and consent in 1907

Kiyoshi receives his countship and Fleet-General appointment early in 1907, before his February petition. The approaching succession and compact renewal supply the deadline. He asks Victoria to acknowledge his senior claim; the western squadron makes its courtesy visit off Kāneʻohe. The pressure remains politically dangerous, but no invented order to fire turns it into a war between the partners.

The claim fails because Tokyo, the ʻAha Aliʻi, the Five, the constitutional officers and Kiyoshi's own professional officers will not make a sovereign by naval pressure. Meiji and Saionji belong to this scene. Taishō, Uehara's December 1912 resignation and the 1914 Siemens scandal do not. Victoria is sixty-eight in February 1907 and turns sixty-nine in November; the argument cannot depend on waiting out a seventy-five-year-old regent.

In March Julian goes without an escort to Pearl Harbor and speaks to Kiyoshi in Japanese. He asks for a wife from his house, offering dynastic recognition and a place in the future rather than immediate surrender of the crown. Chiyo's independent assent and that of her own branch form part of the agreement. She does not become Kiyoshi's biological or adopted daughter to simplify the genealogy.

Kiyoshi renounces for himself and his descendants in March, with Japanese assent. He retains his Japanese name, his branch countship and his command on their institutional terms; Kiyonaka retains the main Kuroda house. The final Act publicly names him “Kuroda Kiyoshi, son of Elias Thorne.” He never takes the Thorne name. His renunciation and the marriage bargain do not replace Hawaiian or Philippine consent.

Preparatory laws pass before the October–March Season closes in March 1907. They provide the lawful procedures for restoring the Hawaiian legislature, publishing terms, convening the representative Philippine Cortes and holding separate votes. Calderón's constitutional work moves into 1906–1907. The Hawaiian legislature and ʻAha Aliʻi submit the agreed terms to Hawaiian subjects; the Cortes offers the Philippine crown under a written instrument before its own vote. Both votes occur in spring–summer, with a narrow Hawaiian result and broad Philippine endorsement. Existing dissent and the conditional southern compacts remain.

The Hawaiian settlement removes the appointed Territorial Council and ends the encroachment condemned by the 1876 judgment. The judgment is neither reversed nor retrospectively satisfied by the earlier illegal ordinances. Restore Hawaiian-medium education through the 1907 settlement and the Hawaiian oath and proclamation at accession, advancing the associated 1914 reforms rather than leaving the new crown without them for seven years. Preserve the ʻAha Aliʻi's custody of crown and kuleana lands and Thalassia's status as land of the Hawaiian throne. The Philippines retains its single-chamber Cortes and permanent commission, its own budget and army, and the restriction on a Gobernador serving his home province. Each crown retains its legislature, language and first place in its own instruments; the Kuhina Nui and Lugarteniente General conduct the respective day-to-day governments.

After the votes and participating institutions' enactments, Senate and Chamber pass the final Act by two-thirds in October 1907. It establishes the Crown of Pacifica in the House of Thorne-Kamehameha and records the renunciation and approved unions. The ʻAha Aliʻi proclaims Julian heir as Kamehameha VI. Retain the succession rule of eldest son, then eldest daughter, otherwise an adopted heir with Senate consent. The Realm assumes the agreed remaining Philippine Redemption Loan obligation at the transfer, valued for 1907. The approximately $9.5 million balance associated with the original later transfer cannot be copied backward.

### The three accessions and the new household

Victoria lays down the regency and Hawaiian crown in November 1907. Julian succeeds directly in Hawaii and assumes the Realm's sovereignty under the Act. Keep the Hawaiian accession and coronation distinct from the Realm's constitutional proclamation. Existing public spaces can accommodate them; later Thalassian buildings need not be finished eight years early.

Julian and Chiyo marry in November, after his accession and before the Manila voyage. In December he swears before the Cortes and is proclaimed under the raised standard. This is the advanced Manila coronation sequence in the narrative's broader sense, while preserving its actual rite: no bishop crowns him; the Philippine crown rests on the offer, ratification and oath. Arrange the Filipino archbishop and Cathedral Te Deum through the earlier ecclesiastical settlement rather than backdating a named historical appointment. Victoria sits in a pew and sings the hymn shared by her church and Rome.

All three accessions are complete by the end of 1907. Ka Lōkahi operates with one vote for each crown and ten-year reviews in 1917 and 1927. The combined sovereign style retains its existing qualification. Victoria remains an influential former queen and hānai mother; she does not remain regent during Julian's tour.

The sealed regency papers pass to Julian in November 1907. Searching for Noe, he discovers the concealed Donner record and learns that Victoria knowingly preserved the lie. He remains silent. This discovery neither identifies Noe's killer nor makes her death unambiguous. Silas's private confession, Julian's discovery and any eventual public disclosure are separate events.

Julian begins his European tour in late spring 1908, with follow-up negotiations into 1909. Chiyo remains in the Pacific during her pregnancy and Kahekili's birth in late 1908. Her own household and political relationships develop during his absence. Kahekili / Ikazuchi retains his stable identity, his parentage and his future Kamehameha VII reign; he is distinct from Elias and Victoria's lost Kalanimoku.

## 4. The programme from 1907 to early 1914

The programme develops capabilities in a deliberate order. Julian first settles his title and the partnership's immediate grievances. European agreements then widen his diplomatic room, while industrial and financial arrangements make the Japanese connection durable. Logistics and Russian contingency work mature before the July Crisis.

There is no fully mobilized Russian expedition in 1907. There is also no assumption that a treaty signed in 1909 has made the Union harmless. Each annual review asks whether the arrangements are functioning, whether partners still benefit, and what Pacifica could actually do if a commitment were invoked.

## 5. European relationships and Union containment

### The purpose of the tour

Julian's delegation includes responsible ministers, financial and industrial specialists, and interpreters. His established languages do not confer French or German fluency. The tour starts after Asquith becomes British prime minister in 1908. In Paris he deals with the offices actually held at the time; Clemenceau leaves the premiership in July 1909. In Berlin Bülow gives way to Bethmann Hollweg that July. In Vienna Franz Joseph is sovereign and Franz Ferdinand heir. Karl is not yet emperor. Tokyo is consulted before and during the tour; Katsura succeeds Saionji in July 1908.

| Relationship | Offer and counterparties | Pacifican return and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Britain | Edward VII, Asquith, Grey and Haldane; naval consultation with Fisher; viable commercial and financial projects | Confirm the existing North American and Pacific settlement and negotiate non-assistance to Union aggression. Commercial access and selected fuel or industrial contracts do not concede British command over Pacifican policy. |
| Germany | Wilhelm II, Bülow or Bethmann Hollweg as appropriate, Tirpitz, Ballin and participating industrial and banking interests | Obtain recognition and reciprocal restraint through executable shipping and engineering business. Germany retains its existing interests unless it agrees otherwise; Pacifica grants no military access to its core. |
| France | Fallières, the responsible cabinet and foreign minister; Caillaux in his actual financial or later premier role; Jules Cambon in Berlin | Offer investment and export opportunities that make French participation useful without binding Pacifica permanently to the Entente. French security concerns survive the commercial bargain. |
| Austria-Hungary | Franz Joseph, Franz Ferdinand, Aehrenthal and competent Hungarian representatives | Link Balkan trade and credit to workable constitutional accommodation and reciprocal restraint. An imperial audience does not supply Budapest's assent. |
| Russia | Nicholas II, Stolypin, Kokovtsov and Izvolsky, followed by the officeholders appropriate to later years | Offer useful civil finance and a less humiliating Balkan settlement while developing a candid assessment of fragility. No unlimited imperial credit guarantee follows. |
| Ottoman and Balkan governments | Authorized ministries, parliamentary channels and affected local representatives | Seek specific agreements on sovereignty, transit, debt and rights. Pacifica does not assume responsibility for maintaining the Ottoman Empire. |

The British agreement builds on the proposed Boer War-era accommodation over canal access, Alaska, Samoa and Pacific recognition. Julian does not pay twice for recognition already obtained. Where earlier terms remain unsettled, the new instrument identifies and completes them instead of implying an entirely new surrender of British claims.

British and German undertakings are separate. Each can agree not to supply state-backed war finance, naval facilities, operational intelligence or military assistance to a Union attempt at forcible revision of the recognized North American settlement. Normal commerce, neutral canal passage and private investment are governed by their own terms. A promise of neutrality is not secretly transformed into a British or German obligation to fight for Pacifica. Ratification and the definition of aggression remain indispensable, particularly if Pacifica itself initiates a disputed operation.

The gain is to make a Union campaign more difficult to organize and support. Britain and Germany need not become friends, accept the same global programme, or help suppress every Union industry. Pacifica cannot enforce a universal embargo against a continental economy. Germany's existing Pacific possessions and Tsingtao do not become Pacifican bargaining property; Chinese rights require Chinese participation. A later Pacifican war against Germany would impose a real cost on this relationship.

### A commercial constituency inside the Union

The Carnegie transaction of 1903 already gives Union wealth an interest in Pacifica. Julian broadens lawful civilian investment through sound bonds, minority holdings, shipping and commercial ventures. Investors should receive returns from productive activity. They acquire neither a claim to reopen settled borders nor control of strategic ports, defence systems or constitutional decisions.

This policy operates alongside defence. Frontier communications, the canal approaches, home forces, reserves and naval readiness remain funded. The Union still lacks a sovereign Pacific port, but can seek borrowed facilities and foreign partners; the European undertakings are valuable partly because they restrict those opportunities. Existing canal neutrality is respected. Pacifica does not turn an advertised international waterway into an arbitrary instrument against lawful Union passage.

Roosevelt's administration, Taft's from 1909 and Wilson's from 1913 receive practical arbitration and commercial proposals on the existing territorial basis. Acceptance requires their own constitutional processes. Civilian investment can make revanchism more costly without abolishing it, and emulation can make the Union stronger even while it trades profitably with Pacifica. Essential long-lived Pacifican assets cannot depend on Union short money that can be withdrawn during a crisis.

## 6. Japan as a durable partner

### Civic equality and mutual defence

The 1907 compact's school, immigration, family and career undertakings require implementation in Pacifica. The school dispute ends through enforceable legal and administrative action. Japanese-origin families receive the agreed rights; examination and service careers become credible routes to influence. Chiyo's marriage gives these commitments a personal expression but cannot substitute for their enforcement.

The existing joint war council gains a permanent planning staff. Kiyoshi provides naval leadership; the two governments retain lawful political authority, and Japanese units retain their national command. Staffs prepare for a Union attack on Pacifica, an attack on agreed Japanese territory, threats to shipping and fuel routes, and the concentration and maintenance of the fleets. They reconcile signals, spares, repair practices and transport requirements before a crisis.

Mutual defence must be dependable in defined defensive cases. If every obligation can be declined at will, Japan has reason to seek a more reliable partner. Conversely, an unauthorized officer or border adventurer cannot create a coalition war by occupying territory and demanding support afterward. Operations beyond the agreed defensive commitments require fresh political authorization and a defined allocation of forces, expenses and objectives.

The historical 1911 Anglo-Japanese treaty demonstrates that period agreements could combine a genuine defensive obligation, consultation before prejudicial arrangements and military planning. Its arbitration exception also shows how an apparently technical provision could qualify that obligation. It is a drafting comparison, not an alliance assumed to exist unchanged in this timeline. [Treaty text, Articles II–V](https://worldjpn.net/documents/texts/pw/19110713.T1E.html).

### Industrial and financial interests that survive peace

From 1909 Julian and successive Japanese governments develop rolling programmes of approved, funded civil and naval orders. Shipyards, tankers, docks, railways, power and machinery receive a credible stream of business. Japanese boards hold real ownership, profits and decisions; engineers compete with Pacifican firms to improve methods. This continues the advanced industrial partnership established in the nineteenth century. It does not introduce modern organization to an otherwise backward Japan.

Projects should be commercially or strategically useful even without a European war. Foreign projects require the host government's participation. Pacifican orders cannot grow without budget authority, and a purchase guarantee is a fiscal liability rather than free diplomacy. A factory or dock is financed over an appropriate term from equity and long debt; short trade bills remain suitable for actual shipments. Permanent capacity is funded against durable peacetime demand; hypothetical war orders do not enter the reference projection.

Matsuo remains the historical Bank of Japan governor during the early tour years. Takahashi is a valuable financial contact, but becomes governor only in 1911 and finance minister in 1913 on the historical chronology. His alternate career can draw on the existing Pacifican connection without assigning him those offices in 1908. [Bank of Japan governor list](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/about/outline/history/pre_gov/index.htm); [JACAR biography of Takahashi](https://www.jacar.go.jp/english/exhibition/nichiro/cloud_takahashi.htm).

By 1911–1913 the governments and competent banking authorities negotiate disclosure of foreign obligations, concentrated exposures and maturity risks. They establish limited procedures for emergency foreign-exchange credit, specifying funding authority, collateral, duration, repayment and losses. Independent currencies and reserve institutions continue. There is no unlimited modern currency-swap promise, automatic guarantee of private bank solvency, or requirement that Japan defend an unsuitable parity indefinitely.

These measures reduce the chance that Pacifica can later obtain sweeping concessions only because Japan has exhausted every alternative. They also reduce the chance that the partnership fails in a banking panic. The scenario retains the 1923 earthquake as an external shock; improved financing changes exposure and recovery rather than eliminating its human losses or all future banking trouble.

### Working with Japanese politics

The relationship operates through successive cabinets and institutions. Saionji supplies an enduring personal connection; Katsura's governments, the Diet, the services and later ministries possess their own authority. Chiyo has political judgment and her own relationships, rather than functioning as an obedient intermediary. Kiyoshi's failed 1907 claim can strengthen his later insistence that officers serve a lawful settlement instead of manufacturing one through force.

The 1912–1913 cabinet conflict provides an opportunity for Japanese-led reform. The historical June 1913 removal of the active-duty eligibility restriction for army and navy ministers establishes a period-plausible measure. It did not end military autonomy or prevent subsequent active officers from holding the posts. Pacifica can support dependable governmental authorization of common resources without dictating a Japanese constitution or importing this reform into the 1907 claim. [National Archives of Japan](https://www.archives.go.jp/ayumi/kobetsu/t02_1913_02.html).

The later treaty in section 10 develops this staff work without creating the original Concord's common Foreign Affairs and Finance ministries. Japanese national government, command and financial authority continue.

## 7. European crises and Mediterranean access

### Bosnia in 1908–1909

Retain the CR-032c working settlement following the October 1908 annexation announcement. By spring 1909 the participating governments seek recognition of Habsburg sovereignty alongside a meaningful Bosnian Diet, local budget powers, community rights and an affordable agrarian programme. Bosnia is not simply handed to either Vienna's or Budapest's exclusive administration, nor automatically made a third South Slav kingdom.

Serbia receives dependable agricultural access, veterinary arbitration and improved transit through the existing Skopje–Salonica route, subject to the controlling sovereign's agreement. Austria-Hungary accepts consultation, inquiry and a temporary mutual standstill before coercion. Serbia accepts effective action against armed conspiracies within its jurisdiction. Austrian police acquire no executive authority in Serbia. Local representatives participate, and a standing commission addresses implementation and incidents.

Ottoman compensation and Habsburg withdrawal from military privileges in Novi Pazar have historical negotiating foundations. The additional constitutional and reciprocal security terms are the counterfactual contribution. Russia gains a public role, protections for Serbia and Orthodox interests, and participation in the connected Bulgarian financial settlement; Julian does not promise Russian warship passage through the Straits without Ottoman and great-power agreement. [Austrian State Archives record of the 26 February 1909 protocol](https://archivinformationssystem.at/detail.aspx?ID=6766872).

This settlement can reduce humiliation and improve the handling of the next dispute. It cannot guarantee that nationalist conspiracies cease or that every local official observes the bargain. Commercial and constitutional delivery during 1909–1911 is part of the programme, rather than a celebration after signatures.

### Agadir in 1911

Retain the attempt to connect French–German accommodation to concrete development and industrial agreements. Following the Panther's arrival on 1 July, Julian works with Caillaux's authorized government, de Selves and Jules Cambon, while consulting Britain and Spain. Germany receives executable shipping, engineering and commercial opportunities, recognition of legitimate interests and a visible diplomatic result. France receives recognition of a defined leading role in Moroccan security and administration, investment and a smaller unilateral fiscal burden. Moroccan participation, public revenue, property rights and administrative development belong in the terms.

The principal departure from the historical November settlement is the greater weight placed on industrial and commercial gains. A limited African frontier adjustment remains an OPEN fallback, with its geography and local consequences requiring a decision. Kiderlen-Wächter's territorial and prestige demands make acceptance of a principally commercial substitute a difficult political achievement. It cannot be assumed from the value of the contracts alone. [Kiderlen-Wächter's 1911 objectives](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/wilhelmine-germany-and-the-first-world-war-1890-1918/alfred-von-kiderlen-waechter-on-his-foreign-policy-goals-1911).

Britain receives early clarity about German naval intentions and fair access to business. The agreement does not become an exclusive Franco-German cartel or erase existing third-party treaty rights. Grey's contemporary statement supplies evidence of the escalation caused by uncertainty and exclusion, while remaining his government's defence of its policy. [Statement of 27 November 1911](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1911-11-27/debates/9149f840-aef6-49b7-b4d0-d15584814f0a/SirEdwardGreySStatement).

### The Balkan Wars and access to the Mediterranean

During 1912–1913 Julian applies the same method to transit, revenue, borders, local rights and limits on coercion. The Macedonia and Edirne discussions supply negotiating options, not an already adopted town-by-town frontier. Serbia, Bulgaria, Greece and the Ottomans must receive gains they can defend domestically; Russia, Austria-Hungary and other interested powers must accept their own responsibilities. Greek or Bulgarian commercial access cannot be created solely by granting rights on somebody else's coast.

Pacifica uses port services, repair contracts, merchant facilities, fuel arrangements and diplomatic access where these provide sufficient freedom of action. A Souda or Leros base remains a separately assessed option. No sovereign base, compulsory Ottoman alliance or anti-Ottoman war is a prerequisite of this programme.

A commercial port contract is not a secure wartime base. Neutral-port restrictions, belligerent rights and the Suez regime limit repair, replenishment and operations during war. Any facility must be assessed for supply, local assent, British reactions, vulnerability and the forces it would tie down. If its protection reduces Pacifica's usable strength in the Pacific, Julian can prefer dispersed commercial access and stronger home capabilities. [Hague Convention XIII](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/hague13.asp); [Convention of Constantinople governing Suez](https://www.suezcanal.gov.eg/french/About/CanalTreatiesAndDecrees/Pages/ConstantinopleConvention.aspx).

The European settlements create procedures and interests that Julian invokes after Sarajevo. The attack still occurs in this scenario; the response in section 8 changes the decisions that historically transformed the crime into war.

## 8. The July settlement of 1914

### Sarajevo and the first week

The working sequence retains the assassination of Franz Ferdinand and Sophie on 28 June. It does not rely on Julian foreseeing the particular conspiracy. The murder removes one of his useful contacts and exposes the continuing strength of nationalist violence despite the 1909 reforms.

On 29 June Pacifican representatives invoke the existing consultation, inquiry and standstill provisions. Julian acts through the resident missions and authenticated cables; he does not cross the world in time to conduct every negotiation personally. Austria and Serbia agree to an initial renewable standstill while an investigation assembles evidence. Vienna preserves the Bosnian constitutional institutions and restrains collective reprisals; Belgrade undertakes to preserve records, secure witnesses and act on substantiated evidence against officials as well as private conspirators.

The commission has Austrian, Serbian and agreed neutral expertise. Serbian authorities retain arrest and judicial powers on Serbian territory. Austrian investigators can submit evidence, attend agreed proceedings and receive records; independent observers report obstruction. The agreement distinguishes individuals and clandestine networks from an unproved accusation that the Serbian cabinet ordered the murder.

Julian's principal external effort goes to Berlin before the historical 5–6 July assurances. Wilhelm, Bethmann Hollweg and Jagow receive the same proposed terms and a request for consistent instructions to Vienna. Germany promises support for Austria's security and verified Serbian compliance, while making further support contingent on observing the agreed process. It does not issue an unrestricted assurance for an Austrian invasion.

This is the decisive departure. Existing commercial projects help make restraint worthwhile, but the change requires a German political choice. Julian offers continued industrial and shipping agreements and public recognition of Austria's legitimate security demands. He makes clear that new Pacifican public credit will support a settlement, not automatically finance a war launched despite compliance. Existing private contracts remain enforceable.

### Tisza's alternative prevails

At the equivalent of the 7 July council, Tisza can point to a substantial outcome: public Serbian recognition of the Bosnian settlement, investigation of implicated networks, effective border controls and continuing verification. Franz Joseph accepts this as an enforceable diplomatic success. Berchtold and Conrad remain dissatisfied, but Germany's conditional support and the emperor's decision prevent them from making an intentionally unacceptable ultimatum the government's policy.

The historical council minutes show why this is a substantive change. Tisza favoured hard demands capable of acceptance, while the other principal participants favoured terms expected to produce refusal and military intervention. The draft changes which policy obtains authority; it does not describe the historical Austrian council as already seeking this compromise. [Council minutes, 7 July 1914](https://www.firstworldwar.com/source/austriancouncilmeeting.htm).

Russia receives a public role and confirmation that Serbia will remain sovereign and lose no territory. Nicholas and Sazonov support the Serbian compliance process while suspending escalation. France and Britain endorse these reciprocal terms. Their relationships with Russia continue, but they do not promise support for rejecting a settlement that protects Serbia. Julian does not demand that Tisza concede a full Hungarian constitutional reorganization in the middle of the emergency.

### Compliance replaces the ultimatum

| Working date | Proposed event and consequence |
|---|---|
| 29 June–4 July | Invoke the standing machinery; preserve evidence; agree the initial standstill and inquiry mandate. |
| 5–7 July | Germany conditions support; the Austrian council chooses demands that can be fulfilled. |
| 8–18 July | Serbia accepts the investigation and border measures; Austria provides evidence. Arrests or suspensions follow substantiated cases rather than a predetermined list known to Julian in advance. |
| 20–23 July | The powers endorse a compliance protocol. It replaces the historical 48-hour ultimatum; no separate demand for Austrian executive jurisdiction is inserted afterward. |
| Late July–August | The first findings and implementation reports establish whether obligations are being met. Courts continue the criminal cases beyond the diplomatic deadline. |
| Autumn 1914 | A review confirms continuing inspection, domestic proceedings and the regional security arrangements. No Austro-Serbian war or general mobilization occurs. |

The protocol obliges Serbia to dismantle armed conspiratorial activity supported by public officers, gives its civilian government international backing to enforce military discipline, and provides procedures for disputes over compliance. Specific criminal guilt remains a matter for evidence and trial. It does not require suppression of every lawful expression of Serbian national identity.

Serbia's historical 25 July reply accepted substantial obligations but disputed Austrian participation where it infringed Serbian legal authority. The proposed commission supplies an agreed route around that dispute before the deadline becomes a test of submission. [Serbian reply](https://www.gwpda.org/1914/serbresponse.html).

The absence of mobilization is a result of the earlier political agreement. It is not accomplished by persuading staffs at the end of July that military preparations have no consequences. Moltke's historical 29 July memorandum illustrates the pressure for rapid action that this branch avoids. [Moltke to Bethmann Hollweg](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/wilhelmine-germany-and-the-first-world-war-1890-1918/ghdi%3Adocument-802).

### A continuing peace after July

The powers maintain the review mechanism rather than proclaiming that nationalism and military rivalry have ended. Austria remains a major power; Serbia remains sovereign; Russia retains Balkan interests; Germany remains allied to Austria; France retains its security concerns and Russian relationship. Alsace-Lorraine is not settled by the Sarajevo protocol. Britain retains its naval and colonial position.

The programme assumes these tensions produce further diplomatic crises, domestic reforms and commercial competition, but no general European war through 1932. This is a defined scenario outcome, not a claim that a July agreement mechanically guarantees eighteen years of peace. Annual strategic reviews and the continued delivery of the Bosnian, Agadir and Balkan commercial agreements help sustain it.

For modeling, the post-1913 Balkan territorial position supplies the initial reference map. Previously discussed Macedonian and Edirne alternatives remain negotiating material, not silent territorial inputs. Transit, agricultural access, minority safeguards and arbitration can develop without assuming a new Pacifican conquest or a Mediterranean base. Any later map change would require a separate documented scenario input.

## 9. Russia without the Great War

### Information and investment before 1917

From 1908 Julian receives independent reports on railway throughput and maintenance, grain movements, urban prices, public finance, strikes, repression, military discipline and conflicts among the court, Duma and provincial authorities. Commercial reports are compared with diplomatic, military and technical evidence. The network survives a minister's dismissal or death; Stolypin's historical death in 1911 is retained as a working anchor.

Kokovtsov, Krivoshein and Maklakov are useful contacts in finance, land administration and constitutional politics, using the influence they possess at the relevant time. They do not all hold ministerial office throughout the period. Wrangel enters the professional network as a capable officer. Julian does not select him as a future ruler before a civil war that never occurs.

Pacifican and Japanese firms participate in commercially viable rail improvements, storage, processing and rural finance with Russian authorization. Long-lived works use appropriate long-term funding. Russian state debt and private obligations continue under their contracts. There is no enormous new wartime credit commitment, no presumption that Julian controls French lending, and no summer-1916 discovery of a failing military front.

### A constitutional revolution in 1917

The author's earlier requirement for a political break at the historical time is retained as a proposed March–October 1917 constitutional revolution. Its causes and outcome change. The no-war scenario does not retain military mass casualties, wartime requisition, army disintegration or the historical sequence of dual power as unexplained background.

The proposed domestic chain is a renewed land-and-budget confrontation during 1916. Ministers cannot reconcile affordable cultivator purchase, provincial investment and entrenched estate interests. Industrial growth produces urban pressure and strikes; failures of grain distribution and administration turn economic grievances into a challenge to the ministry during the winter of 1916–1917. These are new counterfactual events for review, not historical harvest or price observations asserted for this world.

In March 1917 major demonstrations and strikes lead to a Duma demand for a responsible ministry. Provincial leaders, influential financiers and senior commanders refuse to support a prolonged national campaign of repression. Nicholas initially resists, then accepts ministerial responsibility and a constituent constitutional process to preserve the monarchy and the continuity of the state. The political break occurs without the collapse of a wartime army.

Through the remainder of 1917 a representative settlement transfers control of budgets and ordinary government to accountable institutions and establishes protected provincial and national self-government. The monarchy survives with sharply reduced executive authority. A property-respecting constitutional cabinet with a substantial rural constituency is the working governing partner. Krivoshein's land experience, Kokovtsov's financial judgment and Maklakov's legal perspective can inform it; the exact premier and allocation of portfolios remain OPEN rather than invented appointments presented as historical facts.

The programme secures cultivators' tenure, provides affordable purchase and credit, and gives local administration real resources. Land reform imposes costs on public finance and on some owners; it is not a promise that every faction receives its historical demand. Socialist parties and organized labor remain political forces, but no Bolshevik central government or revolutionary warfare state emerges in the selected path. Julian does not install or strategically ally with a socialist national government.

This is one of the scenario's most demanding political assumptions. The draft explicitly proposes a constitutional outcome in place of CR-032's abdication, Bolshevik seizure and civil war. Its feasibility must be tested against repression, land costs, representative support and the ability of a non-socialist ministry to govern. Financial comparison should include a less successful Russian reform case instead of valuing every new Russian claim at certainty.

### Unity and the western lands

European Russia retains territorial continuity. Ukraine receives substantial national, fiscal and local administrative autonomy within the constitutional state; it is not made independent by a war that did not occur. Crimea retains local protections and port arrangements within that state. The Donbas remains within the same sovereign customs and economic area, with language and administrative safeguards rather than a new interstate frontier through its industry.

Finland's constitutional autonomy is protected, and the Polish lands receive negotiated self-government within the Russian settlement. Germany's and Austria's Polish territories are not transferred by a nonexistent peace treaty. The Baltic and other national questions require enforceable institutions and continuing negotiation. Russia remains a substantial independent power; Julian cannot reproduce the western borders imposed after a coalition victory without paying their political and strategic price.

Wrangel remains a professional officer serving the constitutional state, not its installed head. Denikin, Kutepov, Shatilov and Kappel do not acquire their civil-war coalition assignments by default. Lenin, Trotsky and Stalin remain individual political actors rather than rulers whose capture and execution are predetermined. The draft does not invent substitute trials or deaths for them.

## 10. The Asian settlement and Japanese partnership

### Japanese protection in Transbaikalia

The working sequence negotiates a regional agreement during 1918–1921 after the Russian constitutional settlement. Transbaikalia becomes an autonomous Russian land with a representative civilian council and external protection undertaken by Japan under treaty. A civilian Japanese Resident administers the defined liaison and treaty functions. Russia retains overarching territorial sovereignty; Japan receives actual defensive responsibilities and specified transit, depot and jointly authorized railway-security rights. This is not an independent protectorate outside Russia or a Japanese annexation.

The arrangement deliberately narrows the author's earlier territorial objective to make a peaceful agreement with an intact Russia more plausible. It retains Japanese protection while changing its legal form. The difference from CR-032 must remain visible in both political assessment and valuation of rights.

Russia receives dependable commercial access through the Japanese terminal and Chinese railway, civilian transport investment, a settlement limiting frontier escalation and access to assessed reconstruction and development credit. These benefits do not restore Russian sovereignty at Vladivostok. Japan obtains a more secure frontier, influence through lawful institutions and paid civil development. China participates in terms affecting its railway ownership, operating arrangements, navigation and sovereignty.

The regional council and affected Buryat, Cossack and other communities participate in the settlement. Their assent and functioning civil institutions are prerequisites, rather than benefits inferred from an ataman's signature. Semenov and Ungern have no governing mandate in this branch. Personal armed followings cannot commit Japanese or Pacifican resources.

The first treaty period runs from 1921 to 1926, with continuation agreed in 1926 and reviewed again in 1931. The agreement defines troop access, costs, jurisdiction, local revenues and dispute procedures. It includes a negotiated withdrawal or replacement process, so Japanese protection is valuable but not an irrevocable ownership claim. Russia's acceptance, the scope of protection and the recurring Japanese cost are major assumptions to stress-test. A narrower transit-only agreement is the downside comparison, not an identical asset with a different label.

No Siberian Republic, Kamchatkan republic, Harbin victors' settlement, Commander Islands transfer or new Arctic lease is produced by this treaty. Russia keeps the northern territories it has not previously ceded. Pacifica earns only the contracts, access and returns actually agreed. The old Siberian campaign and Company income cannot remain in its accounts.

### China and existing Pacific holdings

The Qing collapse and Sun's difficult political course remain working anchors, with Julian already sovereign. Sun's setbacks, Yuan's choices and the role of regional military power still require patience. The absence of European war changes Japanese opportunities and foreign pressure; the historical Twenty-One Demands are not an automatic 1915 event. A comparable unilateral initiative faces the common consultation rules, Chinese resistance and the partnership's economic alternatives. Victoria may advise or act under commission, but has ceased to be regent in 1907.

Chinese sovereignty, the Chinese-owned Manchurian railway, Japanese running rights and independent guaranteed Korea remain distinct. Japan and Pacifica continue as competing industrial innovators using professional contractors and paid crews. The no-war branch provides no reason to reintroduce coercive labor intermediaries.

Germany retains its Pacific possessions and its existing position at Tsingtao unless a later agreement changes them. Chinese participation is required for changes to Chinese rights. Pacifica and Japan negotiate commercial and security arrangements with Germany without assuming conquest or a permanent surrender of German freedom of action. Britain and France also retain their Pacific and Asian positions.

### The 1923 earthquake and 1927 treaty

The 1923 earthquake remains an external event. Pacifican relief, shipping and supplies reinforce the Japanese relationship, while Japanese savings, insurance, taxation and assessed borrowing bear reconstruction costs. Earlier long-term finance reduces one source of vulnerability but cannot eliminate losses or the need for foreign exchange. No rescue amount from the wartime workbook is treated as a measured no-war requirement.

During 1926–1927 the governments negotiate a Pacific Partnership Treaty, ratified through the competent institutions in 1927. It renews defensive obligations, sustains permanent staff work and establishes regular political consultation and agreed procurement machinery. The parties remain coequal sovereign governments. The treaty does not create the original Concord's common Foreign Affairs and Finance ministries or transfer Japan's currency and budget to a common executive.

Annual meetings at Hayama give the relationship a continuing public form; the host's precedence and Julian's working role are agreed rather than assumed to confer command. Chichibu's proposed Pacifican education in 1925–1926 can remain, followed by a resident representative's role at Thalassia defined by this treaty. It grants no invented decorations or operational authority. Chiyo's relationship with Nagako and Julian's developing relationship with Hirohito support institutions that still require Japanese consent.

The initial treaty term runs to the 1932 review and renewal for 1932–1937. Ka Lōkahi separately holds its three-crown reviews in 1917 and 1927. Neither process overrides the Realm Senate or Japanese institutions. There is no illegal provisional application in 1927 and therefore no 1928 indemnity curing it. The Realm separately enacts Japanese language standing and community-school subsidies in 1927, followed by trilingual status in the relevant Hawaiian counties in 1928. These measures retain their own lawful authority and budgets rather than depending on a common executive.

Japan is a healthier and less dependent partner than in the crisis-driven Concord. Pacifica obtains alignment and practical cooperation, while accepting less unilateral control over Japanese finance and diplomacy. Its value must be assessed as that actual arrangement.

## 11. European and Union politics through 1932

### The Danubian monarchy

Franz Joseph's death in November 1916 is retained as a working historical anchor; Karl succeeds because Franz Ferdinand was killed in 1914. Beginning in 1917 Karl convenes negotiations over national self-government, provincial finance, representation and the common responsibilities of the monarchy. Pacifican projects support specific administrative and commercial gains rather than purchase assent to an imperial decree.

Without wartime exhaustion, Budapest and other established interests possess more resources to resist. The selected course is gradual: national and fiscal negotiations begin in 1917, agreed provincial reforms develop during 1918–1921, and Czech and Slovak self-government is entrenched through a broader settlement during the early 1920s. Exact instruments and regional boundaries require review, but the model includes recurring administration and transition costs rather than an instantaneous free federation.

Hungary accepts bounded national self-government and minority protections in return for a negotiated division of common fiscal and defence obligations, protected commercial access and a viable constitutional position of its own. Czech and Slovak representatives receive real legislative, educational and administrative powers. Representative ratification is necessary. The settlement's survival through 1932 is an explicit political assumption, with delay and failure to be assessed as sensitivities.

There is no Great War Czechoslovak Legion, Russian deployment, homecoming compact or Austrian indemnity. Czech and Slovak military careers and civic honors can develop within the reformed state, without borrowing the identity or obligations of an expedition that never formed. Masaryk's political course requires persuasion by genuine national rights; his historical independence programme is not simply presumed insincere.

### A competitive Europe

Germany remains a strong industrial and continental power. France and Britain retain independent capital markets, naval interests and colonial holdings. Pacifica benefits from competing sources of capital, equipment and markets, while none is weakened by Great War losses or forced into its creditor order. Diplomatic agreements against assistance to Union aggression continue to matter precisely because the signatories remain capable of helping the Union if those agreements fail.

There are no German reparations, Allied war debts, wartime transfers of German possessions or debt-driven British dependence. The original CR-032 already avoids punitive German reparations; peace in this branch instead changes the scale of destruction, borrowing and reconstruction. Industrial growth and naval spending continue and must be compared country by country.

The Mediterranean policy remains selective access, commerce and consultation. No sovereign Leros base, compulsory Ottoman partnership or anti-Ottoman campaign is added to compensate for the absence of wartime gains. Existing sovereign partners retain their own rights. A new base remains available only through a separately justified and funded agreement.

### The Union and the downturn

Union emulation and containment continue under the established presidential sequence. For comparison, the scenario holds the presidential calendar, including Dawes in 1929–1933 and Young's November 1932 victory, as a working political assumption. Their careers and election arguments require rewriting where the original narrative depended on European war loans and reparations. Young takes office in 1933.

Union capital participates in productive Pacifican civilian assets from 1908–1911 onward, with individual project and issue dates respected. Strategic control laws and long-term funding protect essential Pacifican capabilities. Investors form a constituency for continued commerce, while Washington retains the means and ambition to compete.

The reference scenario includes a commercial and credit contraction during 1930–1932 arising from investment and credit imbalances within the peacetime economy. It does not assume the identical 1929 crash, 1931 banking sequence or historical depression severity. Its size, timing and international transmission are MODEL inputs. A matched external downturn should also be applied to the alternative scenarios to distinguish the effect of the timeline from the effect of choosing a milder shock.

Dawes negotiates trade, payment and arbitration arrangements with Pacifica during 1932, but the Union does not accept the original rescue-driven association, binding naval subordination or a forced settlement of every surviving recognition claim. Any limited stabilization lending requires assessed collateral and public authority. Young campaigns for orderly cooperation alongside continued Union independence. This is commercial accommodation between rivals, with fewer concessions than the canonical Thalassia settlement.
