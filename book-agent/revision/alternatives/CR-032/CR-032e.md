## 1. The shape

Julian takes all three crowns in 1907 and spends seven years trying to make a European war too expensive to start. He tours the capitals in 1908–1909, helps settle Bosnia and Agadir, and leaves behind procedures, contracts and friendships meant for the next crisis. In July 1914 the next crisis comes and the procedures exist, but the men who decide in Vienna and Berlin no longer want them. Europe goes to war anyway, and Pacifica goes with it at sea.

The war destroys the Russian monarchy. The Bolsheviks take power, and in September 1918 they declare the Red Terror. Julian had meant to hold a line in Siberia. The terror convinces him that no line will hold beside that state, and his coalition goes west with Wrangel's Russians. Moscow falls in March 1920. Lenin, Trotsky and Stalin are arrested and brought to trial.

The arc gives Julian a failure before his victory. The war he tried to prevent creates the revolution he then destroys, and the methods he learns in failing (that money must be used as a lever, not offered as a bribe) are the methods by which he brokers the peace of 1918 and wins the Russian war.

## 3. Act one: the earlier crown (1905–1907)

### The 1905 war

**SELECTED (A).** Russia keeps the Manchurian confrontation of 1904–1905 but loses it without catastrophe. A small cruiser squadron raids Japanese shipping from a depot at Petropavlovsk and ties down half Japan's cruisers. In May 1905 Kiyoshi catches it: one cruiser captured, two damaged and out of the war. It was the only threat to the army's sea road, and Japan makes him a count for ending it (CR-026's countship, now earned by a smaller victory). Russia's battle fleets survive. Having lost Vladivostok in 1899, Russia turns its money and railways west. That turn matters in 1914 (§5). Russia's 1905 revolution comes from its own causes (the zemstvo demands, Bloody Sunday, the October railway strike), not from a naval catastrophe. CR-040 Stage 1 writes this war out in full as storyline text.

### Silas's renunciation

D advances Silas's confession and death to the end of 1906, because Kiyoshi can claim as the eldest surviving son only once Silas is dead. CR-039 has since put Silas's death in the winter of 1912 into canon. This draft proposes a route that keeps that date.

**PROPOSED.** In 1906 Silas lends his name to Tate's school segregation (canon, CR-022), drinking and privately ashamed. Tokyo's terms for renewing the compact in 1907 include a guarantee that the man whose name stands on that campaign will never stand in the line of succession. That autumn Lieutenant Tanabe Makoto of Kiyoshi's staff asks for Silas's younger daughter, and she wants to be married in her grandmother's chapel, which Tate's board has closed.

Victoria offers Silas a bargain. If he renounces, for himself and his descendants, any claim to the crown the Act will create, the renewed compact reopens the mission school and his daughter can marry in its chapel. If he refuses, Tokyo refuses the renewal and the Regent says publicly why. He renounces in December 1906.

She never mentions Donner. Using it would expose her own silence since 1887 and the bargain on which the regency was founded, and she does not need it. She sees what the renunciation opens, that the founder's second son becomes the eldest claimant, and judges that Tokyo will never back him; she is right, but it costs her a squadron off her shore. Silas does not know she knows, which keeps CR-022's confession of 1912 intact: when he comes to Thalassia to confess, he still believes he is telling her something new.

The alternative is D's original sequence: the confession in November 1906 and death in December 1906. It reverses CR-039's fact and moves the Tanabe wedding to 1906. **R2** asks the author to choose.

### Kiyoshi's claim and the bargain

**FROM D, adjusted.** With Silas out of the line, Kiyoshi petitions early in 1907 as the founder's eldest son who has not renounced. Meiji and Saionji, not Taishō and Uehara, are the Japanese politics of the scene. The western squadron anchors off Kāneʻohe on a courtesy visit; Kiyoshi gives no order. The ʻAha Aliʻi will proclaim only Julian; Tokyo will not let a count of Japan take a crown equal to the Emperor's; the examined officers of the naval party will not strike. Victoria is sixty-eight, not seventy-five, so outlasting her is no strategy.

In March Julian goes to Pearl Harbor without an escort and asks his brother, in Japanese, for a wife from his house. Kiyoshi renounces for himself and his descendants. Chiyo's own assent and her branch's are part of the bargain.

Under the proposed Silas route, the one meeting of the two elder sons at the Tanabe wedding in 1909 (CR-022) stays where it is, and gains something: both men at that wedding have now given up their father's crown.

### The Act and the three accessions

**FROM D.** The 1907 Act of Settlement does what the 1914 Act does in canon, seven years earlier: it creates the Crown of Pacifica in the House of Thorne-Kamehameha, joins the Hawaiian crown after a plebiscite of Hawaiian subjects, restores Hawaii's legislature and Hawaiian schooling, and recites Kiyoshi's renunciation, naming him "Kuroda Kiyoshi, son of Elias Thorne". The Cortes offers the Philippine crown under a written organic law, and the plebiscites pass narrowly in Hawaii and broadly in the Philippines. Victoria lays down the regency and her crown in November 1907. Julian, about twenty-one, accedes to both; he marries Chiyo the same month; in December he swears before the Cortes in Manila, and Victoria sings the Te Deum from a pew.

**FROM D.** The sealed regency papers pass to Julian at his accession. Looking for the truth of his mother's death, he finds Donner instead, and says nothing. **PROPOSED:** Silas keeps the inland command until 1912 and never learns that his sovereign has read the file. Julian goes to Europe carrying it (§4).

### The heir

D has Kahekili born late in 1908, which makes him twenty-three in the 1932 crisis and rewrites his betrothal and every scene that treats him as a boy. **PROPOSED alternative:** Chiyo's first children are daughters, and Kahekili is still born about 1916, as in canon. The succession rule (eldest son, then eldest daughter) keeps him heir. **R3.**

### Ka Lōkahi

Not resolved here; the author has it on hold. One observation for that decision: if the crowns join in 1907 with ten-year reviews, the council's second review falls in 1927, the year of the Concord.

## 4. Act two: the peace programme (1908–1913)

### The tour

**FROM D.** Julian tours Europe from late spring 1908, with ministers, bankers, engineers and interpreters, while Chiyo stays in the Pacific. He speaks English, Spanish, Japanese and Hawaiian; he needs interpreters in Berlin and Vienna. The offices are the ones actually held: Asquith, Grey and Haldane in London; Bülow, then from July 1909 Bethmann Hollweg, in Berlin, with Tirpitz and Ballin; Clemenceau, then Briand, in Paris; Franz Joseph, Franz Ferdinand and Aehrenthal in Vienna; Nicholas II, Stolypin and Kokovtsov in St Petersburg.

His purpose is Pacifica's, not Europe's. A European war would drag in Pacifica's creditors and customers, and it would give the Union the chance to sell to everyone. In 1908 he does not yet believe Europe will fight. He believes it can be made too expensive to.

**FROM D.** Britain and Germany each sign a separate undertaking not to give state-backed finance, bases, intelligence or military help to a Union attempt to overturn the North American settlement. Neither becomes Pacifica's ally. France gets investment and orders without leaving its Russian alliance.

### Three settlements

- **Bosnia, 1908–1909 (FROM D).** The crisis turns on a bargain Aehrenthal and Izvolsky made at Buchlau and remember differently. Julian, months after reading the Donner file, knows what a buried bargain does to those who keep it, and insists on terms in writing (**PROPOSED**). The settlement gives recognised Habsburg sovereignty with a real Bosnian Diet, Serbian trade and transit through Ottoman Salonica with the Porte's consent, and a standing commission with procedures for inquiry and a mutual standstill before coercion. Russia gets a public role instead of a humiliation.
- **Agadir, 1911 (FROM D).** Germany takes shipping and engineering contracts and a public result in place of most of the Congo territory it demanded; France keeps its Moroccan position.
- **The Balkan Wars, 1912–1913 (FROM D).** The same methods (transit, revenue, minority guarantees) help close both wars. Pacifica takes port and repair contracts in the Aegean, not bases.

Each crisis ends with something that works. That is the trap: by 1913 Julian believes the method works.

### What survives

These outlast the failure of 1914, and the war turns each into an asset:

| Asset | Its use after July 1914 |
|---|---|
| The joint war council's planning staff with Japan (1907) | The Pacific war of August 1914 starts from plans, not improvisation. |
| Britain's and Germany's undertakings on the Union (1909) | Germany breaks its own in January 1917 by offering Washington the South and West (canon §30); publishing the offer through Thalassia shows Germany breaking a signed promise. |
| Julian's Russian reporting (1908–1913) | He knows earlier than anyone how fragile Russia is, and in 1917 he is not surprised. |
| Contracts and friendships in Berlin and Vienna | In 1918 he is the one broker both sides already know. |
| Bank reporting and exchange arrangements with Japan (1911–1913) | Wartime credit has rules from the start. |

### At home

**FROM D.** The Senate quarrel over the voting machinery moves to 1910–1911. Success abroad does not make the counties trust him.

## 5. Act three: July 1914, the settlement that fails

### Why the machinery is refused

**PROPOSED.** Every cause is historical, or already in this timeline:

1. **The man Julian counted on is the man who is killed.** Franz Ferdinand was the strongest voice in Vienna against war with Serbia. His murder removes the restraint and gives the war party its grievance.
2. **Vienna blames the method.** The 1909 commission and Bosnian Diet did not stop the conspirators. Conrad and Berchtold argue that Pacifica's procedures gave Belgrade five years to arm the men who killed the heir. Inquiry now looks like another five years.
3. **Berlin fears a Russia that faces west.** Having lost its Pacific in 1899 and kept its battle fleets in 1905, Russia has put its railways and its new army programme of 1913–1914 into Europe. Moltke's staff calculates that a war fought in 1914 can still be won and one fought in 1917 cannot. Commerce cannot outbid that fear.
4. **Russia is confident.** Its army is intact and its 1909 role in Bosnia was a public one. It will not watch Serbia crushed.
5. **The alliances work as written.** France stands by Russia; Britain decides late, over Belgium.
6. **Julian withholds his one real sanction.** Germany imports its oil and Austria's fleet burns it. In early July Victoria, through her own old correspondents' cables, tells him to say plainly in Berlin that the oil stops if Vienna refuses the inquiry. He will not threaten Ballin's Germany over a procedure that has worked three times, and sends offers instead. He says it only on 25 July, to men already mobilising.

Julian made each earlier crisis cheaper to settle than to fight. In July 1914 the decisive men had concluded that war now was cheaper than war later, and the one thing that might have changed their sum he offered too late. Causes 1–5 are other men's decisions; cause 6 is his, and it is why the lesson below is his own.

### What Julian takes from it

**PROPOSED.** He concludes that offering men something to lose does not stop them, and that only taking something away does. In 1918 he stops the credit and holds back the food until the armistice is signed (canon §30), and he gives Wrangel money only against results.

Victoria's verdict is that he built a peace on one man and a procedure, and would not threaten his friends. In 1917 the roles turn: she wants peace and he will use pressure only on his own timetable (canon §30, OPEN-019-13). Their quarrel begins in July 1914.

## 6. Act four: the war (1914–1918)

Canon §§28 and 30 stand, with these changes:

- **Julian is sovereign throughout.** Every wartime decision in canon that Victoria makes as regent is now his, or hers on his commission.
- **Entering against Germany. PROPOSED.** In August 1914 Julian takes Germany's Pacific islands and Tsingtao with Japan, Australia and New Zealand, before Japan can act alone. Ballin and the Berlin contracts become enemy contracts, and that is the personal cost of the decision.
- **The Twenty-One Demands, 1915. PROPOSED.** Victoria no longer acts "in her final months as regent". Julian sends her to Tokyo as his envoy; Kiyoshi backs her. Who gets the credit is a point of friction between mother and son.
- **January 1917.** The German offer to Washington (canon) now also breaks Germany's 1909 undertaking. Thalassia publishes the text of 1909 beside the offer.
- **The brokered peace.** Canon stands: Julian stops the lending in autumn 1918, Hoover holds the food lever, and Victoria negotiates at The Hague in 1919. Julian is believable as a broker because he spent 1908–1914 in those capitals.

## 7. Act five: the revolution and the Red Terror (1917–1918)

### The revolution

**SELECTED (A).** February and October 1917 come on the historical timetable. The monarchy falls; the Provisional Government fails; the Bolsheviks take power, dissolve the Constituent Assembly and sign Brest-Litovsk in March 1918.

### The first intervention: holding the railway (May–August 1918)

Canon §31 and A §4 stand for this phase. Pacifica pays and supplies the Czechoslovak Legion; the coalition of Pacifica, Japan, China and the Siberian regionalists moves west from Urajio along Chinese-owned Manchuria; the Pacifican railway board runs the line west of Chita. Julian's aim in the summer of 1918 is limited: a Siberia that can stand behind the Ob, and the Legion brought home.

### The terror

**Historical.** On 30 August 1918 Uritsky, head of the Petrograd Cheka, is assassinated, and Lenin is shot and wounded in Moscow. On 31 August Cheka men force their way into the British embassy in Petrograd and kill the naval attaché, Captain Cromie, on its staircase. On 5 September the Council of People's Commissars decrees the Red Terror: class enemies to be isolated in camps, anyone connected with White organisations, conspiracies or risings to be shot, and the names of the shot to be published. Most killings are never published. Those that are appear as lists in provincial papers and in the Cheka's own weekly, whose sixth number in October prints ninety names from the several hundred shot in reprisal for the attempt on Lenin, most without a first name or a reason.

**PROPOSED, the Pacifican side.**

- **Hostages.** Pacifica's railway advisory mission, sent to Petrograd in 1917 with the Russian-gauge locomotives (canon §30, CR-030) on the model of the historical Stevens commission, is taken hostage in Petrograd and Moscow with Japanese residents. Several are shot. How many, and who, is **OPEN**: the story needs at least one name the reader has met.
- **The lists in Pacifica.** The Office of Public Measures translates the Cheka's own lists and publishes them beside the decree that orders them. Nothing needs inventing: the regime's own words do the work, and the gaps (the names left off, the "and so many more") say the rest. Victoria's founding principle, that seeing is believing, now works against Moscow.
- **The Romanovs.** The family is killed at Yekaterinburg on 17 July, a week before the Legion takes the town. Whether a faster coalition could have reached them is a reproach Julian hears; A leaves their fate **OPEN**, and this draft keeps it so (R9).

### Why Julian has to go on

**PROPOSED.** In the autumn of 1918, the month he begins to squeeze the Entente toward peace, Julian decides that the Russian war cannot stop at the Ob:

1. **No line will hold.** A frontier with a state that shoots hostages by class will never be quiet. Siberians will not accept it, and refugees will cross it every winter.
2. **The cheaper war.** CR-030 prices the standing Ob front: a corps of 50,000 men kept within two weeks of the river, forever. One campaign that ends the regime costs less than a permanent front against it (**MODEL**, to be tested).
3. **The Pacific order.** Since its appeal of December 1917 to the working Muslims of Russia and the East, Moscow has addressed its revolution to Asia, and the workers of Japan, Korea and China are next. (The Communist International, founded in March 1919, later confirms the judgment.) Of all the powers, Japan has the most to fear and the most to gain.
4. **The partner exists.** Wrangel and Krivoshein offer a Russia that will recognise the settlement and pay its debts.

He does not decide alone. Tokyo has to commit more than the Siberian plan; Beijing has to keep the Manchurian line open; the Senate has to vote the credit. Victoria's position is **OPEN** (R10): she wanted peace in Europe in 1917, and whether the hostage lists make her favour this war or only harden her against Julian's methods decides much of 1921.

## 8. Act six: the road to Moscow and the arrests (1918–1921)

### The campaign

**SELECTED (A).** A §4's chronology stands, from the approach to Wrangel through Skoropadsky in autumn 1918 to the fall of Moscow:

- **Autumn 1919.** A Russian military council transfers the main campaign from Denikin to Wrangel. Shatilov is chief of staff, Kutepov leads the southern striking force, Kappel the eastern army, and Yudenich presses on Petrograd.
- **January 1920.** Kiyoshi holds the Ob crossing at Novonikolayevsk while Vance clears the trains. The Russian and Japanese wounded, the railway families and a final homebound Legion transport cross. Kiyoshi dies of an artillery wound the same day, knowing the crossing is safe.
- **February 1920.** Tula falls.
- **Late February–March 1920.** The Oka line breaks. Moscow falls in March, through defeat, evacuation and local surrender.
- **March–April 1920.** The central regime comes apart, and Petrograd and Kronstadt are taken or surrender.

The Red Terror changes the meaning of the campaign: it is now fought to end a regime, not to hold a frontier. Wrangel's surrender terms protect ordinary soldiers and officials who keep the services running, and the courts, not the officers, deal with the Cheka.

### The arrests

The author's direction makes the arrests the coalition's work. A names no captors and sends all three to Russian courts. **PROPOSED:**

- **Lenin.** He leaves Moscow before it falls, heading north-east to preserve a government, along the line through Vologda toward Vyatka and the Urals. That is the coalition's side of Russia. By 1920 the Pacifican railway board's armoured trains work the line west of the Urals behind the eastern armies (an extension of CR-030's board, **PROPOSED**), and the eastern armies' northern wing holds Perm. In spring 1920 an armoured-train detachment from that wing, with Russian cavalry, stops his train on the Vyatka line and takes him (place and day **OPEN**). Julian's coalition makes the arrest; it hands him to the Russian authorities for trial. The man who brought the revolution home in a sealed German train in 1917 is taken on a railway the Pacificans run.
- **Trotsky. SELECTED (A); captors PROPOSED.** He is cut off with his command group in the retreat from the Oka line, in late February or early March, and taken alive, here by Kutepov's troops.
- **Stalin. SELECTED (A), place PROPOSED.** He slips through the collapse and tries to rebuild the party underground. He is arrested later in 1920. One option: he makes for the Caucasus, and the independent Georgian government, which has its own reasons to fear him, hands him over (**OPEN**).

### Trial and sentence

A selected public trials in Russian courts: Lenin and Trotsky together, executed in late 1920; Stalin separately, executed in 1921. The author's direction says "arrest", so this draft asks rather than assumes (**R7, R8**):

- **Court.** A Russian court (A) keeps the judgment Russian and Wrangel's government legitimate; it also sets up the Altai contrast, in which Julian backs public accountability in Moscow and hides his own commander's crime. A coalition tribunal under Julian would give him more power and make Pacifica an occupier.
- **Sentence.** A's executions; or imprisonment, with Lenin dying of the strokes that began historically in 1922 as a prisoner rather than a ruler, Trotsky exiled, and Stalin in prison. Imprisonment leaves living men who can become symbols; execution makes martyrs. Both are usable.

### Kiyoshi and Vance

**SELECTED (A).** Kiyoshi's death at the Ob in January 1920 is the moral standard that Vance betrays at the Altai in 1921. Julian's falsified Altai report contradicts the public trials he supported months before. Victoria's confrontation and withdrawal in 1921 follow as in canon §32.

## 9. Act seven: the settlement (1921–1922)

A §§6–10 are carried unchanged:

- **Russia.** A federal Russia under Wrangel's provisional leadership, with Krivoshein as premier. Siberia enters by a regional compact, governed from Irkutsk with its assembly at Tomsk. The Ob is an internal river. Siberia keeps the CR-031 lands, including the approved Okhotsk coast west of Ola Bay.
- **Japan.** Transbaikalia stays outside Russia under Japanese protection, and Kamchatka and the Koryak coast remain a republic under Japanese protection.
- **The West.** Ukraine is independent with the western Donbas; Crimea is an autonomous Russian land.
- **Central Europe.** The Danubian federation begins in 1917, the Legion goes home with honours, and Austria pays no indemnity.
- **China.** Chinese rights and the Manchurian railway are recognised.
- **Harbin, 1921.** Wrangel's government signs the treaty as the Moscow party.
- **The alliance, 1922.** Pacifica, Japan, China and federal Russia form the standing alliance.

**Power check (PROPOSED for R11).** The success contract asks whether this beats canon's independent Siberia. A federal Russia that owes its survival, credit and borders to Julian removes a hostile Moscow and the permanent Ob front. But it makes Russia one large partner where canon has a small, dependent Siberia. An independent Siberia beside a Wrangel Russia is possible, and Wrangel would resist it. A chose federal Russia; this draft keeps it and asks again.

## 10. Consequences through 1932

- **Victoria.** She lays down power in 1907, not 1915, so the 1921 renunciation is of her remaining influence and commissions. It still breaks something, because it is the last thing she had.
- **Julian.** By 1932 he has reigned twenty-five years instead of seventeen. The personal rule of Part VI starts in 1907.
- **The heir.** See R3.
- **Kiyoshi's son.** His lesson that a fleet cannot make a sovereign dates from 1907 (CR-026, adjusted).
- **The Concord, the crash and the 1932 crisis.** Canon §§33–37 stand. Federal Russia replaces the Siberian Republic as the fourth alliance party wherever canon names it.
- **Soberanes and Vance.** Soberanes still retires in 1915; an earlier crown does not train Vance faster.
