# Pacifica — Canonical Character Bible

**Current authority:** [Master Storyline](master_storyline.md) and [approved changes](change_requests.json).  
**Companion machine-readable register:** [character_registry.json](character_registry.json).  
**Archival profiles:** [../character_bible.json](../character_bible.json), retained intact for source comparison.  
**Scope:** Character identities, life trajectories, relationships, motivations, psychology, focalization and voice. This is documentation for the second draft, **not rewritten manuscript prose**.

## How to use this bible

**Settled** means explicitly approved or necessarily implied by the approved storyline. **Provisional** means a character or incident continues from the first-draft narrative but its final identity or execution has not been agreed. **Open** means the author has not made the necessary decision. **Proposed** character psychology and scene ideas are editorial possibilities, not approved incidents.

Characters must have motives independent of proving that Elias is great or that empire is doomed. Their changing objectives should create political history. Voice is not a speech filter applied afterward: each person notices different evidence, interprets a conflict differently, and has different knowledge. The Chronicler's account may contradict a character's direct experience.

**Birth and identity discipline:** Elias c.1824; Kaiulani 1838; 1857 marriage; Julian c.1886–87; Elias dies August 1887; Julian is approximately 44–45 in 1931. The real Princess Victoria Kaʻiulani (born 1875) is **not** this novel's fictional Kaiulani. The three Vances, three generations of Shen, two Kuroda generations and repeated Thorne names must never be collapsed into one entry.

## The indispensable characters

### CT-ELIAS — Elias Thorne
**Status:** Settled core; precise inner life develops through scenes. **Born:** c.1824 in Kentucky. **Died:** August 1887.

Elias grows up amid debt, hard labor, animals and a family whose Kentucky home is lost in 1837. A French veteran of the Napoleonic military world teaches him surveying, geology, mechanics, Vauban, Jomini and Plutarch. He has an exceptional memory, restless physical energy, strong intuition about terrain, and long habits of solitary walking and investigation. He can calm a panicked horse with patience and shoot an enemy without hesitation; neither act is an inexplicable superpower. A pastor strikes the boy after a dispute about the age of the earth and mastodon fossils, and the boy breaks his nose in return. Elias grows wary of claims unsupported by observation, but understands religion as a source of serious political and private commitments.

He encounters the devastation of broken faith at the Council House Fight in 1840. The 1841 Santa Fe Expedition and captivity in Mexico, shared with Caleb Vance, develop his Spanish and teach him that people across a border are never one undifferentiated enemy. In California he earns the trust of Californio leaders through negotiation, surveying, and helping prevent bloodshed at Providencia. He backs the Republic of California in 1846 and learns that legitimacy, finance and allies matter as much as strength. Gold discoveries in 1848 make him wealthy and useful; they do not make him owner of the nation's gold. He becomes Director of Mines, Superintendent of Mines and Public Revenue in 1852, First Minister by 1855, Chancellor of the California–Hawaii Confederation in 1856, and Protector through Article XIV in 1861. The temporary office becomes lasting by constitutional settlement in 1865.

His alliance with Hawaii is initially genuine. He forms a close friendship with Lot, finds a political counterpart in Alexander and falls in love with their cousin Kaiulani, whom he marries in 1857. The marriage unites two minds and two political visions, neither subordinate to the other. As the empire expands, Elias ceases to believe that the limits he voluntarily accepted can survive new dangers. In 1874, during Hawaiian succession turmoil, he imposes incorporation and presides over a system that massacres royalist opponents. He believes he has secured the union; his wife's experience is betrayal. Their legitimate son dies later in the 1870s, and the marriage survives amid grief, ambition, estrangement and residual attachment. Elias has other relationships and children, including Julian near the end of his life.

**Principal desire:** Create a sovereign, productive society able to defend itself against financiers and great powers. **Secondary desire:** Make every dependency visible and subject to rational organization. **Fear/contradiction:** A boy whose home was foreclosed ultimately makes entire peoples dependent on credit and centralized power. A witness to betrayed negotiation becomes the author of another betrayal. **Dramatic rule:** Each major triumph must be earned through credible capital, preparation, partners, choices and costs; he is not infallible or ubiquitously prescient.

**Voice:** In conversation he usually states a practical conclusion before explaining it; he notices what can be built, moved, financed and defended. He can be wry, affectionate or intellectually animated among intimates, not always marble-still. His memory produces specificity, not clairvoyance. **POV:** Childhood/first-person encounters and close-third scenes that reveal uncertainty are valuable; the Chronicler's omniscient praise must not substitute for them.

**Relationships:** Wife Kaiulani; lost legitimate son (unnamed); later Julian; older/other sons unresolved; Caleb closest early friend; Lot a friend and political mirror; Alexander diplomatic partner; Californio partners and Japanese reformers as equals until coercion changes the relationship. **Open:** French mentor name; pastor and daughter; lost son's character/accident; all extra-marital genealogical dates.

### CT-KAIULANI — Kaiulani, Kuhina Nui, wife and later regent
**Status:** Settled core; fictional historical divergence. **Born:** 1838 in the Hawaiian royal circle. **Death:** unresolved after her 1931 arrest.

An educated royal cousin of Alexander Liholiho and Lot Kapuāiwa, Kaiulani meets Elias as a schoolgirl in Honolulu in 1851 without romance. As a teenager in a 1854 San Francisco mission she begins developing an intellectual and personal bond with him. In 1855 Kamehameha IV makes her **Kuhina Nui**, the Hawaiian co-executive, rather than the historical incumbent Victoria Kamāmalu; this is a deliberate alternate-history choice. She negotiates a limited coaling-station treaty and refuses Pearl Harbor, proving that her foreign policy has its own boundaries. With Robert Wyllie she shapes the Articles of Confederation and, crucially, obtains a Kuhina Nui countersignature for emergency Protectorship. She and Elias marry after ratification in February 1857.

In 1860 she refuses to countersign Article XIV when Elias wants to escalate the Washoe border crisis. This is a genuine constitutional defeat for him and an important triumph of restraint for her. In 1861 she reluctantly permits emergency command during the Civil War. Lot's 1864 constitution abolishes her Hawaiian office, imperiling her separate constitutional and confederal authority. The exact treaty implications remain disputed. During the subsequent conquests she helps build financial, civil and judicial institutions, and is responsible for much that later becomes coercive.

The Hawaiian succession and integration crisis of 1874 forces her to confront the gulf between her husband's promises and his military plans. The massacre is a direct assault on the people and institutions she has sought to protect; she nevertheless remains politically powerful and personally bound to Elias. Their beloved child dies in an accident in the later 1870s, and she is unable to have another. She is a wife, bereaved mother, ruler, negotiator and architect of empire—not an emblem of maternal injury alone. After Elias has children with other women, a younger Hawaiian-American lover gives birth to Julian. Upon that woman's mysterious death, Kaiulani adopts Julian and comes to love the child even as his origins reopen old wounds.

Elias's death in 1887 unleashes the struggle between armed factions. Kaiulani preserves the Hawaiian islands as a neutral sanctuary and engineers the Truce of Thalassia of 1888. As regent she and Marcus Vance II construct a technocratic peace that protects people from aristocratic rivalries yet imprisons others in records and quotas. In 1914 she helps install an alternative Thorne-Kamehameha dynastic line; the genealogy and why Julian is bypassed remain unresolved. Emperor Kalanimoku's imitation of Elias, later expansions, war profiteering and atrocities bring her into increasingly open conflict with her creation. By the 1920s she has withdrawn to devastated Hawaiian landscapes. Keawe seeks her support; Julian eventually claims the founder's legacy and arrests her through the Restoration government.

**Desire:** Real power to preserve Hawaiian political and cultural life within an ambitious Pacific system—and, increasingly, to shape that system herself. **Contradiction:** She defends constitutional restraints while building instruments of near-total administrative control; her love for Julian can coexist with choices that help exclude him. **Voice:** Political clarity; refined and sometimes scathing, capable of warm domestic specificity in trusted company. Hawaiian land, sea, kinship and legal protocol shape what she notices; do not make all her metaphors imperial machinery. **POV:** Indispensable at court, during the Hawaiian crisis, in the succession, as Julian's adoptive mother and in the long old age.

**Open:** Authority relative to Victoria Kamāmalu; lost child's story; what she knows about Julian's mother's death; exact Kalanimoku kinship; final fate.

### CT-LOST-SON — The legitimate son of Elias and Kaiulani
**Status:** Existence and death approved; identity and characterization open. **Born:** after the 1857 marriage. **Dies:** in an accident in the later 1870s.

He must be given childhood, affections, experiences and preferences before the event that kills him. He is heir in a family, not just an heir in a political equation. Both parents have distinct expectations of him; he may resist both. The accident also injures Kaiulani, who thereafter cannot conceive. The reader's loss must arise from knowing him, not from a retrospective summary of a convenient tragedy.

**Open:** Name, birthday and age, interests, mentors, friends, the accident and medical consequences. **Relationship:** Child of Elias and Kaiulani only; **cannot** be parent of the Emperor of 1914.

### CT-JULIAN-MOTHER — Julian's Hawaiian-American mother
**Status:** Relationship and fate approved; name and life open. **Active:** c.1885–1889.

A younger woman of mixed Hawaiian and American background enters Elias's circle during his final illnesses, perhaps as a caregiver. She has education, family loyalties, skills, and ambitions of her own. Her intimacy with the powerful and increasingly disabled ruler involves both personal feeling and a vast inequality of status. She gives birth to Julian around 1886–1887. After Elias dies she herself dies under suspicious circumstances. The facts available to the household, the political factions and the Chronicler do not establish Kaiulani's guilt. Kaiulani adopts her baby.

**Open:** Name, ancestry, work, agency, age, motives, protection and dependencies, precise death and surviving testimony. Her POV must be possible, not forbidden by the mere fact of a later murder mystery.

### CT-JULIAN — Julian Thorne, later Lord-Restorer
**Status:** Parentage and adoption approved; much of the late public career retained provisionally. **Born:** c.1886–1887; **age in 1931:** about 44–45.

Julian is **Elias's biological son**, not merely a putative descendant or a thirty-two-year-old stranger who happens to resemble him. After his mother's death he is raised and loved by Kaiulani, the founder's widow. His early life belongs in the novel long before he appears as a demagogue: childhood in a politically guarded household, stories of Elias from incompatible witnesses, his view of Kaiulani as a mother, and a growing conviction that he has inherited something denied to him.

**Desire:** Discover and define an identity that is neither a numbered servant of the dynasty nor a substitute for his father. **Possible later distortion:** Recovers frontier rhetoric as a critique of the imperial examination state, then uses mass spectacle and personal rule to reproduce the order he condemns. His racialized political appeal is especially complicated by his Hawaiian-American parentage. It requires deliberate psychological and political explanation, not an accidental contradiction.

**Arc:** Raised by Kaiulani → bypassed or marginalized in the 1914 settlement (precise circumstances open) → independent political formation → leader of the 1931 Restoration → overthrow of Kalanimoku → effort to restore lost sovereignty and expansion → failure in an exhausted world. His followers arrest Kaiulani, intensifying the tragedy of their relationship. **Voice:** Publicly theatrical and rhythmically persuasive, with an intentionally cultivated imitation of frontier directness; privately capable of doubt, affection, anger and genuine intellectual curiosity. Do not make him a hereditary monster. **Open:** Adoption circumstances, education, political allies, rupture with Kaiulani, final fate.

### CT-CALEB — Caleb Vance
**Status:** Core relationship approved; ending retained provisionally. **Born:** early nineteenth century, exact year unresolved. **Death:** Donner Pass crisis in the 1887–1888 succession war (working storyline).

An experienced former American soldier and frontiersman, Caleb survives the 1841 Texan Santa Fe Expedition and imprisonment in Mexico alongside teenage Elias. Their friendship begins with shared hunger, uncertainty and mutual rescue. Caleb understands camp survival, horsemanship, artillery and the reliability of real people under hardship; Elias sees wider geometries of state and war. Each has knowledge the other lacks.

Caleb is among the few men permitted to call Elias by his first name, joke at his expense or tell him he is mistaken. Over decades he becomes a commander and eventually an aged guardian of the founder's memory, but he never experiences the young man simply as a monument. At Donner Pass he saves Marcus Vance II and loses his life.

**Voice:** Informal and often rude; economy of observation rooted in rations, weather, tools, boots, injuries and personal loyalties. His voice may soften privately. Avoid making dialect a performance of illiteracy.

**Open:** Family relationship to Marcus I—brother or uncle/older kinsman—and the exact generational tree. The *rise_to_power.md* proposal that Marcus I is Caleb's younger brother is the working option, but original profiles contradict it. Their kinship must be explicitly fixed before all the Vance dossiers are considered final.

### CT-MARCUS-I — Marcus Vance I, the founding general
**Status:** Historical role in the fiction settled; kinship and early personal biography open. **Active:** 1849–1869. **Dies:** Lake Baikal, 1869 in the alternate chronology.

A younger relative of Caleb, possibly his brother, Marcus joins the growing Californian military establishment around the gold rush. He specializes in organization, staff work, artillery, training and the movement of men and supplies. His talents complement but do not merely obey Elias's plans. From the constitutional reorganization of 1852 and Defense Law of 1855 onward, he helps create the regular army and general staff.

Marcus must be **a genuinely intelligent independent military officer**. His actions during the three-sided American wars include judgments Elias could not make himself; he may disagree with Elias over the sacrifice of men to preserve strategic positions. As the empire expands, service in Japan, China and the northern campaigns deepens his belief that mobility and controlled force can master terrain. In Siberia the demands of the campaign and his deteriorating health culminate in the terrible Lake Baikal attack and his death.

**Voice:** Specific artillery, distances, marching conditions, engineering and limits; clipped under fire but capable of professional admiration for enemies. **Open:** West Point service, birth year, and relation to Caleb. He is **not** the field marshal living through the 1914 Indian campaign.

### CT-MARCUS-II — Marcus Vance II, architect of the Polytechnic state
**Status:** Life-course retained, with corrected generational arithmetic. **Born:** approximately 1858; **age in 1887:** ~29; aged ~56 in 1914.

Son of Marcus Vance I. His father dies when he is about eleven. Educated inside the imperial technical schools, he becomes expert in engineering, naval calculations and the idea that a state can replace unreliable family loyalties with a professionally trained and numbered cadre. He commands the 1887 conquests in the Dutch East Indies and Australia and becomes central to the post-Elias succession. At Donner Pass his impeccable calculations are defeated by terrain and the different experience of older frontier soldiers. Caleb saves his life.

Kaiulani and Vance II forge the regency's durable administrative system. Their relationship is a political partnership, occasionally affectionate in old age, but never interchangeable with their memories of Elias. After retirement Marcus II watches younger officers extend the empire far beyond his own assessments of sustainable power. He recognizes in their cold logic something he helped teach.

**Voice:** Rapid analytic precision in youth; increasingly restrained, self-doubting and less certain of numbers in old age. He may still use calculations to avoid confessing pain. **Open:** Early family circumstances and exactly how he becomes Kaiulani's trusted collaborator.

### CT-MARCUS-III — Marcus Vance III, the inherited officer
**Status:** Later role retained; exact kinship unresolved. **Born:** around 1891 if twenty-eight in 1919.

A third Vance enters military life already protected by a family name and educated in the tightly regulated Polytechnic world. He can operate its weapons and forms without understanding the local human conditions on which success depends. Failure in Afghanistan, catastrophic operations in Siberia and Southeast Asia, and the expectation that every report must show victory teach him to manufacture numbers rather than interpret them. His atrocities and paranoia become part of the breakdown of the officer class. He dies at the hands of guards who cease to obey him.

**Voice:** Initially stiff statutory certainty that becomes frightened, high-pitched insistence. His story must reveal the humiliations, injuries and decisions producing degeneration, not simply announce inherited incompetence. **Open:** Relationship to Marcus II (grand-nephew in a legacy profile, not settled as his literal grandson); parents, military progression and fate of subordinates.

### CT-CHRONICLER — The Imperial Chronicler / Historiographer
**Status:** Central formal role settled; personal identity open.

The narrator writes as a scholar of the imperial order, educated among its archives and steeped in classical models of historical rise and decline. His earliest knowledge of Elias's childhood, the pastor's daughter, Texas and the Californian founding depends on testimony, disputed memoirs and surviving official papers; he did not personally witness those events. By the twentieth century he becomes a participant in diplomatic missions and eventually an eyewitness to the empire's fall.

He is not merely a vehicle for accurate historical exposition. His understanding of the old American republic, the institutions of Pacifica, and the people counted by its clerks is shaped by his own class and education. Accounts from Kaiulani, ordinary soldiers, workers, Indigenous communities, Japanese participants, women in the households and revolutionaries may challenge his interpretations without establishing that he knowingly lies.

**Voice:** Long, learned, retrospective, drawn to analogy and historical symmetry; occasional dry irony and moments of individual tenderness. His certainty can be disproved by scenes he could not have witnessed. **Open:** Name, birth date, age in the 1930s, how he knows Takashima, precise career and number of archival volumes. The legacy account's 82-year-old in 1932 / earlier travels must be age-checked.

### CT-KEAWE — Keawe, revolutionary stoker
**Identity code:** `CT-KEAWE`. **Status:** Later role retained, early years to be developed.

An experienced Hawaiian or mixed-background mechanic and fleet stoker, Keawe grows through labor conditions, revolutionary networks and technical organization into a leader of the unnumbered. He rejects the view of laborers as interchangeable parts while possessing the engineering skill to sabotage the systems that oppress them. He appeals to Kaiulani in her exile without becoming her protege or mouthpiece. He later helps protect the archive at Thalassia's fall.

**Voice:** Collective rhythms learned in workshops and ship holds; blunt practical speech capable of ritual oratory in gatherings. **Open:** Birth date, whether the 1921/1930 descriptions belong to exactly one man (provisionally yes), relationship to Julian and revolutionary aims after victory.

### CT-MALIA — Malia, Kaiulani's niece and confidante
**Status:** Relationship retained; exact age inconsistent in legacy profiles.

A Hawaiian royal relative and trained secretary, Malia serves Kaiulani at court and in exile. She understands accounts, medicine, ceremonial obligations and the increasingly precarious details of their domestic lives. She is an indispensable eyewitness but not merely the old woman's interpreter. She makes choices when officials, revolutionary emissaries and later restoration guards demand information or obedience.

**Voice:** Clear, modest without submissiveness, accustomed to reporting concrete facts to powerful people. **Open:** Kinship link and birth year; her exact fate in the final upheaval.

### CT-DAO — Dao, archive assistant
**Status:** Later story retained; gender and identity ambiguous in first draft.

Dao is a young Cantonese assistant to the Chronicler during the final siege and escape. Their capacity to preserve fragile documents, move under fire and care for an old scholar makes them a moral counterweight to the state's obsession with official archives. Some existing passages refer to Dao as male and later as female; a legacy profile conjectures a disguised woman, but **the user has not approved that explanation**.

**Open:** Dao's gender/pronouns, whether concealment is part of the plot, age and private motives. Until resolved, use the name **Dao** and avoid invented biographical explanations.

## The succession and the imperial households

### CT-SILAS-CLAIMANT — Silas Thorne, claimant of the frontier armies
**Status:** Provisional contender from the later storyline, identity not settled.

A son or close-blood claimant of Elias with a base among older American frontier and cavalry elements. After Elias's death he attempts to claim the Founder’s political inheritance. His reliance on veteran loyalties distinguishes him from Kiyoshi's Japanese faction and Vance II's numbered military. The old draft gives him a 1859 birth to a Virginian woman and portrays drunken decline. Those details are not accepted automatically: the revised family chronology must establish the mother, legitimacy, name and possible relationship to the lost child.

**Voice:** Not simply a second-rate Elias. He should possess a specific charm, grievance, competence or generosity capable of winning real followers. **Open:** whether he is Elias's son, birth year, mother, his political program and the degree of responsibility for the Donner war.

### CT-KIYOSHI — Kiyoshi Thorne
**Status:** Provisional contender.

A claimant tied to Japanese military aristocrats, in the legacy manuscript associated with Chōshū and with seizures that make him vulnerable to being manipulated by powerful officers. The name and public faction remain promising; his parentage, precise personal ties to Takashima, place in the 1857 marriage, and competence require reconstruction.

**Voice:** Avoid reducing him to illness, fanaticism or racial stereotype; his motives should grow from family, Japanese political interests and the differing legal systems of Pacifica. **Open:** Is he Elias's son and, if so, born when and to whom?

### CT-KALANIMOKU-CLAIMANT — Kalanimoku Thorne, young painter and claimant
**Status:** Provisional individual, not yet identified with a later prince.

The early-draft court gives Elias a gentle son or ward called Kalanimoku Thorne, associated with Hawaiian lineage and a desire for an ordinary life rather than rule. His old birth date (around 1868) and death before Elias in 1887 are **legacy details to reconcile**, not new canon. Following the 1874 Hawaiian incorporation, he may still represent a living connection between rival lines, but his mother and exact family status are undecided. He must **not** be collapsed into the deceased legitimate child or automatically made the father of the 1914 Emperor.

### CT-KALANIMOKU-OLDER — Prince Kalanimoku, possible father of the Emperor
**Status:** Provisional, separate identity shell.

Another draft profile names *Prince Kalanimoku the Elder*, a gentle Hawaiian cousin of Kaiulani and father of the Emperor crowned in 1914. The father–son relationship may prove useful, but the current succession, marriage and death dates cannot be made consistent without a deliberate genealogy. Until resolved, this prince is a **separate potential person** from CT-KALANIMOKU-CLAIMANT. He is also unrelated to Elias and Kaiulani's dead legitimate son unless the author expressly establishes a kinship.

### CT-KALANIMOKU-EMPEROR — Sacred Emperor Kalanimoku
**Status:** Crowned in 1914; lineage unsettled. **Born:** approximately 1890 if 24 in 1914.

Young ruler of the Thorne-Kamehameha settlement, married to Lady Chiyo. He initially occupies an office intended to symbolize unity but gradually takes personal control of Senate procedure, naval expansion and administration. He studies phonograph recordings of Elias and mimics the founder's cadence and manner, believing autocracy to be the true content of the founder's legacy. Kaiulani sees the return of the political form she tried to end.

**Voice:** Formal ceremonial diction that becomes consciously imitative of Elias under pressure; distinct private doubts should remain possible. **Open:** his father, mother, exact biological connection to Elias, why Kaiulani sponsors him instead of Julian.

### CT-CHIYO — Lady Chiyo, imperial consort
**Status:** Later role provisionally retained.

A Japanese aristocrat with technical education, associated with Takashima's lineage, whose marriage to Kalanimoku provides dynastic legitimacy across the confederation's founding cultures. She should have an independent purpose within the court—patronage, science, her family's protection, legal authority, or power over succession—rather than serve only as a genealogical ornament.

**Open:** exact lineage, interests, alliance with Kaiulani or court factions, role in the 1931 coup.

### CT-THORNE-FATHER — Silas Thorne, Elias's father
**Status:** Early family history retained; dead by the 1837 foreclosure. **Distinct from CT-SILAS-CLAIMANT.**

A Kentucky homesteader whose labor builds the family's attachment to land without protecting it against debt. His memory affects Elias's politics; he is not an 1887 succession claimant. **Open:** precise life and death circumstances, degree of closeness to son.

### CT-THORNE-MOTHER — Elias's mother
**Status:** Family role retained; personal name open.

A Kentucky widow who confronts foreclosure in 1837. She must not exist only as a suffering tableau. Her expectations, practical intelligence and judgments about Elias could continue to influence his choices long after the house burns. **Open:** her name and later life, whether she follows Elias west.

### CT-FRENCH-MENTOR — The French émigré engineer called 'Colonel'
**Status:** Approved character concept; identity open.

Former participant in Napoleon's military world, perhaps a modest engineering or artillery officer whose apparent colonelcy is disputed by the neighbors. He carries Vauban, Jomini, Plutarch, instruments and practical lessons in war and geology into a Kentucky hollow. His own history of imperial triumph and collapse is a living contradiction Elias only gradually understands. **Open:** name, birthplace, actual rank, service record, temperament and how he came to Kentucky. Avoid making every prediction prophetic.

### CT-PASTOR — Kentucky pastor
**Status:** Approved anecdotal role; name and deeper character open.

He publicly quarrels with Elias over mastodon remains, strata and the age of the earth, strikes first and suffers a broken nose when the boy retaliates. He must have sincere convictions and intelligible behavior rather than be merely an ignorant antagonist. His later attitude toward Elias is disputed by testimony.

### CT-PASTOR-DAUGHTER — The pastor's daughter / early unreliable witness
**Status:** Testimonial role approved; identity open.

She says years afterward that the pastor urged her to marry Elias, although the Chronicler cannot prove the statement and may not know whether she actually knew Elias. Her reasons for telling the story are meaningful in themselves. **Open:** whether she is a plausible lost love, opportunist, admirer, or someone sincerely misremembering family conversation. Do not settle her reliability merely to produce a romantic subplot.

## The founders and adversaries of California

**CT-ALVARADO — Juan Bautista Alvarado.** Former 1836 autonomy leader, veteran statesman and Elias's baptismal godfather. He provides constitutional legitimacy and a way for Elias to become Californian while retaining a distinct concern for Californio authority. **Motivation:** Protect political self-rule and local elite networks from distant capitals. **Voice:** A lawyer-politician reasoning from precedent, legal standing and the dangers of a premature crisis.

**CT-CASTRO — José Castro.** Military leader of the revolt against Micheltorena, Elias's first Californian patron. He needs usable reconnaissance and disciplined soldiers but distrusts losing his command to civilian rivals. **Voice:** Command, terrain, personal authority, pride; not an automatically grateful patron.

**CT-PIO-PICO — Pío Pico.** Southern Californio power broker, provisional president of the May 1846 republic. His willingness to bargain for recognition and trade can conflict with Castro's military priorities. **Voice:** Family, land, regional political agreements, the survival of his government.

**CT-ANDRES-PICO — Andrés Pico.** Military leader whose lancers defeat Kearny's forces at San Pasqual in the alternate 1846 campaign. He must receive independent credit for the victory. **Open:** precise relation to Elias's armed command and the subsequent U.S. standstill.

**CT-VALLEJO — Mariano Guadalupe Vallejo.** Influential northern Californio, pro-American internal dissenter, reluctant signatory to independence and president after the 1850 elections. His political value is that he represents a reasonable alternative to Elias's worldview. Elias receives treasury power while Vallejo remains elected head of state. **Voice:** Practical compromise, land, trade, law, and irritation at Elias's disproportionate influence.

**CT-SEM-YETO — Sem-Yeto, also known as Solano.** A Suisun leader with a past alliance with Vallejo. He negotiates a distinct compact rather than serving as a token delegate representing every California Native community. **Voice:** Grounded in sovereignty, routes, kinship and the physical consequences of ranchos and mining; do not ventriloquize an invented generic Indigenous spirituality. **Open:** Specific rights and obligations negotiated in 1846.

**CT-MICHELTORENA — Manuel Micheltorena.** The Mexican governor whose rule is challenged in 1844–45. His military officers are not fools, and his defeat does not settle the independence question.

**CT-SUTTER — John Augustus Sutter.** Entrepreneur and defender of Micheltorena whose patronage Elias declines. His land, employees and political connections become points of conflict during the founding and gold rush.

**CT-FREMONT — John C. Frémont.** Capable American explorer, officer and recurring political antagonist, confronted first at Gavilan Peak and then by the Bear Flag uprising. His actions and Elias's defensive responses should be independently intelligible.

**CT-SLOAT — John D. Sloat.** U.S. naval commander in July 1846, wary of repeating Jones's mistaken seizure. The fictional independent republic forces a decision between orders, legal legitimacy, initiative and the risk of diplomatic escalation.

**CT-STOCKTON — Robert F. Stockton.** Sloat's successor, negotiator of the proposed maritime standstill; never a mere spectator to Elias's triumph.

**CT-SEYMOUR — Admiral George Seymour.** Royal Navy officer on HMS *Collingwood*, whose arrival in Monterey complicates U.S. calculations without constituting a British guarantee.

**CT-KEARNY — Stephen W. Kearny.** Professional American commander; his defeat by Andrés Pico at San Pasqual is a military event with political repercussions. Opponents must retain professional competence.

**CT-SHERMAN — William T. Sherman.** Banker and militia organizer on the side of California's state government during the 1856 Vigilance crisis, eventually a formidable Union commander who knows the Pacifican system from personal experience. His own ambitions and judgments must not be tailored merely to flatter Elias. **Open:** Exact alternate career after 1856.

**CT-HAYS — John Coffee 'Jack' Hays.** Experienced Texas ranger and military leader who commands California's volunteer intervention in the 1860 Paiute–Washoe conflict. His confidence and initiative contribute to a tactical victory but also precipitate diplomatic disaster. **Open:** Terms of his service in the Californian republic.

**CT-PESQUEIRA — Ignacio Pesqueira.** Governor of Sonora and independent Mexican negotiator. Elias's Spanish and knowledge of Mexican affairs enable a practical commercial relationship around Guaymas and Yuma. He wants to preserve Sonoran interests against both Mexican centralization and Pacifican expansion.

**CT-SONORAN-MINER — The Sonoran placer miner.** Experienced prospector in Elias's 1848 party. Knowledge, rights to discoveries, family interests and potential claims to profit must be as specific as Elias's. **Open:** name, biography, legal position in the mining law.

**CT-MIWOK-GUIDE — The Indigenous guide.** Familiar with the Stanislaus/Mokelumne drainage and the ground's seasonal rhythms. The character's exact people (Miwok or Yokuts), name, community, knowledge and claims remain open. Avoid treating the guide as an exotic map or a servant without agency.

## The Hawaiian royal and political circle

**CT-ALEXANDER — Alexander Liholiho / Kamehameha IV.** Political partner whom Elias meets on the 1849–50 princely tour. His humiliating experience of American racial prejudice undercuts faith in U.S. protection. On taking the throne in 1854, he negotiates limits on the California alliance and appoints Kaiulani Kuhina Nui in 1855. His death in November 1863 leaves Lot to inherit an unequal political alliance. **Voice:** Deliberation under pressure, royal dignity and the experience of a man who has compared empires from within.

**CT-LOT — Lot Kapuāiwa / Kamehameha V.** Elias's closest Hawaiian royal friend, attached to riding, the outdoors and the right of rulers to act decisively. The friendship begins before treaties. Lot succeeds in 1863 and strengthens Hawaiian royal authority with the 1864 constitution, abolishing the Kuhina Nui and beginning an institutional rupture with Kaiulani. He does not live to witness the 1874 annexation, dying in December 1872 without an heir. **Voice:** Direct and stubborn, neither a Hawaiian Elias-clone nor an arbitrary obstacle.

**CT-KAMEHAMEHA-III — Kamehameha III.** Monarch whose government considers U.S. annexation in the 1854 crisis. Elias's offer of protection without absorption helps preserve Hawaiian independence at the time. His death in December 1854 changes the diplomatic situation.

**CT-EMMA — Queen Emma.** Alexander's wife from June 1856. Her own household and interests may become an important constituency after 1863 and in the 1874 succession. **Open:** precise role in the alternate election and relationship with Kaiulani.

**CT-VICTORIA-KAMAMALU — Victoria Kamāmalu.** Historical incumbent of the Kuhina Nui office (1855–1863), whose path must explicitly diverge when fictional Kaiulani receives that office in 1855. She is **not** the fictional Kaiulani and must not disappear without an explanation.

**CT-LUNALILO — Lunalilo.** Ruler during the 1873–1874 succession interval, following Lot's death. His brief reign sets the stage for disputed selection of the next monarch; his independent political legacy deserves treatment.

**CT-WYLLIE — Robert Crichton Wyllie.** Foreign Minister associated with the Hawaiian negotiations. With Kaiulani he works out the Confederation's legal safeguards and contributes professional diplomatic caution to the 1856 Articles.

**CT-DAVID-KALAKAUA — David Kalākaua.** The historical Hawaiian political figure (born 1836), whose claims and supporters matter during the 1874 succession; he is *not* Kaiulani's younger nephew, as some original profiles imply. His alternate political fate after the 1874 annexation remains open. Do not treat the conquest as making him a disposable cipher.

**CT-KALAKAUA-OFFICERS — Officers called Kalākaua.** The old profiles contain colonial and later officers bearing this surname, including young officers in the Khyber and Sydney campaigns. Their family connections are unestablished, and they may be several distinct people; until identified, record their legacy profiles as an unresolved surname group rather than silently calling them David Kalākaua.

## Japanese contacts, military aristocracy and technocrats

**CT-MANJIRO — Nakahama Manjirō.** Maritime survivor and interpreter who meets Elias in California around 1849–50, returns through Ryukyu and Satsuma in 1851, and translates during the *Kanrin Maru* visit to San Francisco in 1860. His friendship helps begin the Satsuma channel but he remains a Japanese participant with his own loyalties. **Voice:** Navigational knowledge and concrete observation, not automatically a westernizing spokesman.

**CT-HECO — Hamada Hikozō / Joseph Heco.** One of the young *Eiriki Maru* castaways in 1851. He is thirteen at first and finds temporary refuge in Elias's household. His experiences in California can give the reader a young outsider's perception of the emerging republic and a connection to Japanese officialdom later. **Open:** how much of his real later life changes, and his exact return and translation career.

**CT-NARIAKIRA — Shimazu Nariakira.** Lord of Satsuma from 1851, experimental industrial modernizer, cautious patron of Californian cooperation. He recognizes useful machinery without imagining that Japan's security requires political subordination. Dies 1858.

**CT-ABE — Abe Masahiro.** Senior shogunal politician who treats Californian contact as one possible answer to the threats posed by Russia and Western fleets. His consultation of daimyo and cautious search for technical support make him independently intelligent. Dies 1857.

**CT-II — Ii Naosuke.** Shogunal leader in the late 1850s whose ascendancy and political repression transform the risks of Californian diplomacy. The definitive **Treaty of Edo is signed in 1858** during this new political situation; the 1860 *Kanrin Maru* is a later visit. Treat him as a figure with genuine state-security problems, not as a convenient villain.

**CT-TAKASHIMA — Takashima, officer and later naval magnate.** The first-draft profiles present an artillery lieutenant from Nagasaki, an admiral and a Marquis as stages of the same name, but their exact lineage and whether the early lieutenant and later Marquis are a single person remain **[OPEN]**. His later faction represents Japanese naval interests, helps build Pacifican sea power, contests centralization and becomes a major antagonist during Elias's death and the succession. **Voice:** Courtesy masking conditional loyalties and genuine strategic calculation; avoid interchangeable mechanical jargon.

**CT-KURODA-I — Kuroda, the older Polytechnic officer.** A Japanese military officer serving Marcus Vance II during the 1887 succession conflict, associated with the overthrow of Kaito. His allegiance to a meritocratic military may divide him from hereditary Japanese factions. **Open:** whether earlier `Major Kuroda` profiles describe exactly this person, and his kinship with Fleet-General Kuroda.

**CT-KURODA-II — Fleet-General Kuroda.** A younger officer, possibly son of Kuroda I, who becomes central to the campaigns in India and Europe and dies in Afghanistan around 1919. The desire to extend naval/industrial systems beyond sustainable geography defines his political and military role. **Voice:** Data is evidence and rhetoric, but he must also show the trained officer's intelligence that made his career possible.

## The state, its financiers and successive bureaucracies

**CT-SCHUYLER — Cornelius Schuyler.** New York financier who becomes a fiscal power within Pacifica. He calculates credit and knows how dependence on bankers might survive military defeat. Kaiulani and Vance II later break the autonomy of old syndicates. **Voice:** Specific interest charges, property rights, contract language and elite entitlement; capable of genuine fiscal foresight, not just bluster.

**CT-ARTHUR-SCHUYLER — Arthur Schuyler.** Younger family member disadvantaged by the supposedly impartial examination system. Useful demonstration that the state can both attack inherited privilege and reproduce arbitrary institutional cruelty. **Open:** exactly which Schuyler generation and place in the revised 1880s.

**CT-SHEN-I — Shen, first-generation magistrate and auditor.** Chinese jurist and fiscal administrator who helps establish legal and examination institutions after the Chinese campaigns. His Confucian and administrative traditions need independent internal logic. **Open:** whether the first magistrate from Beijing and later chief examiner are the same man.

**CT-SHEN-II — Shen, second generation.** Berkeley-trained assessor working in Indian and provincial administration; political identity derives partly from family advancement under the examination regime. **Open:** precise parent-child link and age.

**CT-SHEN-III — Shen, third-generation chancellor.** Skilled official directing liquidations and the numerical networks of empire. His confidence in the state's financial instruments fails when the system produces contradictions it cannot record. Later he burns or falsifies ledgers under fear of purges. **Voice:** Formal procedural mastery that becomes strangled, panicked secrecy. **Open:** exact grandfather and links among three Shen profiles.

**CT-HOSHINO-LEGAL — Judge Hoshino.** Japanese jurist in early imperial legal codification. Not automatically related to every later official named Hoshino.

**CT-HOSHINO-HANKOW — Hoshino, officer killed in Hankow.** Junior prefect killed during unrest. Potential ancestor of a younger auditing generation. **Open:** relationship to other Hoshinos.

**CT-HOSHINO-AUDITOR — Hoshino, younger imperial auditor.** Serves Kaiulani and later works in Siberian audits. His death reveals limits of loyalty to an institution that cannot account for its violence.

**CT-HOSHINO-REBEL — Lieutenant-Assessor Hoshino.** Elite naval officer found among the revolutionary divers beneath San Francisco. His defection is evidence that the bureaucratic peerage has divided against itself. **Open:** degree of family kinship with the other Hoshinos; never conflate merely because of surname.

**CT-KEONI — Keoni, Hawaiian chamberlain.** Knows the island court, its ceremonies and the lives of rulers beyond the political dispatches. A valuable witness to the 1874 crisis, but not an omniscient chorus.

**CT-MULLIGAN — Colonel Mulligan.** One-armed officer and hard military professional serving Pacifica's early and overseas expeditions. The original profile assumes he lost his arm at San Pasqual in the Mexican-American War, which now takes place as an alternate engagement in 1846 between Kearny and Andrés Pico; his injury and early path must be rewritten. He should retain skill, an appetite for status and his own ethical boundaries.

**CT-STERLING — Major Sterling.** Engineer and aide linked to the founder's communications, campaigns and large projects. His role may become a continuing eyewitness to organizational failures, but his timeline must be checked before using him across decades.

## Confederate command and soldiers

These four dossiers implement the author's request for a nuanced Confederate value schema, including the Western soldiers' primary accounts. For each scene separate entry into service, present commitment and a possible breaking point. The Confederacy's commitment to slavery remains part of the political world; other personal obligations neither erase it nor exhaust a character's personality. The [source guide](confederate_value_schema.md) distinguishes letters, diaries, reports, memoirs, later interpretation and fictional extensions. Proposed tests are not additional approved plot events.

### CT-DAVIS — Jefferson Davis
**Status:** Author-approved characterization direction; Civil War meetings, concessions and campaign chronology remain **[OPEN]**. Historical Jefferson Davis in an alternate timeline; fictional actions and assigned motives are not historical testimony.

Davis seeks to preserve a government with legitimate political authority, rather than a Southern administration surviving at Thorne's pleasure. His dominant commitments are sovereignty, inherited republican obligation and public responsibility. He can understand Pacifica's superior force while disputing that force creates a right to govern. His position includes the slaveholding order in which he has a planter's stake; the consent he invokes does not extend equally to the people that order enslaves.

His conflict with Benjamin concerns the permissible price of independence. Immediate relief may preserve soldiers and households while permanently surrendering customs, ports or jurisdiction. Davis's resistance has a coherent object, but the people who bear its cost may not accept his judgment. **Virtue and evasion:** public responsibility must appear in an action with an acknowledged cost; invoking sovereignty must not conceal unequal sacrifice or enslavement. **Pressure point:** whether refusal still discharges the obligations that made independence worth defending.

**Voice/POV:** Precise, formal Southern statesmanship; distinctions among assistance, alliance, obligation and subordination. Pain, anger and pride can interrupt his reasoning without replacing it. He notices the clause that transfers authority and the people who will pay for refusing it. Existing dividers and neuralgic-eye gestures are available physical details, not a substitute for thought.

**Scene options, not settled incidents:** Benjamin offers immediate provisions against a permanent concession; Davis must identify both what remains sovereign and who suffers if he refuses. **Sources:** Semmes and Scrugham for political self-description, Hamner for courage separated from confidence in command, Peticolas and Scurry for courage separated from strategic feasibility. These are comparative analogues, not proof of Davis's private motives. See [Confederate values and source register](confederate_value_schema.md).

**Legacy/open:** Original profile #27 supplies the identity. Its 1853 cabinet encounter belongs to the superseded first-draft scenario and is not restored by this dossier. Precise wartime terms, meetings and outcomes remain subject to campaign design.

### CT-BENJAMIN — Judah Benjamin
**Status:** Author-approved characterization direction; particular negotiations and concessions remain **[OPEN]**. Historical Judah Benjamin in an alternate timeline.

Benjamin's practical judgment serves a positive purpose: keeping institutions able to feed soldiers, protect civilians, recover prisoners and negotiate another day. His dominant commitments are institutional survival, enforceable agreements and responsibility for consequences. He understands Davis's concern that an administration without jurisdiction is independence in name; disagreement arises over what can still be preserved and at what cost.

Every proposed compromise needs a concrete beneficiary and an acknowledged burden. A temporary military arrangement differs from an obligation imposed on successor governments. **Virtue and evasion:** exact legal reasoning may protect people at real political cost, but can also make the rights surrendered by others seem an administrative necessity. His service sustains the slaveholding Confederacy; pragmatism does not establish antislavery convictions.

**Voice/POV:** Controlled, courteous legal phrasing, restrained irony, precise alternatives. He can state Davis's strongest argument before answering it. A neatly folded telegram or carefully revised clause can reveal emotional control without making him empty. His Jewish identity is not an explanation for financial calculation, opportunism or moral detachment.

**Scene options, not settled incidents:** He names the people saved by an agreement and the authority it transfers, then explains why its guarantees can be enforced. **Sources:** Semmes for political and professional standing, Hamner for morale under disputed command, Scurry for material limits on military action. These support fictional possibilities rather than a historical claim about Benjamin's inner life. See [Confederate values and source register](confederate_value_schema.md).

**Legacy/open:** Original profile #52 supplies the identity and Richmond cabinet role. Its specific August 1862 encounter is not automatically canonical. Decisions on the bargain, its date and its result remain open.

### CT-ST-CLAIR — Major Julian St. Clair
**Status:** Author-approved characterization direction for a fictional Southern envoy; early affiliations, injury chronology and exact diplomatic encounters remain **[OPEN]**. Major Julian St. Clair is distinct from Julian Thorne.

St. Clair understands assistance as a pledged relationship. His grievance against Thorne is that repayment with interest is treated as extinguishing a political obligation. His dominant commitments are the pledged word, solidarity and recognition as an equal partner. He can correctly assess Pacifica's strength and still reject its account of what one party owes another.

Honor must govern his conduct toward a subordinate as well as his demands on equals. A costly act of protection or fidelity gives his claim weight. **Virtue and evasion:** practiced reciprocity can coexist with the exclusionary slaveholding order in which the Charleston aristocrat has a stake. Generosity does not make his conception of rights universal. His breaking point concerns an advantage obtainable only by abandoning someone or something entrusted to him.

**Voice/POV:** Polished Southern gentry diction and exact distinctions among payment, alliance and promise. Physical strain may shorten or interrupt his speech without destroying judgment. Naval hulks, mutual guaranties and debts of service belong to his perceptual world. He notices breaches of standing and inconvenient obligations; inherited rank can blind him to other people's claims.

**Scene options, not settled incidents:** Better individual treatment becomes available if he abandons an escort or wounded subordinate. The choice tests whether he practices the fidelity he demands. **Sources:** Semmes for costly allegiance and standing, Guerrant for standards applied to one's own side, Watkins for conscience demonstrated through conduct. See [Confederate values and source register](confederate_value_schema.md).

**Legacy/open:** Original profile #20 supplies this envoy's identity, Charleston background and wounded-service trajectory. Its Knights of the Golden Circle affiliation in 1847 requires independent chronological reassessment; the meeting, the earlier aid and the particular bargain with Thorne are not newly approved incidents. Retain the wounded envoy as a development direction while checking the Shiloh injury against the redesigned campaigns.

### CT-ARMISTEAD — Colonel Armistead
**Status:** Author-approved characterization direction; original Confederate service, transfer terms and later campaign sequence remain **[OPEN]**. The novel's Colonel Armistead is a fictional or composite officer, not an identification with historical Lewis Armistead.

Armistead's central obligations are comradeship, professional competence and continuity after defeat. His possible bridge from Confederate service into Pacifica's Atlantic Auxiliary is a defensible purpose: preserving survivors as a unit, obtaining provisions or arrears, supporting households, or continuing the profession he knows. Which of these drives the actual transfer is not yet settled.

Each subsequent imperial campaign tests whether service still fulfills that first obligation. Protection of familiar men can become a justification for violence against other people's households. **Virtue and evasion:** let professional care cost him something, and let the limits of that care become visible. Former Confederate service neither establishes an antislavery conversion nor proves personal slave ownership; his particular relationship to slavery requires characterization rather than assumption.

**Voice/POV:** Practical Virginia military vernacular, drawling understatement and concrete reports of terrain, supply, orders and named men. Tobacco, the hickory stick and injuries from the original sketch are available details subject to chronology. He distinguishes whether an order can be carried out from whether it honors the purpose for which he accepted service. Western accounts provide comparative pressures, not interchangeable regional voices.

**Scene options, not settled incidents:** Terms permit the regiment to remain together; a later imperial order requires conduct he once condemned when applied to his own country. Refusal, restraint, rationalization and compliance remain choices to dramatize. **Sources:** Jackman for personal networks and limited attention, Hamner for loyalty alongside criticism of command, Guerrant for objections to misconduct, Semmes for professional standing. See [Confederate values and source register](confederate_value_schema.md).

**Legacy/open:** Original profile #83 supplies the Second Virginia Regiment of the Atlantic Auxiliary and Guam, Luzon and Yokohama appearances. These are retained material for reconciliation with the revised 1860s expansion, not a settled itinerary. Define his enlistment motive, transfer date and households before treating proposed motives as events.

## Relationship and identity safeguards

### Thorne–Kaiulani dynastic core
- Elias + Kaiulani: **married February 1857**; one legitimate son, dead in the later 1870s.
- Elias + other women: offspring exist, including potential rival claimants Silas and Kiyoshi, **exact genealogy open**.
- Elias + younger Hawaiian-American woman: Julian born c.1886–87. His biological mother dies mysteriously after August 1887; Kaiulani adopts him.
- Julian + Kaiulani: genuine adoptive mother–son relationship; eventual political opposition and restoration tragedy.
- Sacred Emperor Kalanimoku: installed 1914; **not a descendant through the deceased legitimate son**; exact descent and relationship to Julian and Kaiulani open.
- **Never** attach the name Silas to Elias's father and his later possible son without separate IDs.

### Vance kinship
- Caleb is Elias's friend from 1841, later an old companion and survivor of the first campaigns.
- Marcus I joins around the early gold era, becomes a founding general and dies c.1869.
- Marcus II is his son, b. c.1858; not twenty-three in 1887.
- Marcus III commands after 1919, born c.1891; precise kinship to Marcus II **open**.
- The suggestion that Caleb and Marcus I are brothers is **working but not yet a locked genealogy**.

### Other independent pedigrees
- Takashima: early officer, admiral, marquis—**identity stages unverified**.
- Kuroda: younger Fleet-General is distinct from the older staff officer.
- Shen: multiple generations, exact individual aliases and descent unresolved.
- Hoshino: at least four role-defined people, kinship unresolved.
- Kalākaua: historic David and invented officers require disambiguation.
- Kaiulani is fictional and cannot be equated with Victoria Kamāmalu or historical Victoria Kaʻiulani.
- Manjirō and Hikozō are distinct real Japanese castaways whose careers diverge from historical events.

### Perspective allocation

| Perspective | Material they uniquely perceive | What they tend to miss |
|---|---|---|
| Elias | Terrain, human cooperation, technical possibilities, the cost of dependence | Consent and local identities that survive administration |
| Kaiulani | Legal obligations, court alliances, dynastic threats, the human meanings of sovereignty | Her own complicity and sometimes the autonomy of the young |
| Caleb | Hardship, the character of comrades, informal loyalties | Abstract administrative systems beyond immediate effects |
| Marcus I | Tactical terrain, campaign supply, competent enemy officers | Private costs of an increasingly permanent war |
| Marcus II | Institutional failure, calculation, training and economic necessity | Unquantified human and cultural obligations |
| Marcus III | Punishment, status, inspections, embarrassing deficits | Why people resist rather than behave as a ledger predicts |
| Julian | Stories, inherited shame, mass moods, opportunities for rhetoric | The contingency and cruelty within the founder's original choices |
| Julian's mother | Intimate household power and the vulnerability of those near rulers | Complete strategic plans made above her station |
| The Chronicler | Archival patterns, time, empire, ideological coherence | What sources omit or his own education prejudices him against |
| Manjirō / Heco | Ships, translation, social encounters across borders | The grand intentions subsequently ascribed to them |

**Stylistic instruction:** Give each POV a different objective, perceptual range, memory, emotional defenses and access to information. Do not cycle through three preset 'fractal' prose registers by chapter number. A person may speak differently to a spouse, subordinate, stranger or adversary. Avoid making race or nationality a substitute for personality.
