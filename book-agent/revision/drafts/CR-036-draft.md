# CR-036 (draft) — Science: the scholars Europe would not promote, two elements, and the world that loses them (1855–1910)

**Status:** PROPOSED draft, 28 October 2026; second revision (facts verified). Not adopted: no storyline, register, character or workbook file is changed by this draft.
**Basis:** author decisions of 28 October 2026. Builds on draft CR-034 (the Polytechnic, citizenship with a chair from 1854, the 1858 prestige year) and CR-035 (the Pacifica League).

## Author decisions

> What do you think would make sense for their purposes? … One thing we haven't talked about much is science. The discovery of an element would definitely be a good source of prestige for Pacifica and could come about through the advancements in mineralogy at carquinez. But what about physics and biology? If Pacifica is taking in a lot of the world's jewish scientists, it would make sense for them to have attracted at least some of the leading lights in those disciplines as well.

> Yes let's take them all in including Koch and also Paul Ehrlich if it's believable. Please outline the knock-on effects that this implies for world history. We can keep Indium as an early discovery; Element 31 can be discovered in Pacifica and called Californium in this timeline. I approve of the divisions for the colleges although I note that the interior should not be denied prestigious schools and material and/or scientific advancements of its own.

> I agree and please draft these changes as CR-036.

## Proposed storyline text

Each insertion is a new paragraph, shown with the existing passage it follows, quoted exactly. Where CR-034 or CR-035 inserts after the same passage, CR-036's paragraph follows theirs.

### §8, after the paragraph ending "…**[OPEN]** Arrival, consulting arrangements, experimental works and sequence of technical successes." (D1–D3, D7)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-036]** The offer of a chair with citizenship reaches men Europe will not promote. James Joseph Sylvester came second in Cambridge's mathematics examination and could not take his degree, because he would not sign the Church of England's articles. He comes in 1855 instead of taking a post at Woolwich. Robert Remak named the three layers of the embryo and was the first Jew allowed to teach at Berlin, but never as its professor. In 1858 he takes the chair of physiology and pathology at the Methodists' new medical college. Ferdinand Cohn, kept waiting a year at Breslau before he might take the oath to teach, comes the same year to study the smallest living things. At the gold camp at Murphys a Prussian Jewish merchant's son, Albert Michelson, is learning to read.

### §11, after the paragraph ending "…Both can oppose an unprepared operation while disagreeing about the peace Pacifica should seek." (D8)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-036]** In 1863, with the war at its worst, Genth's spectroscope finds a bright blue line in zinc ore from Washoe that belongs to no known element. Carquinez names the metal indium for its color. Freiberg, testing its own ores that year, finds it second.

### §15, after the paragraph ending "…Reforms do not erase coercion, and the land board becomes a landlord too." (D4, D16)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-036]** The physicians who read the cholera as a matter of water are led by Robert Koch. Cohn brought him in 1872 from a country practice in Posen, and in 1876 he traced the life of anthrax at Carquinez. That winter in Manila his team finds the comma-shaped bacillus in the water and shows how it travels. The Office's tables of deaths by town, before and after the pipes, go round the world.

### §15, after the paragraph ending "…the pairing and exact judgment remain **[OPEN]** within the approved test-case direction." (D12–D14)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-036]** The interior builds schools of its own. The Colegio de Santa Fe, founded in 1864, trains the surveyors of the treaty lines and the engineers of the acequias, and sets an observatory in the high, clear air. When the total eclipse of 29 July 1878 crosses the Rockies, its spectroscopists lead the observation. Deseret's university, revived after accession, measures how much water an acre needs and teaches the arid world to build canals. At Virginia City the Sutro School, founded with the tunnel in 1869, studies the heat of the deep Comstock and the cold that lets men work in it.

### §19, after the paragraph ending "…turn the examination state's learning into firms, equipment and careers.", following CR-034's paragraph there (D5–D7, D9, D11)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-036]** Its learning is not only applied. In 1873 Carquinez finds the metal Mendeleev had predicted beneath aluminium and calls it californium, and the periodic table is proved right on a Pacifican specimen. Yerba Buena College, founded in 1866 by San Francisco's merchant and banking houses of every faith, gives Sylvester his mathematics and, in 1878, the Murphys boy Michelson his measurement of the speed of light. Paul Ehrlich, a Breslau student of Cohn's circle, stains the cells no one could see. From 1885 Kitasato Shibasaburō works in Koch's laboratory, and Japanese medicine begins to learn in English and from Pacifica. In 1887 Michelson's interferometer finds no ether wind, the most famous null result of the century.

### §26, after the paragraph ending "…and containment begins to collide with their portfolios: on Wall Street the \"Pacifican interest\" argues for peace." (D10, D17)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-036]** The Nobel committees confirm what the Union fears. Koch wins in 1905, Michelson in 1907 and Ehrlich in 1908, all three as Pacificans. In 1909 Ehrlich and Hata Sahachirō find the arsenical that cures syphilis. Roosevelt's laboratories are built in the shadow of Carquinez.
