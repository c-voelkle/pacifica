# CR-037 (draft) — No cliques: keeping the examination state's institutions open (1870s–1890s)

**Status:** PROPOSED draft, 28 October 2026; first revision, after one fresh-context review (consistency and literary effect). Not adopted: no storyline, register, character or workbook file is changed by this draft.
**Basis:** author decision of 28 October 2026. Builds on CR-036 (talent from every faith and background; Victoria's balance) and the existing canon of the examination state (§19), the Five's neutrality (§24) and county grades (§25).

## Author decisions

> Another thing that Pacifica should make efforts to prevent is ethnic nepotism, or the practice by which ethnicities form cliques and take control of important institutions by favoring their own.

> Yes can you write that up as a short CR37

## Decisions

| ID | Decision | Label |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | **The aim.** Pacifica works to stop any ethnic or religious group from capturing an institution by favouring its own. The rules apply to every group alike: old-stock Protestant networks, Californio families, Hawaiian chiefly lineages, Irish ward politics, Jewish and Chinese banking houses, Japanese officers. | APPROVED (author) |
| E2 | **The test is the examination, not a quota.** An institution is judged by whether its appointments and promotions follow the examination results, not by its mix of origins. Quotas would be ethnic politics run by the state, and they would break the 1854 rule of no religious or national test. | PROPOSED |
| E3 | **Anonymous scripts.** From the late 1870s Soberanes's examinations identify candidates by number, and clerks recopy the scripts so that no examiner knows a hand. Song China did the same to stop examiners favouring their clans. | PROPOSED |
| E4 | **Avoidance.** No examiner judges a candidate from their own state (later county) or college. Officials take their first postings away from home; this practice is the root of the 1887 rule that a Commissioner never serves in his home county (§21). Boards that appoint to chairs and senior posts include outsiders from other colleges. These follow the Qing law of avoidance; canon also applies it to the Philippine Gobernadores (§27). | PROPOSED |
| E5 | **Published appointment tables.** From 1888 the Office of Public Measures publishes, for each institution, how appointments and promotions compare with the examination ranking. A gap that persists brings an audit (of the Polytechnic itself by the Office), which is an inspection and not a quota. Officials declare relatives and former students in a register, and an appointment of anyone declared is void unless an outside board confirms it. | PROPOSED |
| E6 | **Mixed cohorts.** The English bridging year (§25), the cadet corps and the college residences deliberately mix origins, languages and faiths. | PROPOSED |
| E7 | **Where it bites.** The rules cover what the Crown charters, funds or licenses: the estates, the League colleges, champions in which the Crown holds shares, and the licensed professions. Private firms are left to competition. In the counties, a finding of capture counts against the "order" standard, so county machines risk their chartered status. | PROPOSED |
| E8 | **The first great audit (1890) hits an established clique.** It falls on the board of the state savings bank in San Francisco, held for a generation by intermarried Yankee, Californio and Irish families, so that the policy is never read as aimed at newcomers. The auditors act on their own authority; Victoria refuses both to stop them and to set quotas. | PROPOSED; whether it grows into a full scene OPEN |
| E9 | **The treaty exception.** The command of the western squadron is reserved to officers of the Japanese naval party (§24, existing canon). This is a reservation by treaty with a partner state, not a clique inside the Realm. Critics still attack it, and it remains a crack Julian may have to deal with. | Existing canon; how critics use it OPEN |

## Proposed storyline text

### §19, after the paragraph ending "…Financial syndicates, officers and hereditary court factions compete to direct the machinery in anticipation of Elias's death." (E3, E4)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-037]** Soberanes guards the machinery against capture by patrons as well. From the late 1870s candidates are known to their examiners only by number, and clerks recopy every script so that no hand is recognised. No examiner judges a candidate from their own state or college, officials take their first postings away from home, and every board that fills a chair includes outsiders. A patron, a family or a guild can still recommend a candidate; it cannot pass one.

### §25, after the paragraph ending "…Julian later uses commissions and ordinances more freely, giving county oligarchies reason to fight through their senators." (E1, E2, E5–E8)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-037]** The Office also counts who gets the posts. From 1888 it publishes, for every chartered body, college and licensed profession, how appointments and promotions compare with the examination lists, and a gap that lasts brings in the auditors. The first great audit, in 1890, falls on the board of the state savings bank in San Francisco. Its seats have passed for a generation among a few intermarried families, Yankee, Californio and Irish, and the examined clerks below them never rise. The auditors void three appointments and the families appeal to the Regent. Victoria refuses to stop the audit, and she refuses the newspapers' demand for quotas: the measure is the examination, not the mix. From then on officials declare their relatives and former students, and a county whose offices pass by family or congregation loses marks for order.

## Impact notes

- **Storyline:** two new paragraphs, in §19 and §25, each with a hidden CR-037 coverage comment. No existing sentence changes.
- **Existing canon kept:**
  - §19: the examination state "promises fairness across races and provinces", and factions compete to direct it.
  - §24: the estates "never let a faction capture them".
  - §25: county standards, the English bridging year and Victoria's honest numbers.
  - §27: Gobernadores never posted to their home province.
  - CR-036: talent from every faith, admitted one by one and never as communities.
- **Character documents:** Soberanes (E3, E4) and Victoria (E5, E8) gain a line each on adoption.
- **Historical background:** on adoption, add the Song dynasty's anonymous and recopied scripts, the Qing law of avoidance, and the Prussian appointment practice of outside referees.
- **Commitments ledger:** none. These are laws and administrative rules, not commitments between parties.
- **Workbook:** none.
- **Review:** one fresh-context review; applied: the first audit falls on a mixed established clique, and the auditors act without Victoria steering them; a chartered bank, not a private one; gender-neutral candidates (women sit the examinations from 1859); states rather than counties before 1887; the avoidance practice tied to the 1887 Commissioner rule; the §25 list cut to a short scene.
