# CR-038 (draft) — The machinery's own safeguards: retirement, Vallejo College, inspectors, hard numbers, bonded scholarships, official pay, county halls, results-based pride and a trades congress (1887–1890s)

**Status:** PROPOSED draft, 28 October 2026; fourth revision, with the author's answers to the open choices (F1, F2, F6, F8, F9, F10, F13). Fresh-context reviews applied to the third and fourth revisions. Not adopted: no storyline, register, character or workbook file is changed by this draft.
**Basis:** author decisions of 28 October 2026, after comparisons with the Chinese Communist Party's personnel system under Deng Xiaoping and his successors and with Singapore under Lee Kuan Yew. Builds on the 1887 charters (§21, §24), the Office of Public Measures (§25) and draft CR-037 (no cliques).

## Author decisions

> Please write them into a short cr-038.

"Them" are four proposals from the comparison with China: retirement ages for the heads of the estates and senior posts; a common college for mid-career officials; unannounced inspectors; and numbers that are hard to fake.

> Let's leave that idea [a party] on hold for now. Fold the bonded scholarships, benchmarked official pay and chartered county halls.

> Yes let's add it [a chartered labour congress] with the suggested cautions into CR-038.

> Write it up as background for CR-038. [The author kept the general guest-worker programme in 1890 and asked for its earlier, sector-limited precursors as background.]

> Can you come up with a suitable name, nine months or one year seems reasonable. I would suggest no sex-based eligibility restriction, but expect a Norway-style "gender equality paradox" in chosen careers such that an unofficial women's campus exists to cater with health, welfare and education themes, whereas the engineering, technology and management sector corresponds to a men's campus, characterized by men's bonding activities, ergo the "Carquinez men". Retirement at 68 is approved also for judges and county Commissioners and especially for senators. Five years is approved. There could be different types of scholarships and grants with different regulations and purposes. One could be for retaining general graduate talent in Pacifica's economy - for that one of course teaching at the league should count. Others could be granted for the purpose of filling specific job or expertise vacancies, in which case those and not the league teaching posts would have to be filled in order to redeem the bond. The county halls should be required to meet certain minimal standards by the central government and to that extent they are the responsibility of an Office inspector and his trustees, however they should not be allowed a great deal of freedom and individuality as long as they live up to the ideal of fostering cohesion and pro-Pacifican sentiment. An important factor in this that came up in the discussion as well is nationalism and the promotion of a results-based pride in being Pacifican. Hawaii abolishes penal contracts upon confederation. 1895 is approved as the congress's date; its to be understood as a separate institution from the residence tests taken by guest workers, though they are part of the same ecosystem. As in Singapore, guest workers do not self-register into the labor congress, but rather interact with it indirectly through specialized auxiliary arms, which act as state-backed safety nets to support a productive labor ecosystem. The party question remains on hold for now.

**Reading of the county-hall sentence.** The draft reads "should not be allowed a great deal of freedom … as long as they live up to the ideal" as *should now be allowed* a great deal of freedom and individuality, provided they foster cohesion and pro-Pacifican sentiment: minimum standards from the centre, freedom above them. If the author meant the opposite (tight central control), F8 changes accordingly.

## Decisions

| ID | Decision | Label |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Retirement at 68.** Every estate head and senior officer appointed under the charters of 1887–88, every judge, every county Commissioner and every senator, whether chosen by a county board or appointed, retires in the year they turn 68. So each generation leaves on schedule and no clique sits for life. Only the Crown, the Regent and the Truce's named holders, who keep their offices for life (§22), are outside the rule. The Office's twelve-year heads (§21) serve their term or to 68, whichever ends first. Soberanes, a Truce holder, retires by his own choice in 1915, the year he turns 68 (born 1847). This is the Deng-era fix (the informal rule that officials of 67 may stay and those of 68 go) made into law. | APPROVED (author) |
| F2 | **Vallejo College.** From 1889 the old Council offices at Monterey, which the government left for Thalassia in 1885 and which stand beside the Court of the Realm, house Vallejo College, named for President Vallejo, in the town that holds the Court and Californio legitimacy. Rising officials of the Polytechnic, the Office, the navy, the chartered champions and the counties spend one academic year there together (September to June, about nine months), mixed by origin and language. There is no restriction by sex. Their loyalty runs to the Realm and to their year rather than to an estate or a community. The model is the Communist Party's Central Party School. | APPROVED (author); name PROPOSED |
| F2a | **Two informal sides.** Free choice produces the pattern later called the gender-equality paradox: women officials gather in health, welfare, education and the Office's measures, men in engineering, technology and management. The College grows two unofficial sides. The men's side, in the old Presidio barracks, keeps its rowing, shooting and drinking clubs; its graduates from the Polytechnic and the champions become the "Carquinez men", the clique of the examined that CR-037 warns about. The women's side, called the Kaʻahumanu House by its first students after the society Victoria founded, keeps its own circles in the hospitals, schools and the Office. Neither side is a rule; both are where the networks of the next generation form. | APPROVED direction (author); names and details PROPOSED |
| F3 | **Inspectors.** The Office sends inspectors, unannounced, to check the counties' returns on the ground. They report to the Regent and publish. They check the estates only through the returns the estates themselves publish, because the Crown does not touch the estates' appointments or charters (§24). The model is the Party's inspection tours. | APPROVED (author); mechanics PROPOSED |
| F4 | **Numbers that are hard to fake.** Every county return is checked against figures that are hard to falsify: freight tonnage, coal and power used, savings deposits, burials and vaccination records. The lesson comes from Chinese growth targets, which officials gamed. It is Victoria's honest numbers with an instrument. | APPROVED (author) |
| F6 | **Bonded scholarships and grants, from 1888, with a five-year bond.** The model is Singapore's Public Service Commission scholarships. Two kinds:<br>• **Realm scholarships** keep general graduate talent in Pacifica's economy. The bond is redeemed by five years of work anywhere in Pacifica, in the Realm's service, an estate, a county, a champion or private employment, and teaching in a League college counts.<br>• **Posted grants** fill named vacancies or scarce expertise: county physicians, interior surveyors, the Philippine and Formosan services, naval engineers and the like. The bond is redeemed only by five years in those posts; League teaching does not count.<br>The bond is otherwise repaid. Other kinds may follow. | APPROVED (author); the list of posted grants PROPOSED |
| F7 | **Benchmarked official pay.** From 1888 the Senate sets the pay of senior officials each year against what the chartered champions pay their managers, and publishes both. An official earns less than a manager but enough that corruption does not pay; the CR-038 inspectors and the CR-037 audits are the other half of the bargain. The model is Singapore's ministerial pay. | APPROVED (author); formula PROPOSED |
| F8 | **Chartered county halls.** From the 1890s, under a Realm enabling law, each county opens a hall open to every resident, offering what hometown associations, clan houses and congregations offer their own: sick and burial funds, interpreters, night classes, notices of work and help with papers. The centre sets minimum standards, and to that extent each hall is the responsibility of an Office inspector and the hall's trustees; measures grants pay for halls that meet them. Above the minimum each hall has wide freedom of character, language, festivals and programme, so long as it fosters cohesion and pride in Pacifica. The hall does not replace the community bodies; it competes with them, so that no one needs a clan to get by. The model is Singapore's People's Association. It answers CR-037's problem of hidden ties. | APPROVED (author; see the reading note above) |
| F13 | **Results-based pride.** The Realm's nationalism is pride in measured results, not in ancestry: anyone who contributes can share it. The halls post the county's tables beside the Realm's; counties celebrate the year they reach the four standards; the press seat publishes the Realm's achievements, from the 1855 rescue and the 1858 results to californium and Koch, with the weak spots alongside, as Victoria insists. Pride in Pacifica becomes pride in what the tables show, which is open to every newcomer who adds to them. | APPROVED (author) |
| F9 | **A chartered trades congress (1895).** After the Bread March of 1894, Victoria wants Pacifican workers to have the lawful channel the Union's marchers lacked, and a congress of the trades is chartered by law. It keeps sick, accident and burial funds; it keeps the register of tradesmen who have passed the Polytechnic's trade tests; and it publishes, with the employers' associations and the Office as arbiter, a reference table of wages and conditions that contracts may cite. **Cautions:** (1) It never controls hiring, training or pay: no closed shop, hiring hall or gang of its own, so contractors keep recruitment, training, payment and control of the work (CR-031-R1). (2) It is chartered by law, not affiliated to the Crown, so that no wage dispute becomes a dispute with the Crown. (3) Independent unions stay legal; the congress wins members by its benefits (CR-031-R1 bans no association or collective bargaining). (4) It stays in the background like the guest permits: CR-020 took the strike and labour-charter arc out of the story. | APPROVED (author), 1895 approved; details PROPOSED |
| F9a | **Guest workers and the congress.** The guest workers' residence test (§25) is a separate institution, run by the Realm, though part of the same ecosystem. Guest workers do not enrol in the congress. They reach it through its auxiliary arm, the Guest Workers' Aid, a state-backed safety net paid partly from the employer levy: interpreters and legal help in contract disputes, a sick and accident fund, shelter and passage home when an employer fails. Its purpose is a productive labour market, not a union of guests. The model is Singapore's Migrant Workers' Centre. | APPROVED (author); name and funding PROPOSED |
| F10 | **Background: Hawaii's inspected contracts.** Historically the first Chinese contract labourers reached Hawaii's sugar plantations in 1852, under the 1850 Masters and Servants Act's penal contracts, in which a worker who quit could be jailed; Japan's government-sponsored contract emigration followed in 1885–94. In Pacifica the kingdom abolishes penal contracts upon joining the Confederation in 1856, over the planters' protests, and replaces them with inspected ones: inspected housing, protected wages, paid passage home and the right to change employer after a contract. Plantation labour becomes a free-labour market in line with CR-031-R1. | APPROVED (author) |
| F11 | **Background: contract crews for the great works.** In the 1860s and 1870s the Sierra railway and the interior links hire fixed-term crews, many of them Chinese, on contracts with return passage, inside the years of the open door. Canon's Chinese construction crews keep their say over working conditions. These contracts give the Realm its first experience of running temporary labour fairly. | APPROVED as background (author); details PROPOSED |
| F12 | **The general programme stays in 1890.** Before 1888 the door is open, and those who might come as guests come as settlers, which serves Pacifica better: settlers become citizens, taxpayers, soldiers and examinees. The 1890 programme copies the Hawaiian contract (F10) and the works crews' contracts (F11) after two years of labour shortage following the 1888 restriction. | APPROVED (author) |
| F5 | **What is not copied.** No term limit is lifted for anyone; anti-corruption is never used as a purge; and there is no assimilation by force. These stay temptations for Julian, whose test is OPEN (CR-036). A Crown-affiliated party is on hold. | PROPOSED; party on hold (author) |

## Proposed storyline text

Each change is shown with the existing passage it follows or replaces, quoted exactly. Where CR-037 inserts after the same passage, CR-038's paragraphs follow it.

### §8, after the paragraph ending "…California retains English–Spanish guarantees, Hawaii its Hawaiian-language institutions. Japan remains a treaty partner." (F10)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-038]** Hawaii pays a price of its own for union. A republic that punishes no Californian for leaving his employer will not share a common market with plantations that jail Chinese labourers for quitting theirs. Over the planters' protests, the kingdom's legislature abolishes penal labour contracts in its ratifying session. From then on a plantation contract buys inspected housing, a protected wage and the passage home, and at the end of its term the worker may take his labour wherever he likes.

### §21, replace "Life appointments form up to a quarter of the Senate," with "Appointments held to the age of sixty-eight form up to a quarter of the Senate," (F1)

### §25, after the paragraph ending "…Julian later uses commissions and ordinances more freely, giving county oligarchies reason to fight through their senators." (F1–F2a)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-038]** Victoria and Soberanes make the machinery guard itself. The regency extends the Constitution's age of sixty-eight for appointed senators to every senator, judge, Commissioner, estate head and senior officer, except the Truce's named holders. From 1889 the government offices left empty at Monterey when the seat moved to Thalassia house Vallejo College, beside the Court. Rising officers of the Polytechnic, the Office, the navy, the champions and the counties, women and men, spend a year there. Their rooms are assigned by lot across origins; their evenings are not. The engineers and managers take over Monterey's old Presidio barracks with their rowing and drinking clubs, and many of the women of the hospitals, schools and Office gather in the house they name after Victoria's Kaʻahumanu Society. A woman surveyor rows with the barracks crew; Polytechnic men sit at the House's lectures on sanitation. The barracks crowd of the 1890s will later be called the Carquinez men.

### §25, directly after the CR-038 paragraph above (F3, F4, F6–F8, F13)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-038]** The Office's inspectors arrive in a county without warning and check its returns against what is hard to fake: freight tonnage, coal and power, savings deposits and burials. They publish what they find. From 1888 a Realm scholarship binds its holder to five years' work in Pacifica, and a posted grant to five years in the county surgery, survey camp or island service it was made for. Each year the Senate prints senior officials' pay beside what the champions pay their managers. In the 1890s every county opens a hall for all its residents, with sick and burial funds, interpreters, night classes, notices of work and the county's tables on the wall beside the Realm's. An Office inspector holds it to the Office's minimum of services; above that it keeps its own language, festivals and character. In 1897 Fresno's hall rings its bell for a week when the county first meets all four standards. Pacificans learn to be proud of what the tables show.

### §25, after the paragraph ending "…These routes sustain the industrial economy while leaving a class of workers outside citizenship." (F11–F12)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-038]** The programme's contract is the one Hawaii's plantations have used since 1856 and the railway and interior works since the 1860s, now made the Realm's law.

### §25, after the paragraph ending "…The affair damages silver populism and revanchism while exposing the exclusions of Victoria's settlement." (F9, F9a)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-038]** Victoria means Pacifica's own workers never to need a march. In 1895 a trades congress is chartered by law. It keeps sick and burial funds and the register of tradesmen who have passed the Polytechnic's trade tests, and with the employers it publishes a reference table of wages that contracts may cite. The contractors still hire, train and pay. Guest workers do not join the congress. Its Guest Workers' Aid, paid partly from the employer levy, finds them interpreters and lawyers in a dispute, and shelter and the passage home when an employer fails.

## Impact notes

- **Storyline:** one new paragraph in §8, one replaced phrase in §21 (with a hidden CR-038 coverage comment and an inline PROPOSED marker on adoption) and five new paragraphs in §25, each with a hidden CR-038 coverage comment. The Fresno celebration of 1897 is an illustrative example; any county and year will do.
- **Review:** one fresh-context review of the third revision; applied: Truce holders exempt from retirement; the Office's twelve-year heads kept; the Court still at Monterey; the congress neither trains nor sets pay, and the Polytechnic sets the trade tests; the Bread March link given a motive; paragraphs given actors and placed after the county standards, the guest-worker programme and the Bread March; the Senate sets official pay; halls under a Realm enabling law. A second fresh-context review, of the fourth revision, was applied: Hawaii's 1856 abolition raised into its own §8 paragraph instead of a subordinate clause; the senators' age limit given one origin (the Constitution for appointed senators, extended by the regency to all); the Carquinez men introduced; the Monterey buildings and the Presidio made unambiguous; the College's two sides shown as free choice after rooms assigned by lot, with crossings; reason clauses cut and the inspectors given their own paragraph; a county's celebration added; the congress's motive reworded; halls "opened", not chartered, to avoid confusion with chartered county status.
- **Existing canon kept or changed:**
  - §21: the Senate's life appointments become appointments held to 68; the Truce holders' life offices (§22) and the Office's twelve-year heads stay.
  - §8: the Confederation's ratification in 1856; Hawaii's abolition of penal contracts is new there.
  - §24: the Crown does not touch the estates' charters or appointments. The retirement rule is in the charters themselves, and the inspectors check the estates only through their published returns.
  - §25: Victoria's honest numbers and the county standards; the 1890 guest-worker programme with its employer levy and residence test.
  - §28: Soberanes retires in 1915.
  - §17: the Seat of Government Act moves the offices from Monterey in 1885; the Court of the Realm stays there (§21).
- **Dependencies:** CR-037 (no cliques) sets the rules; CR-038 gives them machinery. Adopt them together or CR-037 first.
- **Character documents:** Soberanes (F1) and Victoria (F3, F4, F13) gain a line each on adoption.
- **Historical background:** on adoption, add the Chinese Communist Party's retirement norms, Central Party School, central inspection tours and gamed growth figures; Singapore's scholarships, ministerial pay, People's Association, NTUC and Migrant Workers' Centre; Hawaii's 1850 Masters and Servants Act; and the gender-equality paradox in career choice, with sources.
- **Commitments ledger:** on adoption, add the retirement condition to the chartered-estate entries and check the Senate and Truce life-office entries in `commitments/data/ledger.toml`.
- **Labour canon:** CR-031-R1 (contractors keep recruitment and control; no ban on association) and CR-020 (no strike or labour-charter arc) are kept by F9's cautions.
- **Guest workers:** canon's 1890 programme (§25) is unchanged; F10–F12 give it a past, F9a a safety net. The Hawaiian and works contracts fall inside the workbook's existing arrival streams.
