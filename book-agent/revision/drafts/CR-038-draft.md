# CR-038 (draft) — The machinery's own safeguards: retirement, a common college, inspectors and hard numbers (1887–1890s)

**Status:** PROPOSED draft, 28 October 2026. Not adopted: no storyline, register, character or workbook file is changed by this draft.
**Basis:** author decision of 28 October 2026, after a comparison with the Chinese Communist Party's personnel system under Deng Xiaoping and his successors. Builds on the 1887 charters (§24), the Office of Public Measures (§25) and draft CR-037 (no cliques).

## Author decisions

> Please write them into a short cr-038.

"Them" are four proposals from the comparison: retirement ages for the heads of the estates and senior posts; a common college for mid-career officials; unannounced inspectors; and numbers that are hard to fake.

## Decisions

| ID | Decision | Label |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Retirement at 68.** The 1887 charters retire every estate head and senior officer in the year they turn 68, so each generation leaves on schedule and no clique sits for life. The Crown, the Regent and the life offices of the Truce are outside the rule. Soberanes, born in 1847, retires in 1915 under it. This is the Deng-era fix (the informal rule that officials of 67 may stay and those of 68 go) made into law. | APPROVED (author) |
| F2 | **A common college.** From 1889 the old government buildings at Monterey, empty since the offices moved to Thalassia in 1885, house the Realm College. Rising officials of the Polytechnic, the Office, the navy, the chartered champions and the counties spend a year there together, mixed by origin and language. Their loyalty runs to the Realm and to their year rather than to an estate or a community. The model is the Communist Party's Central Party School. The college is also where the "Carquinez men" learn to recognise one another, a clique of the examined. | APPROVED (author); name, date and course PROPOSED |
| F3 | **Inspectors.** The Office sends inspectors, unannounced, to check the counties' returns on the ground. They report to the Regent and publish. They check the estates only through the returns the estates themselves publish, because the Crown does not touch the estates' appointments or charters (§24). The model is the Party's inspection tours. | APPROVED (author); mechanics PROPOSED |
| F4 | **Numbers that are hard to fake.** Every county return is checked against figures that are hard to falsify: freight tonnage, coal and power used, savings deposits, burials and vaccination records. The lesson comes from Chinese growth targets, which officials gamed. It is Victoria's honest numbers with an instrument. | APPROVED (author) |
| F5 | **What is not copied.** No term limit is lifted for anyone; anti-corruption is never used as a purge; and there is no assimilation by force. These stay temptations for Julian, whose test is OPEN (CR-036). | PROPOSED |

## Proposed storyline text

### §25, after the paragraph ending "…Victoria insists on honest numbers and Pacifica's weak spots appearing alongside its gains." (F1–F4)

> **[PROPOSED — CR-038]** The machinery guards itself. Under the 1887 charters every estate head and senior officer retires in the year they turn sixty-eight, so no one holds a seat for life except the Crown. From 1889 the empty government buildings at Monterey house the Realm College. There rising officers of the Polytechnic, the Office, the navy, the champions and the counties spend a year together, sharing rooms with strangers of other origins and languages. The Office's inspectors arrive in a county without warning. They check its returns against what is hard to fake, freight tonnage, coal and power, deposits and burials, and they publish what they find.

## Impact notes

- **Storyline:** one new paragraph in §25 with a hidden CR-038 coverage comment. No existing sentence changes.
- **Existing canon kept:**
  - §24: the Crown does not touch the estates' charters or appointments. The retirement rule is in the 1887 charters themselves, and the inspectors check the estates only through their published returns.
  - §25: Victoria's honest numbers and the county standards. The inspectors give the standards teeth.
  - §28: Soberanes retires in 1915.
  - §17: the Seat of Government Act moves the offices from Monterey in 1885, which leaves the Monterey buildings empty.
- **Dependencies:** CR-037 (no cliques) sets the rules; CR-038 gives them machinery. Adopt them together or CR-037 first.
- **Character documents:** Soberanes (F1) and Victoria (F3, F4) gain a line each on adoption.
