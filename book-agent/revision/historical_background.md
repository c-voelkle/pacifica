## Japan — 1858 treaty, 1860 naval visit

**Binding fictional chronology:** `Shimoda agreement 1855 → Treaty of Edo 1858 → Kanrin Maru visit March 1860 → guaranteed transition at Edo in 1868 and equal alliance in 1870–1871`.

Historical context:
- Abe Masahiro died in 1857, Shimazu Nariakira in 1858, and the new shogunal leadership made treaty negotiations more politically perilous.
- The 1858 date is the author's explicit correction and overrides conflicting tentative dates in the original `rise_to_power.md` table and earlier planning text.
- The *Kanrin Maru* historically crossed to San Francisco in 1860 with Nakahama Manjirō as interpreter. It is **not** the occasion for a new Treaty of Edo in the fictional timeline; it illustrates and deepens the treaty signed two years earlier.

## Founding date, fiscal and constitutional accuracy

- **31 May 1846 was Pentecost Sunday** in the Gregorian calendar; a fictional Santa Barbara declaration on that date is symbolically coherent, although the real council did not create this republic.
- **31 July 1856**, the fifteenth anniversary of Hawaiian Sovereignty Restoration Day, is the approved founding declaration of the Confederation; the two member legislatures ratify in September and November.
- **November 1858** is Elias and Victoria’s marriage after the Confederation and Edo negotiations (CR-027).
- Elias remains Californian **First Minister and Revenue Superintendent** while becoming confederal Chancellor after the 1856 ratification. He acquires the temporary title **Protector** only after Article XIV's first invocation in 1861.
- **Washoe 1860** is a coalition victory: Victoria persuades Hawaiian institutions to withhold state assent. **1864–1865** supplies the working dissolution/election and enabling settlement, adopted through amendment majorities and member ratifications. Lot retains the Kuhina Nui; confederal guarantees concern state institutions rather than a personal countersignature.
- Model values in the uploaded document (population `1.2m`, annual revenue `$24m`, wartime army `180k`, fleet `90` ships) are not historical reference facts. The feasibility audit and planned war_base_assumptions.md remain pending; these figures have not been validated.

## High-impact unresolved mechanics

1. **1846**: Why Sloat extends his hesitation beyond the historical July 7 occupation, what Frémont's irregulars do, how Pico and Castro are reconciled, and why the crisis ends without American annexation.
2. **Debt**: Which lender committee receives a California debt assumption, who authorizes Mexico's recognition, and how early payments are funded before the rush.
3. **Revenue**: Claims register, exact royalty assessment, rights on grants and tribal lands, bullion assay, borrowing, and treasury legitimacy.
4. **Hawaii:** CR-027 resolves Victoria’s identity, retained Kuhina Nui, 1866 survival, 1872 accession and the lease/ordinances distinction. The exact 1874–1876 instruments, judgment wording, Kiyoshi disclosure and scene-level factions retain their OPEN/PROPOSED labels.
5. **Family chronology**: CR-022 and CR-027 settle earlier relationships: Silas is born in 1851 to Abigail Merritt and Kiyoshi in 1859 to a Satsuma mother. They precede the lost child’s later-1870s death; that is no longer an unresolved chronological choice. Victoria’s discovery of Kiyoshi and the lost child’s precise dates remain open.
6. **Japan**: How the Californian treaty supersedes the historical Perry mission, how Japanese domain and court politics remain plausible, and how currency reforms interact with treaty terms.
7. **Institutional dates**: The 1852 revenue office should not be described as the sovereign or first Providentor's presidency; later titles depend on the American wars.

## CR-037 — keeping institutions open (28 October 2026)

- **Anonymous and recopied scripts.** From the Song dynasty, China's examinations covered candidates' names and had clerks recopy scripts so that examiners could not recognise a hand; Pacifica adopts both from the late 1870s.
- **The law of avoidance.** Qing officials could not serve in their home provinces or examine their kin; the Chinese Communist Party still rotates officials away from home ([Christian Science Monitor, 1990](https://www.csmonitor.com/1990/1114/ochin.html)). Canon already applies the principle to Commissioners (1887) and the Philippine Gobernadores.
- **The passed-over test.** The two-thirds threshold is the examination state's counterpart of the "four-fifths" rule that U.S. federal agencies adopted in 1978 for adverse impact in hiring; it is a measure of exclusion, not a quota.
- **Divergence.** No historical state combined these rules with published appointment tables; the 1890 audit and the county rule are fictional.

## CR-039 — loose ends in the commitments (28 October 2026)

- **Japanese place names.** From the transfer onward, canon uses the names Japan itself used. Urajio (浦塩, short for 浦塩斯徳 Urajiosutoku) was the established Japanese name for Vladivostok before 1945. Karafuto (樺太) was Japan's name for Sakhalin and for its southern territory from 1905 to 1945. - **Treaties ended by war.** In nineteenth-century practice war between the parties ended most political and commercial treaties between them, and the peace treaty said which ones revived. - **The Gulf and the canal.** Canon's ranges for the Gulf Republic (1899–1900) and the canal's opening (around 1900–1902) meet only in 1900. Historically Colombia's Thousand Days' War ran from 1899 to 1902.

Amanzholova, "The movement for Siberian regional and national autonomy in the early 1920s", Sibirica 3:1, 2003, pp. 89–91). Alash militia 13,500 planned, about 9,500 in September 1919 ([Alash militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alash_militia)); Bukeikhanov's February 1919 proposal to Omsk for Alash cavalry on the Cossack model was refused; the Turkestan Front RVS amnesty of 4 November 1919; Western Alash's assembly of 10 December and its capture of the Iletsk Corps headquarters at Kyzyl-Kuga on 27 December 1919; the Kirghiz MRC's liquidation order of 9 March 1920; Kazakh ASSR 26 August 1920 (Amanzholova 1997, "Defeat", pp. 49–83). **Divergences:** the tri-partite treaty (signed in the storyline at Omsk on 4 August and Samara on 6 August 1918, after the commission's sittings), the restored recognition clause, the Emba concession (signed by the Western Alash Orda, whose ground is the Uralsk oblast) and the Tomsk vote of 1921 are fictional; the storyline's Japanese line-guard battalions as far as Omsk and the Altai branch are fictional (historically Japan's troops stayed east of Baikal); the historical Alash–Siberia quarrel over north-eastern Kazakhstan continued between the Siberian and Kirghiz revolutionary committees in 1920 (ibid., p. 75).

Shilovsky's work on Alash Orda and Siberian regionalism; S. Sabol, Russian Colonization and the Genesis of Kazak National Consciousness, 2003, ch. 8).

## CR-043 — the peace of 1919 (31 October 2026)

- **Special mediation.** The 1907 Hague Convention for the Pacific Settlement of International Disputes treats good offices and mediation in Articles 2–8. Article 8 recommends "special mediation": each side chooses a power, the two mediating powers deal directly, the parties stop direct communication on the dispute for the mandate (thirty days unless stipulated otherwise), and "in case of a definite rupture of pacific relations, these Powers are charged with the joint task of taking advantage of any opportunity to restore peace" (wording verified by the author, 30 October 2026; [Convention text, Avalon Project](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/pacific.asp)). **Divergence:** first used to end a general war, with Pacifica a belligerent at sea and the Union neutral (CR-043 P2).
- **William Jennings Bryan.** Secretary of State 1913–1915. He signed some thirty "cooling-off" conciliation treaties in 1913–14; in August 1914 he advised Wilson against loans to belligerents, money being "the worst of all contrabands"; he resigned on 9 June 1915 rather than sign the second Lusitania note ([Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/departmenthistory/people/bryan-william-jennings)). Died 26 July 1925. **Divergence:** the Union's mediator at The Hague.
- **The end of the war.** The Salonika offensive broke the Bulgarian front in September 1918 and Bulgaria signed an armistice at Salonika on 29 September; the Ottoman armistice was signed at Mudros on 30 October 1918 aboard HMS *Agamemnon*, negotiated by Britain alone. **Divergence:** the 11 November armistice is signed on the line Germany then holds, inside France and Belgium (CR-043 P5).
- **The Peace Palace and Carnegie.** The Peace Palace at The Hague opened in 1913, built with Andrew Carnegie's gift; Carnegie died on 11 August 1919.
- **Strasbourg, November 1918.** A soldiers' and workers' council took power in Strasbourg on 10–11 November 1918 and a Republic of Alsace-Lorraine was proclaimed; it ended when French troops entered the city on 22 November ([Alsace-Lorraine Soviet Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace-Lorraine_Soviet_Republic); **VERIFY** the dates against a printed source). **Divergence:** the republic survives because the armistice line keeps the French army out, and becomes neutral in union with Switzerland (CR-043 P7).
- **Austria-Hungary.** Emperor Karl's manifesto of 16 October 1918 proposed turning the Austrian half of the monarchy into a federation of national states. The Fundamental Articles of 1871 (the Hohenwart ministry) would have given the lands of the Bohemian crown a status like Hungary's; they were withdrawn in October 1871. **Divergence:** the peace binds Karl to his manifesto, and the Bohemian lands become a federal kingdom (CR-043 P8, detail PROPOSED).
- **The Czechoslovak army in France.** A French decree of December 1917 created an autonomous Czechoslovak army under French command, of which the Legion in Russia was formally part (**VERIFY** the decree's date, 16 or 19 December 1917); France recognised the Czechoslovak National Council as the supreme organ of the nation in June 1918 (**VERIFY** the day). **Divergence:** France pays the Legion in francs from the Entente credit Pacifica raised (CR-043 P9), and withdraws its recognition under the Bohemian settlement.
- **Kocher.** Theodor Kocher of Bern, Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 1909 for his work on the thyroid; by the end of his career he had performed some five thousand thyroid operations at a mortality near half a per cent (**VERIFY** the figures). Staged operations (ligation of the thyroid arteries before partial resection) were the practice of the period for toxic goitre.
- **Kaʻiulani.** Princess Kaʻiulani (1875–1899), schooled in England from 1889; caught a chill riding in the rain on the island of Hawaiʻi in late 1898 or January 1899 and died on 6 March 1899; the illness is recorded as inflammatory rheumatism, and exophthalmic goitre is also reported. **Divergence:** Kocher's operation in 1897 and her survival (CR-043 P1).
- **Germany's crown.** Wilhelm II's grandson Wilhelm, eldest son of Crown Prince Wilhelm, was born on 4 July 1906. **Divergence:** Wilhelm II abdicates for him under a regency (CR-043 P12; the regent OPEN).

## CR-044 — Omsk, the hostages and the Russian war's loose ends (31 October 2026)

- **The Omsk coup, 17–18 November 1918.** On the night of 17 November, at a city banquet in honour of General Janin, officers demanded "God Save the Tsar"; the Directory ordered the singers arrested, and Colonel Volkov arrested the Directory's left wing before he could be arrested himself ([Kolchak Coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolchak_Coup)). Janin reached Omsk only in December 1918 (**VERIFY** the date). Ivan Mikhailov, the finance minister, was the coup's political organiser and patron, not present at the arrests (Budberg, Syromyatnikov via Ilyin, Parfenov via Vologodsky; Gins dissents); see the project dossier on Mikhailov and M. M. Stelmak and D. I. Petin, *Istoricheskii vestnik* XLII (2022), [PDF](https://historicalreporter.ru/upload/iblock/698/2qnwfc4sw5xa6n8jjl4qixrj371b50aq.pdf). **Divergence:** the coup fails by morning (CR-041 N2).
- **The British at Omsk.** Major-General Alfred Knox headed the British military mission; Colonel John Ward MP commanded the 25th Middlesex, whose memoir is *With the "Die-Hards" in Siberia* (1920). How far the British were party to the coup is debated: Lt-Col. Syromyatnikov's letter of April 1919 says Colonel Neilson and Captain Steveni assured him of Allied neutrality if the coup was bloodless; R. G. Gagkuev judges this evidence of contact and neutrality, not of British planning, and finds the "British conspiracy" version largely sourced from the French mission ([Gagkuev, *Istoricheskii vestnik* XLVII, 2024](https://www.historicalreporter.ru/upload/iblock/686/m6xfk0pcnun8vch65sv8mn9n4qs70ata.pdf)). **Divergence:** Knox's foreknowledge of several days, Ward's battalion standing by, and Volkov's papers documenting both are PROPOSED canon (CR-044 C3).
- **Cromie.** Captain Francis Cromie, British naval attaché, was killed resisting Cheka men at the British embassy in Petrograd on 31 August 1918. His burial place is **VERIFY** (often given as the Smolensky Lutheran cemetery). **Divergence:** the Pacifican advisers are shot in the same week, and the Pacifican and British dead are buried together after Petrograd falls (CR-044 B2).
- **Stalin and Krasnaya Gorka.** In June 1919 Stalin directed the recapture of the Krasnaya Gorka fort, mutinous for the Whites, from the sea, and reported to Lenin that he deplored the naval science that called it impossible. **Divergence:** he goes south to Georgia when Moscow falls; the Democratic Republic of Georgia (1918–1921, Menshevik-led) does not give him up (CR-044 B6).
- **Dzerzhinsky.** Felix Dzerzhinsky founded and led the Cheka from December 1917. **Divergence:** he is the commissar on Kronstadt in May 1920 and dies by Kiyoshi's cut (CR-044 B6).
- **The Dowager Empress and the British crown.** Maria Feodorovna was the sister of Queen Alexandra; in 1917 George V withdrew the offer of asylum to Nicholas II's family. **Divergence:** Victoria meets the British royal party at the Romanov burial (CR-044 B4).
- **Lenin's birthday.** Lenin was born on 22 April 1870 (New Style). **Divergence:** taken at the Kotelnich bridge on his fiftieth birthday (CR-044 B1).
- **Patton.** George S. Patton was born at San Gabriel, California, on 11 November 1885; grandson of Col. George S. Patton of the 22nd Virginia, mortally wounded at Third Winchester (1864), and of Benjamin Davis Wilson, mayor of Los Angeles in 1851; fifth in the modern pentathlon at the 1912 Olympics; designer of the M1913 cavalry sabre; in May 1916 he led a raid in three cars at San Miguelito that killed Julio Cárdenas. **Divergence:** a Pacifican cavalry officer who commands *Pele* at Kotelnich (CR-044 C5); the 1916 raid OPEN.
- **The Gannenmono.** The first group of Japanese emigrants reached Hawaiʻi in 1868, the first year of Meiji. **Divergence:** Nagai Shōzō, an invented grandson, is a Polytechnic engineer (CR-044 C5).
- **Pele.** The Hawaiian goddess of fire and volcanoes, who in the chants travels from Kahiki across the sea to Hawaiʻi. **Divergence:** the name of the Pacifican armoured train (CR-044 A1, superseding *Māui* in CR-041 N10).

## CR-045 — Omsk in depth: Mikhailov, the paymaster and Maria (31 October 2026)

- **Ivan Adrianovich Mikhailov.** Finance minister of the Provisional Siberian Government from 1918 at twenty-six; called Vanka-Kain, Ivan Intriganovich and the Siberian Machiavelli by his enemies; the political organiser and patron of the 18 November coup, absent from the arrests and late to the morning Council. In Harbin he headed the Chinese Eastern Railway's economic bureau and worked on *Zarya*, *Kharbinskoe vremya* and the *Manchurian Economic Review*; deported to the USSR in 1945 and shot on 30 August 1946. Birth date **VERIFY**. Sources: the diaries of Vologodsky, Ilyin and Budberg; Serebrennikov, *Velikii otkhod* and his memoirs, ch. III; Krol, *Za tri goda*; Gins, *Sibir', soyuzniki i Kolchak* (istmat.org/node/25476); Stelmak & Petin; Zhuravlev 2011. Full dossier in the project's `Mikhailov-dossier.md`. **Divergence:** he sells the coup to Pacifica on 17 November 1918 and is shot at Harbin by Volkov in January 1928 (CR-045).
- **The September crisis of 1918.** A closed session of the Siberian Regional Duma on 18 September resolved to bring A. E. Novoselov into the government; on the night of 20–21 September Colonel V. I. Volkov arrested Krutovsky, Shatilov, Yakushev and Novoselov; Novoselov, who would not sign, was shot by his escort in the Zagorodnaya grove on 22–23 September; on 24 September Czech counter-intelligence (Colonel Zaichek) tried to arrest Mikhailov, who hid in Annenkov's barracks; Argunov's investigation found nothing to charge him with, and Volkov swore he had no accomplices (Serebrennikov, pp. 396–401; Gins; Zhuravlev 2011). **Divergence:** the Czech search is called off on Pacifican orders (CR-045 M3).
- **After the night.** Volkov, Krasilnikov and Katanaev were acquitted by an emergency court on 21 November 1918; Volkov was promoted major-general and sent to Chita. His later fate **VERIFY**. **Divergence:** the coup fails, and Volkov is cashiered (CR-045).
- **Grishin-Almazov and Maria.** General A. N. Grishin-Almazov, first commander of the Siberian Army, was dismissed in September 1918 and went south to Denikin; on 5 May 1919, crossing the Caspian with Denikin's letters to Kolchak aboard the *Leila*, he was taken by the Red destroyer *Karl Liebknecht* and shot himself. Maria Alexandrovna Grishina-Almazova (1891–1976), a former Khabarovsk café singer, kept a monarchist salon at Omsk and was Mikhailov's mistress; her diary of 17 January 1919 records a fortune-teller's prophecy; she refused Mikhailov's proposal at Irkutsk on 3 January 1920 on her way to Japan, was imprisoned by the Political Centre and the Cheka for about eleven months, married Mikhailov at Harbin in 1921 (son Georgy, born 25 October 1921, later at Waseda), and died in San Francisco in 1976 (Petin, *Noveishaya istoriya Rossii* 9:2, 2019; *Radio Svoboda*). **Divergence:** the steamer that takes the *Leila* is out of Astrakhan; she refuses Mikhailov, goes to Japan and marries Commander Kashiwagi (CR-045 G2–G5).
- **The Alaskan Kashevarovs.** A. F. Kashevarov (1809–1870), a Creole navigator of the Russian-American Company, surveyed the Arctic coast of Alaska in 1838 and rose to general's rank in Russian service (**VERIFY** dates and rank); the Kashevaroff family remained at Kodiak and Sitka. **Divergence:** the paymaster Pavel Kashevarov is invented (CR-045 M1).

## CR-046 — The Petrograd account (31 October 2026)

- **Rasputin's murder.** Rasputin was killed at the Yusupov palace on the Moika on the night of 16–17 December 1916 (Old Style). Andrew Cook (*To Kill Rasputin*, 2005) argued that Oswald Rayner of the British intelligence station fired the last shot; Douglas Smith (*Rasputin*, 2016) found no trace of British involvement in the service's files, and Samuel Hoare, head of British intelligence in Russia 1916–17, held that Russian rightists tried to frame the British ([Oswald Rayner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Rayner)). **Divergence:** a British payment for "the December affair" (CR-046 P1).
- **The palace-coup plot.** Guchkov, with General Krymov and others, planned in the winter of 1916–17 to force Nicholas II to abdicate in March 1917; the February Revolution overtook them. British fear of a separate peace and the embassy's contacts with the Duma opposition are documented; British money in the plot is invented. **Divergence:** CR-046 P1.
- **The banks.** The Bolsheviks nationalised the banks in December 1917 and seized their records.
- **Copenhagen, 1920.** On 12 February 1920 James O'Grady and Maxim Litvinov signed the Anglo-Soviet agreement for the exchange of prisoners at Copenhagen ([UK treaty record](https://api.parliament.uk/uk-treaties/treaties/6792); *Historical Journal*, "Lloyd George and the Copenhagen Conference of 1919–1920"). **Divergence:** gold for the Cheka's British file (CR-046 P1).
- **Steveni.** Leo Steveni (1893–1972) joined the British military mission in Petrograd in 1915 and was Knox's liaison officer at the White Army's Omsk headquarters from November 1918 to November 1919 ([King's College London, Liddell Hart Centre](https://archives.kingscollections.org/index.php/steveni-leo-1893-1972-colonel)).
- **Hoare and Churchill.** Hoare was Secretary of State for Air 1922–24 and 1924–29; Churchill was Secretary of State for War 1919–21 and Chancellor of the Exchequer 1924–29.
- **Kamo.** Simon Ter-Petrosian, "Kamo" (1882–1922), led the Tiflis bank robbery of 1907, was arrested in Berlin later that year and died in Tiflis on 14 July 1922 when a lorry struck his bicycle. **Divergence:** Stalin's courier to Ankara in 1921 (CR-046 P6).
- **1927 and 1928.** The Metropolitan Police raided the Soviet trade organisation Arcos in London on 12 May 1927, and Britain broke relations with Moscow that month; Japanese officers of the Kwantung Army killed Zhang Zuolin on 4 June 1928. **Divergence:** the raid on Pacifica's Moorgate agency (CR-046 P7); Stalin killed in Tiflis in 1921 (P6).

## CR-047 — The three districts (31 October 2026)

- **The three districts.** Kars, Ardahan and Batum (the Ottoman *elviye-i selâse*) passed to Russia in 1878. Brest-Litovsk (March 1918) returned them subject to a vote, which the Ottomans held under occupation in July 1918; more than 100,000 Armenians were expelled from the Kars province. The National Pact of 1920 claimed the districts on the 1918 vote and accepted a new plebiscite if needed.
- **1919–1921.** The South-West Caucasus Republic governed Kars until Armenian and British forces took it in April 1919; Georgia held Ardahan; Britain held Batum until July 1920. Karabekir took Kars on 30 October 1920. The treaties of Alexandropol (3 December 1920), Moscow (16 March 1921) and Kars (13 October 1921) gave Kars, Ardahan and Surmalu (Iğdır and Ararat, formerly of the Erivan province) to Turkey, and Batum to Georgia with autonomy for its Ajarian Muslims and free transit for Turkish goods.
- **The people.** 1897 census, Kars oblast (290,654), by mother tongue: Kars okrug 134,142 (Armenian 34.8%, Karapapakh 16.4%, Russian 12.6%, Greek 11.0%, Turkish 7.9%, Kurdish 6.8%); Kağızman 59,230 (Armenian 36.6%, Kurdish 29.9%, Greek 12.2%, Turkish 8.7%); Olti 31,519 (Turkish 62.6%, Kurdish 11.1%, Armenian 9.9%); Ardahan 65,763 (Turkish 42.7%, Kurdish 19.1%, Karapapakh 12.0%, Greek 11.9%) ([Kars oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kars_oblast) and the okrug articles). The 1916 *Kavkazskii kalendar'* figures by nationality show the Armenian share rising in Kars and Kağızman; their "Roma" category looks like a misreading in the secondary tables. The Upper Silesia plebiscite of 20 March 1921 let natives who had moved away vote.
- **Divergence:** no Turkish–Armenian war in 1920; a Pacifican-supervised vote by district on the 1914 rolls; Kars and Kağızman to Armenia, Olti and Ardahan to Turkey; Ararat Armenian; Batum a Georgian free port without a garrison (CR-047).

## CR-048 — the rump Confederacy and the long emergency (31 October 2026)

Census figures for the five states are recalled from the Census Bureau's compilation (Gibson & Jung, *Historical Census Statistics on Population Totals by Race, 1790 to 1990*, Working Paper 56, 2002) and remain **VERIFY**; Georgia's totals for 1910–1930 were spot-checked. The population model's closure and departures are in `population/neighbours.py` and `population/outputs/neighbours.md`.

- **The five states in 1900.** South Carolina 1,340,316 (58.4% Black), Georgia 2,216,331 (46.7%), Alabama 1,828,697 (45.2%), Mississippi 1,551,270 (58.5%), Florida 528,542 (43.7%): together 7,465,156, 50.7% Black. **Divergence:** about 7.1 million and 48% Black after a generation of departures under the Freedom Act; about 9.15 million and 29% Black in 1932 against 10.97 million and 39% historically (MODEL, CR-048 R1).
- **Cotton.** The boll weevil crossed the Mississippi about 1907, reached Alabama about 1909, Georgia in 1915 and South Carolina in 1917; cotton prices collapsed in 1920. Alabama leased convicts to the Birmingham mines until 1928. Mississippi's Parchman farm in Sunflower County opened in 1901.
- **Dividing a state's debt.** Gran Colombia's debt was split by population in 1834 (New Granada 50%, Venezuela 28.5%, Ecuador 21.5%); the treaties of Saint-Germain (1919) and Lausanne (1923) divided Austrian and Ottoman debt by the revenue each territory had yielded. **Divergence:** the Protocol of Richmond of 1898 uses the security and the beneficiary as its rules (CR-048 R2).
- **Readmission.** Congress readmitted Virginia in January 1870 on fundamental conditions, among them that its constitution never deprive any citizen or class of citizens of the vote except for crime. Section 4 of the Fourteenth Amendment repudiated Confederate debt and compensation for emancipation. North Carolina's suffrage amendment of 1900 and Virginia's convention of 1901–1902 disfranchised most Black voters. George Henry White, the last Black congressman of the period, left North Carolina's Second District in 1901. White supremacists overthrew Wilmington's elected government on 10 November 1898. **Divergence:** Virginia readmitted in 1898 and North Carolina in 1899 on such conditions, with ten years of federal supervisors in North Carolina; White holds his seat until 1910 (CR-048 R9).
- **Harpers Ferry.** John Brown raided the arsenal on 16 October 1859 with money from six Northern backers; Virginia hanged him on 2 December 1859. **Divergence:** the Parchman raid of 16 October 1910 under the invented Josiah Haynes (CR-048 R14).
- **Universities and oaths.** Columbia dismissed Cattell and Dana over the war in 1917. Howard University has received an annual federal appropriation since 1879. **Divergence:** the Parchman Act of 1911 attaches oaths to the emulation programme's grants (CR-048 R10).
- **Du Bois and Washington.** Du Bois taught at Atlanta University from 1897 to 1910 and directed the Atlanta Studies; his data charts were shown at the Paris Exposition of 1900; he founded the Niagara Movement at Fort Erie in 1905, helped found the NAACP in 1909 and edited *The Crisis* from November 1910. Booker T. Washington spoke at Atlanta in 1895 and led Tuskegee until his death in 1915; Carnegie gave Tuskegee $600,000 in 1903; Rosenwald's school programme began in 1912. Franz Boas gave Atlanta University's commencement address in 1906.
- **Ernest Everett Just.** Born in Charleston on 14 August 1883; at Howard from 1907; first Spingarn Medal 1915; doctorate at Chicago 1916; worked summers at Woods Hole and, denied a research post in the United States, in Naples, Berlin and Paris from 1929. **Divergence:** Carquinez fellow from 1920 and director of the Moku o Loʻe station from 1922 (CR-048 R11). The station sits on the islet that became the Hawaii Institute of Marine Biology in our history.
- **Violence.** Atlanta (22–24 September 1906), stoked by Hoke Smith's campaign; Black residents of Brownsville armed and defended their district. Springfield, Illinois (August 1908); the second Klan refounded on Stone Mountain (1915); *The Birth of a Nation* (1915); East St. Louis (July 1917); Chicago (July 1919); Tulsa (31 May–1 June 1921); the Greenville levee camp in the 1927 flood. The Stone Mountain carving began in 1923.
- **Marcus Garvey.** Born at St. Ann's Bay, Jamaica, in 1887; timekeeper for United Fruit in Costa Rica and editor in Colón, 1910–1911; in London 1912–1914; founded the UNIA in 1914 and came to Harlem in 1916; the *Negro World* from 1918, the Black Star Line from 1919, the Madison Square Garden convention of August 1920, the Liberia programme of 1920–1924. He met Edward Young Clarke, the Klan's acting Imperial Wizard, in Atlanta in June 1922. Convicted of mail fraud in June 1923, imprisoned in the Atlanta federal penitentiary from 1925, his sentence commuted in November 1927, deported to Jamaica in December 1927. British colonies banned the *Negro World*; British and American officials exchanged intelligence on him. The UNIA had more divisions in the rural South than in the North (Rolinson, *Grassroots Garveyism*, 2007). Bilbo's Greater Liberia bill of 1939 drew Garveyite support. **Divergence:** Atlanta is in the rump, so Garvey serves at Leavenworth; Pacifica refuses him ships and a Honolulu lecture (CR-048 R17).
- **Spindletop.** Pattillo Higgins's Gladys City Oil, Gas and Manufacturing Company (1892) failed to drill through the salt dome's quicksand; Anthony Lucas took over in 1899 and, with the Hamill brothers' rotary rig, brought in the Lucas gusher on 10 January 1901. **Divergence:** with oil in greater demand, Higgins's company keeps drilling and, with a Pacifican crew and rotary rig, brings it in in January 1897 (CR-048 R19).

### CR-049 atomic branch, reworked (approved 2 November 2026)

Anchors for requirements 1–5 of the rework.

- **Joachimsthal.** Adolf Patera's process for uranium compounds from pitchblende went into production at a new works at Joachimsthal in 1853, making uranium yellows for glass; its radium-rich residues were what the Curies later worked ([Adolf Patera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Patera); [ORAU, Jáchymov](https://orau.org/health-physics-museum/articles/jachymov-cradle-of-the-atomic-age.html)). **Divergence:** Carquinez buys the residues from the 1860s.

- **Cathode rays and the electron.** Plücker deflected the discharge glow with a magnet in 1858; the Sprengel pump dates from 1865; Hertz failed to deflect cathode rays electrically in 1883; J. J. Thomson measured their charge-to-mass ratio in 1897. Eugen Goldstein (born Gleiwitz, 1850, to a Jewish family; a student of Helmholtz) named cathode rays in 1876, worked at the Berlin Observatory from 1878 to 1890 and then at Potsdam, and found canal rays in 1886 ([Eugen Goldstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugen_Goldstein)). **Divergence:** Goldstein at Carquinez from 1876; the electron 1879 (CR-049 S1).
- **Helium.** Found in the solar spectrum in 1868. William Francis Hillebrand (born Honolulu, 12 December 1853; doctorate at Heidelberg 1875; Leadville assay office 1879; Geological Survey laboratory at Denver from 1880) took a gas from uraninite for nitrogen; Ramsay found helium in such minerals in 1895 ([W. F. Hillebrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Francis_Hillebrand)). **Divergence:** Hillebrand Pacifican by birth; helium identified in 1881 with Morley.
- **Scintillation and the nucleus.** Théodore Sidot prepared phosphorescent zinc sulfide in 1866 ([Théodore Sidot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%A9odore_Sidot)); Crookes's spinthariscope (1903) showed single alpha flashes on it ([ORAU, spinthariscopes](https://www.orau.org/health-physics-museum/collection/spinthariscopes/index.html)); Geiger and Marsden counted scattered alpha particles by eye in 1909; Rutherford's nuclear atom followed in 1911. **Divergence:** 1883 (CR-049 S1).
- **Isotopes.** Soddy's chemically inseparable radioelements and Thomson's positive-ray masses (1913); Richards's atomic weight of lead from uranium ores (1914). **Divergence:** 1884–1886.

- **Atomic weights and counting.** Morley's determination of the oxygen–hydrogen ratio was published in 1895; Rutherford and Geiger's gas-multiplying counter for single alpha particles dates from 1908. **Divergence:** Morley's ratio 1884–1885; a counter at Ralston College 1885–1887.
- **The neutron.** Bothe and Becker (1930) and Irène Curie and Frédéric Joliot (1932) produced a penetrating radiation from beryllium under polonium alpha particles and took it for gamma rays; Chadwick showed in 1932, from proton and nitrogen recoils, that it was a neutral particle of about the proton's mass ([APS News](https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2007/05/may-1932-chadwick-discovery-neutron)). Nagaoka Hantarō (born 1865) graduated in physics at Tokyo in 1887, studied in Berlin, Munich and Vienna from 1893 and proposed his Saturnian atom in 1904 ([Hantaro Nagaoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hantaro_Nagaoka)). **Divergence:** neutron 1899, confirmed 1901; Nagaoka in Pacifica from 1887.
- **Fission.** Hahn and Strassmann found barium among the products of neutron-irradiated uranium in December 1938; Meitner and Frisch interpreted it as splitting, and Frisch measured the large ionisation pulses, in early 1939; neutron multiplication was measured later in 1939; the first controlled pile went critical at Chicago on 2 December 1942. **Divergence (re-paced):** fission 1908, neutron multiplication 1909, a research installation c. 1913 (CR-049 S2).
- **Western Slope uranium.** Tom Talbert found yellow uranium-vanadium ore on Roc Creek in 1881; carnotite was found in western Montrose County in 1897–1898 (named by Friedel and Cumenge in 1899); ten tons of ore of about 20% uranium oxide were shipped from the Copper Prince claim in 1898; Standard Chemical began the Colorado radium boom at Paradox Valley in 1910 ([Timeline of mining in Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_mining_in_Colorado)). Under the 1865 line the Western Slope is Pacifican and Leadville is Union (master storyline). **Divergence:** Western Slope ore assayed 1881–1882 and mined for uranium from 1893.
- **Graphite and uranium metal.** Acheson's electric-furnace graphite dates from 1896; Moissan made uranium metal in the electric furnace in the 1890s. **Divergence:** Tesla's furnaces in the 1890s.

## CR-050 — the sealed envelope and the walled college (3 November 2026)
