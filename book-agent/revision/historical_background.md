## High-impact unresolved mechanics

1. **1846**: Why Sloat extends his hesitation beyond the historical July 7 occupation, what Frémont's irregulars do, how Pico and Castro are reconciled, and why the crisis ends without American annexation.
2. **Debt**: Which lender committee receives a California debt assumption, who authorizes Mexico's recognition, and how early payments are funded before the rush.
3. **Revenue**: Claims register, exact royalty assessment, rights on grants and tribal lands, bullion assay, borrowing, and treasury legitimacy.
4. **Hawaii**: The 1857 office of Kaiulani relative to the historical Victoria Kamāmalu, the specific 1874 candidate and factional coalition, and the reasons annexation was enacted without a breach of naval lease by the monarchy.
5. **Family chronology**: If affairs and other children follow the legitimate child's death in the later 1870s, adult claimants in 1887 cannot simultaneously have been born before 1860 *without earlier relationships*. Either an earlier affair, altered ages, altered lost-child date, or a different succession faction must be explicitly decided by the author.
6. **Japan**: How the Californian treaty supersedes the historical Perry mission, how Japanese domain and court politics remain plausible, and how currency reforms interact with treaty terms.
7. **Institutional dates**: The 1852 revenue office should not be described as the sovereign or first Providentor's presidency; later titles depend on the American wars.
