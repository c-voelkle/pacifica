## Japan — 1858 treaty, 1860 naval visit

**Binding fictional chronology:** `Shimoda agreement 1855 → Treaty of Edo 1858 → Kanrin Maru visit March 1860 → enlarged treaty pressure at Edo in 1868`.

Historical context:
- Abe Masahiro died in 1857, Shimazu Nariakira in 1858, and the new shogunal leadership made treaty negotiations more politically perilous.
- The 1858 date is the author's explicit correction and overrides conflicting tentative dates in the original `rise_to_power.md` table and earlier planning text.
- The *Kanrin Maru* historically crossed to San Francisco in 1860 with Nakahama Manjirō as interpreter. It is **not** the occasion for a new Treaty of Edo in the fictional timeline; it illustrates and deepens the treaty signed two years earlier.

## Founding date, fiscal and constitutional accuracy

- **31 May 1846 was Pentecost Sunday** in the Gregorian calendar; a fictional Santa Barbara declaration on that date is symbolically coherent, although the real council did not create this republic.
- **31 July 1856**, the fifteenth anniversary of Hawaiian Sovereignty Restoration Day, is the approved founding declaration of the Confederation; the two member legislatures ratify in September and November.
- **1857 February** is the marriage after ratification.
- Elias remains Californian **First Minister and Revenue Superintendent** while becoming confederal Chancellor after the 1856 ratification. He acquires the temporary title **Protector** only after Article XIV's first invocation in 1861.
- **Washoe 1860** demonstrates that Article XIV's Hawaiian countersignature gives Kaiulani a real veto. **1864 abolition of her office** creates a conflict among member constitutional and confederal authorities, and **1865 amendment** makes the wartime office durable through an explicit political/legal process.
- Model values in the uploaded document (population `1.2m`, annual revenue `$24m`, wartime army `180k`, fleet `90` ships) are not historical reference facts. See [war_base_assumptions.md](war_base_assumptions.md) for sources, calculation limitations and feasibility conditions.

## High-impact unresolved mechanics

1. **1846**: Why Sloat extends his hesitation beyond the historical July 7 occupation, what Frémont's irregulars do, how Pico and Castro are reconciled, and why the crisis ends without American annexation.
2. **Debt**: Which lender committee receives a California debt assumption, who authorizes Mexico's recognition, and how early payments are funded before the rush.
3. **Revenue**: Claims register, exact royalty assessment, rights on grants and tribal lands, bullion assay, borrowing, and treasury legitimacy.
4. **Hawaii**: The 1857 office of Kaiulani relative to the historical Victoria Kamāmalu, the specific 1874 candidate and factional coalition, and the reasons annexation was enacted without a breach of naval lease by the monarchy.
5. **Family chronology**: If affairs and other children follow the legitimate child's death in the later 1870s, adult claimants in 1887 cannot simultaneously have been born before 1860 *without earlier relationships*. Either an earlier affair, altered ages, altered lost-child date, or a different succession faction must be explicitly decided by the author.
6. **Japan**: How the Californian treaty supersedes the historical Perry mission, how Japanese domain and court politics remain plausible, and how currency reforms interact with treaty terms.
7. **Institutional dates**: The 1852 revenue office should not be described as the sovereign or first Providentor's presidency; later titles depend on the American wars.

## Confederate values and Western soldier voices

The author's CR-012 directs nuanced Confederate characterization rather than a new campaign outcome. [Confederate character values and source register](confederate_value_schema.md) records the supplied Semmes, Herbert, Phillips, Scrugham and Masters texts alongside Watkins, Jackman, Peticolas, Scurry, Hamner and reproduced Guerrant passages. It specifies the consulted selections, links, dates and evidentiary limits. Letters and diaries convey particular writers' knowledge and concerns; reports address superiors; postwar memoirs and later interpretations have different purposes and cannot establish every soldier's beliefs.

Use the Confederacy's commitment to slavery alongside individual obligations to family, comrades, locality, faith and professional standing. Keep a writer's observation distinct from rumor, later recollection and the novelist's inference. The source guide's constitutional anchors are the Mississippi secession declaration and Confederate Constitution; its fictional applications are not testimony about Davis's or Benjamin's private motives.

The newly mapped characters are CT-DAVIS, CT-BENJAMIN, CT-ST-CLAIR and CT-ARMISTEAD. The original St. Clair sketch's 1847 Golden Circle affiliation, his injury timeline, and Armistead's transition and overseas appearances require reconciliation. Neither a shared name nor a primary-source analogy identifies the fictional Armistead with Lewis Armistead. Historical campaigns after Pacifica's divergence provide comparisons rather than automatic events in the novel.
