# Planning note — Britain and Pacifica, 1918–1932: the trap that is not sprung

**Status:** Planning instrument, non-canonical. Nothing here is APPROVED; the decision list in §6 is what the author must settle before any CR is drafted.
**Written:** 29 October 2026, after the Galkovsky review of `review/cr040-stage3-4` and CR-041.
**Depends on:** CR-040 Stages 3–4 and CR-041 being integrated first (this note is applied *on top* of them, never instead); CR-019 (the peace and the 1923 debt funding), CR-021 rev. 9 and CR-024 (the Concord and the 1927 rescue), CR-039 (Nanjing 1928).
**Labels:** as in CR-040. *hist.* marks a historical anchor.

## 0. Thesis

The Russian war does not only end the Soviet state. It ends the century in which Britain decided what happened in Eurasia, and it does so from inside a coalition Britain thought it led. Between 1918 and 1932 Britain and Pacifica — creditor and debtor, co-guarantors, two navies at the same anchorages — move through the classic sequence of a rising and a ruling power: friction inside an alliance, a humiliation accepted on paper, a cold confrontation by proxy, and a settlement imposed by the balance sheet. The trap is set at Omsk, baited at Baku, armed in a Moscow courtroom, and *not sprung*, because both powers find they are holding the same hostages: each other's debts, Japan, and the truth about 1917.

Two sentences already in canon govern the whole arc and must survive it:

- §28: *"The imperial strategy avoids a British war through the 1932 ending."* (CR-019 note on OPEN-019-05 repeats it: sovereignty over Singapore is refused because it *"would bring back the war with Britain the author has ruled out."*)
- §37: *"Britain is managed through debt."*

So the arc is a Thucydides trap **studied and declined**, not fallen into. Its dramatic question is *how close*, and *what it costs to stay out* — which is the same question the book asks about Japan, Siberia and the Altai. Britain is the fourth place where Julian's method ("a creditor is obeyed longer than a conqueror") is tested against a power that invented the method.

## 2. Historical scaffolding (what Britain actually did, 1917–1927)

So that the confrontation stands on real ground. *hist.* throughout; sources to go into `historical_background.md` at drafting.

- **Siberia and Omsk.** Britain sent the 25th Middlesex (Col. John Ward MP) and the 1/9th Hampshires to Omsk in 1918; Maj-Gen. Alfred Knox headed the British military mission and was Kolchak's principal foreign supporter. The coup of 18 November 1918 was carried out by Siberian Cossack officers; British troops did not act, but Knox and Ward were in Omsk, Ward's battalion guarded the Directory's quarter, and London recognised the result within days. Kolchak had offered himself to the British Admiralty in 1917. *Canon consequence:* in CR-041 the Pacifican paymaster is defeating a government London wants.
- **Baku and the Caucasus.** Dunsterforce (Aug–Sept 1918), the British occupation of Baku Nov 1918–Aug 1919, the Batum garrison to July 1920; British policy explicitly aimed at the oil and at keeping Russia (any Russia) out of the Caucasus. *Canon consequence:* N14 is historically right that Britain earns Baku; it is also right that Britain would never have intended to share it.
- **The Baltic.** Rear-Adm. Walter Cowan's squadron at Reval/Biorko 1919; the CMB raid on Kronstadt (Aug 1919) which sank *Pamiat Azova* and damaged *Andrei Pervozvanny*; the Royal Navy's view of the Baltic as its lake. *Canon consequence:* K1 has a Japanese admiral take Kronstadt in Britain's sea, in Britain's sight, with motor torpedo boats — the weapon of Cowan's own raid. The Admiralty will not forget it.
- **Intervention politics.** Lloyd George wanted out by 1919 (Prinkipo, trade with Moscow, the 1921 trade agreement); Churchill wanted in, loudly, and lost. *Canon consequence:* in a world where intervention *wins* without Britain's leadership, Churchill is the British politician whose life's argument Pacifica vindicated and whose country it humiliated — the ideal ambiguous antagonist for 1924–32 (Chancellor 1924–29; returns Britain to gold 1925).
- **The money trail.** Well-documented: German funding of the Bolsheviks via Parvus (Helphand), the Nya Banken in Stockholm, the Kühlmann memorandum of 3 December 1917 claiming the Bolsheviks could not have held power without German money; the sealed train. Documented but ambiguous: Trotsky's release from Amherst, Nova Scotia, in April 1917 at the Provisional Government's request after British detention; the Lockhart mission (Jan 1918) and Lockhart's own dealings; Sidney Reilly's "Lockhart plot" of August 1918. *Canon consequence:* a court in 1920 would find the German money; it would find the British *file* and not be able to read it; and it would find Pacifica's own March 1918 railway offer and depot stock laid down "at Soviet request." The money trail has three strands, and Julian is on one of them.
- **Anglo-Japanese Alliance (1902, renewed 1905, 1911).** Historically lapsed at Washington in 1921–23 under Dominion and US pressure. *In this world Japan has been bound to Pacifica since the 1887 compact*; canon has not said whether the Anglo-Japanese Alliance ever existed, and it must (A1 below). If it did, its non-renewal in 1917 or 1921 is Britain's first open loss to Pacifica.
- **Debt.** The real Anglo-American funding agreement (Baldwin, January 1923: 62 years at 3–3½%) is the model for canon's 1923 settlement. Britain's return to gold at the pre-war parity in April 1925 (Churchill), the General Strike 1926, the Arcos raid of May 1927 and the break with Moscow, and the departure from gold on 21 September 1931. *Canon consequence:* 1927 — the year of the Concord and of Japan's rescue — is historically the year Britain raided a trading mission and broke relations over subversion. The inversion writes itself (§4, Phase 3).
- **The Bank.** Montagu Norman, Governor of the Bank of England 1920–44: the man who treated central banking as a priesthood and the dollar as a rival religion. He is the antagonist a debt-driven confrontation needs, and he is historically available in exactly these years.
- **Dominions.** Imperial Conference 1921 (Hughes and Massey on the Pacific; Meighen against the Japanese alliance); the Singapore naval base decided 1923, suspended 1924, resumed 1925; the Statute of Westminster 1931. *Canon consequence:* Australia and New Zealand, which canon already gives a Pacifican defence treaty in 1932, are the prize of Phase 4, and Singapore is the hinge.

## 3. The courtroom: how the trap is armed

CR-040 R8 (APPROVED): Lenin and Trotsky tried in Moscow, executed late 1920; the Office publishes the record. The Galkovsky review's one unanswerable objection: *"the evidence exists; that is why there will be no trial."* A real Moscow court in 1920 would be a trial of the Entente's conduct in 1917 as much as of two men. Canon can turn this from a weakness into the arc's turning point without touching the approved outcome.

Proposed shape (all PROPOSED):

1. **The charges stay as approved** — the Terror decree, the Penza telegram, the hostage order of 30 September 1918, the Romanovs. Those are the counts on which they hang, and they are Russian counts.
2. **Trotsky, from the dock, opens the money.** He has every incentive: it dilutes his guilt into Europe's. He names the sealed train and Parvus (true, German, and damaging to the Germany Pacifica has just saved); he names his own release at Halifax in April 1917 and asks who asked for it (true, ambiguous, and damaging to Britain); and he names the Pacifican depot stock of March 1918 laid down "for the transit" and asks whose transit (true, and damaging to Julian).
3. **The court rules the money inadmissible.** Wrangel's government needs recognition from London and Paris, and credit from Thalassia; Krivoshein sees to it. The ruling is public. The Office, under Victoria's rule that it "publishes only what is true, lists and photographs," prints the ruling and *the questions*, not the answers — and this is the first time the rule is used to tell a lie by omission with Victoria's knowledge. It should be written as the hinge of her later judgement on the Altai lie: she has already let one file stay shut.
4. **The sealed file.** Pacifica's investigators (the Polytechnic chemists and Office men who found the Koptyaki grave) have the Cheka's own archive from Moscow. In it is what Moscow knew of British dealings — Lockhart, Reilly, the Halifax exchange — and what it knew of Pacifica's. Julian holds a weapon against London he cannot use without turning it on himself. **This is the whole mechanism of the "trap not sprung": mutual assured embarrassment.** London, through Knox's officers in Omsk and Cowan's at Reval, knows the file exists.
5. **The Chronist** names the thing: "the second archive," kept at Thalassia beside the Donner report and the Altai figures. The book's last section is already called "the last archive"; this gives it a precursor.

## 4. The arc in phases (where it goes in the storyline)

### Phase 0 — Acquaintance (1908–1917), §27–§30
*Existing PROPOSED text; add one or two beats.* Julian's London friends are Liberals; in 1908 Grey is still hoping the Japanese alliance holds Japan in Britain's system, and Julian's compact already holds it in his. Decide A1. One scene-beat: Haldane or Grey asks, in 1908 or 1911, whether the 1887 compact is compatible with the Anglo-Japanese Alliance; Julian says yes and means no.

### Phase 1 — Friction inside the alliance (1918–1920), §31
*Rewrite of connective detail only; every APPROVED beat stands.*
- **Omsk, 18 November 1918 (N2):** add Knox and Ward to the night. Ward's battalion stands to in its quarter; Knox has been told by Volkov's officers what is coming and has not told the Directory. The Pacifican paymaster's announcement is made *to Knox* as much as to the Council of Ministers. Kolchak's refusal of the title is a refusal of a British offer. London protests through its ambassador at Thalassia the next week and is answered with the ledger: who is paying the Siberian army. First confrontation, in a station waiting room.
- **Baku (N14):** make the joint concession visibly a *forced* share. Britain holds Baku from November 1918; Pacifica's claim rests on the credit Wrangel's government owes and on Pacifican tankers being the only ones that can carry the oil out through a Black Sea the Royal Navy controls. The compromise is reached in 1919–20 and both sides book the price for later. ("London's price… collected in 1927" — see Phase 3.)
- **Archangel–Kotlas (CR-041 §2.4 movement 4):** Pacifica's White Sea supply line runs through a British-held port. A line of friction about who controls the Dvina barges; low-key, administrative, exactly the register of Ironside's real complaints.
- **Reval, winter 1919–20 (K1, §3.3):** two squadrons, one anchorage. Cowan's officers watch a Japanese admiral prepare to take Kronstadt — the fortress their own CMBs raided in August 1919. After Kronstadt the Baltic ensign on the surrendered fleet is Russian under a Japanese-brokered surrender, and the Royal Navy was in the roads as a spectator. Admiralty memory; feeds the 1919–22 naval-limits resentment.
- **Cromie and the hostages:** Britain has its own dead on the embassy staircase and its own hostages in Moscow. It wants a say in the trial and is refused it: the court is Russian, the guarantor is Pacifican. Motive for Phase 2.

### Phase 2 — The courtroom and the paper (1920–1923), §32, §30 (the 1923 settlement)
- The trial as in §3 above.
- **The 1923 funding agreement (OPEN-019-05)** becomes the first *use* of the sealed file: not threatened, merely known. Britain funds at reduced interest and gives base rights at Singapore and Hong Kong (not sovereignty — the author's rule). The scene is Baldwin's or Norman's, in Thalassia or San Francisco; a Treasury man says afterwards what the Americans said in 1923: that the debtor has been taught who the creditor is.
- **Decide A7:** Wrangel's federal Russia recognises the Russian debt. Pre-1914 Russian bonds are held in Paris and London; Pacifican savers hold the wartime locomotive credit. *Who is paid first* is the most concrete casus the arc can have, and canon has not asked it. Recommendation: Pacifica's credit is serviced first, by the terms of the 1918 Samara conditions; Paris and London get a funding agreement on Russian paper negotiated *through Thalassia*. France is the loud loser, Britain the quiet one.

### Phase 3 — Cold confrontation by proxy (1924–1927), §33
Britain cannot fight and will not pay; it does what it does — it finds instruments.
- **The true believers.** The Soviet state is dead; the Comintern's men are in Berlin, Vienna and Zurich (decide A9: who survived — Zinoviev, Radek, Bukharin, Kollontai; who was tried in Moscow; who recanted). A defeated Bolshevism is a European religion with martyrs, and its sermon is *Japanese imperialism and a Russian Junker hanged Lenin for Wall Street's eastern cousin.* London does not fund it; London lets it exist where it is useful — in the Dominions' labour movements, in India (where the "Pacific empire" is the new Russian menace), in Germany's Reichstag. One beat only: a British official in Berlin declines to extradite a Comintern figure to federal Russia, "for want of a treaty."
- **The Zinoviev inversion (1924).** Historically a forged letter from Zinoviev to the CPGB brought down Labour. Here: a letter, genuine or not, from a Thalassia address to Australian labour appears in the British press in an election year. Decide A10 whether Pacifica's Office (which "publishes only what is true") is the victim or the author.
- **Japan.** Britain, having lost the Japanese alliance (A1), courts what Pacifica has not captured: the Restoration circles and the home army. Chichibu at Carquinez instead of Oxford (1925–26) is the public sign that the court chose Pacifica; the Restoration officers' "Thalassia bakufu" is the private sign that someone else is listening. Canon's 1931 Manchurian plot and 1932 coup acquire a London shadow — **not** British direction (that would be Galkovsky's thesis, not the book's), but British sympathy, British loans to the national-policy companies, a British naval attaché who is friends with the wrong admirals.
- **1927, the collection.** Three things canon already dates to 1927 become one British stroke: (a) London acceptance houses refuse to roll Japan's short funding (CR-024, MODEL) — not only for commercial reasons; (b) Britain collects "its price" for the Baku share (N14) — recommend: a demand that Pacifica's share of Baku be ceded against the funding arrears Britain claims Pacifica caused; (c) an *Arcos-style* raid: the Metropolitan Police raid the Pacifican Trade Agency in Moorgate on a tip about the sealed file, find nothing, and break off a negotiation. Julian's answer to all three is the Concord's provisional application — illegal at home, decisive abroad. The Concord, which canon frames as the Japanese bargain, is *also* the moment Pacifica stops being a Pacific power London can manage and becomes the one that manages London. Tate's "$300 million to foreign coupon-clippers" should name British coupons.

### Phase 4 — The settlement that is not a war (1928–1932), §33–§37
- **Nanjing 1928:** China recovers the Inspectorate and the tariff under Concord guarantee. Britain's China position of 1842 is liquidated by treaty between two parties neither of which is Britain. The Shanghai Municipal Council and HSBC appear once, protesting.
- **1929–31:** the crash; Norman's Bank fights for sterling; Britain leaves gold in September 1931 — canon's "sterling-priced aluminium undercuts Hilo" becomes a deliberate trade stroke as well as a monetary necessity. Britain's last lever is to export deflation to the Pacific.
- **The Dominions:** Singapore's base, half-built and now partly Pacifica's by 1923 terms, is the hinge. Australia and New Zealand sign the Pacifican defence treaty (1932, OPEN-019-06) over London's objection — Hughes's 1921 fear realised. Canada stays. India is the question nobody in Thalassia wants asked.
- **Thalassia 1932:** Britain "enters the monetary settlement." The scene: Norman or Chamberlain signs a document Pacifica drafted, in the city where the Concord's acts are kept. Someone British says that Britain was never defeated, which is true. The Chronist records that the two archives — London's and Thalassia's — stay sealed by mutual consent, and that *this* is the peace.

## 5. What the arc must not do

1. **Not war.** Approved and stated three times in canon. The arc is cold at its hottest (a police raid, a naval anchorage, a court ruling).
2. **Not Galkovsky's thesis.** Britain is a competent, cornered power with reasons; it does not run the Bolsheviks, the Legion or the Japanese army. Its hand in 1917 is *ambiguous* (Halifax, Lockhart), and the book keeps it ambiguous — the sealed file is sealed from the reader too. The review's lens is spice, not structure.
3. **Not a displacement of Japan.** The Japanese relationship remains the book's central bond; Britain is the external pressure that makes the Concord necessary and Hirohito's 1932 choice meaningful. Every British beat should bear on Japan or Russia, never stand alone.
4. **Not a new Victoria.** Victoria already carries Donner, Puʻuloa, the Siberian promise and the Altai lie. The sealed file gives her one more complicity; it must be written as the same complicity (seeing is believing, and she let a file stay shut), not a fresh one.
5. **Not more than the canon can hold.** Four or five new British names at most (Knox, Cowan, Norman, Churchill, Baldwin); the rest are offices.

## 10. The wedge: Britain and France (proposal)

**The problem.** Both powers are aggrieved at Pacifica, for different reasons. France's grievance is the peace itself: Alsace-Lorraine stays German, Germany pays no indemnity, and the 1919 limits put France's navy *below Germany's*. Britain's is money and the sea. Left alone, they combine against the broker. Canon's ending (§37: "France's grievance, Russian resentment of the lands lost in 1919–1921 and Union revanchists can still find one another") says France must still be aggrieved in 1932. So the wedge buys a decade; it does not make France a friend.

**The constraint.** Every wedge that pushes Britain away from France pushes it toward the Union and its German bloc. The combination Julian must prevent above all is London and New York, which historically took the form of the Norman–Strong central-bank partnership and the American credit that carried sterling back to gold in 1925. The aim is therefore hub and spoke: each Atlantic power tied to Thalassia bilaterally and to the others as little as possible.

**What France wants that Britain will not give and Pacifica can:**

1. **Security against Germany: Russia.** France's natural ally against Germany is Russia (the alliance of 1894). Wrangel's Russia is Pacifica's client. Pacifica can revive the Franco-Russian alliance under its own credit. Britain historically refused France a guarantee; here Pacifica offers a better one at second hand.
2. **The Russian bonds.** About 1.6 million French savers held Imperial Russian bonds. Only Pacifica's Russia can pay them. Settling B7 so that federal Russia resumes service on the French bonds, through Thalassia and after Pacifica's own claims, moves French domestic politics more than any treaty. This is the strongest single wedge.
3. **Gentler debt terms.** France owes both Britain and Pacifica. Pacifica funds the French debt on easier terms, or in kind, while Britain insists on its own claims to pay its debt to Pacifica (the logic of the historical Balfour Note). Britain becomes the hard creditor in French eyes.
4. **The Levant and Turkey.** France historically made a separate peace with Kemal (Franklin-Bouillon, October 1921) and pulled its troops out at Chanak. Pacifica only has to encourage what France will do anyway: broker the Franco-Turkish agreement, support France's Syrian frontier, and give France its share of Mosul oil through a Red Line it helps arrange.
5. **The Mediterranean.** Pacifica's Black Sea convoys refuel at Bizerte and Toulon instead of Malta and Gibraltar. French ports earn the trade, and British leverage over the route falls.
6. **The naval rank (optional, at the 1929 review).** Lift France to parity with Germany. Britain resists, which is itself the wedge.

**What Pacifica must not do.** Offer Alsace. Nobody can deliver it, and holding it out as a carrot through the arbitration court would be a Julian move too cynical even for him, unless the author wants exactly that.

**Where it bites.** 1921 (France's separate deal with Ankara); September 1922 (France leaves Britain alone at Chanak); 1923–24 (Russian bond settlement and the revived alliance); 1925 (Britain's return to gold: Pacifica must supply the credit itself, so that New York does not); 1927 (the Concord and the oil settlement). A Locarno-type guarantee of the Franco-German frontier by Britain and the Union, if one happens, makes Britain the power that signed Alsace away in French eyes.

**New decisions.** W1: revive the Franco-Russian alliance under Pacifican credit? W2: the order of service on Russian debt (B7) with the French bonds second? W3: who carries sterling back to gold in 1925, Pacifica or New York? W4: is there a Locarno, and who guarantees it?
