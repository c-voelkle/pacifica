# Elias's Rise to Power, 1844–1861 — Current planning companion

**Updated:** 18 October 2026 · **Source status:** Derived from the current master, CR-014 and CR-015. The original author-supplied proposal is preserved at [../sources/rise_to_power.md](../sources/rise_to_power.md). This derived companion does not replace or modify that archival source.

Elias arrives in California in 1844 with Caleb, earns standing through Castro's coalition and Providencia, and helps bring competing Californio and English-speaking constituencies into the 1846 republic. Civil allegiance, bilingual public institutions, religious liberty and enforceable rights attract people and capital while leaving contested grants, water and Native authority to real negotiation.

Kaiulani holds the Kuhina Nui from 1855 in an explicit historical divergence. Lot retains it after 1863. State assent follows domestic institutions, not her exclusive personal signature. The office's survival does not itself confer the crown or later imperial regency.

The next stage, in 1864–1865, uses dissolution before a threatened revocation, a timely election and enabling legislation adopted through constitutional amendment and founding-state ratification. The cabinet can legislate beyond specified constitutional provisions, with institutions and a renewable sunset preserved. Elias's appointment and agenda powers concentrate that authority. Providentor belongs to this later ascent, not his 1852 revenue office. Exact legal text, tenure and dates remain open.

CR-015 carries the preparatory consequences forward: religious liberty makes Deseret's 1862–1863 accession credible; Washoe is recovered in 1863–1864; the 1865 Western line is ratified in 1867. Assisted women and family immigration extends inland with Asian partners. The later fugitive agreement and Hawaiian massacre expose the limits of the republic's founding claims.
