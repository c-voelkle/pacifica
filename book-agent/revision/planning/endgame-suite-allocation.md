# The summer of 1932: who decides what in CR-084, CR-085 and CR-086

**Status:** planning note, 23 November 2026. Not canon. It allocates subjects among three unmerged drafts so that they stop deciding the same things twice. CR-083 is left out at the author's direction.

**Authority.** The author delegated these choices: "Please just do what you think makes for the best story and then report back to me" (23 November 2026, 18:18). They are Claude's decisions under that delegation, taken from the six fresh-context reviews on this branch. The author may overrule any line; until a draft carrying a decision is approved, the decision is PROPOSED in that draft.

**Why a note and not three revisions.** The reviews agree on what is wrong, and it is not in any one file: the drafts overload one conference, give Japan more than it could extract and less than it would notice losing, and leave the Realm's own interest thin. Line references are to `master_storyline.md`.

## 1. The story these three CRs tell

In May 1932 Japan is weak for a season and strong for a decade. It is in a slump, on a rescue loan, and its officers' coup has failed (L1732). But it holds the one card with a date on it: "Japan may refuse in 1937" (L1734).

So Julian's problem in 1932 is not 1932. It is which Japan exists in 1937. In 1931 he stopped the army with three fast levers, "oil from Pacifica and the Gulf Republic, transport by the common navy and the yen's convertibility through common finance" (L1720). In 1932 he knowingly trades two of them for slower ones, and pays the navy, the houses and the villages so that in 1937 they have more to lose than the army has to offer. He is choosing which Japan to strengthen, and he may be wrong.

That gives the three drafts one spine:

- **Every gain Japan gets is a hostage.** It is either certain now and paid for by a named Pacifican who objects, or it falls due only if Japan renews in 1937.
- **The fast levers really are spent.** Someone in Thalassia says aloud that what stopped the army in 1931 will not stop it in 1937. The sufficers and the oil trust say it; Victoria thinks it.
- **Each side is refused one thing that costs.** Japan asks for a commandant of its own at Labuan and is refused (CR-084 T-L1 case A). After the coup Julian asks for the home army under common inspection in peace and is refused: "The home army stays national except in a Concord war" (L1734). Canon already holds that sentence; the drafts should make it Julian's defeat.
- **1937 stays open.** No draft may give every party a reason to renew. The stakes rise on both sides and the odds are not stated.

## 3. What Japan gets, and what it costs whom

| Japan receives | When it is real | Who in the Realm pays or objects |
|---|---|---|
| The Navy seat by treaty law, for the reserved squadron it loses (L1734: "making the Navy ministry part of the bargain") | 1932 | The Navy's Pacifican officers; the seat is no longer Julian's to give. |
| The yen reset inside a consultation band | 1932 | The Reserve Bank's board and the common Finance minister; Shanghai's mills; Julian's second lever. |
| Two leased commercial piers and auction entry in the Indies and Brunei; shared use of Labuan | 1932, on the Dutch ratifying in the autumn | The oil trust, which loses Asian sales; Julian's first lever. |
| Entry of Japanese smelters to the Concord pool at the pool's price | Only on renewal in 1937 | The aluminium champion. |
| The Kansai station, half built in 1937 on a fuel lease that ends if Japan leaves | Only on renewal in 1937 | The Works, whose cores sit on Japanese rock. |

Japan pays with things canon has not already taken: the piers stay unfortified and south of 10° S is closed to it (CR-084); the quota and the last rescue instalments cost more in the new yen (CR-085); and two of its gains can be lost by walking out.

The common Navy minister does not speak for Japan. He is Julian's appointee whose own ministry is on the table, and both sides doubt him. Japan's terms are settled in its cabinet and carried by Prince Chichibu and, to Takahashi, by Chiyo, who is canon's channel there (L1722) and whom the officers' manifesto names.

**The Realm's own interest, to be stated in cabinet in each draft:** a Japan that is fed and supplied stops looking at Manchuria's land; Japanese yards and capital sit inside the pool and not outside it; every Japanese station sends its bred elements back to the Works; the fleet stays whole. The price is the two levers.

## 4. The calendar

Canon already puts into June–July 1932 the Union's settlement, the monetary settlement, the renewal's instruments and the dominions' treaty (L1746–1752). When everyone signs everything in one place, no signature matters. Thalassia keeps only what must be there.

**At Thalassia, June–July 1932:**
- canon's items;
- the British retrenchment treaty, signed (CR-084);
- the adjustable parity, with France's reservation and Germany's terms (CR-085, CR-086);
- China's currency protocol, ratified, having been negotiated in 1930–31 (CR-086);
- a test-notice convention, proposed by the Realm and **not concluded**. Britain will not promise notice of a test it has not yet made, and Szilárd asks inside the wall how the Realm can ask the world for candour while its own record of 1924 is sealed to 1947. It is unfinished when the book ends.

**Before the conference:**
- the Tokyo station's financing, 1927–28;
- the ore purchases, 1919–1931, and Brazilian monazite, 1928;
- the kelp compact, at the Hayama Council of 1930;
- the Atomic Power Board and the renewal, agreed at Hayama in May 1932.

**After it:**
- Brunei's and Tonga's instruments, executed in July once Britain's release takes effect;
- the Realm's chambers and the Cortes vote in July–August;
- the States General ratifies the Batavia Compact in the autumn. Its talks are at Batavia, not at Thalassia.

**Dropped from the suite:**
- Canada's monetary association. It contradicts "Canada remains British" (L1752) for no gain the Realm can name.
- The Guaymas protocol. Canon has had the port since 1867; the chartered district still "waits on Mexico".
- Siam's protocol. One sentence remains under CR-082's R-5: the legation reports the revolution of 24 June and the cabinet's minute reads "watched".
- Leasing Works fuel to any third state. It would put purified graphite and bred-element chemistry abroad.
- The Pampas contracts as the granary's purchase. If kept at all, private houses buy as merchants, in the autumn, in one paragraph.
