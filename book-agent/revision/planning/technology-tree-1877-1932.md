# The electric century: a plan for CR-077's knock-on technologies (1877–1932)

*Planning note for the author, 20 November 2026. Nothing here is canon. Every Pacifican date is PROPOSED. A historical date is given beside each for comparison (Hist.).*

## 1. Why a plan first

Canon already has the roots of a whole electric century:
- the electron of 1879 (and with it the cathode-ray tube);
- valve amplifiers about 1895 and hard valves about 1908;
- Tesla's alternating current and his navy wireless;
- Dickson's moving pictures for the Five's press from 1895;
- 96% of homes wired by 1929;
- aluminium aircraft, the most motorized place on earth, and Los Angeles "film and sound";
- CR-077's computers.

The branches that grow from those roots are mostly missing: the telephone, broadcasting, television, sound recording, radar, instruments, appliances and medicine. Done one CR at a time, they would collide. Every one of them depends on the same valves, the same tubes, the same estates and the same people.

This note draws the whole tree once. It sets the rules for dating and proposes how to package it into as few CRs as possible.

## 2. The rules

1. **Parts, need and people (CR-077's rule).** A technology comes early only when three things are true: its parts exist in canon, someone in Pacifica needs it, and someone historical who could have built it is in Pacifica. If any one is missing, it keeps its historical date.
2. **The story filter.** A date goes into the storyline only if it changes the plot, the society or the picture of 1932. Everything else goes into one background table and stays off the page.
3. **No new estates or seats.** The Five stay five (CR-077 D-4). Each technology belongs to an existing body:
   - the telegraph estate under the Polytechnic (wire and cable);
   - Tesla's and then Ryan's power estate (equipment and wireless);
   - the press seat (pictures and sound);
   - the Office of Public Measures (numbers);
   - the General Staff (war).
4. **Open licensing at one royalty (CR-057).** The only exceptions are the cipher room and the Works (CR-077 D-5).
5. **Historical people over invented ones,** and few of them.

## 3. Pacifica's biases: why the order differs

These seven tendencies follow from canon. They explain each date below, and they are where this timeline's technology looks different from ours, not just earlier.

| Bias | Source in canon | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Pulse first** | Electronics grows out of the Works' counters and CR-077's latch, not out of radio | Pacifica's engineers think in pulses and counts. Radar, digital machines and pulse telephony come relatively early; the analogue radio culture is the Union's. |
| **Picture over text** | Six languages, tongue votes, Chinese and Japanese partners; Victoria's "seeing is believing" | Pictures sent by wire (fax) and moving pictures matter more than teleprinters, because telegraph codes cannot carry kanji. |
| **Air over water** | An ocean empire whose cables are costly and cuttable (Port Hamilton, 1885; the Aleutian cable, 1904) | Radio across the sea is pushed hard. Undersea telephony waits for repeaters on the seabed (Hist. 1956). |
| **Measure everything** | The Office of Public Measures, honest numbers, the four standards | Instruments, meters, counters and recorders spread widely: the household meter, the traffic counter, the fruit grader. |
| **Teach at a distance** | The examination state, the interior's settlers, the islands | Broadcasting serves education early: examination lessons and the Office's tables on the air. |
| **Cheap power** | Hydro, about 2,600 kWh a head in 1929 | Electric appliances, electric traction and electric heating spread early. |
| **Japan as partner** | The Concord, CR-077 G10 | Japanese inventions that history ignored in Japan are used at once in Pacifica. Yagi's antenna (1926) and Okabe's magnetron (1927) are both from Tohoku. |

**Where the order changes:**
- **Digital before analogue.** Pacifica's machines start electronic (pulse) and do without the great analogue computers of 1925–1945, which become the Union's road (CR-077 H).
- **Electronic television from the start.** There is no mechanical Nipkow and Baird era, because the cathode-ray tube is decades older (electron 1879) and amplifiers exist from 1895.
- **Short spark era.** Continuous waves from valve oscillators arrive soon after 1895 (Hist.: the valve oscillator came about 1912–1913), so spark and arc transmitters fade early.
- **Radar before television is common.** The navy's pulse work and the cathode-ray tube give radio location in the 1920s. Television reaches homes only about 1930.
- **Fax before teleprinter in Asian trade,** by the picture-over-text bias.

## 6. How to package it

**Option 1: one big CR** ("CR-078: the electric century") with Parts A–E and R, built outline-first and decided part by part, as CR-071 was. It holds everything together, but it would be the size of CR-068, which was too big to read.

**Option 2 (recommended): two CRs, with this note as their common spine.**
- **CR-078: Wire and air.** Telephone, long lines, cables and the Guam route, fax and teleprinter, radio and broadcasting as infrastructure, the air school, navigation, radio location, echo sounding, ocean warnings: A, B, D2, D4, R1–R4. It is about the state, the links of the empire and defence, and it ends at canon's 1932 defences.
- **CR-079: Picture and sound** (the media CR of CR-077 D-8). Recording, sound film, television, public address, electric music, broadcasting's content, Hearst, Dickson's successors and Farnsworth: C, R5, R7. It is about Victoria's medium and culture.
- **No CR for E.** Household and medicine go into the background table of CR-078. The model already carries the wiring path and women's work (CR-056); X-ray medicine is in CR-050.

**Option 3: four CRs** (wire, air, picture, sound). This is the proliferation you want to avoid.

**Order of work.** Integrate CR-077 first, so that 1893, 1909 and the machines are canon. Then CR-078, then CR-079.
