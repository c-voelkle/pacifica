## Sequencing and historical limits

The one-major-move cadence explicitly permits several short 1870s operations, but no simultaneous fronts and no further military addition before 1880 after Vladivostok. Foreign military adventures pause around the 1887 succession. CR-018 replaces later British operations with the victorious Siberian coalition and fixes the railway route and jurisdictional sequence. A Pacifican expeditionary reserve is a later proposed role; the pre-1900 no-land-army rule remains period-specific. Australia’s 1932 treaty is a CR-019 recommendation.

Russia retains the northern coast until 1921. CR-021 revision 9 gives Kamchatka, the Okhotsk coast and Nikolayevsk to Siberia, superseding CR-018’s Japanese destination; Japanese access is treaty-only and creates no automatic base. Model that sequence separately from the 1899 Ussuri/Sakhalin/Amur settlement. Fong is twenty-six in 1885; test appointment credibility through institutional safeguards instead of changing his approved birth date.

Historical anchors verified for documentation are linked in [historical_background.md](historical_background.md). Exact historical casualty counts, frontier census estimates, Hart's succession details and ambitious industrial delivery dates require further source work; no source can prove the alternate outcomes.
