## Retrieved historical sources

### H1 United States Mint historical overview

- Source: United States Mint, History of the U.S. Mint, section on the California Gold Rush. Institutional retrospective; publication date not displayed; accessed 26 October 2026.
- URL: https://www.usmint.gov/learn/history/overview
- Finding: local conversion addressed the delays and risks of shipping gold east. The actual San Francisco branch opened in 1854.
- Relevance: settlement services can offer a reason to use an official channel. This supports considering a service mechanism, not a quantitative compliance estimate.
- Limit: it does not establish Pacifica's 1850 equipment, staff, cost or royalty capture.

### H2 Director of the Mint report for 1854

- Source: Report of the Director of the Mint, dated 30 January 1855, transmitted to the House 9 February 1855, 33rd Congress, 2nd Session, House Executive Document 62, printed pp. 2–3.
- URL: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/SERIALSET-00788_00_00-004-0062-0000/pdf/SERIALSET-00788_00_00-004-0062-0000.pdf
- Finding: the report describes San Francisco's April 1854 opening and says material supply, particularly locally manufactured parting acid, reduced coinage. It distinguishes deposits, coins, bars and transfers.
- Relevance: local capacity and operating supplies need their own commissioning schedule. Official processing is not equivalent to extraction or fiscal receipts.
- Limit: retrieved text was available; the browser page-image request failed. No numerical deposit/coinage series from this OCR is used to calibrate the pilot. The report does not demonstrate an earlier alternate mint or a collectible 5% royalty.

Later compilation of contemporary instruments; publication date of the displayed edition not established from the retrieved appendix.

- Limit: this is neither Pacifican recognition nor a transfer contract. It does not establish assumed arrears, maturity, consent, grace or Pacifica's applicable coupon. Translated descriptions here are analyst paraphrases.

## Evidence synthesis

The service-based collection hypothesis has contextual support: useful settlement can attract bullion to an official channel. The size of that response, associated coercion and administrative cost are unestimated. Mint capacity, tax collection and debt-creditor cooperation require different evidence.

Competing possibilities remain open. A lower rate might improve participation or output; it might simply surrender receipts. Licensed intermediaries could expand reach but create private gatekeepers. Creditor protection might improve security while constraining independent use of revenue. No retrieved source selects one of these counterfactual outcomes.

Relevant personages are Elias and his Californian coalition in the authored history, and historical assayers/contractors as research leads. Humbert or Moffat is not silently assigned a Pacifican role. Julian's later authority follows the existing accession and institutional passages, not a new genealogical or office claim.

No defensible historical collection percentage or numerical probability has been found in this bounded investigation. The [research checkpoint](research-checkpoint.md) records the specific next sources; the author's answer supplies no additional source preference.
