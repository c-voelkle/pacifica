**Reading the results.**
- **The money is in 1919–1927.** The halls are built in the dearest dollars of the period; the Budget surplus is $80–155m a year lower in 1920–24, and because the Development Fund invests most of every surplus, its stock is $0.8bn (4.5%) lower in 1929 and real GDP 0.47% lower. That is the whole GDP effect of CR-051: the model credits the programme with no spillovers.
- **The champions add little value, much power.** Aluminium's value added at 1920s prices is small ($39m in 1929 on 150 kt), so Champions!AO falls below its old, unexplained path in 1913–29 ($134m against $180m in 1929) and rises above it in 1931–32 ($156m against $110m), when the naval and air programme is the order book. The electrochemistry adds $80m in 1929, electric steel's premium ($52m) most of it. Net, the champions at home are 0.7% higher in 1929 and 2.3% higher in 1932.
- **1931 is a champions' bad year, not the Realm's.** The export third loses $5–6m of price a year in 1931–32, the rubber and hydrogenation works lose about $10m at world prices, the pool and the order book hold; champions' value added at home falls 25.2% from 1929 to 1931, against 25.7% in the old build.
- **Debt is practically unchanged** (+$22m in 1932): the Story case pays the Works from surpluses. The Reserve Fund is $71m lower in 1932; the fleet's payment for the strategic works costs $8–10m a year in 1931–32.
- **The estate's bonds** are 1.3% of GDP by 1932, the Reserve Bank's share of them $103m: gold cover in 1932 0.57 instead of 0.58, well above the rule. The private-saving test passes in every Story year with them in it.
- **Residents** are 36.75 million in 1932 against 36.76 (about −9,000).

## Stress

Stress takes the halls at three times Y-12 and `atom_scale` 1.5 on all the Works' costs except the compacts, so the halls are **in effect about 4.5 times Y-12 ($495m in 1913 $)**, with `halls_op` at $45k a MW-year, dearer power and its own energy paths. Its atomic programme costs $346m in 1922 (was $80m); its Budget is in deficit in 1921–25 (−$229m in 1921, was +$8m) and in 1930–32. Public debt is **$5.5bn in 1932 (was $3.4bn)**, the Development stock $1.18bn in 1929 (was $1.95bn), GDP 0.5% lower in 1929, residents 21.64m (21.65). Checks failing: 17 year-checks (was 12): the old ones (private saving capacity 1887 and 1914–20, the Siberian line 1918–19, the Japan FX buffer 1927, gold cover 1932), plus **private saving capacity in 1921–24** (the Treasury borrows to build the halls) and **debt over 30% of GDP in 1932** (33%). The failures say that the halls at the top of CR-051's range with Stress's cost overrun cannot be paid for without borrowing in the early 1920s.
