## 1. Summary

This is the European and North American half of the triumphant ending; CR-018 is the Pacific half. By 1932 the Pacific is the dominant power on earth not because it conquers Europe or absorbs the Union, but because it holds what both depend on: oil, gold, credit, the canal and the sea routes.

The Union stays out of Europe's Great War. Pacifica fights at sea and in the Pacific but never sends an army to the Continent; it is the Entente's banker and oil supplier, as the United States was before 1917. Russia's collapse does not end the war, because Germany thinks it can now win and the Entente still has Pacifica's credit. Julian keeps that credit flowing through the winter of 1917–18, so that Germany cannot dictate a peace at its peak, lets the German spring offensive of 1918 exhaust itself, and only then turns the credit off. With no American army the war ends in deadlock, and Pacifica brokers a peace without victory, using its credit against the Entente and its food against the Central Powers. Kaiulani, as dowager diplomat, makes the peace of 1919, as she made the armistice of 1894. Germany stays dominant on land in central Europe but has no oil and no colonies; France keeps its grievance; Britain keeps its fleet and empire and owes Pacifica in gold dollars.

The war leaves two systems of credit: the Union's money in Berlin and Vienna, Pacifica's in London, Paris and Asia. In 1931 the first collapses and the second holds. Pacifica does what, in the historian Charles Kindleberger's account, neither Britain could nor the United States would in the real depression: it keeps its market open, lends against the cycle and acts as lender of last resort. It rescues the Union a second time, as in 1895, and brings it into its order by association, not absorption: the Union keeps its sovereignty, its democracy and its problems, and accepts the conditions of the Thalassia Conference of 1932. In November 1932 the Union elects Owen D. Young, the electrical industrialist and author of the Young Plan, a measure of how far the timeline has moved; Franklin Roosevelt plays along. The Union's great challenger came a generation earlier: Theodore Roosevelt, president from 1901 to 1909. At home, Keawe's movement wins labour reforms instead of burning Thalassia, and the Chronicler records the triumph and its cost.

### B. Principles

**B1. Association, not absorption.** Some 80–85 million Union citizens with full votes would swamp Pacifica's franchise and import its race politics, unemployment and revanchism. The model is how the United States took Britain into its order in 1940–46: fifty old destroyers for leases on British bases (1940), supplies on credit with conditions (Lend-Lease, 1941), a dollar-based monetary order (Bretton Woods, 1944) and a loan that forced sterling's convertibility (1946) (Hist.). Britain stayed sovereign and imperial but became the junior partner, and the United States took on none of its colonial wars, debts or class politics. Pacifica plays the United States of 1945; the Union plays Britain. PROPOSED

**B2. Harbours, not populations: the chokepoints.** PROPOSED

| Lever | Why the Union, and Europe, depend on it |
|---|---|
| Oil | California and the Gulf Republic's Texas, Sequoyah and Louisiana, historically the leading American fields (Hist.), give Pacifica and its partner something like half the world's output around 1930 (to the audit). The Union's own fields in Pennsylvania, Ohio, Illinois and Kansas cover only part of its needs. |
| Gold and the reserve currency | Pacifica rescued the Union's gold in 1895 (CR-015 E10). When Britain leaves gold in September 1931 (Hist.), the Pacifican gold dollar is the only anchor left. |
| Credit | Europe's war is fought on Pacifican loans; the Union's 1920s boom and its loans to Germany run partly on San Francisco credit. |
| The canal and the sea routes | The Union's only route to Asia runs through Pacifica's canal and ports. |
| Food | Pacifica's grain fed Belgium in the war and feeds Central Europe after it (C2). |

**B3. The duties of the leading power.** Kindleberger argued that the depression of the 1930s was so wide and deep because Britain could no longer and the United States would not yet stabilise the world economy: keep a market open for distressed goods, lend against the cycle and act as lender of last resort in a crisis (Hist., Kindleberger, *The World in Depression, 1929–1939*, 1973). Pacifica does all three, and that, more than any battle, is why it is the dominant power by 1932. PROPOSED

**B4. What Pacifica refuses.** No common citizenship and no free movement with the Union, as with the Gulf Republic; the 1888 immigration law stands, and the Bread March of 1894 showed why (CR-015). Loans with conditions, never a common budget. A guarantee of security, not shared defence, so Pacifica is not drawn into the Union's quarrels. PROPOSED

**B5. The moral price.** Pacifica's success rests partly on keeping the Union's poor and its Black citizens at arm's length, continuing the thread of the 1888 immigration law and the fugitive agreement (CR-015). Its critics name it, and the novel should let them. PROPOSED

### C. The war, 1914–1918

**C1. Why the Union stays out.** Its rival is Pacifica, and Pacifica is the Entente's banker. Its long frontier with British Canada matters more to it than Belgium. Its German- and Irish-descended voters, the largest groups in the historical North, oppose the war (Hist.). Britain's blockade nearly drove the neutral United States into a quarrel with London in 1916 (Hist.); here nothing pulls the other way, so the Union stays neutral and leans toward Berlin. PROPOSED

**C2. Pacifica's war.** Pacifica is a belligerent at sea and in the Pacific, as Japan historically was, and never on the Continent. Its gold dollars finance the Entente; its oil and the Gulf Republic's fuel the Allied fleets and armies, which historically drew about 80% of their oil from the United States (Hist.); it supplies the Atlantic through Galveston and New Orleans rather than New York. Its relief administration feeds occupied Belgium, as Herbert Hoover's Commission for Relief in Belgium historically did from 1914 (Hist.). Hoover, born in Iowa and raised in Oregon from 1885 and educated at Stanford (Hist.), is a Pacifican here (OPEN-019-04). Because the Entente buys on Pacifican credit, the Union's war boom is much smaller than America's was. PROPOSED

### D. The peace of 1919

**D1. The broker.** Kaiulani, aged 80, as dowager diplomat (CR-018 D1, M), makes the peace as she made the armistice of 1894. Julian, three years on the throne, chose its moment; she makes its terms (OPEN-019-02). Woodrow Wilson called for "a peace without victory" on 22 January 1917 and failed to get one, because he had no leverage (Hist.); Pacifica has it. The conference meets at The Hague from January 1919 and signs in June (OPEN-019-01). PROPOSED

**D2. The terms.** PROPOSED

| Party | Terms |
|---|---|
| Germany | Evacuates Belgium and northern France; keeps Alsace-Lorraine; pays no indemnity; loses its colonies. Its eastern clients, Poland, the Baltic duchy, Ukraine and Finland, are recognised. Parliamentary monarchy (OPEN-019-08). |
| Austria-Hungary | Survives as a federation on the lines of Emperor Karl's manifesto. |
| Britain and France | Keep their Ottoman and African conquests. France does not recover Alsace-Lorraine. |
| Pacifica and Japan | Keep Germany's Pacific islands. Pacifica keeps the Tsingtao anchorage on lease; the rest of Shandong returns to China (CR-018 H, 1919). |
| Russia | Absent. The Siberian war is already under way (CR-018 G). |
| The covenant | A permanent court of arbitration at The Hague, where the Permanent Court of Arbitration has sat since 1899 (Hist.); the racial equality clause Japan historically lost at Paris in 1919 (CR-018 I3); naval limits for every party (D3). |

**D3. Naval limits.** A ratio on the model of the Washington treaty of 1922 (Hist., 5:5:3): the common fleet of Pacifica and Japan and the Royal Navy at parity, Germany below them, France and Italy below Germany. The Union, not a party, stays outside until 1932 (I). PROPOSED

**D4. Who loses what.** Clemenceau's France feels robbed; Italy's "mutilated victory" (Hist.) is worse than in history; the Entente's generals blame the banker; Germany's nationalists blame the October reformers. Each grievance is smaller than Versailles's, and none is Pacifica's problem until the 1930s. PROPOSED

### F. Britain and the debts

**F1. War debts in gold dollars.** Britain and France owe Pacifica in gold dollars, as their debts to the United States were in dollars (Hist.). Sterling's later fall does not reduce them. PROPOSED

**F2. The settlement of 1923.** On the model of Britain's war-debt settlement with the United States in January 1923 (Hist.), Britain funds its debt over sixty-two years at reduced interest, and pays part in kind (OPEN-019-05). Canon §30's "financial officials convert foreign debts into imperial claims over railways, ports and public revenues" happens here. PROPOSED

**F3. Pacifica overtakes Britain through debt, not war,** as the United States historically did. Britain sits inside the Siberian coalition (CR-018 E4) and becomes the senior junior partner of Pacifica's order. PROPOSED

**G1a. Theodore Roosevelt, the Union's great challenger.** PROPOSED unless marked
- *1898–1900:* the Union fights Spain over Cuba (CR-015 E12), so he can still lead the Rough Riders (Hist.) and become governor of New York in 1899. Whitney steps down after one term, and the war hero wins the election of 1900.
- *A president without a West.* His historical book *The Winning of the West* (1889–96) becomes a bitter irony, since the West was lost. He is the revanchists' natural voice, but in a big-stick style of competition and pressure, not war, consistent with CR-015's judgment that no majority will fight for the old claim.
- *No canal.* Pacifica owns Panama and the Nicaraguan rights (CR-015). His Roosevelt Corollary of 1904 (Hist.), which declares the Caribbean the Union's to police, collides with the joint guarantee of Cuba by Pacifica, the Gulf Republic and the Union.
- *1905, turned away.* He offers to mediate between Russia and Japan as Russia's traditional friend; Japan refuses on Kaiulani's advice, and there is no Portsmouth and no Nobel (CR-018 F, 1905). APPROVED (no mediated peace)
- *1906–08, courting Japan.* While Silas's nativists start the San Francisco school crisis (CR-018 F, 1906), he offers Tokyo friendship. His Great White Fleet goes round Cape Horn, since it cannot use Pacifica's canal, and calls at Yokohama in October 1908, as it historically did (Hist.). Japan uses the courtship as leverage in the 1907 renewal of its compact with Pacifica. His Gentlemen's Agreement of 1907 still restricts Japanese immigration to the Union, so Tokyo uses him without trusting him.
- *Afterwards.* His Bull Moose split elects Wilson in 1912. From 1914 he attacks Wilson's neutrality, wanting the Union in the war so that Pacifica is not left as the Entente's only banker. He dies in January 1919 (Hist.), while The Hague makes the peace he wanted no part of.

**G1b. Owen D. Young, the president who accepts the order.** Chairman of General Electric, founder of RCA in 1919, a member of the Dawes committee of 1924 and chairman of the committee that produced the Young Plan of 1929; widely discussed as the Democratic candidate for 1932, he declined in history (Hist.). Here he runs and wins on accepting the Thalassia settlement and rebuilding through cooperation. An electrical industrialist whose company long competed with Pacifica's electrical standard (CR-015 C2), he knows Pacifica's methods from the inside. His election shows how far the timeline has moved. APPROVED (Young); PROPOSED (platform)

**G1c. Franklin Roosevelt plays along.** Without the South, Roosevelt's historical coalition does not exist, and he does not win. He does what his career would otherwise have held: Assistant Secretary of the Navy under Wilson from 1913 to 1920 (Hist.), governor of New York from 1929, where he founded the first state emergency relief agency in 1931 (Hist.), so the New Deal stays in one state. He backs Young at the 1932 convention and may become his Secretary of the Navy, a navy man who signs off on second place at sea (OPEN-019-12). APPROVED (plays along); PROPOSED (form)

**G2. The 1920s.** The Union lends to Germany, Austria-Hungary and Germany's eastern clients, filling the space Pacifica's credit leaves, since Pacifica lends to the Entente. Its navy, revived since the 1880s (CR-015 E10), grows outside the 1919 limits. Its revanchists stay underground but alive, and Theodore Roosevelt's naval legacy keeps the navy lobby strong. Its 1930 tariff mirrors the historical Smoot–Hawley tariff, whose sponsors came from Utah and Oregon (Hist.) and here are Pacificans, so the Union's version carries other names. Pacifica, unlike the Union, keeps its market open (B3). PROPOSED

**G3. The Union's risks.** Depressions produce demagogues; the revanchists who went underground in 1896 (CR-015 E10) are the obvious candidates. Huey Long, Louisiana's governor from 1928 (Hist.), belongs to the Gulf Republic here: its problem, and Pacifica's partner's. PROPOSED

### I. The Thalassia Conference, 1932

**I1. The Union's conditions.** PROPOSED

| Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| The end of the grievance | The Senate, which ratified the Pacifica treaty in 1865 but never surrendered the claim to the South (CR-015, split recognition), recognises the Confederacy and the Gulf Republic and renounces every claim under the 1865 and 1871 lines. This is the symbolic heart of "into the fold": the Union accepts the order it has resisted for 67 years. |
| Gold-dollar order | The Union's currency is tied to the Pacifican gold dollar at an adjustable rate, on the model of the Bretton Woods system of 1944 rather than the rigid gold standard of the 1920s (Hist.): the Union's devaluation is agreed at Thalassia, and governments stay free in domestic policy, the bargain the political scientist John Ruggie called "embedded liberalism" (Hist.). |
| Naval ratio | The Union accepts a ratio below the common fleet and the Royal Navy, as Britain historically accepted parity with the United States at Washington in 1922 and London in 1930 (Hist.). |
| Pensacola | A Pacifican lease on Pensacola, the Union's Gulf base fifty miles from Mobile (CR-015 E6), on the model of the destroyers-for-bases lease of 1940 (Hist.). |
| The court | The Union joins the court of arbitration of 1919 as a founding signatory of its new charter. |
| The embargo | Union firms cut their Soviet contracts. Historically Albert Kahn's Detroit firm designed hundreds of Soviet factories in 1929–32 and Ford built the Gorky car plant (Hist.); the precedent for the embargo is the Western export controls of 1949–50 (Hist.). |

**I2. What the Union gains.** Its currency saved; its German losses partly recovered through the settlement of Germany's debts (I3); co-guarantor of Cuba, which it already is (CR-015 E12); a founding seat on the court; and primacy in the Atlantic under Pacifica's monetary umbrella. A humiliating settlement backfires, as Versailles did; this one gives the Union real standing, and keeps its energy pointed east and north. PROPOSED

**I3. Europe.** The moratorium becomes a settlement of war debts and of the Union's German loans; Germany, Britain and France join the gold-dollar order; Japan and China attend as members of the standing alliance (CR-018 G7). PROPOSED

### J. Pacifica at home, 1930–1932

**J1. Keawe's labour charter.** Keawe, the stoker and organiser of canon §33, leads strikes in the ports and the bauxite works in 1930–32 against labour registration and the union's common taxes (CR-018 F, 1927–31). He approaches Kaiulani, as canon has him do (§33), and she, at 93, brokers between him and Julian one last time. The result in 1932 is a labour charter that abolishes labour registration and protects the right to organise, in the same year as the Union's own Norris–La Guardia Act of March 1932, which limited court injunctions against strikes (Hist.). APPROVED (reforms, not destruction); PROPOSED (form; OPEN-019-09)

**J2. The Chronicler's last archive** (canon §37). Thalassia does not burn. The Chronicler completes the history of the empire in 1932, and the sealed copper cylinders of canon now hold what the triumph hides: the true Altai report that Marcus Vance III left before his death in May 1932 (CR-018 N7). Dao still carries them. APPROVED (triumph and cost); PROPOSED (form; OPEN-019-10)

### K. The final shadow

The losers of the Pacifican order can find one another: revanchist France, the Soviet rump and the Union's revanchists (CR-018 L). Australia accepts Pacifica's protection but resents the racial equality clause. The Union's poor and Black citizens are kept at arm's length (B5). The triumph is real, and so are its creditors' enemies. PROPOSED

## 5. Alternatives considered and not adopted

- **The war ends in early 1918, after Russia's collapse.** Not adopted: a peace at Germany's peak would leave it dictating terms, with Belgium as a satellite; Julian keeps the Entente afloat until neither side can win (C3).
- **Franklin Roosevelt elected in 1932**, as the New Deal president, or as a threat to Julian. Not adopted by author decision (A9): without the South he cannot win.
- **Theodore Roosevelt mediates in 1905 at Portsmouth**, as in history. Not adopted by author decision (A10).
- **The Union fights in Europe's war, as the United States did in 1917.** Not adopted by author decision (A2): it would make little sense for the Union in this timeline.
- **An Entente victory in 1919, financed by Pacifica.** Not adopted: closer to history, but it brings back the Versailles grievance and a defeated Germany.
- **A German victory.** Not adopted: it gives Pacifica a rival dominating Europe in 1932.
- **Absorbing the Union into a single Pacific state** (constellation A of the triumph workshop). Not adopted: the Union's size and problems would become Pacifica's (B1).
- **The Second Founding of 1931.** Dropped by author decision (A8).
- **A war with Britain for Singapore in the 1920s.** Not adopted by author decision (A5).
- **Pacifican military control of the Continent** (canon §30). Not adopted: Pacifica's power in Europe is credit, oil, food and the sea (C2).
