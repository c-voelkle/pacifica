# CR-052 — third review: the Max case against the story (4 November 2026)

Review of the fourth build's Max case (M1–M9) for what the story would have to say for each part to apply, and where a part does not follow logically or needs more explanation than CR-052 gives it. Read against `claude/CR-052.md`, `energy/CR-052-workbook.md`, the master storyline on `main` (industry §, Keawe 1919–1932, the 1931–32 slump) and CR-051 S7–S8 (the Columbia station, Rock Island, the compacts). Figures are MODEL.

## Verdict

Five parts can be taken into the Story case with a sentence or two each: M1, M6, M7 (Boulder only), M8 and M9. Four cannot be taken as drafted: M2 (the industrial weight is an economic decision, not an energy one), the 1.5 GW mid-Columbia chain inside M7 (collides with approved CR-051), M3 (moves aluminium away from Keawe; wrong bauxite) and M4 (contradicts the Japan partnership canon; mis-frames arc against Haber). The author's response (4 November): "that sounds reasonable"; the narrowed take is recorded in CR-052 as the Max decision, with M2 referred to the financial workbook and M3/M4 sent back for rework.

## Findings

1. **M2 is the real lever, and it is not an energy decision.** CR-052's own sensitivity table shows the multiplier is mostly `ind_w`. Raising the industrial weight from 0.5 to 0.75 at the same GDP means a more industrial economy with a smaller service sector — a decision about what Pacifica is, which belongs to the financial workbook and the author, not to a power assumption. Separately, electric process heat (Norway, Switzerland) happened where there was no coal or oil. In Story Pacifica has coal at four times history and twice history's oil, so electric boilers in California need a state-capitalist reason (price regulation, oil reserved for the navy and export) that CR-052 does not give. On the Columbia, with spilled water, the electricity is free; in California it is not. *Disposition: refer `ind_w` to the financial workbook's next build; keep electric process heat in the Northwest (M9) and drop it from California unless a policy sentence is written.*

2. **The 1.5 GW mid-Columbia chain "decided 1918" collides with approved CR-051.** CR-051 S7 makes Rock Island (built 1920–23 by a hydro company of the Works) the first main-stem dam, forbids Priest Rapids (the Wanapum village) and says "not yet Grand Coulee". A chain of that size in the 1920s needs Priest Rapids/Wanapum or Coulee-scale works, or a named set of sites above Wenatchee plus Bonneville, each with its own compact (S8). It also fills the station's reach with industry and people, eroding the "few people" siting criterion. *Disposition: drop the chain from M7; M7 is Boulder early plus the Sierra 35% faster. A later CR may add named sites with consents.*

3. **M3 moves aluminium away from Keawe.** 140 of 180 kt on the Columbia makes Hilo a 40 kt branch plant, which deflates the 1931 sterling undercut, the Waimanu dam fight and Keawe joining the hydro board (storyline, 1931–32). The bauxite is also wrong-footed: British Guiana during the confrontation with London is an unreliable source, and Bintan was not worked until the mid-1930s. The obvious canon source is **Arkansas**, the main US bauxite district, which lies in the Gulf Republic and is not used by CR-052. *Disposition: rework M3 with Hilo as the flagship, Arkansas bauxite through the Gulf compact, and a smaller Columbia works (the CR-051 smelter at Wenatchee) — amounts to be set with the financial workbook.*

4. **M4's Japan market contradicts the partnership canon.** 150 kt N is roughly Japan's whole 1930 fertiliser demand. CR-031-R1 makes Japan a reciprocal industrial innovator with its own hydro; historically Noguchi built electrolytic ammonia at Nobeoka in 1923. A Columbia ammonia industry that owns Japan's fertiliser market needs either a Concord division of labour or a Japanese objection on the page. The arc-against-Haber framing in F9 is also off: Norsk Hydro's route is Haber synthesis on electrolytic hydrogen, so M4 still needs Haber–Bosch know-how, and "seized German patents" does not fit a peace in which Germany was never defeated (CR-043). *Disposition: rework M4 for the Central Valley only, or for a Concord division of labour; the hydrogen source is the only arc/electrolytic question.*

5. **Money and timing.** Max's check 6 fails in 1916–32; power investment at 2.3% of GDP in 1922 comes straight after the Russian war, the Entente loans and the atomic programme, and the storyline has the Reserve Bank leaning against the Los Angeles boom. A $1bn-a-year power boom is that boom. The financial workbook must show who buys the bonds and why the Bank allows it.

6. **The slump.** At 180 kt of aluminium (two thirds of world output) and 150 kt N for export, Max makes Pacifica far more exposed to the 1931 commodity collapse than the "milder crash" the storyline promises; Max 1932 holds generation flat, which implies someone still buys that output. The narrowed Max (finding 3 and 4) removes most of this exposure.

7. **Oil.** Max still binds on Gulf crude (285m bbl in 1929) and still peaks in 1924: maximal use of energy stretches the late-1920s decline, it does not remove it. The remaining options are new fields beyond the 22, less export, gas (85–125 PJ a year is flared and the 1929 gas shortfall is 230 PJ — pipelines from Kettleman and Buttonwillow to Los Angeles are the cheapest single fix), or concessions abroad (CR-053).

8. **Nuclear is not a bridge for 1924–32.** In Story in 1929 the power stations burn only about 37m bbl of oil (224 PJ), 68 PJ of gas and 103 PJ of coal for 26 TWh; replacing all of it with reactors cuts oil imports by about 13%. The demand is motor fuel, the navy, bunkers and lamps (about 195m bbl of oil that must be oil) and the oil railways and industry want. Canon (CR-050/051) forbids a Pacifican power station before the 1927 opening (the piles make heat, not steam; the industry is sealed to 1947; Tokyo is the first station, 1931). From 1927, a natural-uranium graphite gas-cooled line of 20–50 MW units on the historical UK pace gives perhaps 300 MW by 1932, 1–2 GW by the mid-1930s and 4 GW (the 1929 steam fleet) about 1940; a 200 MW station holds about 250 t of uranium, so a 4 GW fleet eats the whole 1932 stockpile before the breeder matures. Nuclear is the 1930s answer, not the bridge.

9. **Story-case side note (CT6).** Oil-fired Hilo sits badly with the 1932 scene, in which the smelter's problem is the hydro board's bond service while ten-cent oil is on the market: if Hilo burned oil, 1931 would make its power cheap and the Waimanu argument evaporates. The scene wants hydro with heavy fixed capital cost. *Recommend: Hilo on windward hydro through a Hawaiian hydro board (`hilo_src` = 2), with its bonds the price problem of 1932; revisit the Strategic loads sheet's view that the windward streams cannot carry a smelter, or size the smelter to them.*

## Recommended Max decision (recorded in CR-052)

Take M1, M6, M7 (Boulder only), M8 and M9 into the Story case. Refer M2's industrial weight to the financial workbook. Rework M3 around Hilo and Arkansas bauxite, and M4 for the Central Valley or a Concord division of labour. Drop the mid-Columbia chain. Fill the power seat. The multiplier this gives is to be read from the fifth build.
