# Harbor novel: current synopsis

This is a wholly fictional test manuscript, unrelated to Pacifica canon. This file and `records.md` are the two current, equally authoritative documents. Dates use the same Gregorian calendar throughout. The narrator states facts unless dialogue, belief or deliberate uncertainty is explicitly identified. No unstated amendments, adoptions or revised accounts are part of this fixture.

## A. The arrival, June 1904

The Swallow, vessel S-1, leaves Eastport on 1 June 1904. It travels directly to Westport with the passengers aboard throughout, and arrives there on 5 June 1904. The ship makes no intermediate stop and uses the usual route and propulsion described in the sailing record. The narrative dates refer to departure and arrival, not the dates on which messages arrive.

## B. The treasury speech, January 1905

The finance minister reads the final audited account for the Harbor Office's unrestricted fund for the fiscal year ending 31 December 1904. Its closing cash balance is twelve million crowns. This is the same fund and final account kept in the financial record, not a later revision. Restricted reserves are excluded from that statement.

Elsewhere in the speech he describes the exhibition: twelve million crowns of gross receipts leave eight million after four million of costs. He refers separately to a restricted reserve of four million. He does not say that the exhibition's net receipts or the restricted reserve equal the Harbor Office fund balance.

## C. A lawful dismissal, August 1905

The sovereign lawfully dismisses the Basalt Harbor master under the existing Basalt Harbor Charter through her sole personal order. The board neither votes nor delegates a power, and no emergency exception, amendment, court order or separate legal instrument applies. The dismissed master is the office holder whose appointment and removal are governed by that charter.

## D. The family supper, April 1906

Merra Vale, person N-04, welcomes her younger full biological sister Nila Vale, N-05. They share both biological parents, Dain Vale and Irena Moss. Both sisters are living. The narrator describes the relationship as a fact, not an adopted title, social convention, rumor or false cover story.

## E. The birthday, May 1906

On 12 May 1906, Tavi Noll, person N-01, celebrates his twenty-fourth birthday. The narrator states his age directly. There is no adopted birthday or calendar convention involved.

## F. The cipher, May 1906

At noon on 3 May 1906, only Arin and Bela know the full harbor cipher. This statement concerns everyone alive, wherever located, rather than the people in a room or the people publicly acknowledged as knowing it. Arin knows that Daro has heard that a cipher exists, but Daro has never seen any of its contents.

## G. The winter licenses, 1907

On 2 February 1907 the sovereign lawfully grants an emergency Whitecap fishing license herself. She follows the Whitecap amendment effective the previous month. The Whitecap license and the Basalt Harbor master's office belong to separate institutions and legal regimes.

## H. The cousins at the quay

Iona Reed, N-07, the mathematician, speaks with her cousin Iona Reed, N-08, the young mariner. The narrative distinguishes them by their IDs and occupations, even though both use the same name. Neither has changed identity.

Rook tells the watchman, “The north gate is closed.” He knows it is open and lies to delay pursuit. The narrator confirms that the gate is open throughout their conversation.

## I. Two councils

The Temporary Shore Committee, institution I-01, meets in 1897. The chartered Council of Shores, I-02, is established in 1901. The latter takes over the committee's papers, but is a distinct institution. An old character sometimes casually calls the committee “the council”; this is a colloquial name rather than a claim about the chartered institution's establishment date.

## J. The unwritten treaty and the missing key

The government proposes transferring the lighthouse to the harbor council in 1908. The proposal is still awaiting consent in the final scene; the lighthouse remains government property. A note about what would happen if the transfer were approved is conditional, not a later completed event.

The author deliberately leaves the thief of the brass key unknown. Arin suspects Bela; Bela suspects Rook. Neither suspicion is endorsed by the narrator. The existing plot supplies no solution.
