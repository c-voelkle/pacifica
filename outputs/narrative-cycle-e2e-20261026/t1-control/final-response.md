## Why her claim goes nowhere at the moment

Her claim fails where canon currently points it, at the Realm. The regency article names Victoria Regent of the Realm, the Truce factions accept her, and §24 says her regency isn't a guardianship that ends at Julian's majority. Guardianship gives Noe nothing to stand on there. So the loyalists court her, nothing comes of it, and she dies. That's why she reads as passive: canon gives her a claim but no place to use it.

Hawaii is different. There the regency article took effect only through the Territorial Council's assent, and the Supreme Court voided the Council in 1876. Also, nobody has lawfully named an heir to Victoria's Hawaiian crown since Kalanimoku died. Noe was a loyalist protégé at Emma's hospital and knows exactly where the regime is illegal. In Hawaii her claim has real force.

## The directions I considered

1. **Claim the Realm regency outright.** This fails legally, as above. Making it work would need factional or armed backing, which collides with "no succession war." She loses, and that's passive again, just with more noise.
2. **Become the loyalists' figurehead, with Albert behind her.** This turns her into an instrument of other people's cause, which the cautions specifically warn against. It also drifts toward a rising in her name. That repeats Victoria's 1874 choice, and canon supplies no loyalist revolt.
3. **Ally with Silas's party against the Regent.** It's plausible leverage, but Silas's party are nativists, and Victoria holds the Donner report over Silas. It makes Noe a pawn and doesn't suit her.
4. **Take Julian back, to Maui for example.** It's dramatic, but it breaks the 1886 bargain. It also gives Victoria such an overwhelming motive that the death stops being ambiguous. AGENTS.md and CR-021 R9-09 both forbid that tilt.
5. **Use the registers as leverage**, by threatening to publish figures that embarrass the Council. This works better as texture than as the main move. On its own it points the registers' month-long disappearance at Victoria.
6. **Petition the Hawaiian court, then trade the claim (recommended).** In the winter after Donner, Noe petitions Hawaii's Supreme Court as natural guardian. She doesn't ask for the Realm. She asks who may speak for her son in the islands. Victoria can't answer without defending the Council or overriding the 1876 judgment she let stand. Noe doesn't want to win; she wants a second bargain. In 1888 she withdraws the petition, acknowledges the regency and Victoria's raising of Julian, and refuses her name to Albert. In return Victoria undertakes that no body created by the ordinances will ever proclaim Julian Hawaiian heir. Noe then sets down her Maui genealogy from Kahekili before loyalist chiefs, who pledge to proclaim her son when they are lawfully asked.

## Why the sixth is clearly the strongest

- **Noe acts first and on her own terms.** It fits her character: she writes things down and has no use for a symbolic fight. She trades a claim she can't win for a guarantee she won't live to see. That fits "she lives to see neither."
- **Victoria loses nothing she lawfully had.** She keeps an uncontested regency, Albert is isolated and the loyalists split. What it costs her is a debt to Noe and a promise to keep for 26 years. Her rivalry with a dead woman gets something concrete to be about.
- **The death becomes more ambiguous, not less.** The death, certificate, servants' testimony, missing registers and "Regent's year" are untouched. But Victoria, Albert's circle, Silas's people and anyone who needs a martyr now all gain something from her death. No culprit is named and no evidence is added.
- **It costs almost nothing downstream.** The 1888–1914 gap, the chiefs' refusal in 1913 to proclaim anyone but Julian, the weight of Noe's descent in the 1914 Act, and the CR-029 plebiscite all stay exactly as approved. They now trace back to something Noe did. The plebiscite remains Victoria's own decision.
