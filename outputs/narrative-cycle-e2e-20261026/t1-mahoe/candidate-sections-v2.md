> After Elias dies in August 1887, Noe retains a household, a mother's claim and her health work. For a narrow price she gets Noe's public refusal of the loyalists and the chiefs Noe has won, and an heir of the Council's making would be no more hers than Noe's. In a written undertaking she binds herself that Julian's Hawaiian heirship will be conferred not by the Territorial Council, nor by the Realm constitution that already names him its Heir, but only by her own appointment with the consent of a lawful body of Hawaiian chiefs. It names no body and no date **[OPEN-033-03]**: a promise about how he will be made heir, not that he will be. For Noe that is enough; the chiefs she has turned would never accept the ordinances' heir. They witness it, and it is not published **[OPEN-033-02]**. Noe tells the loyalists she is no one's banner and leaves the petition with the Queen, who keeps both papers **[OPEN-033-09]**. She keeps her registers out of it: they are figures, not terms. Then she goes back to the wards: the rest of her year is vaccination in Honolulu and Maui, the district registers and, in the autumn, typhoid.
