# Candidate directions for Noe Ward, 1887–1888 (neutral sketches)

The author's note: "Noe feels passive to me in 1887–88. The loyalists court her, she could claim the regency as natural guardian, and then she just dies. I'd like her to make a real move in the succession."

Fixed in canon for all directions: Victoria is Regent of Pacifica from 1887 and remains Queen of Hawaii; no succession war; Noe dies in autumn 1888 during a typhoid outbreak she is fighting, with an official certificate, poisoning testimony and no culprit ever established; the 1886 hānai bargain stands as written; in 1914 the ʻAha Aliʻi proclaims Julian heir to the Hawaiian crown and the union of crowns goes to a Hawaiian plebiscite. The sketches are listed in no particular order.

- **A. Considered refusal.** The loyalists offer to back her. She weighs it on the page and declines, for reasons of her own (the health work, the bargain, the boy's safety), and the refusal has consequences.
- **B. Claim to the regency.** She openly claims the regency of the Realm as Julian's natural guardian, presses the claim through petitions and allies, and fails or withdraws.
- **C. Guardian's bargain over the Hawaiian succession.** She does not seek the Realm regency. She uses her standing as natural guardian (a petition to the Hawaiian Supreme Court over the boy's person, filed or threatened) as leverage on a different question: whether Julian will ever be Hawaii's heir by Hawaiian forms. She extracts an undertaking from Victoria about how the Hawaiian succession will be settled.
- **D. Bringing the loyalists to her son.** She turns the loyalists' courtship around: instead of becoming their figurehead, she works to bring loyalist chiefs to accept Julian (her son, of Maui chiefly descent) as their future candidate in place of Albert Kūnuiākea, on conditions.
- **E. Reclaiming the child.** She moves to revoke the hānai and take Julian back.
- **F. Alliance with a Truce faction.** She allies with one of the three adult factions of the Truce (Silas's American officers, Kiyoshi's Japanese naval party or Soberanes's Polytechnic) to gain a guardian's place in the regency.

Directions may be combined. Each must still end with the canonical death and its unresolved culpability.
