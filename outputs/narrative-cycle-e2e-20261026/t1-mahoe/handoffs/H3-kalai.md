## Selected direction (from Mahoe, after independent literary review and the Clio dossier)

Between August 1887 and autumn 1888 Noe makes a move on the **Hawaiian succession**, not the Realm regency:

1. **Refusal.** The loyalists offer to make her their figurehead (queen-mother of a counter-court against the Regent). She refuses, for her own reasons (the health work, the 1886 bargain, the boy's safety). Weigh, in one clause at most, the temptation to take the boy back and her rejection of it: revoking the hānai would destroy half of the claim she wants for him.
2. **Turning the courtship.** She turns the loyalists' interest from Albert Kūnuiākea toward her son: Julian, of her Maui chiefly line (Kahekili's), as their future candidate. She wins *some* chiefs, not the movement; Albert's partisans and irreconcilables keep their purposes and refuse. Do not name new invented chiefs.
3. **The guardian's bargain.** With those chiefs behind her and a guardian's petition drafted (as the boy's natural guardian; the 1886 hānai is customary) for the Hawaiian Supreme Court — the court whose 1876 judgment Victoria let stand unenforced — she makes Victoria choose: honour a Hawaiian court she once failed, or fail it a second time in public. Default: the petition is drafted and shown, not filed (enforcement against the sovereign repeats the 1876 problem). Victoria keeps her own counter-leverage visible (Elias's written consent, her command of the state, Noe's board dependent on the regency) and concedes by choice, because the bargain also serves her: Noe stops being the loyalists' banner and brings chiefs to the Regent's own heir.
4. **What Victoria concedes (narrow).** A written undertaking that Julian's Hawaiian heirship will not be conferred by the Territorial Council or the Realm constitution, but only by her appointment with the consent of a lawful body of Hawaiian chiefs. Which body, and when it can exist, stays **[OPEN]** in the draft. It is witnessed by the chiefs Noe brought over and not published (default). It survives Noe's death: her death releases no one from it.
5. **She goes back to the typhoid wards.** Her death in autumn 1888 follows exactly as canon has it (certificate, servants' testimony, registers missing a month then in the Office, loyalist and Silas-faction murder stories, Silas/Abigail mutual suspicion). The move widens the plausible motives — irreconcilable loyalists who see their courted figurehead make terms with the Regent; factions for whom a Hawaiian-forms heirship disturbs the Truce balance; the Regent who signed under pressure — without making any one of them likelier. **Never establish or imply a culprit.**
