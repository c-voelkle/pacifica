## Overall judgment

The candidate gives Noe a real move. The baseline (§23, line 509) only tells us that the loyalists court her and that she "could claim a regency". The candidate shows four steps: she refuses, she redirects the courtship toward her son, she uses a threat, and she extracts a limited written promise. The move costs her something and leaves opponents who keep their own purposes ("Some chiefs come over to her. The movement does not."). Its strongest sentence explains why she wants what she gets: *"an heir made by the Council would be the ordinances' heir"*. That ties the move to her canonical desire, her son on a restored Hawaiian throne (CT-JULIAN-MOTHER *Desire*). The candidate also gives the canonical regency sentence a dramatic payoff without rewriting it.

Three problems need fixing before the move fully works:

1. **The bargain's exchange is out of order.** Noe has already refused to be the banner before she bargains, but the bargain treats that refusal as her payment.
2. **The threat contradicts her own choice.** The petition asks for custody that, two sentences earlier, she refused to take.
3. **The death paragraph now leans toward Victoria.** The added motive sentence ends on her and contradicts "concedes by choice".

The downstream clauses are mostly earned. The line-632 insertion is the clumsy one, and two trajectory additions are bookkeeping.

## 1. Noe's move: agency, choice, opposition, consequence

**Gains**
- **Her own desire.** She wants the *form* of Julian's Hawaiian heirship to be Hawaiian, not to be made by the ordinances. This develops her 1886 bargain and fits her perceptual world (the hānai negotiation).
- **Alternatives rejected with reasons.** She rejects both the counter-court and taking the boy back. "However tempting" is the only place her personal longing shows, and it carries a lot.
- **Opposition keeps its purposes.** Albert's partisans keep their candidate. The irreconcilables "will take nothing from the Regent's house".
- **Consequences reach the future.** They carry to 1914 and to the sealed papers.

**Regression 1: the exchange does not add up (§23 candidate, paragraphs 1–2).** Paragraph 1 says flatly "She refuses" the banner. Paragraph 2 then gives Victoria's gain as "Noe stops being the loyalists' banner". If the refusal is already made and known, Noe hands Victoria nothing new. Victoria's only real gain is the chiefs, and the bargain reads as Victoria paying for something already given. *Smallest repair (author choice):* make the refusal private and still pending, so that announcing it becomes Noe's price. For example: "She will not be their banner, but she does not yet tell them so." Paragraph 2's "Noe stops being the loyalists' banner" then makes sense.

**Regression 2: the threat contradicts her choice (§23 candidate, paragraph 2).** The petition asks the court to recognize her as natural guardian, which in practice means custody of the person. Paragraph 1 has just shown that she will not take him back, because doing so would cut away half his claim. A careful reader, and certainly Victoria, will see that the threat is one Noe does not want to carry out. That can be a strength, because it is exactly why Victoria's concession has to be "by choice". But the text needs to say so, or it looks like an oversight. *Smallest repair (author choice; it adds a perception, not a fact):* "Both women know she does not want the custody she asks for; what she can use is the hearing."

**Still summary at its centre.** The confrontation itself, "She shows it to Victoria.", gets four words. The analysis that follows ("Filed, it would leave the Queen two courses…") is the Chronicler's reasoning, not either woman's. That is acceptable in a synopsis, but the most dramatic exchange of Noe's last year should belong to someone. A clause would do it: what Victoria reads, or what Noe watches her read. This is not a regression.

**The chiefs who come over have no motive of their own.** The reader learns why opponents refuse but not why allies join. OPEN-033-06 (b) would solve this without inventing anyone (see the OPEN list below).

## 2. Victoria's agency

**Gains.** She is not compelled. Her counter-leverage is named: Elias's written consent, command of the state, and the health board's dependence on the regency. She concedes only something narrow, and the text says outright that "the bargain also serves her". She does not become a debtor in §23.

**Weakness.** Her counter-leverage is listed but never used. And her reason to concede is defined entirely by what Noe offers her. One clause would give her a reason drawn from her own character. Her bible entry gives her "legal protocol" and Hawaiian commitments, and she fought the Council's English Schools Ordinance. She may not want the ordinances' heir either. *Author choice, with a trade-off:* this makes the concession partly a convergence of aims, which strengthens Victoria but slightly reduces Noe's win. Example: "An heir of the Council's making is no more hers than Noe's."

**Regressions that make her a debtor elsewhere:**
- **§23 death sentence (candidate line 511):** "a Regent who signed under pressure" contradicts "concedes by choice" two paragraphs earlier. It recasts her as the coerced party, which is also what gives her a motive (see §3 below).
- **Victoria trajectory (line 838 candidate):** "and in 1914 gives her people more than she promised" mixes up two subjects. The undertaking concerns the *form of Julian's heirship*. The plebiscite concerns *the union of the crowns*, and its stated motive is 1874 ("as she did not ask them in 1874"). The phrase turns her approved act of conscience into overpayment on a debt to Noe. *Repair:* delete the clause. The §27 line-634 candidate leaves the plebiscite sentence untouched, and that sentence stays her own act.

## 3. The protected mystery

The design keeps the death unchanged and introduces no evidence. That much holds. But the *sequence* now leans toward Victoria in three ways:

1. **What the reader has just seen.** The only dramatized antagonistic exchange in Noe's last year is now with Victoria, and the next paragraph opens "certified by Victoria's physician". The design itself causes this, so it cannot be removed entirely. That is why the additions around it should not add weight.
2. **Added motive sentence (line 511, OPEN-033-07 (a)).** It is placed *after* the baseline's strong close ("the Chronicler can prove none guilty") and reopens the case. It ends on "a Regent who signed under pressure". The last item in a list carries the emphasis, and "under pressure" supplies a grievance. It also assumes that irreconcilables and factions know about terms that, under OPEN-033-02 (a), were never published. That knowledge question goes to Aumakua; under OPEN-033-02 (b) the first two motives largely disappear. *Literary preference (author choice):* OPEN-033-07 (b), leaving the paragraph verbatim. The baseline already names Victoria, the factional rivals and those who need a martyr. If (a) is kept, put the sentence before "Victoria, the factional rivals…", drop "under pressure" and do not end on the Regent.
3. **"It is the Queen's promise, and Noe's death would release no one from it" (§23 candidate, paragraph 3).** This is the Chronicler making a case. It sets the reader asking who would want to be released, and the only answer is the Queen. Read the other way, it argues her innocence. Either way it is the narrator weighing Victoria's motive, which the protected mystery should not do. *Repair:* cut it from §23. The 1914 clauses show that the promise survived, and A7 can stay in the CR record.

**Placement on the Julian/Noe trajectory (line 850 candidate).** "Beside them lie the undertaking and his birth mother's unfiled petition" comes straight after "Noe's death had more suspects than he knew". In that position the documents read as evidence about those suspects. *Repair:* delete it, since §29 already says this, or fold it in before the suspects clause.

The §29 addition itself is well guarded: "They show what Noe bargained for him; they do not show how she died."

## 4. Weight and pacing

**§23.** The three new paragraphs make Noe's bargain the longest block in Part V's opening, about three times the length of the Donner paragraph (§22, line 505). They do not physically crowd Donner, and "the factions the Truce has only just bound" usefully connects the two. One resonance is unused: Noe makes terms with the Regent in the very months Silas's people are calling "the Regent's year". The section title still names Noe's death and the registers first, and the move now dominates it. *Repair:* compress to two paragraphs. Specifically:
- Cut the four-item reason list in paragraph 1 to two or three reasons. As a list, the reasons all carry equal weight and read like a checklist. Keep "however tempting".
- Merge the register sentences into one. "They are figures, not terms; she keeps them honest and unbargained and does not ask who will use them after her" makes the same point twice, "unbargained" is a coinage, and "does not ask who will use them" is the narrator announcing her blind spot. One clause is enough: "She keeps her registers out of it: they are figures, not terms."
- Cut the release sentence (see §3 above).

**Downstream clauses**
- **§26 (line 622): earned.** It fills a real gap in the baseline: why Julian is not named Hawaiian heir by a reigning Hawaiian Queen before 1914. But it collides on the page with line 632, where the ʻAha Aliʻi takes a position in 1913. The draft's resolution (a declared intention, not a lawful act; B5) is not visible to the reader. That is a consistency question for Aumakua. Literary point: the reader will notice.
- **§27 line 632: clumsy.** The relative clause hangs off "Julian" and splits the passage's "X, and Y" pattern of the coalition coming apart. It doubles "only" ("proclaim only Julian… confer only with"). It restates the undertaking a third time without saying why it matters *to Kiyoshi's bid*. *Options:* (a) drop it, since the line-634 clause carries the payoff; or (b) make it explain something: "The ʻAha Aliʻi, among whose elders are chiefs who witnessed the Queen's promise of 1888, will proclaim only Julian…". This is an author choice, because it assumes the witnesses sit in the ʻAha Aliʻi (OPEN-033-03/06).
- **§27 line 634: earned.** It is the plot's payoff. "As she had undertaken in 1888" reads slightly better.
- **§27 line 638: earned, the best of the downstream clauses.** "The two halves Noe refused to separate in 1888" picks up "half of his claim" from §23. It turns a genealogical fact into the consequence of a choice.
- **§29 (line 662): earned, but the insertion point costs something.** Appending after "He tells no one that he knows." takes away that sentence's place as the section's close. *Repair:* insert the CR-033 sentences before it. The trade-off is that "he knows" then refers back to Donner across the new sentence. In my reading the close still lands.
- **Trajectories.** The Noe sentence on line 850 is earned and well compressed. The second line-850 addition and the "more than she promised" clause on line 838 are bookkeeping or regressions; cut them (see above).
- **Unresolved item 11 (line 895):** administrative and appropriate.

## 5. Clarity

**Why an unfiled petition has force.** The answer is on the page: the Queen would have to fail her court a second time, in public, before chiefs. But the sentence order works against it. "She does not file it: a judgment against the Queen would end on paper" states the weakness first, and a reader may wonder why Victoria should care. *Repair (reordering, no new content):* put the "Filed, it would leave the Queen two courses…" sentence first. Then give the reason for not filing as Noe's tactic: a threat kept in hand is worth more than a ruling no one can enforce.

**What the undertaking promises.** "It is a promise about how he will be made heir, not that he will be" is clear and well placed. One phrase is unclear: "conferred… by the Constitution of the Realm". A reader will not see how a Realm constitution could confer a *Hawaiian* heirship until reminded that the 1887 constitution already names Julian Heir *of the Realm* (line 483). *Repair:* "not by the Territorial Council, nor by the Realm constitution that already names him its Heir".

## OPEN-033 choices: literary preferences (author choices, not corrections)

- **OPEN-033-01: prefer (a), unfiled.** Filing repeats 1876 in public, and a court record would become evidence in the death mystery.
- **OPEN-033-02: prefer (a), witnessed and unpublished.** It gives the 1913–14 chiefs a remembered stake. Pair it with OPEN-033-07 (b), because (a) leaves it unclear who knows enough to have the added motives.
- **OPEN-033-03: no strong preference.** (d), with the story assuming (a), works only if line 632's 1913 stance is made readable as an intention.
- **OPEN-033-04: prefer (a).** It keeps the lever simple.
- **OPEN-033-05: prefer (a).** (b) brings in a jurisdiction quarrel the synopsis cannot carry.
- **OPEN-033-06: prefer (b), unnamed Maui kin and Queen's Hospital patrons.** It gives the allies a motive from Noe's approved backstory (Queen's Hospital, loyalist patrons) without inventing anyone.
- **OPEN-033-07: prefer (b), death paragraph verbatim.**
- **OPEN-033-08: strongly prefer (a), winter 1887–88.** (b) puts the bargain right against the death, and the closeness alone implies cause and points toward Victoria.
