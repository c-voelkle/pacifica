## Fixed premises the move must respect (R1, R3)

- Victoria Regent of Pacifica from 1887 and still Queen of Hawaii (CR-027 A24, APPROVED); regency article names her Regent and Julian Heir of the Realm; no succession war, consolidation 1887–89 (CR-016 A1).
- 1886 hānai bargain: Noe's price = health board, place in Julian's life, Hawaiian upbringing; Victoria's = raised as her son and successor (CR-021 F1, APPROVED).
- Noe dies autumn 1888 in a typhoid outbreak she is fighting; certificate by Victoria's physician; servants allege poison; registers vanish a month and reappear in the Office of Public Measures; loyalists and Silas's people call it murder. **No culprit, ever** (CR-021 R9-09; R3 protected mystery).
- Loyalists court her; after Emma's death they also look to Albert Kūnuiākea; no reign or successful revolt for him (CR-027 A16).
- 1914: ʻAha Aliʻi proclaims Julian heir to the Hawaiian crown (Kamehameha VI); plebiscite because Victoria "will not join her crown without asking her people" (CR-029, APPROVED); Noe's Maui descent strengthens the claim; loyalists dispute a hānai son's right; Kahekili named for Noe's line.
- The Office of Public Measures is Victoria's own life's work (CR-027 A23, APPROVED) — Noe's move must not take that from her.

## Mahoe's working analysis (round 1)

**Recentering the instrument.** The baseline frames Noe's opportunity as "claim a regency". That is the wrong prize for her. The Realm regency is an office created by the 1887 Constitution, whose Hawaiian assent came from the illegal Territorial Council; the loyalists who court her reject that constitution, so they cannot coherently back her for it, and she has no force, no faction in the Truce and a bargain she made herself. Her stated ends (character bible) are (1) Hawaiians stop dying and (2) her son on a restored Hawaiian throne. The live contest is therefore not the Realm regency but **the Hawaiian succession and the boy's person** — and here canon has a real, unfilled gap: between 1887 and 1914 nothing establishes Julian as heir to *Victoria's Hawaiian crown*. The regency article makes him Heir of the Realm only; a hānai son is not an heir of her body; the lawful Hawaiian bodies (legislature, Nobles, court) were displaced or ignored after 1876. Noe sits exactly on that gap: Maui chiefly blood, loyalist patrons, a colorable guardian's claim and the confidence of the Regent's opponents.

**Leverage she actually has (to verify, H1):** a natural-guardian claim to the boy's person (strength depends on whether the 1886 hānai was customary only or a legal adoption); the Hawaiian Supreme Court whose 1876 judgment Victoria let stand unenforced (she cannot easily ignore it twice before the loyalists); the loyalists' need for a candidate better than Albert; the registers, which the Regent needs.
