# Literary review: candidate replacement for §23 ("Noe's move, the registers and the heir")

**Input:** `outputs/review-input/candidate-23.md` (marked "Not approved").
**Compared against:** current `book-agent/revision/master_storyline.md` §23 (lines 507–515), §20, §22, §24; `character_bible.md` CT-JULIAN-MOTHER, CT-KAIULANI, CT-ALBERT-KUNUIAKEA and the perspective table; `CR-021.md` F1; `CR-027.md` A16.
**Status of this review:** It gives an editorial opinion only. No canon, CR register or character file was changed.

## Verdict

The aim is right, but this draft doesn't achieve it. Noe needs a move of her own between Elias's death and hers. As written, §23 lets her be courted and then lets her die. The candidate does add a move, but the move has no cost, meets no resistance and has no consequence. It also brings in three problems that make the section weaker than the current text:

1. A new detail settles the deliberately open question of Noe's death against Victoria.
2. Noe is given knowledge of the future that contradicts her established blind spot.
3. A vaccination figure wipes out Victoria's later achievement, which the section says she builds on Noe's work.

There are also several continuity errors, listed separately below. I recommend keeping the goal and the new section title, and rebuilding the inserted paragraph around a move that costs Noe something and draws a reply.

## What works

- **The goal.** Under CR-021 F1, Noe is "a possible regent" whom loyalists court. Turning her from someone courted into someone who acts gives the 1887–88 stretch a dramatic shape. It also puts her alongside Kiyoshi's petition in 1913 (§27), so two of Elias's heirs or heirs' parents try a lawful route to the crown.
- **"Noe does not wait to be courted."** This is a strong hinge sentence. It turns the previous paragraph's passive framing against itself, and it should stay.
- **Timing in January 1888.** Placing the move in the same month as the "Regent's year" telegraphist fire is useful, even if the candidate doesn't use the overlap yet. Noe acts while the factions are counting deaths.

## Main literary problems

### 1. The move succeeds without friction, so nothing happens

> "the Chamber grants the petition by a large majority. Victoria, who admires her courage, accepts every condition at once."

A move that every party accepts straight away isn't a dramatic event. It's an administrative note. Several things are missing:

- **No cost to Noe.** The loyalists who court her reject the 1887 constitution (§21: "Hawaiian loyalists reject it"). When she petitions the Realm's Chamber of Deputies, she accepts the legitimacy of the order her supporters deny. That is a real price, maybe a betrayal, and the candidate never notices it. Spelling it out would give her move weight and give the loyalists a reason to be ambivalent about her as a martyr afterwards. That ambivalence fits the open question of her death.
- **No counter-move from Victoria.** The canon relationship is "respect and fear" (§20; bible CT-JULIAN-MOTHER). "Admires her courage, accepts every condition at once" replaces the fear with admiration and names the emotion directly. The repo's own voice guidance warns against naming emotions directly (`skills/voices.md`, Voice C). A Regent who negotiates hard, gives way on the form and keeps the substance would be truer to the Victoria of §§17 and 24, who "governs through bargaining."
- **No consequence.** The death paragraph that follows is word for word the old one. The petition doesn't change what happens to the registers, how the factions read her death, or what Julian later finds. The move is put in place and then dropped.
- **It repeats what she already has.** The 1886 hānai bargain already gives her "a place in the boy's life and Hawaiian upbringing" (§20). Being named "guardian of the heir's Hawaiian education" mostly re-signs that bargain with a different body. The section doesn't say what new power she gains, so the reader can't tell what she has won.

### 2. The new certificate detail settles the ambiguity against Victoria

> "certified by Victoria's physician, who signs the certificate before he has seen the body, on instructions from the palace."

This is the most damaging change in the candidate. The author has explicitly decided that Noe's death stays ambiguous (CR-021 revision 9; AGENTS.md: "Never establish a murderer"). The character bible says the approved event "preserves real uncertainty rather than making Victoria guilty by implication." A certificate signed unseen on palace orders is guilt by implication.

Seen purely as craft, it still loses more than it gains:

- The same paragraph still ends "the Chronicler can prove none guilty." After the palace detail, that line reads as coy rather than honest.
- The candidate's new paragraph has just given Victoria a fresh motive, a rival who has beaten her before the Chamber. Put together with the certificate detail, the section now reads as a whodunit with the answer supplied.
- The section's closing note, "care, rivalry with a dead woman and possible guilt remain inseparable", only works if the reader really cannot separate them. Julian's later discovery in the sealed papers that "Noe's death had more suspects than he knew" (character trajectories) also needs several suspects to carry weight.

If the author wants more suspicion around the certificate, it has to fall evenly. A detail that also points at Silas's people or the loyalists would deepen the uncertainty instead of resolving it.

### 3. Noe is given knowledge of the future and the narrator's praise

> "Noe sees further than her rivals: she knows that her district registers will one day decide which territories enter the Gulf and how the county league tables rank them."

- **It contradicts her defined blind spot.** In the bible's perspective table, the thing Noe "tends to miss" is "how her rivals will use her honest figures after her death." The candidate makes her foresee exactly that. Her tragedy depends on the opposite: she makes an honest instrument and doesn't see what it will become.
- **It's anachronistic.** The Gulf Republic doesn't exist until 1899–1900 (§25), and its admission test comes later.
- **It gets the direction wrong.** In canon the Gulf is tested for admission to Pacifica. No "territories enter the Gulf."
- **It tells rather than shows.** "Sees further than her rivals" is the narrator's verdict, not something Noe does. Her voice is "clinical observations, exact numbers, dry gallows humor and impatience with waste." A Noe who acts on what she can count now, such as deaths in a district this month, is more convincing than one who prophesies league tables.

### 4. The Anne of Austria comparison breaks the storyline's register

> "Like Anne of Austria during the minority of Louis XIV, she understands that a mother's guardianship of a royal child can outlast any council of ministers."

- AGENTS.md keeps explanatory historical comparisons out of the storyline and in `historical_background.md`. The master storyline "must read as what happens."
- The analogy points to regency. Anne was regent, which is the very claim Noe *doesn't* make in this candidate. It suggests a bigger ambition than the petition delivers.
- It's a European frame imposed by the narrator on a character whose frame is Hawaiian (hānai, a chiefly Maui line from Kahekili). If the author wants a precedent in Noe's own mind, the stronger version is one she could actually know and use, taken from Hawaiian practice for raising aliʻi children. That would need to be chosen and sourced in `historical_background.md`, not made up here.

### 5. "Forty thousand Hawaiians" undoes Victoria's achievement

> "By the summer her vaccinators have reached forty thousand Hawaiians."

CR-021 F1 gives about 40,000 as the whole Hawaiian population in the 1880s. With this figure, Noe has vaccinated practically everyone by mid-1888. That breaks several things:

- §20: "the unfinished work is her achievement, not a miraculous reversal of mortality in three years."
- CR-021 F1 / bible: vaccination is "begun in Honolulu and on Maui and unfinished when she dies."
- The candidate's own next paragraph: Victoria "completes vaccination across the islands in 1888–1892". There would be almost nothing left to complete.

The literary cost is larger than the arithmetic error. The section's design is that Noe lays the unglamorous foundation and Victoria, partly from rivalry, finishes it. The figure gives the finish to Noe and leaves Victoria's motive (outdoing a dead woman) with nothing to act on. If the author wants a number that shows Noe's drive, it should be small, exact and incomplete, like a district count, a set number of weeks or a list of villages not yet reached. That suits her voice and the "limited and foundational" brief. Any figure would need the author's approval and would count as a MODEL estimate.

## Suggestions for an effective move (author's choice, not canon)

These options keep every approved fact (the 1886 bargain, the autumn 1888 typhoid death, the vanished registers, no culprit). Each gives Noe a move with a cost and draws a reply. They're options for the author to judge, not proposed canon.

1. **Custody of the registers.** Noe's real leverage is her figures. Suppose she moves to put the district registers beyond any one faction's reach: lodged with someone other than the Crown, or tied to a condition. Then the existing detail that "the registers disappear for a month and return in the new Office" becomes the payoff of her move instead of a stray mystery, and every faction gains a motive, which deepens the ambiguity.
2. **Using the loyalists without joining them.** She accepts the loyalists' courtship far enough to make the regency threat believable, then trades it to Victoria for something concrete for the health work or for Julian. Victoria gets a victory and a grievance. The loyalists get a reason to call her death murder and a reason to have resented her. That serves the "those who need a martyr" thread.
3. **A move about the boy.** She acts directly on the "Hawaiian upbringing" clause, for example by taking time with Julian on Maui among her mother's people. Victoria is then forced to choose, in public, whether to honour the hānai terms or override them. The scene stays within the 1886 bargain and puts the two women's "respect and fear" on stage.

Whichever option is chosen, some rules keep it in tune with the rest of the section:

- Write the move in Noe's register: numbers, bodies, waste.
- Let Victoria answer with a bargain, not admiration.
- Leave one visible cost to Noe.
- Keep the second paragraph's evidence evenly balanced among suspects.
- Keep the vaccination unfinished, so Victoria still has something to finish and rival.

## Smaller points

- **Title.** "Noe's move" fits the aim. "Noe's death" in the current title gives away the ending, and the new title doesn't.
- **Register.** The inserted paragraph switches from narrating events to the narrator commenting ("sees further", "understands that"). The surrounding sections stay with events and consequences. Cutting the commentary sentences would also fix most of problems 3 and 4.
- **Final paragraph.** It's unchanged and still works. If option 1 is used, "He later finds Noe's registers in the Office's archive" gains an echo: Julian finds the documents his mother tried to protect from the Office he will later corrupt.
