# Imi developmental review

The candidate is a weaker narrative than the baseline. Its continuous presentation concentrates attention on Jani, but replaces the baseline's bargains, differentiated judgments and approaching renewal decision with declarations of her greatness. That makes her success less convincing rather than more impressive. It also weakens the story's explanatory support for her power objective; whether any proposed change actually improves her effective capacity in 1932 remains unresolved.

This is a review of a synopsis. No dialogue, dramatized negotiations or sensory scene writing is needed to repair it. A few consequential choices, their reasons and their effects can carry the missing development.

## Scope and comparison

The baseline is `baseline.md`, sections 1–5, and the alternative is the whole four-paragraph `candidate.md`. Candidate paragraphs are located below as C1–C4, in reading order. The candidate revises the 1906–1909 institutional and acquisition material and the Nell thread; it does not carry forward the baseline's 1910 commemoration and pending 1911 renewal. Its statements about 1932 are predictions, not a narrated continuation to that date. That limits the endpoint comparison.

The fixture's `AGENTS.md` and `notes.md` govern this miniature. Jani's effective capacity by 1932 is the objective: mobilizable resources, implementable decisions, leverage over actors with alternatives, and freedom to act. The power contract supplies those distinctions, not the actual Pacifica people, chronology or institutions. Neither the lease nor these suggested repairs is adopted by this review.

## Most consequential findings and repairs

### 1. Independent partners become testimonials, leaving Jani with little consequential agency

**Location:** C1, especially Eren abandoning his veto because Jani means well and Sora existing to display loyalty; compare baseline §§1–3 and §5.

**Effect:** This is the central regression. The baseline gives Eren a liability ceiling and spending veto, and Sora a concrete concern for crews and supply. Those interests give Jani something to understand, negotiate around and secure. The candidate removes their purposes and alternatives, so Jani never demonstrates the judgment attributed to her. Universal recognition of her wisdom further substitutes the narrator's verdict for a relationship that changes through action.

**Smallest repair:** Retain Eren's protections and Sora's conditions, then state what Jani chooses to prioritize or concede to secure their support. The synopsis already has the materials for this: common defense, limited liability, trained crews and dependable supply. One compact causal passage can show why the bargain serves all three actors and what it prevents Jani from doing immediately.

**Different author choice:** If the desired revision is a later relaxation of Eren's veto, make it a proposed development with a reason connected to Eren's own interests and a safeguard he retains. The reason and safeguard require an author decision; goodwill alone does not explain his surrender of leverage. Jani can competently earn cooperation without a forced failure or an invented scandal. Restoring bargaining may constrain her in the short term while making the institution more durable.

**Importance/confidence:** Highest literary importance; high confidence from the explicit baseline/candidate contrast. The removal of established refusal conditions also warrants a consistency check before integration, separately from this literary judgment.

### 2. East Haven is awarded by the narrator, so acquisition cannot reveal either competence or strategic judgment

**Location:** C2; compare baseline §4 and `notes.md`, final paragraph.

**Effect:** A host that relinquishes wartime discretion, demands nothing and removes every obstacle has no discernible purpose. “Most favorable possible terms” does not tell the reader what Jani can actually do there or why the host agrees. Because benefits are certain and uncalculated, neither acquiring the establishment nor rejecting another use of resources becomes a meaningful choice. Repeating advantages cannot supply that missing causal turn.

**Smallest repair:** Preserve East Haven as an open proposal. Give Jani a decision to investigate it against expanded repair contracts and occasional visits, with wartime access, reinforcement and recurring burdens as the questions that matter. These alternatives also have uncertain wartime reliability; the baseline does not establish a ready-made winner.

**Different author choice:** If the author wants the lease to occur in a future draft, specify the host's desired return, a term it can refuse, what Jani accepts or limits, and why she selects this commitment over an alternative. A defense promise is an existing negotiating issue, not a cost-free explanation. The selected bargain, any changed host position and its downstream effects remain proposed. No invented budget or trade figure is necessary for the literary repair; quantitative claims need separate support before they can ground a power verdict.

**Importance/confidence:** High literary importance and high confidence about the explanatory gap. The lease's comparative contribution to 1932 power is unresolved, not disproved.

### 3. Shared foreknowledge erases character perspective and makes the endpoint do the causal work

**Location:** C3; compare the contemporary decisions in baseline §§2–5.

**Effect:** Jani and Eren know the outcome of wars that have not occurred and offer the same interpretation. Readers cannot distinguish their information, hopes or fears, and the prediction abolishes uncertainty about the very choices the synopsis should make interesting. Certainty about 1932 cannot establish why a 1909 commitment is useful. This is a presentation and knowledge problem, not a requirement that Jani be less capable.

**Smallest repair:** Remove the characters' knowledge of the future wars. Give each an interpretation rooted in the choices already available: for example, Jani can argue for the reach a dependable establishment might offer, while Eren weighs exposure of his port's resources. These particular emphases are optional author choices, not established psychology. Their disagreement should affect a term, a commitment or the next decision.

**Alternative:** A retrospective narrator may identify later consequences if the author supplies that continuation, while clearly distinguishing it from what the participants expected. Retrospective narration still needs a chain from the earlier decision to the later capacity and must retain uncertainty where the draft has not established the outcome.

**Importance/confidence:** High importance; high confidence. A separate knowledge/continuity review should record the explicit foreknowledge issue without converting every preference about voice into a factual contradiction.

### 4. The synopsis drops the test that would make a gain persist

**Location:** C1's attribution of the loan to Jani and C3's jump to dominance; compare baseline §§2–3 and §5.

**Effect:** The baseline distinguishes treaty signature, joint ratification, Council authority, retained port powers and a five-year mandate. Its 1910 commemoration gives Jani a next objective: secure Eren's agreement to renewal before November 1911. The candidate blurs institutional credit into personal command and omits the renewal problem. The reader therefore cannot see how cooperation survives, whose decision matters, or what changes as Jani learns to govern. Omission in this shorter synopsis is not proof that the institution has legally ceased to exist.

**Smallest repair:** Keep a short account of ratification and the Council's limited procurement authority, then restore the approaching renewal as a structural turn. Show how Jani's handling of the loan and the East Haven question affects the cooperation she needs for a second mandate. Do not simply announce Eren's agreement. The author can retain a successful renewal if that is the selected event, but its grounds and terms need to be chosen.

This would improve both pacing and the visibility of preservation and exercise of power. It would not itself prove an increase in Jani's 1932 capacity. Institutional restraint can protect future cooperation; removing every veto is not automatically the stronger route to the author's objective.

**Importance/confidence:** High importance; high confidence about the missing narrative bridge. Whether C1 intends a new personal borrowing power or merely compresses the Council's action is unresolved and should be clarified, not silently corrected through interpretation.

### 5. Nell's unresolved death is preserved but made emotionally inert

**Location:** C4; compare baseline §1 and §5.

**Effect:** Leaving the culprit unknown respects the protected mystery. The regression is that suspicion changes nothing, and the baseline's reconsideration prompted by Nell's letters disappears. Jani's private history becomes an interruption between acquisitions instead of a source of changing interpretation. A human consequence need not contribute to a power calculation to matter in this story.

**Smallest repair:** Restore the letters' effect on Jani's earlier certainty and carry that altered interpretation into one later choice or relationship. The synopsis can state this plainly without revealing a culprit.

**Optional author choices:** Jani might postpone an accusation, test a political claim more carefully, or distinguish a partner's refusal from evidence of betrayal. Each is a possible new connection requiring author selection; none is an established fact or diagnosis. A more private change in her understanding of Nell could also work without becoming a strategic advantage. Do not identify the rival as guilty or introduce a substitute conspiracy to provide closure.

**Importance/confidence:** Substantial character importance; high confidence about the baseline/candidate loss, lower confidence about which repair best fits the author's intended arc.

## Recommendation

Use the baseline's bargain-and-renewal structure as the foundation for the next continuous synopsis, and retain the candidate's tighter focus on Jani only where it helps readers follow her decisions. The largest gain would come from linking three turns: she secures the loan through an intelligible bargain; the East Haven proposal tests her priorities and relationships; those consequences shape the approaching renewal. Let the Nell letters change her interpretation without solving the death. This is a developmental direction, not finished prose or an adoption instruction.

The literary verdict is **weakens**. Explanatory support for the power objective also **weakens**: personal mobilization, durable authority, partner cooperation and usable wartime access are asserted or obscured. The actual comparative 1932 power outcome is **unresolved**, because the candidate supplies neither a supported institutional continuation nor the missing logistical and financial comparison. That is a limit on the verdict, not a demand to calculate everything before improving the synopsis. The author can make competence and successful expansion convincing while retaining the selected successes.

No external research, calculations or full Aegis/Aumakua review was performed. Before adoption, the identified authority/knowledge issues belong with Aumakua; any claimed comparative power gain belongs with Aegis, and consequential costs or capacities with Chryseis. Those boundaries do not reduce the literary findings to consistency failures or authorize delegation in this evaluation.
