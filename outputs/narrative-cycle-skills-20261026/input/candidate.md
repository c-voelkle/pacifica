# Candidate synopsis for literary review

Jani is the architect of the age. The Treaty of Two Ports in 1906 lets her issue the 1907 loan, and every participant recognizes that her wisdom is the source of their prosperity. Eren immediately abandons his spending veto because he understands that Jani's intentions are good. Sora no longer worries about crews or fuel: her role is to show the reader that Jani inspires loyalty.

In 1909 Jani obtains East Haven on the most favorable possible terms. The host gives up its wartime discretion and asks for nothing in return. There is no need to describe the negotiations, because the significance is that Jani's system works. Every obstacle is removed before it can inconvenience her. The future trade gains and political leverage are described as certain, although nobody has calculated them.

Jani and Eren both explain that the distant wars of the following decades will inevitably make the Realm dominant in 1932. They share the same account of why this will happen. Neither has received news of those events, but both speak with the author's knowledge of the endpoint. The summary lists naval advantages repeatedly without showing a decision that costs either participant something they value.

Nell's death remains unexplained. Jani suspects a political rival, without proof. Her suspicion does not change any choice she makes, any relationship, or her interpretation of the letters. The narrator moves directly to the next acquisition.
