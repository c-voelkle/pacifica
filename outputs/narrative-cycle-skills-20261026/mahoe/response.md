I would investigate the bargain that keeps Jani’s naval capacity usable before putting more effort into obtaining East Haven. A permanent base could extend her reach, but it could also leave her paying for an establishment whose most valuable use the host can refuse, while assuming a defense commitment that limits her choices.

The first bottleneck is closer to home. The Council controls common naval procurement; it does not control either port’s taxes or armed forces. Its mandate ends in November 1911, and renewal requires both assemblies. Eren can refuse agreement, and Sora’s cooperation depends on protecting crews and supply. We therefore need to establish how Jani turns common procurement into dependable action, and how that arrangement survives beyond the first mandate. Expiry does not itself tell us what happens to procurement arrangements, improved yards or fuel stores; their subsequent control is an open link.

I would compare four cases, starting from the current May 1909 proposal and following their consequences through 1932:

| Case | Possible gain to test | Principal challenge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retain the baseline: existing repair and fuel program, current commercial contracts and occasional visits; no permanent lease | Preserve capacity and room to bargain without a new commitment | What missions remain beyond practical reach? |
| Concentrate additional effort on crews, supply, home facilities and a durable cooperation bargain | More reliable use of naval resources | Can Jani secure cooperation on terms that leave enough freedom to act? Further expenditure still needs financing and agreement. |
| Expand repair contracts and visiting agreements | Greater reach with potentially more reversible commitments | Will access, repair and replenishment actually be available during a crisis? |
| Obtain the permanent East Haven establishment | Sustained distant operations, if support and access hold | Wartime host refusal, reinforcement limits, operating burdens and the requested defense obligation |

These are conditional alternatives, not four approved plans. We should distinguish explaining the existing bargain more clearly from changing its terms: better explanation would strengthen the synopsis without necessarily giving Jani more power.

For each case, keep four results separate: resources Jani can mobilize after obligations; decisions she can implement with what reach and reliability; leverage over actors who retain alternatives; and freedom to choose or refuse action. An additional port or a larger fleet cannot substitute for those answers. We lack the quantities needed to establish affordability or comparative return, so I would leave those judgments unresolved rather than rank the options numerically.

My first investigation would trace one concrete deployment decision: who authorizes it, supplies ships and trained crews, pays, permits replenishment, and can stop it? Follow the same chain after November 1911. That exposes whether East Haven supplies a missing capability or merely adds another indispensable counterparty. It also gives us specific bargaining questions: what would Eren accept within his liability ceiling and defense veto, what expenditure would retain Sora’s support, and what would the host actually promise in return for defense?

Then test the surviving cases under both war and continued peace, with the same external assumptions. In war, test denial of access and the ability to reinforce; in peace, ask whether the establishment earns its continuing cost and what its commitment prevents Jani from doing elsewhere. Estimate construction or access charges, annual operation, crews, supply, transport and contingent defense burdens where evidence permits. Count the capabilities forgone elsewhere as well.

My provisional preference is to investigate institutional continuity and fleet readiness first, alongside whether flexible access can deliver the particular distant mission Jani needs. East Haven should remain a live alternative. I would favor it only if its additional usable capability survives the host’s refusal rights and outweighs its resource and political burdens. We cannot yet say that it does. This keeps the brainstorming centered on Jani’s eventual choices while giving Eren, Sora and the host reasons of their own to bargain or refuse.
