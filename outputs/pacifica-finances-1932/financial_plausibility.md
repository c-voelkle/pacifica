# CR-018 / CR-019 financial assessment through 1932

Updated 18 October 2026. These are alternate-history planning scenarios in period gold-dollar equivalents, not newly approved numerical canon. CR-018 and CR-019 determine the events and political endpoint; this assessment tests the resources required to reach it.

The Siberian coalition is financially plausible at the working scale, with substantial Japanese, Chinese and Siberian contributions. Pacifica can also act as the decisive lender and settlement center in a syndicated creditor order. Financing the full historical US war-loan programme exceeds the modeled capacity. The 1932 order still needs an explicit Union bank recapitalization: the gold rescue alone leaves **$36.96 million of bank equity unfunded**. The working case also exceeds the extended 4% peacetime military/overseas guideline in 1931–32.

## Current deliverables and use

- National financial model, 1846–1932 (local workbook; not published): the existing treasury, population, economy, credit and programme schedules, with new **Siberia**, **External credit**, **Common finance** and **Reserves** schedules.
- Kaiulani programme, 1858–1932 (local workbook; not published): six further funding phases, annual age cohorts, public/private cash funding and housing debt.

The national model has one case selector, **Assumptions!D120**: 1 Working scale, 2 Adverse, 3 Full Entente scale. Annual driver groups show Active first, followed by those three input cases. Both files are saved in the working case. **Checks** is a terminal diagnostic sheet; operating results do not depend on it. Red funding requirements remain unresolved. Results after a case's first funding failure describe conditional obligations rather than a financed historical path.

## What changed financially

The extension prices Japan's 1904–05 war credit, Pacifican wartime lending and naval operations, the 1918–22 Siberian coalition, the permanent frontier, Japanese earthquake reconstruction and bank support, the 1927 common institutions, depression losses, moratoria, countercyclical credit and the Union gold rescue. It also removes the artificial end to programme funding in 1900.

Japanese crisis loans have matching cash uses. The working case includes $250m of earthquake reconstruction in 1923 against $150m of foreign credit, and $200m of bank equity subscriptions in 1927 against $200m of foreign credit. Japan's own revenue, cash and domestic bonds cover its remaining obligations. Its domestic economy and taxes stay outside Pacifica's accounts.

There is no assumed Russian indemnity, German reparations windfall, sale value for treaty base rights, annexation of foreign GDP or access to all Chinese customs revenue. Gulf oil belongs to an associated sovereign. Core oil is inside the minerals allowance and is taxed as a fraction of sales; the unverified claim about supplying half the world's oil supplies no extra revenue.

Continuing the programme changes 1901–05 settlement within the inherited migration framework. Relative to the previous model, 1905 core residents rise from 7.760m to 7.828m and GDP from $3,641.89m to $3,670.78m. Closing treasury cash falls from $337.82m to $208.28m, principally because public participation in the new $400m Japanese syndicate costs $140m. Public debt remains $54.55m. Fiscal history through 1900 and programme cohorts through 1900 are preserved.

Japan's $1bn two-year war allowance includes active fleet operations; ordinary navy spending is therefore excluded from that same war-period cash charge. Home-army and civilian costs continue separately. The $400m foreign syndicate covers part of the war, with Japanese taxes and domestic bonds financing the rest. An official Japanese historical exhibit puts the real war's cost near 2bn yen against roughly 400m yen of annual 1905 government receipts, supporting the need for substantial domestic as well as foreign financing. The alternate budget is an allowance, not a reproduction of those accounts. [JACAR historical exhibit](https://jacar.go.jp/exhibition/nichiro2/sensoushi/hayawakari03_03.html).

## Siberia: the affordable version and its limits

Partner loans are financing, additional to Pacifica's direct expenditure; they are not added a second time to coalition resource cost. Japanese domestic bonds and external debt service are tested in its separate budget. Chinese cash availability refers to regional collection headroom after ordinary needs, rather than the entire Chinese tax base. Siberian availability likewise assumes civilian subsistence is funded first.

The modeled force reaches 300,000 in 1921, including 90,000 Japanese, 75,000 Chinese, 100,000 Siberians, 25,000 Pacificans and 10,000 Czechs. National annual pay/equipment allowances are $700, $350, $300, $1,500 and $700 per person respectively. Material, railway repair/rolling stock, sea/air/river transport and relief are additional. Czech upkeep is funded inside common procurement, so every coalition cost has an assigned contributor. These force strengths and unit costs remain estimates.

This is a significant campaign, consistent with the scale warning in a contemporary diplomatic report estimating 100m yen annually to keep three Japanese divisions in Siberia. The report is a historical comparator, not an audited cost of this alternate operation. [FRUS, 1919 Russia, document 656](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1919Russia/d656).

At peak force, the working supply allowance requires **4,100 tonnes per day**, against an assumed **5,040 tonnes** of usable rail capacity. It assumes 600 useful tonnes per loaded train, twelve loaded departures daily and 70% usable capacity. A 5,000km route at an effective 10km/hour plus terminal time implies roughly 536 equivalent trainsets. Annual sea cargo demand peaks near **0.90m tonnes**, against **1.44m tonnes** of allocated carrying capacity. These are capacity requirements to verify, not established railway or fleet inventories. The railway provision repairs and equips an existing system; it does not construct 5,000km of new railway.

The adverse case raises procurement costs 50%, lowers partner collections, and restricts transport. Coalition cost rises to **$2,603.25m**, Pacifica's direct share to **$1,211.85m**, and unfunded partner obligations total **$269.59m**. Rail shortages reach 3,260 tonnes/day and sea shortages 642,100 tonnes/year. More treasury cash cannot resolve the modeled physical shortages. Actual 1918 Chinese troop movements were delayed by rolling-stock shortages, which supports treating this as an independent constraint. [FRUS, 1918, document 107](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1918/d107).

After 1922, the working case retains an $80m annual standing-front budget, split 60% Pacifica / 40% Japan under the alliance in 1923–26. From 1927 the common navy costs $300m annually, with $10m for common administration, rising to $18m in 1932 for the enlarged overseas command. Pacifica then pays 65%; Japan pays 35%. The earlier cost sharing does not create common ministries before 1927. The quota is an open treaty choice represented by an editable estimate. Japan's separate budget accumulates cash and borrows for residual deficits, with assumed domestic bond issuance capped at 15% of its GDP annually; actual domestic investor capacity still needs evidence. The naval allowance is broadly comparable at old gold parity with Britain's approximately £58m 1927 estimate; fleet composition and combat effectiveness still need a separate audit. [Hansard, 24 November 1927](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1927-11-24/debates/b1b044f1-191f-480c-8b53-7c582174f50a/CommonsChamber).

## The creditor order: feasible scale and financing sources

The working Entente facility advances **$3bn during 1914–18**: $150m, $400m, $700m, $900m and $850m. The 1918 allowance is committed before the November cutoff. The model allocates 35% to the treasury, 45% to core private subscribers and 20% to neutral foreign subscribers: **$1.05bn public, $1.35bn local private and $0.60bn foreign**. Public lending is an asset subscription, not ordinary expenditure; private syndication is not treasury cash.

This requires higher wartime taxes, private saving of 25% of nonmining output and compression of domestic private investment to 12%. Potential productivity growth slows and civilian output availability falls during the wars. Core private subscriptions, new government bonds and bank equity all compete for the same finite savings. Neutral foreign funding is independently capped at $200m/year in the working case. Loan-financed exports redirect existing production and create no second GDP or tax gain.

The full-scale case uses **$8.5bn** of Entente advances, reflecting a contemporary US Treasury expectation for historical government war loans. The same document put Britain's debt to the US near $4bn; the working model's British share is only $1.8bn. This comparison tests scale, not identical instruments or borrowers. [FRUS, December 1918 Treasury correspondence](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1919Parisv02/d455).

Under that full-scale case, treasury cash requirements go unfunded in 1916–18, domestic private funding is short by as much as **$446m in 1917**, and neutral foreign funding is short by about **$392m that year**. Gold cover fails from 1916, reaching a roughly **$1.07bn deficit in 1918**. A recovered 1932 cash balance does not finance those earlier failures. The narrative needs Pacifica's control of settlement and marginal credit, substantial syndication, and credible alternative sources for the rest of the Entente's requirements.

The inherited prosperity assumptions also matter. Working GDP is $5.62bn for 9.61m core residents in 1913 and $12.15bn for 13.17m in 1929; those are planning estimates rather than verified censuses or national accounts. A separate recalculation rebased nonmining productivity 25% lower from 1906, preserving subsequent growth, population and commitments. It leaves treasury target shortfalls of **$14.48m in 1931 and $191.76m in 1932**, domestic investor-funding shortages up to **$208.74m**, and a smaller neutral-funding gap in 1917–18. Gold remains positive. The original input was restored. The order therefore depends on maintaining the prosperous core assumed in the model, or reducing commitments / changing its financing; a low debt ratio alone does not establish affordability. The recalculated results are retained in [macro-sensitivity.json](macro-sensitivity.json).

British debt uses a reduced 2% cash coupon and a fixed principal installment over 62 years from 1923. France uses separate terms. Gold-dollar denomination protects the unit of account when sterling falls, but cash payments stop in 1931–32 under the modeled moratorium. Book impairments are noncash losses, distinct from missed principal and coupons. Treaty concessions supply no invented cash payment.

## 1931–32: gold and bank equity must both be available

The Union's illustrative European loan book reaches **$900m**, funded by **$500m of San Francisco advances and $400m of its own financing**. This onlending is counted once. The modeled European lenders' assumed $300m initial equity absorbs $180m of losses in 1931 and $108m in 1932, leaving $12m against a $612m surviving loan book. At an 8% capital convention it needs $48.96m equity: **the remaining $36.96m is unfunded**. This is a test of that lending portfolio, not a complete Union banking-system balance sheet. It can be supplied by Union owners, its treasury, or an explicitly agreed capital tranche; the model does not silently convert a gold loan into equity.

Pacifica supplies **$400m of gold rescue loans** over winter 1931–32, and approximately **$292m of domestic bank recapitalization** over those two years. The latter is an additional public financial asset, not an overseas gold shipment. Both cash uses enter the treasury once. The Union rescue's size is provisional: a full Union balance of payments and bank/deposit inventory is still needed to establish whether $400m is sufficient.

Treasury cash and monetary gold are distinct tests and must not be added together as spendable resources. Monetary gold includes treasury holdings, foreign gold deposits have matching callable liabilities, and loan books are not bullion. The gold result depends on an unverified $400m opening stock in 1901, a normal external surplus of 0.8% of GDP, 55% core procurement from new foreign loans, and retention of 60% of new core gold output. It is therefore conditional on an actual export and bullion-acquisition history.

The adverse case lowers export procurement, raises deposit withdrawals and rescue needs, and increases bank losses. Gold cover first fails in 1917; physical gold is **negative $458m in 1932**, with a **$975m cover deficit**. A rigid, unlimited redemption promise is incompatible with that case. The adjustable 1932 monetary settlement needs explicit reserve rules, refinancing and redemption terms.

The 30% core debt and 15% interest diagnostics pass after 1900 in the working case, but military and overseas cash exceed the extended 4% peacetime guideline in 1931–32. Its original authority is period-specific; continuation here is a visible planning convention. Crisis financing, military reductions or a revised later rule must be stated explicitly. Earlier 1866/1876 military and 1868 interest exceptions remain visible.

## Programme continuation and checks

Through 1932 the programme spends **$347.31m**, receives **$63.70m in direct public grants**, and attributes **2.907m surviving additional residents** to its cohorts. Closing programme reserves are **$7.52m**, and housing credit outstanding is **$40.36m**. Its attributed annual fiscal return turns negative during 1930–32, although cumulative cash return remains $351.73m and discounted return $12.12m under the inherited attribution rules. These are cohort estimates, not an extra national revenue stream. Public grants enter the national treasury once; attributed taxes, services, capital and descendants are already within aggregate envelopes.

All three national cases and the 75-year programme passed **3,267 independent annual comparisons**, with representative loan-size, retention and lower-productivity changes restored. Saved workbook formulas, cached results, historical preservation, native chart links and roll-forward identities passed **2,382 saved-result / identity checks and 1,035 historical-cell checks**, recorded in [verification.json](verification.json). No formula errors or missing cached results were found. Failed funding and policy diagnostics remain visible, especially the Union's $36.96m capital gap. These checks validate the calculations; they do not establish lender consent, coalition victory, Russian-majority political consent, actual train capacity or the sufficiency of the Union rescue.
