# Pacifica financial effects of CR-015 and CR-016

**Updated:** 18 October 2026. **Status:** Reconciled financial scenarios; historical and operational feasibility remains conditional.

The changes substantially reduce projected borrowing by 1895. They replace costly Philippine pacification and a succession war with smaller commitments, remove an extra Western cash payment, and defer most canal construction until after 1889. A smaller population and tax base partly offset those gains. Both tested cases meet their cash requirements, but several policy ceilings still fail.

The current deliverables are the national model for 1846–1905 (local workbook; not published) and the international programme for 1858–1900 (local workbook; not published). They supersede conflicting assumptions in the dated 17 October variants, which remain unchanged as comparison records. The governing story decisions are [CR-015](../../book-agent/revision/CR-015.md), [CR-016](../../book-agent/revision/CR-016.md) and the [Power Timeline](../../book-agent/revision/power_timeline_1865_1900.md). New prices, participation shares and military allowances remain model inputs, not newly approved canon.

## Effect by 1895

All money is in millions of period gold-dollar equivalents. The saved baseline’s Philippine local receipts are netted against local expenditure to make the budget comparison consistent with the revised core treasury. This normalization changes neither its budget balance nor debt.

The old headline population of 16.35 million included 7.5 million Philippine residents and 2.5 million Formosan residents. Their removal from the headline is a change of jurisdictional accounting. Japan, China, Korea and the Confederacy also remain outside core GDP. The revised core population includes a small proposed Baja population; selective generic migration produces the net reduction above.

The reduction in debt compounds over time. Through 1895, the cumulative cash budget before interest improves by approximately **$49.55m**, after accounting for the old noncash vendor recognition. Lower borrowing then reduces cumulative interest by approximately **$79.04m**. Loan subscriptions, repayments, bond redemptions and retained cash explain the remainder of the debt difference. The favorable result still depends on inherited productivity, mineral production and fiscal collection assumptions.

## Costs and commitments that changed

| Item | Revised financial treatment |
|---|---|
| Western settlement | $40m entirely as vendor credit, recognized in 1867 and repaid in five $8m instalments through 1872; retained 5% coupon estimate. The old additional $10m immediate payment disappears. |
| Baja and Alaska | Alaska retains the $7.2m comparator. Baja adds an explicit $5m base purchase allowance, $10m in the downside case; proposed acquisition and population are conditional. |
| Spanish islands | Retain a $15m purchase allowance in 1876. Replace $6m of earlier ground operations with a $6m Madrid loan syndicate, 20% publicly funded. Reduce the full extraordinary-operations provision from $47.5m, including those earlier operations, to $12.25m after purchase through 1895. The ordinary Philippine budget remains separate; only its shortfall enters the core treasury. |
| Managed succession | Replace $8m of emergency operations with $1.5m over 1887–1889. Remove the extra 1888 army mobilization, combat replacements and national output penalty. The possible Donner standoff is within the consolidation allowance. |
| Canal | Base gross budget $265m: $10m railroad in 1880, $30m French works in 1889 and $225m construction over 1890–1902. At the retained 20% public share, full public funding is $53m versus $75m previously. Cash paid by 1895 is $28.77m versus $32m; the full $22m difference is not an immediate saving. No canal dividends are assumed. |
| Compensation bonds | Suspend borrower coupons and amortization in 1893–1895. Pay $5.70m of guaranteed coupons to external holders. Impair the public asset separately, with no invented cash recovery for guarantee calls. |
| China and Japan | Remove the Liaodong / Triple Intervention indemnity increment. Use an editable $75m moderate Chinese facility and $20m Japan gold facility, drawn 1895–1897, each 15% publicly funded. Formosa contributes no annexed population or tax base. |
| Later commitments | Extend the forecast to 1905. Include Primorye sea operations of $6m in each of 1898 and 1899, a $30m Japanese war syndicate with 20% public participation, Chinese rail credit, Cuban debt at cash acquisition cost, Cuban health spending, Nicaragua rights, Baring participation, cable capital and node operations. |
| Trans-Siberian prevention | Price $0.5m annually in 1899–1905 as support to Japan, $3.5m total. The outcome is canonical; this allowance chooses neither a prevention method nor a permanent settlement. The downside allowance is $2m annually. |

Oil, turbines, electricity, docks, statistics and public services must fit the existing core capital and civil-service envelopes. Those envelopes prevent duplicate spending but do not establish the quantities, engineering capacity or delivery dates. No extra GDP or foreign customs windfall is added to finance them. Removing British, Afghan or automatic 1903–1904 Russian war claims yields no measured saving: the old model ending in 1895 had not priced those wars.

## Programme effect

Removing the 1887–1888 birth, mortality and private-subscription shocks raises projected additional residents in 1900 from **616,362 to 622,578**. The legacy intake schedule, training places, scholarships and direct public grants remain unchanged. Direct public grants total **$12.92m** over 1858–1900, and programme expenditure remains **$81.56m**.

More surviving children initially require services and capital before entering the adult tax base. Cumulative attributed public cash return therefore falls from **$15.16m to $14.88m**, and the discounted return falls from approximately **$1.00m to $0.96m**. Programme cash remains positive. These attributed receipts and costs are already inside national aggregate envelopes and must not be added to the treasury again. Regional aggregate demography and the standalone age cohorts are not yet one unified population model.

## Checks and remaining decisions

The exported workbooks contain no formula errors. Independent calculations reconcile all 60 national years and all 43 programme years. A saved-file review passed **1,544** numerical checks, verified restored base inputs and confirmed that the source workbooks are unchanged. Cash, debt, loan assets, population, local budgets and funding splits reconcile; changing case inputs and the railway allowance recalculates the outputs. The [verification record](verification.json) retains the exceptions.

| Policy test | Base result | Higher-cost and tighter-migration result |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum treasury cash | No shortfall | No shortfall |
| Gross debt ≤30% of North American GDP from 1865 | Maximum approximately 29.94% in 1867 | 31.27% in 1867; fails |
| Interest ≤15% of core receipts from 1865 | 15.0020% in 1868; narrowly fails | 15.6960% in 1868; fails |
| Military and overseas cash ≤4% in peacetime | 4.0323% in 1866 and 5.3999% in 1876; fails | Same years fail |
| Transport works, housing investment, domestic saving and programme reserve diagnostics | No shortfall in the specified checks | No shortfall in the specified checks |

The 4% test uses a conservative definition that includes overseas public loan and equity subscriptions. Declared combat years 1865, 1870–1871, 1878 and 1898–1899 are visibly exempt from that peacetime test; debt and interest ceilings are not exempt. Founding years before 1865 remain outside the Power Timeline’s policy period and have much tighter finances.

The normal case has only about **$0.24m of debt capacity** below the 1867 ceiling. A larger Baja payment or earlier Western debt recognition can remove that margin. The 1876 Spanish purchase causes the largest peacetime cash spike. Staging acquisition payments or revisiting the ceiling would require a financial or story choice; no such choice is made silently.

Ten-year acquisition payback, actual lender consent, sovereign customs coverage, fuel and shipping capacity, and canal operating returns remain unproved. The 1861 core population is approximately 0.76 million rather than the open 1.2 million aspiration, while the migration filter lowers the inherited five-million coastal target. Ultimate Pacific Empire control and later British / Indian / Siberian wars remain unselected. The model is a conditional financing case, not a completed historical feasibility finding.
