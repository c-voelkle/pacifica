# European technical institutions 1850–1890: size benchmarks

Purpose: calibration data for the fictional California "Polytechnic". These are historical figures only, gathered on 2026-10-27.
Rule: no invented numbers. A blank cell (–) means no source was found in this pass. Each figure is tagged by reliability:
**E** = exact figure from a primary or official statistic; **A** = approximate (rounded, OCR-read, or from a secondary source); **C** = sources conflict.

Caveats that apply throughout:
- "Students" means different things in different sources. Some count regular students only. Others include auditors (Hospitanten/Hörer), preparatory classes, or both semesters. Figures from different institutions are not strictly comparable.
- Several of the numbers were read from OCR scans by an extraction tool, not from a clean transcription. These are marked **A** and should be checked against the scan before they are quoted in print.

### Bergakademie Freiberg
- Source: de.wikipedia "TU Bergakademie Freiberg", citing a university history (footnotes 7, 52–54). https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universit%C3%A4t_Bergakademie_Freiberg [A, as a pointer]. Note that Freiberg was very small in this period, never more than about 150 students.
  - Enrolment: 1805: 70 students; 1830s: about 75; 1845: 64; 1866: about 150 enrolled at once; 1870/71: only 56. Average new enrolments 1801–1850: 23/yr.
  - Cumulative matriculations to 1850: 1,681 (981 Saxon, 464 other German, 203 other European, 33 overseas).
  - Cumulative to 1866: 2,465 (1,225 Saxon, 782 other German, 326 other European, 132 non-European). Non-German students were therefore 19% of all matriculations since 1765.

- Zeuner was director 1871–75. He held this jointly with Dresden from 1873. https://saebi.isgv.de/biografie/Gustav_Zeuner_(1828-1907)

## 3. Growth speed: the fastest growers

| Institution | Start | Peak / later | Time | Factor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ETH Zürich | 71 students (1855/56) | about 400/yr (1860–65 average) | 5–8 yrs | about 5.6× |
| ETH Zürich | about 490/yr (1865–70) | about 570/yr (1870s), then a dip to 423 (1880–85) | — | — |
| ETH Zürich (professors) | 21 (1855) | 49 (1879/80) | 25 yrs | — |
| ETH Zürich (Privatdozenten) | 3 (1855) | 39 (1879/80) | 25 yrs | — |
| TH München | 380 (1868/69) | 1,329 (1875/76) | 7 yrs | 3.5× |
| Karlsruhe (boom under Redtenbacher) | 448 (1855/56) | 876 (1860/61) | 5 yrs | 2× |
| Berlin TH | — | 1,180 students in its first semester (1879) | — | — |
| Dresden (1870s–80s) | 281 (1871) | 308 (1890) | 19 yrs | flat |

- ETH's foreign share settled at 45–58%.
- Munich fell back to 717 by 1885/86. Its foreign share peaked at 28% in 1875/76.
- Karlsruhe then fell to about 480 by 1867/68. Its growth in this period came mostly from students outside Baden.
- Berlin TH did not grow into its size: it was created by merging the Gewerbeakademie and the Bauakademie.
- Dresden's student boom came only in the 1890s, rising to 1,007 by 1900.
- **Pattern:** every boom was followed by a 30–50% slump after 1875. That slump was cyclical and affected the whole sector, not one institution.

## 4. Poaching a founding cohort: ETH 1855

- The founding faculty was 21 professors plus 11 other teachers (Senti), or "31 original professors" (Princeton). ETHistory names Kinkel, de Sanctis, Vischer, Cherbuliez, Clausius, Culmann, Reuleaux and Semper as the newly appointed stars who drew students "from all over Europe".
- **German recruits verified in this pass:**
  - Gustav Zeuner, a Saxon from the Bergakademie Freiberg: technical mechanics, 1855–71; director from 1865 (Sächsische Biografie).
  - F. Th. Vischer, "deutsche Literarhistoriker", from Tübingen (Senti).
  - Carl Culmann, a Karlsruhe graduate called in 1855 from the Bavarian state railways (Princeton page).
- **German recruits from standard biography, not re-verified this pass:**
  - Gottfried Semper (architecture), a refugee from the 1849 Dresden uprising, called from London.
  - Rudolf Clausius (physics), from Berlin.
  - Franz Reuleaux (mechanical engineering), called in 1856.
  - Pompejus Bolley (chemical technology).
  - Georg Städeler (chemistry).
- **Others in the founding group:** Francesco de Sanctis, an Italian exile, and Swiss scholars such as Burckhardt, Escher von der Linth, Heer and Mousson.
- **Tally:** at least 3 Germans are verified and about 8 are probable, out of about 31 teachers. That is roughly a quarter of the founding faculty, and the core of the engineering and science chairs.
- The traffic later reversed (standard biography, not re-verified): Reuleaux went to the Berlin Gewerbeakademie in 1864, Vischer returned to Tübingen in 1866, Clausius went to Würzburg in 1867, and Zeuner went to Freiberg in 1871. ETH served as a springboard as well as a magnet.
- The ETH Schulrat president Kappeler personally visited candidates and took advice from German authorities; for example, he asked Dirichlet about Riemann and Dedekind. Source: EMS Newsletter 109. https://ems.press/doi/pdf/10.4171/NEWS/109/7
- **Other examples:**
  - The Royal College of Chemistry in London (1845) recruited A. W. Hofmann from Liebig's circle as its first professor.
  - ETHistory says ETH's early years "benefited from the repressive climate at other European polytechnics" after 1848–49, a supply of politically displaced academics.
