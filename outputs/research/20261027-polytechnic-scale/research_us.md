## PART C: Claims checked

**1. Haraszthy at the San Francisco Mint: CONFIRMED, with one date conflict.**
- He was the first assayer when the branch mint opened in April 1854 (some sources say 1855). In **August 1855** he became **melter and refiner**. He also ran private refining firms: the Eureka refinery, which held a Treasury contract, and Haraszthy, Uznay & Co. (about 1856–57).
- He resigned in 1857: "early in the year" per E-Sylum, June 1857 per Stack's Bowers. In Sept 1857 he was indicted for embezzling **$151,550** in gold (other figures given: "over $150,000"; rumours of $130,000 "wasted").
- The criminal charges were dropped. A civil trial cleared him in **Feb 1861** after five days.
- Sources: https://maritimeheritage.org/vips/haraszthy.html (Medium); https://noehill.com/SF/LANDMARKS/sf034.asp (Medium); https://coinbooks.org/esylum_v16n22a16.html (calls him "Melter and Refiner"; Medium-High); https://stacksbowers.com/coin-resource-center/us-coins/gold-ingots/haraszthy-uznay-and-company (Medium-High); https://library.sonoma.edu/research/guides/regional/notablepeople/haraszthy (High).

**2. James Lick: MOSTLY CONFIRMED.**
- Arrival: "January 1848" per the Lick biography on Wikipedia and per Linda Hall ("1848"). The Lick Observatory article on Wikipedia says "late 1847". Use **Jan 1848** and note the variance.
- Trust: created **16 Jul 1874** with $700,000 for the telescope (Lick placed about $3M in the hands of 7 trustees). Dissolved and re-formed in **Mar 1875** (Richard S. Floyd president) and again in **Sep 1876**.
- Lick died **1 Oct 1876**.
- Other bequests: Lick Baths $150,000; **California School of Mechanic Arts $540,000**; city statuary $100,000; Key monument $60,000.
- Observatory: summit construction began 23 Jul 1880; first light 3 Jan 1888 (Saturn observed 7 Jan); handed to the UC Regents on 1 Jun 1888. Lick was interred under the pier on 11 Jan 1887.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Lick (Medium); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lick_Observatory (Medium); ASCE *Civil Engineering* Nov 2024, https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/civil-engineering-magazine/issues/magazine-issue/article/2024/11/san-joses-lick-observatory-took-telescopes-to-new-heights (High); https://www.lindahall.org/james-lick (High).

**3. French oath of 1852: CONFIRMED.**
- Legal basis: Constitution of 14 Jan 1852, art. 14. Ministers, senators, deputies, councillors of state, officers, magistrates "et les fonctionnaires publics" swear: « Je jure obéissance à la Constitution et fidélité au président. » (https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution_du_14_janvier_1852, High). From 1853 the formula was "…fidélité à l'Empereur".
- Refusals:
  - **Jules Michelet**: his course was suspended in 1851; in 1852 he refused the oath and was dismissed from the Collège de France and the Archives nationales (https://victorianweb.org/history/historiography/micheletchron.html, Medium-High).
  - **Jules Simon**: refused and lost his chair of philosophy at the University of Paris (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Simon, Medium).
  - **François Arago**: refused in May 1852 and resigned from the Bureau des longitudes, but Louis-Napoléon made an exception and kept him (https://wehd.com/bios/Francois_Arago.html, a 1911 Britannica text; Medium).
  - Edgar Quinet had already been removed in 1846 and went into exile after the coup (Brussels, then Switzerland), so his case is not an oath refusal (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Quinet).
  - Mickiewicz's formal removal in 1852 is **NOT VERIFIED** here.
- Counter-example: a provincial law faculty swore as a body on 9 May 1852 and again on 25 Feb 1853, with no refusals recorded (*Revue d'histoire des facultés de droit* 7, 1988, pp. 107–123, https://univ-droit.fr/docs/recherche/rhfd/pdf/07-1988/07-1988_p107-123.pdf; High). Most professors complied.
- Total number of dismissals: **NOT FOUND**.

**4. Jewish scholars in German states: CONFIRMED.**
- Prussia's 1812 edict allowed Jews to hold academic teaching posts on paper. After Eduard Gans was refused a chair, the 1822 "lex Gans" withdrew that access.
- Only 3 Prussian Jews had habilitated by 1822, and none of them became a professor without converting. Baptism was the practical price.
- Königsberg's 1843 statutes admitted only Protestant teachers. At least 7 converted Jews taught there by 1848. Berlin had 24 university teachers of Jewish origin who had converted to Protestantism (de le Roi; Richarz dates the figure to 1856).
- Source for the above: Pulte, "Assimilation and Profession" (on Jacobi), https://www.pe.ruhr-uni-bochum.de/mam/wtundwg/content/team/pulte/assimilation_and_profession.pdf (High, scholarly).
- **Moritz A. Stern** (Göttingen, Hanover) was the first Jew to become a full professor at a German university without converting: 1858 per Wikipedia, 1859 per Pulte. He had waited more than 20 years (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moritz_Abraham_Stern).
- The North German law of **3 Jul 1869** abolished all restrictions on civil and political rights that came from religion, including eligibility for public office (text: https://germanhistorydocs.org/de/reichsgruendung-bismarcks-deutschland-1866-1890/ghdi:document-598, High). The federal laws took effect in Bavaria on **22 Apr 1871**, completing formal emancipation (DHM LeMO, https://www.dhm.de/lemo/kapitel/kaiserreich/das-reich/juedische-emanzipation.html, High).
- Discrimination continued in practice. Unconverted Jews still rarely became Ordinarien: about 1% of full professors in 1917 (DHM).

**5. One-year volunteer (Einjährig-Freiwilliger): CONFIRMED (origin); effect on schooling PARTLY SOURCED.**
- Created by Boyen's Wehrgesetz of **3 Sep 1814**, "Über die allgemeine Verpflichtung zum Kriegsdienst". Men who passed an examination and could pay for their own equipment and keep served one year instead of three (NDB/ADB, https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz5461.html, High; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-year_volunteer, Medium).
- In the Kaiserreich the certificate could be earned at the end of Untersekunda. It was much sought after, and some employers hired only holders (https://german1914.com/?p=2291, Low-Medium).
- No statistic was found that measures the privilege's effect on demand for schooling. The claim fits the standard view of the "Berechtigungswesen", but treat it as interpretation. Leads: Albisetti, *Secondary School Reform in Imperial Germany*; D. K. Müller.

**6. Japanese students: CONFIRMED.**
- **Chōshū Five**: left Japan in May 1863, illegally; the voyage took 135 days. They were Itō Hirobumi, Inoue Kaoru, Yamao Yōzō, Inoue Masaru and Endō Kinsuke, hosted in London by Prof. A. W. Williamson of UCL (https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/museums/2013/08/14/the-fathers-of-modern-japan, High).
- **Satsuma, 1865**: **19 men**, "a mix of students and supervisors". They left from Hashima (Kushikino), led by Niiro Hisanobu and Godai Tomoatsu, and included Mori Arinori and Terashima Munenori (https://samurai-archives.com/wiki/Satsuma_students, Medium; UCL blog, High).
- The usual split of 15 students and 4 envoys/supervisors is **NOT VERIFIED** by these pages.

**7. Chinese Educational Mission: CONFIRMED.**
- **120 boys** in 4 groups of 30 (1872, 1873, 1874, 1875), with headquarters in **Hartford, Connecticut**, living with host families across New England.
- Ended **June 1881**. At the recall, 43 were in college, 20 of them at Yale. About 100 returned to China.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Educational_Mission (Medium; consistent with connecticuthistory.org).

**8. Panic of 1857: PARTLY CONFIRMED. No data specific to engineers was found.**
- **US**: Ohio Life Insurance & Trust's New York office suspended on 24 Aug 1857. Several railroads went bankrupt or shut down, including the Delaware, Lackawanna & Western, the Illinois Central, Erie, Reading, and the Pittsburgh, Fort Wayne & Chicago. Hundreds of thousands were unemployed; more than 5,000 businesses failed within a year. Railroad mileage added fell from 3,642 (1856) to 2,487 (1857), 2,465 (1858) and about 1,830 (1859–60).
  - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panic_of_1857 (Medium); https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/panic-1857/ (Medium); FRED/NBER (High).
- **Britain**: Liverpool Borough Bank failed 27 Oct; Western Bank of Scotland 9 Nov; City of Glasgow Bank 11 Nov. The Bank Charter Act was suspended by the Treasury letter of 12 Nov, and Bank Rate reached 10%. The crisis ended on 24 Dec 1857 (Bank of England blog, https://bankunderground.co.uk/2016/12/20/the-nightmare-before-christmas-1857-financial-crises-go-global/, High).
- **Hamburg**: 145 bankruptcies by 12 Dec 1857, and 10 of the 12 firms trading with Scandinavia failed. A 15 million Mark rescue fund was set up. Rothschild, Baring, Hambros and Fould refused to lend; Vienna (the Austrian National Bank) sent a silver train, unloaded on 12 Dec. Goods worth about 500 million Mark sat in the port.
  - Ullberg & Co. had 12 million Mark Banco in liabilities against 300,000 in capital (Marx, NY Daily Tribune, Dec 1857).
  - Sources: https://www.boerse.de/boersenwissen/boersengeschichte/Die-Weltwirtschaftskrise-1857-78 (Medium-Low); https://en.19491007.xyz/MECW/15/MECW15-063 (primary journalism, Medium).
- **Engineers specifically**: no numbers on layoffs in railway or machine works were found for any of the three places. Any statement about engineers losing work is an **inference** from the railway shutdowns and the drop in construction.

**9. Hallidie and Ott: CONFIRMED, with one correction.**
- **Andrew S. Hallidie**: born 16 Mar 1836, London. Arrived in California in 1852. Made wire rope from 1856 at American Bar; A. S. Hallidie & Co. in San Francisco from 1857. Built bridges in California and British Columbia 1861–63. First Clay Street Hill Railroad run 2 Aug 1873; regular service from 1 Sep 1873. UC Regent 1868–1900; president of the Mechanics' Institute 1868–77 and 1893–95. Died 24 Apr 1900 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Smith_Hallidie, Medium).
- **James J. Ott**: **opened his assay office in 1857** (not 1859), in the back of a store in Nevada City. In **1859** he assayed the Washoe silver ore that set off the Comstock rush. He ran the office until he died in **1907** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ott%27s_Assay_Office, Medium; the assay itself is confirmed by https://noehill.com/nevada_county_california/nat1975000447.asp, which gives the building date as 1855; Wikipedia gives 1851 and 1863). Birth date and place: NOT FOUND.
