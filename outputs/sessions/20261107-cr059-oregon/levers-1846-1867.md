# CR-059 levers — what else California and Pacifica can do, 1846–1867

**Status:** Brainstorm for the author, 7 November 2026. It assesses moves that make Oregon's 1866 choice and the 1867 cession more likely, including underhanded ones. Every move is PROPOSED. Effects on the vote are MODEL.

## The two constraints

**Before 1861, deniability.** The Querétaro pledge, the Buchanan–Vallejo Convention and the sectional lock all rest on Washington believing California covets nothing north of the 42nd parallel. That is why Elias forbids political approaches to Oregon (OR-P5). Moves in this period must be private, commercial or open-institutional, so that Washington sees business, not policy.

**After 1861, legitimacy.** The outcome is worth only what a clean 1866 vote and a two-thirds Senate in 1867 make it worth. Anything that taints the count destroys the asset it was meant to win.

**Pacifica's style already in canon: "silence, never a false paper"** (CR-050, CR-049). Pacifica deceives by timing, selection and silence, by lawful acts with unstated purposes, and by true facts placed well. It never forges or lies. The good underhanded moves below work upstream, on interests, people and information. None touches a ballot.

## I · 1848–1858: making Oregon's interests Californian

| # | Move | Mechanism | Effect by 1866 | Risk | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** | **Oregon's gold rush runs through Californian law** | In 1848 roughly two-thirds of Oregon's men went to the California mines (commonly given; Hist.). They get the same light licences as everyone else (CR-033). The State Assay Office coins their dust at cost, so Oregon never needs its own coinage (historically the "Beaver" coins of 1849) | A generation of Oregon men made their stake under Californian courts. Oregon's money is Californian | Low | **Yes** |
| **L2** | **Open scholarships** | Carquinez, the Polytechnic and the travelling fellowships from 1854 (CR-049) admit Oregonians by examination (CR-037: the test is the examination) | By 1866 the young surveyors, engineers, doctors and lawyers of Oregon's towns trained in Pacifica, including some of the convention's younger delegates | None; not even sneaky | **Yes** |
| **L3** | **San Francisco capital on the Columbia** | William Ralston and San Francisco houses take minority stakes in the Oregon Steam Navigation Company in 1860–62 and act as correspondents for Portland's banks | The OSN's board and Ladd's bank have Pacifican partners at the table | Low to moderate | **Yes** |
| **L4** | **Buy the Hudson's Bay Company's rights south of the 49th** | Through a London house, buy the possessory rights of the HBC and the Puget Sound Agricultural Company: Fort Vancouver, Cowlitz, Nisqually. Historically the U.S. bought them out in 1869 (to verify) | In 1861 the occupation stands on friendly ground at Vancouver. French Prairie's old HBC families have a Pacifican-linked landlord | Moderate, if traced | Optional |
| **L5** | **Protestant philanthropy** | A San Francisco merchants' fund endows chairs and libraries at Willamette University (Methodist) and Pacific University (Congregational) | Blunts the clergy's fear of a "Catholic, Mormon republic," Pacifica's worst liability in the Willamette | Low | **Yes** |

## III · 1861–1865: the occupation as an argument

| # | Move | Mechanism | Effect | Risk | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L9** | **Pay in gold, generously** | The army buys Willamette wheat, beef, lumber and teams at gold prices slightly above market. The Idaho trade adds to the boom | The Willamette's best years. Farmers vote their pocketbooks | Low | **Yes** |
| **L10** | **Oregon's customs held for Oregon** | Customs collected at Astoria and Portland under occupation are credited to a published Oregon account. It pays the compact's courts, county officials' salaries and the 1861–62 flood relief | Demonstrates the charter's revenue share years in advance. **Sneaky part:** the officials who will run the 1866 polls are paid from it. The Union will object; Pacifica answers that it is Oregon's own money | Moderate. Mitigated by Union observers and Deady's commission | **Yes, with the protocol** |
| **L11** | **A refuge from the Union's war** | From 1863 thousands of Missouri refugees, Confederate deserters and draft evaders came west along the Oregon Trail (Hist.: the Boise Basin's "Dixie"). Pacifican-held Oregon publishes terms of passage and extradites no one for evading a foreign draft | US citizens resident before spring 1865 are electors under part 2's rule, and they vote against Washington. **The quiet move:** Washington asks for the 1861 rolls; Elias "concedes" the armistice date, which admits them. Perhaps 1,000–2,000 votes (MODEL), decisive in a 51–49 contest | They amplify the Richmond taint. If Washington counts them first, it will insist on 1861 | **Yes. The strongest lever here** |
| **L12** | **The news wire** | Eastern news reaches Oregon only through San Francisco's wires and papers. Pacifica's wire service forwards what is true and chosen: draft riots, the greenback discount, casualty lists, Sand Creek (November 1864), slavery still legal in Kentucky. It never sends anything false | Shapes five years of Oregon's view of the Union | Low in 1861–65. In the campaign, Victoria's protocol gives both sides equal access to the wires, so the lever ends in 1866 | **Yes** |
| **L13** | **Build into the hostile south** | Build the Sacramento line north to the Siskiyous in 1863–66. Hire Jackson County men on the grade at gold wages, through professional contractors with paid crews (CR-031-R1). Promise a railhead at Jacksonville. **Not Chinese crews in southern Oregon** | Dents the south's Union lean. The railway is the argument the Union cannot make | Low to moderate | **Yes** |
| **L14** | **Schedule around the liabilities** | Deseret's accession charter goes to its legislature in November–December 1866, after Oregon votes. John D. Lee's trial and hanging (canon) are publicized in Oregon in 1863 | The Mormon fear is fainter in October 1866 | Low | **Yes** |

## Dead ends: do not do these

- **Fraud, intimidation, or bringing in voters after the armistice.** Any authorized stuffed box destroys the 1867 two-thirds and breaks Victoria's protocol. The vote's whole value is that it is clean.
- **Funding Lane, Copperheads or Richmond's friends in the Union (L7).** One exposure, and the Senate cession is dead.
- **Any political approach to Oregon before 1861.** Washington garrisons Oregon, the 1848 convention fails and the sectional lock unravels.
- **Forged papers.** This breaks canon's rule that Pacifica never states anything false.
- **Chinese crews in southern Oregon, or Mormon publicity before October 1866.**
- **Paying the war claims directly.** That contradicts CR-033. L8 is the grey version, and it should be visibly grey.

## Recommendation

**Adopt:** L1, L2, L9, L10, L11 with the electorate date, L12, L13, L14, L16 and L18.

**Author's decision:** L8 (the scrip), L15 (the Senate gamble), L17 (Lane abroad, instead of the Roseburg scene), L4 and L19.

**Effect (MODEL).** L9–L11 and L13 move the margin from about 51–49 back to about **53–47**. That is enough that the Native article and the Chinese floor no longer put the outcome at risk. L11 alone may be the difference.

## Canon tensions

1. **L8 against CR-033's 1851 refusal.** If adopted, write it as a known contradiction that Victoria and the coast nations name.
2. **L11 against the 1871 fugitive agreement.** Pacifica shelters white men fleeing a draft and, five years later, returns Black men fleeing slavery. The irony is deliberate and should be visible, not explained.
3. **L10.** Customs collected under occupation becomes a published Oregon account. This is additive; it is a commitments-ledger item.
4. **L2.** The fellowships of 1854 (CR-049) are open to Oregonians. Additive. Any Oregon scholar named must be a historical person, per the author's preference against invented scientists.
