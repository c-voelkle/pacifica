# CR-059 (outline, part 3) — The Native nations and Oregon's accession, 1861–1867

**Status:** Outline for the author, 7 November 2026. It continues `CR-059-outline-oregon-vote-1866.md` and corrects two of its offers (§6). Everything is PROPOSED unless marked Hist. or canon. Vote effects are MODEL.

**Short answer.** The nations should not sit in the Salem convention. They should be **parties to the accession itself**, through three instruments:
- a succession article in the treaty;
- a Native article in the Charter of Accession that Oregon must accept whole and cannot amend;
- compacts that each nation signs with the Confederation, conditional on accession.

Fifteen or so peoples, in four legal situations, have a claim (§2). Their inclusion **costs Pacifica votes** in southern Oregon and on the coast and **wins some** in the eastern mining country. It narrows the margin to about 51–49. It strengthens the cession in the Senate in 1867. It also reverses the Nez Perce, Modoc and Siletz histories downstream.

---

## 1 · Why not delegates at Salem

**Canon doctrine.** CR-033 A22 (APPROVED direction): a homeland "never enters the public domain, can't be sold to anyone, can be leased only through the Republic, and the compact can be undone only with the nation's consent." The Republic, not a member state, is each nation's treaty partner and trustee.

**What seating would mean.** A nation sitting in Oregon's convention would be a constituent of Oregon. Its land and government would then be Oregon's to legislate, which is exactly what the doctrine forbids. White Oregon would also refuse it, and the Union would call the convention a Pacifican contrivance. Seating the nations would bring them inside Oregon's power.

**What the nations get instead.** Standing as parties, at a level above the convention:

| Instrument | Parties | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Succession article** in the Oregon article of the Western treaty (CR-D) | United States, Pacifica | Whoever holds Oregon owes the ratified treaties' obligations. Pacifica assumes them at least in full and is credited for the annuities it paid from 1862. The Union's unpaid debts under its unratified treaties are **deducted from the cession price**. |
| **Native article** of the Charter of Accession | Oregon, Pacifica; drafted with the nations | Homelands outside Oregon's jurisdiction and public domain; confederal courts; fishing places; titles confirmed; no volunteer companies (§4). The convention may accept or reject the charter but **may not amend this article**. |
| **Compacts of protection** (CR-033 A22 form) | Each nation, the Confederation | Signed at councils in summer 1866, in force on accession. Each nation may accept the compact or keep only its assumed U.S. treaty. |

**Speaking right (a scene).** Deady lets a delegation of the councils address the convention once, on the Native article, before the vote on the charter. Billy Chinook, the Wasco chief educated in Washington and Philadelphia, who guided Frémont and lived in California 1847–51 (Hist.), speaks in English. The convention cannot change a word of what he defends. It can only decide whether Oregon accepts it.

## 2 · Who should be party

The test is **any people whose homeland, treaty rights or usual fishing places lie within Oregon's boundaries.** That brings in some who live outside Oregon, such as the Yakama, who fish at Celilo.

### A · Ratified U.S. treaty nations: succession and compact

| People | Treaty (Hist.) | 1866 situation | What they want |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grand Ronde:** Kalapuya bands (Santiam, Tualatin, Yamhill and others), Molalla, Clackamas, Umpqua, Takelma and Shasta peoples removed from the Rogue | Willamette Valley, Molalla, Umpqua and Rogue River treaties, 1853–55 | Reservation in the Yamhill; annuities unpaid from Washington since 1861 | Annuities on time; the agency reformed; fishing at Willamette Falls |
| **Siletz (Coast Reservation):** Tututni, Chetco, Shasta Costa, Coquille and other Rogue River and coast peoples | Partly ratified (Rogue River 1853–54); partly unratified | Executive-order reservation since 1855 | Keep the reservation whole; Yaquina only by consent |
| **Warm Springs** (Confederated Tribes of Middle Oregon): Wasco, Tenino, Wyam (Celilo), Tygh, Dock-Spus | Middle Oregon treaty, 1855, ratified 1859 | Reservation; Paiute raids; fishing at Celilo | Celilo and the river; protection from the Paiute; pay for scouts |
| **Umatilla, Cayuse, Walla Walla** | Walla Walla treaty, 1855, ratified 1859 | Reservation; memory of Peopeomoxmox, killed under a flag of truce by Oregon volunteers in 1855 (Hist.) | Justice against volunteers; the river fisheries |
| **Nez Perce,** Wallowa band of Tuekakas (Old Joseph) and Lawyer's treaty party | Nez Perce treaty, 1855, ratified 1859 | **Here no 1863 treaty** (CR-B dependency): the Wallowa stays Nez Perce | The Wallowa held under the 1855 lines; no divide-and-sign |

### B · Unratified-treaty nations: unfinished business

| People | Treaty (Hist.) | Situation | What they want |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coos, Lower Umpqua, Siuslaw, Alsea** | The 1855 Coast treaty, the eighth of Palmer's treaties and the only one never ratified (Hist.) | Confined on the Coast Reservation's southern subagencies; land settled at Coos Bay without payment | **Payment for the land taken** |
| **Clatsop, Tillamook, Chinook** (and lower Columbia bands) | Tansey Point treaties, 1851, never ratified (Hist.) | No reservation; fishing and the Astoria shore | Payment; fishing; a homeland on the north coast |

### C · Nations without a treaty in 1861: open negotiation

| People | Historical event | Here |
|---|---|---|
| **Klamath, Modoc, Yahooskin** | U.S. treaty of Council Grove, 14 October 1864; the Modoc's discontent at living on Klamath land leads to the Modoc War of 1872–73 | Southern Oregon is Pacifican-held in 1864. **Pacifica's commissioners make the 1864 compact.** The Modoc get a **separate homeland on Lost River and Tule Lake**, which straddles the California line, so Pacifica can offer it outright. ### D · For Washington Territory's convention (CR-059, Washington part)

Yakama, the Puget Sound treaty nations (Medicine Creek, Point Elliott, Point No Point), the Makah (whaling under the Neah Bay treaty) and the north-bank Chinook. Not part of Oregon's accession, except that the **Yakama are party to the Columbia fishing article.**

## 3 · The record that matters to them, 1861–1866

1. **Washington stopped paying.** From summer 1861 no U.S. annuity crosses the lines.
2. **Pacifica paid instead.** From 1862 the occupation command pays the ratified treaties' annuities in goods, on time, on the treaty schedules, and publishes the accounts on CR-033's trustee model. The agents at Grand Ronde and Siletz are replaced. **[PROPOSED]**
3. **No volunteer companies.** The compact bars any use of Oregon's men for military purposes, and Pacifica forbids volunteer expeditions on its 1851 principle (CR-033). For five years no Oregon company rides against a Native village.
4. **The Klamath compact of 1864** and the **Paiute peace of 1865–66** are Pacifica's work, and both are in force.
5. **A test of the scouts.** In 1866, historically, Wasco scouts under Billy Chinook killed 32 Paiutes at Dry Creek against orders (Hist.). Here they are in Pacifican pay. Pacifica must try its own scouts under the doctrine it applies to settlers, during the Oregon campaign. **[OPEN — strong scene option]**

These five years are the nations' evidence, the way the relief-boat judgment is white Oregon's.

### Their fears

- **The sole buyer.** As the only purchaser, the Republic sets the price (CR-033 A17). Land leaves through long leases and assigned annuities (A23). The nations are not naive about trustees.
- **The divide.** The Nez Perce remember how treaty commissioners worked: sign with one faction, bind all.
- **The vote they cannot cast.** Each compact takes effect only if white Oregon votes for accession. If Oregon votes for the United States, their treaties revert to a government that stopped paying in 1861.

### Their decisions

The nations have agency. They do not act as one bloc, and their strategies differ:

- **Ratified-treaty nations** (Warm Springs, Umatilla, Grand Ronde): they sign their compacts in July–August 1866, conditional on accession, but **choose to publish them only after the vote.** They know an Indian endorsement would cost Pacifica white votes. They also secure the succession article's crediting clause, so that a Union result still owes them what Pacifica advanced. **[PROPOSED]**
- **Unratified-treaty coast peoples** (Coos, Lower Umpqua, Siuslaw, Clatsop): they have nothing to lose and **petition publicly**. Only Pacifica's deduction clause pays them.
- **The Nez Perce:** they refuse any compact that the Wallowa band has not signed. Pacifica, which needs the Snake River route (CR-B), accepts. Tuekakas signs last.
- **The Modoc:** Kintpuash signs for the Lost River homeland and refuses any return to Klamath land.

## 4 · The Native article of the Charter

| Clause | Content | Canon basis |
|---|---|---|
| N1 **Homelands** | Reservations and compact homelands lie outside Oregon's jurisdiction in internal affairs and never enter its public domain | CR-033 A22 |
| N2 **The public domain** | **Only land ceded by ratified treaty, or later bought by the Land Fund,** passes to Oregon. This narrows part 2's offer | CR-033 A17 |
| N3 **Titles confirmed** | Every donation claim, patent and pre-emption entry, including those on unratified-treaty land at Coos Bay and the north coast, is confirmed in the register. The nations are paid by the deduction from the cession price, not by settlers | CR-033 A20–A21 analogue |
| N4 **Fishing** | Usual and accustomed places are guaranteed: Celilo, the Cascades, Willamette Falls, the lower Columbia. No franchise, portage or cannery grant may close one | Seed of CR-055 ("none at The Dalles") |
| N5 **Force** | Oregon may raise no volunteer company against a Native nation. Force only under confederal command | CR-033 (1851) |
| N6 **Courts** | Killings and land disputes between settlers and nations go to confederal courts, **with Native testimony admitted.** This overrides Oregon's 1854 law barring Black, mulatto and Indian testimony against whites (Hist.) | New |
| N7 **Marriage** | The marriage floor covers marriages between whites and Native people | CR-015 I2 (APPROVED) |
| N8 **Amendment** | N1–N7 change only with the consent of the nation affected and the Confederation. Oregon's consent is not enough | CR-033 A22 |

**Who insists.** **Elias holds this line, not Victoria.** The Land Fund, the register and the compacts are his system and his finances (CR-033). He bends on exclusion, which is not his system, and never on this. Victoria fights for the member-citizen exemption (part 2). Each holds the line on what is theirs, and both are negotiated in Hawaii's ratifying session of summer 1866.

## 5 · Effects on the decision to join

### By region

| Region | Effect of the Native article | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **Southern Oregon** (Jackson, Josephine, Douglas, Curry, Coos) | Rogue River War veterans lose their claims and future warrants. The testimony clause, prosecutions and fear for titles on unratified coast land combine with the Chinese floor | **Strongly against.** Lane's country splits further |
| **The coast** (Coos, Curry, Clatsop) | Confirmed titles (N3) neutralize most of the fear, but the Union campaign says Pacifica will give Coos Bay back | Against, slightly |
| **Willamette** | Native land settled since 1855. The testimony clause is resented. French Prairie's mixed families gain (N7). Benton County gets Yaquina by deed (§6) | Neutral to slightly against |
| **Eastern Oregon** (Wasco and The Dalles, Umatilla, Grant and Canyon City, Baker and Auburn) | Paiute peace and safe roads to the mines; the Warm Springs and Umatilla compacts hold | **For.** But many miners are Californians, Pacifican citizens who cannot vote (part 2's electorate rule), so the gain is smaller than the population |
| **Portland and The Dalles** | The OSN must accept the fishing article at Celilo, where its portage runs. Ainsworth accepts because the Idaho trade is worth more | Neutral |

**Net (MODEL):** the Native article costs Pacifica about two points in the south and on the coast and regains about one in the east. **The margin narrows to about 51–49.** That makes Deady's certification and the protocol's cleanness matter more.

### In the Senate, April 1867 (CR-D)

- The succession article, the deduction for the coast treaties and the compacts answer the question "is anyone abandoned?" for Sumner and Reverdy Johnson.
- **James R. Doolittle,** chair of Indian Affairs and author of the joint committee's report *Condition of the Indian Tribes* (January 1867, Hist.; to verify), is a natural target. His report surveys the Union's record, including Sand Creek. Pacifica's Klamath compact and Navajo homeland sit beside it.
- The deduction makes the Union's own unratified treaties part of the price. Some senators would rather settle that debt this way than in an appropriation.

### For the nations themselves

Oregon's vote, cast only by white men, decides which government owes them. The nations cannot change that. They shape what accession means and secure what they can under either result. **The narrator should state plainly that the people whose land it is have no ballot.**

## 6 · Corrections to part 2

1. **"The ceded public domain passes to the State of Oregon"** is narrowed to N2. Unratified-treaty land and compact homelands are excluded. Oregon still gets the Willamette, the Rogue valley and the ceded middle-Oregon lands.
2. **"Volunteers offered land warrants on Oregon's own public land"** is **withdrawn.** It contradicts CR-033 A19 ("a volunteer gains land by waiting for the treaty, not by killing"), and the coast peoples' petition would attack it first. The Union's payment of the war claims stays a real Union card that Pacifica does not match.

### Canon tensions

1. **CR-015 H6** lists the interior nations (Navajo, Apache, Ute, Shoshone, Bannock, Paiute, Nez Perce, Blackfeet) but none of western Oregon. This is additive.
2. **CR-055:** the 1908 Bonneville compact "sets its own terms." N4 guarantees the fishing places in 1866 but does not set dam terms, so the 1908 compact keeps its independence. Word it so 1866 is the guarantee and 1908 the bargain, not model and copy.
3. **Protected intent ("no campaign of extermination").** The Paiute peace and the Dry Creek prosecution must be written so that Pacifica's force never becomes one.
4. **CR-B dependency.** The Nez Perce outcome requires that no U.S. commission reaches Lapwai in 1863.
