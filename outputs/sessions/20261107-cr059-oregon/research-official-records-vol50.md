**For CR-059.** In history the interior's garrison after October 1861 was Californian, then Oregonian and "Washington Territory" volunteers raised largely in California. In this timeline none of those sources exists for the Union:
- California's volunteers are Pacifica's.
- Under the compact, Oregon's men serve no military purpose outside Oregon.
- San Francisco cannot be a Union recruiting depot.

The regulars ordered east in October 1861 cannot leave by the Columbia, because Pacifica holds it. They are the "interior concentration" of canon §10.

## 2. Supply ran up the Columbia

| When | Fact | Page |
|---|---|---|
| 28 Jan 1862 | **Lt. Col. H. Lee, Fort Walla Walla, to Wright:** "there are no supplies on hand by any of the farmers or dealers in this section of the country. All of the grain that was grown last year is now or will be by spring consumed. Flour is worthy now $20 per barrel; oats from 6 to 7 cents, and very scarce; hay none on hand; barley not to be had; bacon 30 cents per pound … beef 15 cents per pound, and none in the country fit to eat; beans none to be had at any price; potatoes $1 per bushel … wood $30 per cord. … the Government cannot rely upon this country for its supplies at this post. … Requisitions have been made … and will probably reach here as soon as navigation opens. The unprecedented severity of the winter and the influx of strangers has destroyed and consumed nearly everything in the country. Nearly all the cattle have or will die before spring." | I.829–830 |

| 16 May 1862 | Wright to a cavalry captain in Nevada: "Economize your forage. We can send none from here. Purchase as little as possible. Graze your horses." | I.1076 |

**For CR-059.** The draft's mechanism holds and is stronger than stated:
- In the winter of 1861–62 the valley was stripped bare, and the post waited for the river to open. Here the river opens for Pacifica.
- Even in a normal year the interior could not feed its own cavalry.

**For CR-059.** The Union's Pacific coast paymasters historically paid in gold drawn in San Francisco. Here San Francisco is Pacifica's, so the interior can be paid only in notes or drafts. Halleck's historical refusal of coin is the draft's "Halleck refuses" in his own words.

## 5. Consequences for the draft

1. **I2 and I3, rewritten on the evidence.** The October 1861 order sends every regular east. With the Columbia Pacifican, the interior regulars can obey only over the Mullan Road, which is not passable before late June 1862. They winter at Walla Walla on Wright's stores in a valley "destroyed and consumed", waiting for a navigation that no longer serves them. In 1862 the Union has no Californian, Oregonian or San Francisco-raised volunteers to relieve them, no gold to pay valley contractors, and no recruits (2 June 1862). The Walla Walla convention lets the Union carry out its own order. Date: from late June 1862, after the road opens and the lower-Columbia attempt fails; September 1862 stays possible but runs into the Missouri's low water.
2. **The Missouri route.** Boats reach Fort Benton only in early summer, so a column arriving in autumn finds none. That argues for leaving Walla Walla in July or August 1862.
3. **Halleck and coin.** Cite II.303 and II.356 for the draft's payment mechanism.
4. **Interior politics.** Kendall's "fully one-half" (I.650) and Idaho's secessionists (II.449) anchor I8's Southern Democrats and the refugees of W7.
5. **The Nez Perce.** Historically the 1861 mining agreement, the 1862 council and the coercive 1863 treaty council. Here Pacifica's Lewiston lease replaces the 1861 agreement, and no six companies gather at Lapwai in 1863.
6. **Columbia fortifications.** Historically their batteries were begun only in 1863, and Totten recommended an armored floating battery and ram for the river (I.157), a historical echo of canon's turret ship.
