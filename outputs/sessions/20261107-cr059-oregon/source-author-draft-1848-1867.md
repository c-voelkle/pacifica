# Pacifica’s continental settlement, 1848–1867

**Working draft for review.** The 1867 settlement preserves the territorial allocations in the master storyline and CR-015 H5, including the districts that supply later mineral discoveries. Oregon becomes Pacifican without becoming a U.S. state. Santa Fe and the rest of New Mexico within the canon line become Pacifican. Denver and the Front Range stay Union. Wartime lines of control can differ from the final border. This draft proposes the events, local bargains, and treaty terms that connect the two.

The 1846 Oregon Treaty and California’s declaration of independence provide the starting conditions. Washington holds a prewar territorial claim to Oregon. The draft uses the **1859 statehood delay** as the Oregon divergence and carries forward the existing Civil War framework and CR-058’s Mexican and northern settlements. ## 1847–50 · Recognition and the first borders

**December 1847–March 1848.** At Querétaro, Mexico recognizes the Republic of California. California assumes a negotiated share of Mexico’s obligations to London creditors, secured on customs and prospective mining revenue, and pledges against annexation by the United States. Elias knows of the Mokelumne discovery when he bargains. The creditors have a stake in a solvent California, and Mexico gains a friendly government on the coast and a settlement it can present separately from its peace with Washington.

**February 1848.** In this timeline, the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo settles the Mexican War by ceding New Mexico and the Great Basin to the United States while respecting California’s recognized frontier. The Buchanan–Vallejo Convention gives Washington commerce and defined naval access to California’s ports, including the revocable Sausalito storehouse. Its twelve-year commercial and naval terms create a known deadline in 1860. California can use the convention to offer predictable treatment to Oregon shippers while Oregon remains a U.S. territory. [Historical Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/treaty-of-guadalupe-hidalgo), [Proposed Querétaro and Buchanan–Vallejo conventions](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/master_storyline.md#L170-L176)

**1850.** The Treaty of Monterey fixes the Californian frontier and access toward the lower Colorado. Washington keeps the Great Basin and Salt Lake approaches. In New Mexico, Congress resolves the Texas boundary dispute and organizes a territory after Santa Fe’s bid for statehood fails. Keep that local convention in the story. It gives the Rio Grande towns a political tradition of their own when California later offers elected government, local courts, and continuity of land claims. U.S. territorial organization and the transfer of federal claims leave Native title and political authority to separate agreements. [Treaty of Monterey in the master storyline](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/master_storyline.md#L189-L200), [Historical Compromise of 1850](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/compromise-of-1850), [New Mexico’s 1850 statehood bid](https://www.senate.gov/states/NM/timeline.htm)

## 1850–56 · Oregon commerce and the split Gadsden bargain

**Early 1850s · Make California Oregon’s dependable market.** California publishes duties and wharf charges, allows bonded onward shipments through San Francisco, and uses contracts for its yards, docks, and public stores to anchor orders for Oregon produce, lumber, and coal. Private wholesalers finance crops against orders and carry the cargo on regular sailings. As California becomes competitive in wheat, Oregon’s connection increasingly rests on diverse goods, shipping, and credit. Portland merchants keep their customers and arrange loans locally. Coos Bay and the south coast gain mills and outlets of their own. The relationship is worth defending in Oregon because local firms and producers share the gains. [Portland’s merchant economy](https://www.oregonhistoryproject.org/narratives/commerce-climate-and-community-a-history-of-portland-and-its-people/the-making-of-a-market-town/portland-as-a-marketing-center/), [Oregon coastal commerce](https://www.oregonhistoryproject.org/narratives/the-oregon-coastforists-and-green-verdent-launs/development-of-the-coastal-economy/mining-lumbering-and-shipbuilding/), [California’s wheat transition](https://www.scielo.org.mx/scielo.php?pid=S1405-22532010000200002&script=sci_arttext)

**1853–54 · Divide the Gadsden purchase.** Mexico agrees to two coordinated instruments. California buys the western portion of the proposed southern strip, with access to the lower Colorado crossing and territory extending through Tucson. Washington settles the disputed Mesilla Valley and buys the eastern portion. California offers funded payment and enforcement against private filibustering from its territory. Washington gets an enforceable commercial wagon and future railway right across the Californian segment, with published through rates and access to the coast. Mexico retains Sonora and Baja California. The parties survey the division between Tucson and Mesilla before the transfers take effect.

California then pays for depots, water and forage contracts, and a usable road east of the Colorado. It negotiates with the Native nations whose country the route crosses. By 1861 Tucson can sustain a field force and its transport, with additional stages needed east of the prewar border. The purchase provides a military starting point for 1862. Santa Fe and the central Rio Grande valley remain U.S. territory. [Historical Gadsden negotiations](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1830-1860/gadsden-purchase)

**1855–56 · Demonstrate credit and shared government.** In the Page, Bacon crisis, California’s emergency response extends to sound Oregon cargo bills through its merchant houses. Portland partners decide which local advances to make. California subsequently backs Oregon-owned wharves, warehouses, and shipping with published rates and access for independent carriers. In November 1856 the California–Hawaii confederation comes into force. Oregon can see a member retain domestic government while sharing customs, external relations, and defense. After that date, tariff and foreign-policy commitments need confederal authority. [Financial crisis and founding of Pacifica](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/master_storyline.md#L267-L316)

## 1857–60 · The statehood delay

**1857–58.** Oregon votes strongly for a convention and approves a state constitution. Keep both votes. The electorate wants the benefits of statehood, and many of the same voters will later support the Union. Joseph Lane and Delazon Smith remain prospective senators, giving Lane a substantial personal reason to see admission through. As in history, Oregon’s bill can pass the Senate in 1858. Southern senators divide. Some value the expected votes of Lane and Smith even though Oregon would be a free state. Without California in the Union, and assuming Minnesota’s admission proceeds on schedule, there are sixteen free states to fifteen slave states before Oregon. Sectional arithmetic alone is a poor reason to expect the Senate to block it. [Oregon admission chronology](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R47747.html), [1858 Senate roll call](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Oregon_Historical_Quarterly/Volume_27/The_Creation_of_Oregon_As_a_State%2C_Part_2#cite_note-23)

**Late 1858–February 1859.** With the 1860 deadline approaching, Pacifica offers Washington a fixed-term schedule covering named agricultural and timber products, port charges, and bonded transit. Portland merchants can show which contracts depend on its terms. Thomas Jefferson Dryer argues for keeping the federally supported territorial administration while Oregon seeks a trade settlement. Lane asks Alexander Stephens and the House to hold the bill for one session and stakes his standing on obtaining reciprocity. His Southern allies can support the pause after the defeat of proslavery statehood in Kansas. Oregon’s territorial legislature publicly asks Congress to defer admission and seeks a fresh local vote on any renewed bill. This gives later voters a real choice even though they approved a constitution in 1857. Washington remains the party competent to make the trade treaty. [Oregon’s statehood cost debate](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Oregon_Historical_Quarterly/Volume_26/The_Creation_of_Oregon_as_a_State), [1854 reciprocity precedent](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/statute/10/1089), [Stephens’s historical admission speech](https://www.loc.gov/item/19001993/)

**1859–60 · Keep the trade dispute alive.** Pacifica offers ordinary commercial renewal at Carson. Buchanan’s administration seeks stronger guarantees for the Sausalito naval privilege in exchange for the preferential schedule, which Pacifica declines. Keep the Washoe confrontation and Pacifica’s withdrawal under the Carson Convention. Oregon merchants can see both the value of Pacifican trade and the danger of Elias using force across a border. Statehood remains attractive if Washington delivers reciprocity or can guarantee a secure alternative. The delay gives Oregon time to compare those promises with events. [Washoe and the Carson Convention](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/master_storyline.md#L340-L348)

## 1861 · The Pacific war and the Oregon civil compact

**Winter and spring.** Southern withdrawals from Congress reopen the federal path to Oregon statehood. Kansas’s admission demonstrates the opening. Oregon’s earlier request for a fresh vote matters here. A clean offer ready for local acceptance before the Pacific war would probably win. This draft has Congress revive admission discussions in 1861 and bring a firm, locally ratified offer forward in 1864, when it can attach a concrete restoration plan. Oregon Unionists organize independently of Lane. James Nesmith can lead a faction that supports restoration of federal authority. Asahel Bush and the *Statesman* remain outspoken Unionists. Whiteaker can help negotiate civilian protections in his own political capacity. Oregon’s admission never took effect, so his historical state governorship supplies no authority here. [Kansas and the withdrawal of Southern senators](https://www.senate.gov/history/CivilWar_chronology.htm), [Oregon’s Unionist coalition](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/election-of-1860/)

**Spring and summer.** Keep the Hawaiian packet incident, the detention of passengers, and Victoria’s demand for redress. Hawaii and California authorize limited operations under Article XIV. Elias turns down a route to release and investigation because he wants broader guarantees. The Sausalito storehouse is sealed as the two powers go to war. Marcus takes Astoria, Portland, and Fort Vancouver as Wright withdraws mobile troops and stores. Casey preserves the Union foothold at Port Townsend, with further federal positions inland. Britain maintains its San Juan detachment and its separate sovereignty claim. The Americans’ actual June withdrawal order and reversal show how quickly a temporary reduction could be undone. Pacifica still has to defeat a recovery attempt and sustain the occupation. [Hawaiian incident and Northwestern campaign](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/master_storyline.md#L355-L377), [Douglas’s San Juan dispatch and enclosed U.S. order](https://bcgenesis.uvic.ca/V61040.html)

**Autumn and winter · Give Oregon’s courts authority they can use.** County officials and territorial legislators, including Unionists, negotiate a temporary civil compact with the occupation command. Oregon’s civilian courts and elected bodies continue to govern local affairs. The army holds designated positions, pays for supplies, and accepts civilian jurisdiction over requisition disputes. The compact bars compulsory allegiance and preserves ordinary mail, property, and trade across the lines where commanders can secure them. Local officials can sign for services and administration. Sovereignty remains for a treaty and an Oregon vote. Indigenous nations negotiate independently over their land, passage, fishing, and security.

In December the Willamette floods. Local steamboat crews and county officials lead relief. A Pacifican officer tries to requisition a relief boat. An Oregon court orders its release and Victoria obtains compliance, making observance of the compact a condition of renewing the Protectorship. California is suffering its own flood emergency, so the visible act is releasing Oregon’s boats and stores for Oregon’s use. [Willamette flood and eyewitness recollection](https://www.oregonhistoryproject.org/articles/historical-records/the-great-flood-of-1861/), [California floods of 1861–62](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/california-water-science-center/californias-history-large-storms-floods)

## 1862–63 · Two campaigns and several local bargains

**Lower Columbia and the Sound.** Washington attempts recovery from its surviving footholds. Pacifica’s coastal supply and river defense hold the lower Columbia. The working draft uses the first Ericsson turret ship here, contingent on a protected assembly yard, delivery of machinery and plate, and a credible commissioning date. The Union keeps enough strength at Port Townsend to contest a later settlement. Oregon’s Unionists now have a reason to keep the civil compact in operation while still favoring federal restoration. [Working campaign and ironclad sequence](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/master_storyline.md#L369-L381)

**Mesilla, Albuquerque, and Santa Fe.** Preserve the Texans’ advance up the Rio Grande and their loss of supplies at Glorieta. Colorado volunteers and federal regulars keep Fort Union viable. Pacifica intervenes through Tucson, with a staged supply line to Mesilla. Keep the clash with Baylor’s Texans and Henry Pruett’s death before Richmond renounces its territorial claim. In this version, Pacifica moves into the gap left by the Confederate retreat and reaches Albuquerque and Santa Fe under interim defense arrangements with local officials and militia. Governor Henry Connelly and General Edward Canby still have reason to seek federal restoration. Their acquiescence to a local ceasefire has to be earned by a Pacifican force that can hold and feed Santa Fe through a winter while Fort Union remains supplied.

The Rio Grande compact offers elected local government, bilingual courts, and adjudication of Spanish and Mexican land grants. Pueblo communities bargain for their own land and governing authority. Passage and security agreements with other Native nations have their own parties and terms. Richmond can renounce its Arizona claim and receive a commercial bargain on the Matamoros route. Washington’s title is a separate matter. Fort Union stays federal under the wartime ceasefire. McClellan trades an orderly withdrawal and eventual cession for continued civilian transit from the Union’s own routes east and north of the agreed border. [Glorieta campaign](https://www.nps.gov/peco/learn/historyculture/battle-of-glorieta-pass.htm), [Fort Union and the Santa Fe Trail](https://www.nps.gov/foun/learn/historyculture/santa-fe-trail.htm), [Pruett and the Arizona renunciation](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/master_storyline.md#L383-L405)

**Salt Lake and the overland corridor.** Brigham Young has reasons to bargain with both governments. Historically he demonstrated cooperation with Lincoln’s administration. Make the federal dispute over local government, marriage law, military presence, and control of the mail the opening for negotiations. Pacifica offers continuation of local courts and institutions, published terms of passage, and agreed militia obligations. Pacifica gets supplies, repair stations, and a secure route through the basin. Use a **provisional association in 1863**, with full accession contingent on federal cession and local approval. Young surrenders John D. Lee as part of the bargain. Keep the Pacifican trial and execution. This moves the current storyline’s formal Utah accession later and gives the confederation time to resolve representation. The Eastern Shoshone and other nations are parties to separate passage and land bargains. Bear River still takes place under a Pacifican commander and makes later agreements harder to win. [Brigham Young’s 1862 correspondence](https://brighamyoungcenter.org/s/byp/item/14570), [Historical Eastern Shoshone treaty](https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-eastern-shoshoni-1863-0848), [CR-015, Utah and the interior](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/CR-015.md)

**Idaho and western Montana.** Develop influence through working supply routes and local agreements. In Idaho, merchants and mining districts need freight, specie, courts, and communications through the Columbia and Snake corridors. In the western Montana gold country, use the Salt Lake route and the Mullan Road to supply Bannack and Alder Gulch, then Last Chance Gulch in 1864. Test their delivered costs and reliability against the Missouri route through Fort Benton. Pacifica wants the gold country on both sides of the divide. Leaving Fort Benton as a Union riverhead and keeping civilian trade open makes the larger cession easier to defend in Washington. [Columbia transport network](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/oregon_steam_navigation_company/), [CR-015, sections H4–H5](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/CR-015.md)

**Southwestern Wyoming and western Colorado.** Around Fort Bridger and the Green River, Pacifica ties post supplies and stage service to Salt Lake and negotiates local and Native agreements on passage. South Pass is the eastern hinge of this route. In Colorado, Pacifica gets leverage west of the main crest through the Green River approach and from the south through the San Luis Valley’s Rio Grande links. Local civilian compacts give towns and settlements a stake in keeping those connections. The 1865 bargain reserves Denver, the Front Range, Pikes Peak, and the Leadville district for the Union and uses the high passes and the Sangre de Cristo crest to make the agreed line defensible. The named allocations govern the survey where a simple divide line would give the wrong result. [CR-015, sections H4–H5](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/CR-015.md), [Territorial survey notes](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/war_base_assumptions.md#L9-L18)

**Gulf operations and December 1863 cutoff.** Preserve the initial Pacifican technical aid at New Orleans and the engineering work around the Confederate withdrawal from Vicksburg. In December 1863, Elias orders Marcus and the official staff home and stops new overt military credits and government shipments. The recall is completed as Marcus returns. Logan stays with a reduced clandestine mission and a narrow remit for technical work. While Elias funds or directs the work, it remains state assistance. For Oregon, the December change reduces the visible commitment to Richmond and gives Unionists room to defend the civil compact. An endorsement of Pacifica must wait for the actual cutoff. ## 1864 · Oregon gets a federal offer and Nevada gets a choice

**Oregon.** Congress offers a genuine route to statehood through a fresh local vote, ordinary representation, land grants, and a program for restoring federal courts, mail, and trade. The offer remains available through the postwar decision. Oregon’s Unionists split between immediate acceptance and waiting to compare peace terms. Nesmith can lead the second faction because he sought federal restoration and has seen Oregon’s civilian institutions work under the compact. Bush keeps campaigning for the Union. Lane’s earlier role explains the delay and gives him a voice, with a diminished ability to deliver a majority once the debate turns to Richmond and slavery. [Historical Nevada enabling act as a model](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-13/pdf/STATUTE-13-Pg30-2.pdf), [Oregon’s divided Democrats](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/salem-clique/)

**Nevada.** Keep the first convention and the rejection of its constitution in January 1864. Pacifica then offers a written mining tax schedule, recognition of valid mining titles and liens, local courts, and a financed route to California markets. The offer has to reach working miners, merchants, and towns as well as the large mine owners. In history the second convention produced a more acceptable constitution and won the vote. The working divergence is that Nevada’s electorate defers federal admission again, with an elected convention authorized to compare terms at the peace. This would revise CR-015’s approved October 1864 admission, while retaining its final allocation of all Nevada to Pacifica. If the statehood vote succeeds, the treaty needs a separate Nevada consent and implementation path to reach the same border. [Proceedings of the first Nevada convention](https://archive.org/details/officialreportof00neva), [Nevada’s two conventions](https://nsla.nv.gov/utah-nevada-territory/nevada-constitution), [CR-015, section H4](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/CR-015.md)

**The U.S. election.** Preserve the working McClellan victory, contingent on the wider war. The Union has failed to secure the decisive victories the administration needs before the election, including at Atlanta. The Western deployments and loss of Pacifica’s public credit add pressure. Logan’s remaining work can help hold Mobile, using local workshops and supplies already secured or purchased by Richmond. Test those stocks and the actual impact of the reduced aid before relying on the outcome. ## 1865–66 · Armistice, actual cutoff, and the Oregon vote

**February–June 1865.** In the working Gulf sequence, Logan’s earlier and continuing technical work contributes to the Union defeat at Mobile in February. McClellan takes office in March and negotiates separate armistices with Pacifica and Richmond. **When the Pacifica–Union armistice takes effect, Elias ends Logan’s Pacifican orders and funding.** Victoria insists that the cutoff can be verified. Oregon’s compact bars the use of its troops, ports, and revenues for military assistance to Richmond. Pacifica’s commercial and diplomatic relationship with Richmond is handled separately. Keeping Logan under Elias’s direction after the armistice would give Oregon’s Unionists a concrete reason to reject accession. [Mobile, McClellan, and the armistices](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/master_storyline.md#L413-L457)

**1865 · Agree the Western terms in principle.** Freeze the lines of control and restore civilian commerce under the armistice. McClellan seeks gold payments, predictable commercial routes to the Pacific, and a defensible boundary retaining Denver, the Front Range, Fort Benton, and the eastern approaches outside the cession. Pacifica seeks the agreed Western line and a continuous southwestern border. At Fort Union, the federal garrison gets a withdrawal timetable and protection for the evacuation. The Santa Fe Trail remains open to civilian freight. Write the Oregon cession as conditional on its vote, and keep the federal restoration offer available under separate legislation. The parties agree on port access and evacuation obligations under either result. Washington’s constitutional process and the Pacifican accession approvals follow.

**1865–66 · Settle terms with the nations and communities inside the line.** The Navajo homeland treaty takes the place of the Long Walk. The proposed Apache peace pairs protected homelands and paid scouts with enforcement against settler massacres and a separate Mexican agreement on scalp bounties. In New Mexico, the abolition of debt peonage and the Native captive trade by 1866 tests the Rio Grande compact and provokes the brief Hispano revolt already in canon. These bargains shape the security of the southern corridor and the real extent of Pacifican authority within the cession. [CR-015, section H6](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/CR-015.md), [Southwestern agreements in the master storyline](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/master_storyline.md#L383-L387)

**1865–66 · Protect the Oregon terms against Pacifica’s own executive.** The proposed accession charter gives Oregon an elected legislature and courts, representation in the common institutions, a defined customs revenue share, and limits on federal military authority in civilian affairs. It secures property, a choice of citizenship under the treaty, and equal civil protection. The existing Oregon constitution’s Black exclusion clause creates real resistance to that last term. Oregon’s charter must specify which protections stand beyond cabinet legislation under Elias’s new Providentorship and how a court can enforce them. California and Hawaii approve the protected terms through the required constitutional procedures. Victoria’s leverage matters as much in peacetime as it did at the relief-boat hearing. [Confederal amendment rules](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/master_storyline.md#L303-L316), [Providentorship and protected provisions](https://github.com/c-voelkle/pacifica/blob/main/book-agent/revision/master_storyline.md#L459-L469), [Oregon’s original constitution](https://records.sos.state.or.us/ORSOSWebDrawer/Record/9479967/File/document)

**October 1866 · Oregon chooses.** An elected convention publishes both complete offers. County officials run the vote under a joint armistice protocol. Both armies stand back, opposition papers campaign openly, and displaced voters and qualified men serving with Union forces have a way to participate. The federal option remains statehood with representation and a reconstruction program. Pacifica offers continuity of the local institutions already tested during the war, a vote over confederal trade rules, and a defined local role in defense. Nesmith backs conditional accession after the military cutoff. Bush and some Unionists vote for statehood. A majority chooses the Pacifican terms. County returns, the published terms, and the opposition’s objections reach Washington before the winter negotiations. The vote supplies Oregon’s consent. The 1867 treaty supplies the transfer of U.S. title. [Contemporary provision for voting by absent servicemen](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-13/pdf/STATUTE-13-Pg30-2.pdf)

## 1866–67 · Secure Russia and build the Senate majority

**Summer–December 1866 · Put a funded offer before Russia.** Pacifica sends an empowered envoy to St. Petersburg before Alexander II’s winter decision on Russian America. The envoy works through Foreign Minister Alexander Gorchakov and brings a prepared purchase treaty, a plan to wind down the Russian-American Company, and evidence of London underwriting. At the December meeting, Alexander can compare Pacifica’s offer with authorizing Eduard de Stoeckl to seek a U.S. bid. Pacifica offers the equivalent of **$7.2 million in gold** as a working price, a guaranteed payment at handover, and orderly evacuation and property terms for Russia’s officials, company, and residents. Russia gets a buyer able to take over the territory and pay on a date it can plan around. Its concerns about defending Alaska against Britain and supporting the company already give it reasons to sell. This draft changes the buyer Russia chooses at that point. Historically, Stoeckl opened formal talks with Seward on March 11, 1867. [Russian decision to sell](https://www.loc.gov/collections/meeting-of-frontiers/articles-and-essays/alaska/the-alaska-purchase/), [Stoeckl’s negotiations and the price](https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/1994/winter/alaska-check)

**November–December 1866 · McClellan tests the votes in Washington.** McClellan’s first count falls short. Oregon’s certified vote helps with consent, but senators still see a deferred payment and the loss of their last sovereign Pacific port. John Sherman agrees to open talks through his brother and sponsor a review of the financing. He leaves his vote conditional on the result. McClellan keeps the Richmond armistice and any recognition of Richmond separate. He asks the War and Navy departments to put the alternative to a treaty on paper, starting with the forces, transports, bases, and appropriations required to hold Port Townsend and recover the Columbia. The finance and access terms stay in the treaty or public schedules. The question for Grimes is whether Washington can afford and sustain an alternative. If the Navy makes a credible case for recovery, the President will struggle to hold a two-thirds coalition even with the gold on deposit. Any demand for a permanent U.S. naval base would also break this package, given the fixed border and Pacifica’s prior experience at Sausalito. **February 25 and March 8, 1867 · Bind Russia before the U.S. treaty goes to committee.** Russia and Pacifica sign in St. Petersburg on **February 25** and exchange ratifications there on **March 8**. For that schedule to work, the parties settle the text in principle during the winter talks. Pacifica obtains assent to that text before signature and sends the approved instrument and full powers ahead for exchange. Any material change requiring fresh assent costs time. The treaty takes effect at exchange, with an autumn handover and payment against delivery backed by a separate London bank undertaking in place by March 8. The company’s claims, the evacuation schedule, and residents’ property terms are attached. Indigenous lands and rights require their own arrangements. The exchange makes the sale binding even if McClellan’s treaty fails. Pacifica accepts that exposure and finances it separately. An authenticated copy of the exchange travels from Europe to Washington in March, while an advance dispatch can carry the news. Russia has accepted a guaranteed price and delivery timetable before the date Stoeckl historically opened talks in Washington. **Late March · Send the full cession to the Senate.** McClellan transmits the boundary, trade and evacuation schedules, Oregon’s certified returns, and the Treasury and Navy assessments. He discloses the already-binding Russian sale to Foreign Relations. A U.S. bid for Alaska could have become more attractive as a replacement for Puget Sound, so the settlement depends on Russia having made its choice before the Senate debates the cession. Pacifica’s founding governments have approved the Western terms through their own procedures. The Western treaty is independent of the Russian purchase and the Richmond armistice.

**April 1–17 · Convert the conditional commitments.** McClellan calls an executive session on the historical April timetable. In the first week Grimes presents the naval comparison and Treasury officers confirm the payment guarantees. In the second week, target **April 11** for a favorable Foreign Relations report led by **Sumner, Fessenden, Harlan, and Reverdy Johnson**. Those four are a majority of the seven historical committee members. Sherman and Morgan work the Republican finance votes, Harlan and Ramsey the inland votes, and Johnson the Democratic votes. Budget for opposition from Benjamin Wade and Oliver P. Morton. On April 17 the administration needs **32 firm commitments**, with a working split of **26 Republicans and six Democrats**, or another combination reaching the same total. McClellan also obtains an undertaking from House supporters to bring the implementation and appropriations bills forward after Senate consent. ## 1867 · Exchange and the resulting map

**Exchange and transfer.** After the Senate vote, Congress passes the necessary implementation and appropriations measures. McClellan and Pacifica exchange ratifications later in 1867 against the first Western payment. Survey, evacuation, property, archives, transit, and claims schedules govern the handover. The Western instrument carries the proposed **$40 million in gold through 1872**, while the separate Treasury loan goes through its own authorization. | Region | Working settlement |
|---|---|
| **Oregon** | Joins Pacifica as a confederal state on the charter it approved. Astoria, Portland, and the Oregon coast are Pacifican. U.S. merchants receive guaranteed commercial access to the Columbia. |
| **Washington Territory** | Pacifica receives the Northwest to the agreed British frontier, including the mainland Puget Sound, lower Columbia, and inland Washington districts. The Union evacuates Port Townsend and relinquishes its sovereign Pacific ports. A separate arrangement with Britain handles the San Juan garrisons and preserves the dispute for settlement. |
| **Nevada and Utah** | Federal territorial title passes under the treaty. Nevada and Utah implement their own locally approved terms of accession. Nevada’s mining and claims protections and Utah’s local government and passage terms go into separate instruments. |
| **Idaho and western Montana** | All Idaho is Pacifican. The Montana line follows the mountain front roughly along 111°W to Yellowstone, with the named allocations controlling the survey. Pacifica gets the Missouri headwaters gold country, including Bannack, Virginia City, Helena, and the Gallatin Valley. Fort Benton stays Union. |
| **Southwestern Wyoming** | The line follows the divide through Yellowstone and the Wind River crest, then goes around the Great Divide Basin on the eastern branch toward the Sierra Madre. South Pass, Fort Bridger, the Green River basin, and Rock Springs coal are Pacifican. |
| **Colorado** | Pacifica gets the Western Slope, North and Middle Park, and the San Luis Valley. The line uses the divide and the Poncha Pass–Sangre de Cristo segment, with surveyed exceptions. Denver, the Front Range, Pikes Peak, and Leadville remain Union. |
| **Arizona and New Mexico** | All Arizona and New Mexico within the canon perimeter pass to Pacifica, including the entire Tucson–Mesilla–Albuquerque–Santa Fe route, Taos, and Fort Union after evacuation. The line runs along 37°N to 103°W and then south to Texas. Raton Pass and Fort Union serve as frontier strongpoints under the final allocation. The Union retains no land border with Mexico. Confederate Texas remains a separate neighbor. |

The survey must preserve the Comstock, Idaho and Montana gold, the Butte copper district, Utah and Rock Springs coal, Arizona copper, and the San Juan silver country on the Pacifican side. Leadville’s mineral district remains Union. These are checks against downstream canon. In the 1860s the negotiators can argue from known mines, trade routes, defensible terrain, and future transport costs. **Mexico and the north.** From 1865, Pacifica recognizes and lends to Juárez against Sonora customs. A written wartime transit arrangement and subsequent treaty title let rifles reach his forces through Paso del Norte from Pacifican-held New Mexico. Set the crossing and inspection point with Mexico and keep the route viable independently of Confederate El Paso. By the final cession Washington lacks a land border with Mexico, as CR-058 requires. Maximilian is executed at Querétaro in June 1867. Any Baja purchase or Guaymas concession is a separate, optional bargain. Mexico keeps Sonora.

Alaska is handed over in autumn under the separate Russian treaty already in force. Survey and budget it independently of the Western cession. Keep the existing Pacific article of the Western treaty, which settles U.S. claims to named islands including Midway and preserves the scheduled rights of guano companies. British Columbia remains available as a Canadian partner. The navigation bargain follows in 1871.
