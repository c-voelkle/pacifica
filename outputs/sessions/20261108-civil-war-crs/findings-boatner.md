# Boatner, The Civil War Dictionary — findings (agent pass, 8 Nov 2026; headwords, pages where visible)

- Hampton Roads (MONITOR–MERRIMAC DUEL pp.560–561; NEWPORT NEWS p.592; PENINSULAR CAMPAIGN p.633): Virginia 22 ft draft, 4 knots, 30 min to turn; 8 March rammed Cumberland (sank ~3:30), Congress aground and surrendered ~4:30; Minnesota saved by the ebb tide; Virginia retired ~5 pm meaning to return for Minnesota. "When Norfolk was evacuated 9 May the Confederates had to destroy their ironclad because she could not go up the James and was not sufficiently seaworthy to sail to a new coastal base." McClellan moved 12 divisions by transport to Fort Monroe from 17 March; "Since the Confederate ironclad Merrimac had appeared, the Federal Navy could not protect the transports for a movement up the James." Drewry's Bluff 15 May (Monitor and Galena).
- New Orleans (pp.591–592): Ship Island occupied 3 Dec 1861; Butler staged 15,000 there Feb–Mar 1862; the chain-and-hulk barrier "partially destroyed" by high water in late Feb and again 11 April; Louisiana "unpowered", Mississippi unfinished; mortars from 18 April (2,997 shells on the 18th) did "little damage"; passage 2 am 24 April, no ship lost in the passage, Varuna lost to the river fleet; Union 36 killed/135 wounded; forts mutinied and surrendered 28 April; Butler occupied 1 May; Baton Rouge occupied 12 May 1862. No Duncan/Lovell–Mitchell placement dispute recorded.
- Pensacola (pp.641–642): Confederates burned and abandoned the mainland 9 May 1862; Fort Pickens held throughout; "Pensacola became headquarters for the West Gulf Squadron"; Steele's 13,000 marched from Pensacola on Mobile, March 1865. ~10,000 of Bragg's Pensacola army went to Corinth for Shiloh.
- Mobile Bay (pp.558–559; forts 3–23 Aug 1864): four monitors, 14 wooden ships; Tecumseh mined 7:45; Tennessee "under-powered", stopped firing ~9:40 from defective fuzes, surrendered 10 am; 319 Union casualties; Granger's 5,500 (XIII Corps); "Diversion of land forces to take part in Banks's Red River Campaign had delayed Farragut's proposed operations against Mobile." Chickasaw (4-gun monitor) attacked Fort Powell. Siege train for Fort Morgan came from New Orleans.
- FARRAGUT (p.276): returned to the Gulf Jan 1864; "again returned in Dec. '64 to N.Y.C., this time in failing health"; Vice Admiral 23 Dec 1864; entered Richmond after its fall. PORTER: North Atlantic Squadron from Oct 1864; Fort Fisher Dec 1864 (Butler, 6,500) and Jan 1865 (Terry, 8,000; "nearly 60 vessels and 627 guns"); XXIII Corps redirected to Cape Fear after Fort Fisher.
- Shiloh (pp.753–757): Buell "delayed 10 days by the flooded Duck River"; Confederate 40,000 "took a day longer than estimated to cover the intervening 25 miles"; attack 6 April; Johnston killed ~2:30 pm; Nelson's lead brigade (Ammen) crossed in time on the 6th, rest of the division that night; Lew Wallace arrived ~7:15 pm; all of Buell's divisions but Thomas's fought on the 7th. Iowa Brigade (11th, 13th, 15th, 16th Iowa): 15th Iowa colonel commissioned 22 Feb 1862, 16th Iowa colonel 15 Mar 1862, both "led [the] regiment at Shiloh"; 13th Iowa (Crocker) at Shiloh.
- Vicksburg (pp.872–877): Porter ran the batteries 16 April; Bruinsburg 30 April; "On 18 May Johnston had warned Pemberton ... advised the city be abandoned so that the army might be saved. After a council of war, however, Pemberton elected to defend Vicksburg." XIII Corps transferred to the Dept of the Gulf 28 July 1863.
- Port Hudson: Farragut's passage 14 March 1863; Banks's XIX Corps from 26 May; surrendered 9 July after Vicksburg; ~7,200 Confederates, 5,500 prisoners; Banks took it "only after the fall of Vicksburg had rendered it untenable".
- Texas coast (pp.833 etc.): Galveston taken 5 Oct 1862 by Renshaw's naval force, garrisoned by 260 men of the 42nd Mass.; retaken 1 Jan 1863. Sabine Pass: "Franklin left New Orleans on 5 Sept. with 4,000 men of his XIX Corps (Gulf)". Rio Grande: "On 26 Oct. 3,500 men of the XIII Corps, under N. J. T. Dana, left New Orleans with an escort of three warships"; Brownsville occupied 6 Nov; Banks's stated purpose "to discourage any idea the French might have of invading the state from Mexico"; Brownsville "opened to commerce" 18 Feb 1864. Ford's July 1864 recapture: not covered.
- Atlanta (pp.31–34): Johnston relieved "the evening of the 17th" July — "Johnston's Fabian tactics had exasperated the Confederates"; Hood evacuated 5 pm 1 Sept. Sherman's "140-mile line of communications to Chattanooga". Wheeler's raid 10 Aug–10 Sept 1864.
- Election: LINCOLN — "the military successes of Grant and Sherman swung sentiment to him". Sheridan: Winchester 19 Sept, Fisher's Hill 22 Sept, Cedar Creek 19 Oct. Hampton Roads conference 3 Feb 1865 "failed". Early's raid: Fort Stevens 11–12 July 1864; 1st Div XIX Corps "had arrived at Fort Monroe from New Orleans" and was diverted to Washington.
- New Mexico (pp.590–591): Baylor took Fort Fillmore 27 July 1861, proclaimed Confederate Arizona 1 Aug (capital Mesilla); Sibley 2,600 up the Rio Grande Jan 1862; Valverde 21 Feb; Federals emptied Albuquerque depot 1 March and took 120 wagonloads out of Santa Fe 4 March; Sibley took Santa Fe 23 March; Glorieta 26/28 March "indecisive"; Sibley "being unable to resupply his forces ... decided to withdraw"; Canby (860 regulars + 350 volunteers from Fort Craig) let him go; on Carleton's approach Sibley retreated to San Antonio "with 1,700 fewer men". Baylor "removed by Davis". Slough fought Glorieta against Canby's orders. Connelly, Chivington, Mesilla's summer-1862 garrison: not covered.
- California Column: 11 infantry companies, 2 cavalry, 2 batteries, ordered by George Wright; left southern California 13 April 1862, reached Santa Fe 20 Sept 1862; Carleton B.G. 28 April 1862, Dept of New Mexico 18 Sept 1862–27 June 1865.
- Connor (p.172): Col. 3rd California 4 Sept 1861; Bear River "in Washington Territory 29 Jan. 63"; B.G. 30 March 1863. Camp Douglas (Utah), Lot Smith, Morrill Act, Nevada, Nye, Fort Churchill: not covered.
- Pacific commands (pp.614–615): Dept of the Pacific HQ San Francisco — Sumner 25 Apr–Oct 1861, Wright to 1 July 1864, McDowell to June 1865; District of Oregon — Cady 23 Oct 1861–7 Apr 1862, then Alvord throughout. Casey B.G. USV 31 Aug 1861, in the East (IV Corps division at Fair Oaks). Withdrawal of the Pacific regulars, Panama, Pacific Squadron, Lancaster: not covered. McClellan's 1853 Cascades survey: not covered.
- Wagon doctrine, Santa Fe Trail freight, Confederate rail detail: not covered.
