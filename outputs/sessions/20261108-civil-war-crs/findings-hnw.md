# Hattaway & Jones, How the North Won — findings (agent pass, 8 Nov 2026; printed page numbers)

- Wagon radius: fn.14 p.78 — animals eat 5–10× men; carrying fodder "reduced the practical radius of wagon supply to less than 100 miles"; at 100 mi wagons deliver "not ... one ounce of cargo unless fodder could be supplied along the route"; "An army far smaller than Sherman's could have received essentials by wagon because the animals of a smaller number of wagons could be fed along the route". Rosecrans's 60-mile Chattanooga road "the extreme limit of the radius of wagon transportation" (p.457). Meigs elated Sherman's raiders could move >25 mi from rail/river "without perishing" (p.661). Sherman: 160 cars/day for 100,000 men = 36,800 six-mule wagons at 2 t, 20 mi/day (p.59).
- Rivers: "no large army could move without rail or water communications" (p.58); Union western advances ended where "the secure, efficient communications of the Tennessee River ended" (p.684); Mississippi line "completely secure" under gunboats (p.410).
- CSS Virginia: 22 ft draft, 4 knots, rode too high, "remained permanently in her first port" (pp.128–130); 8 March destroyed Cumberland and Congress, Minnesota grounded (p.131); Stanton's panic vs Totten ("Fort Monroe ... as inaccessible to the Merrimack as if it were in the Moon"), Meigs: "If she gets out, there is nothing in this country that can beat her" (pp.132–133); "powerful effect on McClellan's strategic thinking" until Norfolk evacuated ~9 May; Confederates then had to destroy her (p.178); York blocked by batteries (p.171); Drewry's Bluff 15 May (p.179). Union built 21 more ironclads in two months, 60 monitors eventually (pp.130, 136).
- New Orleans: mortars from 18 April, Farragut passed 24 April, Butler occupied 1 May (p.143); 5,000 men had gone from New Orleans to Corinth (p.157). Louisiana/obstruction: not covered.
- Mobile Bay 5 Aug 1864: only 4,000 army troops; Tecumseh sunk, 319 US casualties; authors: the belated occupation "mattered little" (pp.620–624). Farragut's health etc.: not covered.
- Shiloh: 40,335 CS vs 42,682 US before Buell's 20,000; attack planned 4 April, army barely left Corinth 3 April; Beauregard postponed 24 h; slow march 4 April; 5 April spent deploying in fog, ready only 4 pm (pp.163–164); Beauregard wanted to withdraw, Johnston: "I would fight them if they were a million"; Buell's army at Savannah by 5 April (p.164); Johnston died mid-afternoon (p.168); Beauregard halted on an erroneous report that Buell was far away (p.169); Lew Wallace and Buell's 20,000 ferried overnight, "Grant's two stalwart allies—night and Buell" (p.169). Casualties 10,699 CS / 13,047 US.
- Vicksburg: Grant crossed at Bruinsburg 30 April; Johnston ordered Pemberton to save the army and march NE, Pemberton "remained in paralyzed inactivity" (p.410); surrender 4 July, 2,166 officers/27,230 men/172 guns (p.411). Port Hudson four days later. Authors (Goff): the trans-Mississippi link "was not a vital blow in terms of supply management, for the two sections ... had long since ceased to rely on each other"; the Mississippi conquest "almost a white elephant" except psychologically (pp.421–422).
- After Vicksburg: XIII Corps (13,000) to Banks, who went by sea to the Texas coast (p.432); Sherman's corps to Chattanooga via Memphis–Corinth, marching most of the way (pp.455–457). Lincoln/Halleck's trans-Mississippi preoccupation "contributed to Chickamauga and ended with Banks's fiasco on the Red River" (p.690).
- Sabine Pass 8 Sept 1863: Dowling's 43 men turned back Banks's expedition (p.457). Rio Grande expedition unnamed. Matamoros: open border, Texas told to "trade with Mexico for supplies" (pp.18, 421).
- Atlanta: 100,000 vs 60,000; Johnston relieved 17 July for revealing no plan (pp.604–606); Hood lost ~15,000 vs 6,000 in July; Jonesboro, evacuation 1 Sept, Sherman in 2 Sept (pp.608–623). Authors: Johnston could not have held Atlanta by defence; a flank position on the Blue Mountain–Selma railroad "could ... perhaps have forestalled Atlanta's capitulation" and left "a stalemate", but Grant's strategy of raids "would have had equal effect without Atlanta's fall" — "Sherman could have begun his raid at any time" (pp.630, 688). Atlanta's fall hurt logistics "badly but not fatally" (p.623).
- Sherman's line: single source of supply; blockhouses; "the Confederates never did manage to do any effective damage to Sherman's supply lines" (p.612); Wheeler ineffective.
- Election: before Atlanta "many observers foresaw a Democratic victory"; Greeley et al. 2 Sept letters to shelve Lincoln; Atlanta "changed the political climate", reinforced by Sheridan's victories (Winchester 19 Sept, Fisher's Hill 22 Sept, Cedar Creek) (pp.617–625); result 55%, 212–21 (p.701). Mobile Bay given no political weight. 23 Aug memo, October states, soldier vote: not covered.
- Overland: >60,000 Union losses in six weeks (p.590); Early 14,000, Fort Stevens 11–12 July, VI Corps arrived by ship from City Point (pp.600–602). Hampton Roads conference "futile" (p.666), undated.
- 1864 strategy: "the strategy distinctly belonged to Grant" (p.696); Sherman the executor who gave it its political dimension; Baldy Smith and Sheridan shared the outlook; Thomas did not ("would never do to conduct one of your campaigns", p.661); Halleck and Lincoln never grasped the North Carolina raid (p.513). Meridian raid Feb 1864 "a dress rehearsal" (p.506).
- Halleck: SF lawyer, railroad president, California militia major general in 1861; regular major general Aug 1861; general in chief 11 July 1862 (pp.54–55, 209).
- New Mexico: Sibley; Glorieta 28 March, trains destroyed, desertions, retreat (p.162). Carleton, Connor, Nevada, Oregon, Panama, Iowa regiments: not covered.
- Transfers: Longstreet ~1,000 mi over ten railroads, first troops in 10 days, last 16, two-thirds in time (p.444); Hooker's 25,000 1,200 mi, first in 7 days, last 11.5 (p.456); Sherman to Virginia by sea would take two months (p.660).
