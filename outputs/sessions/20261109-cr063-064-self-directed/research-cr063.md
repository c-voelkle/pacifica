# CR-063 research previews (fact-check of the real record)

Session 2026-11-09. This checks the historical record only, paraphrased, with quotes under 15 words. "OR" = *Official Records*. Weak sources (blogs, Wikipedia) are marked.

## Load-bearing: Vicksburg, 17–19 May 1863 (V1, V3, open decision 8; task 8)

**L1. Johnston's message.**
**Finding:** Johnston wrote on 17 May: if not too late, evacuate Vicksburg and "march to the northeast". Pemberton received it about noon on 18 May. Johnston's 18 May answer to Pemberton's reply said he was gathering a relief force: "Hold out." The same day he ordered Gardner to leave Port Hudson.
**Source:** NPS Handbook 21 ch. 6 (https://npshistory.com/handbooks/historical/21/hh21c6.htm); OR I/24/1 p. 242 (https://civilwar.com/official-record/991-vicksburg-part-i/182722-242-series-i-volume-xxiv-i-serial-36-vicksburg-part-i.html).
**Verdict:** partly. Pemberton received it on 18 May, but it was written on the 17th.
**Draft change needed:** V1 should read "Johnston's letter of 17 May, received on the 18th".

**L2. The council of war.**
**Finding:** It met in Vicksburg on 18 May, right after the letter arrived. The generals "unanimously" judged the army could not be withdrawn in useful condition, and Pemberton decided to hold. Union guns opened while it sat. No council at the Big Black on 17 May was found. The earlier council was on 14 May near Edwards.
**Source:** OR I/24/1 p. 242 (above); OR I/24/3 p. 890 (https://civilwar.com/official-record/993-vicksburg-part-iii/184878-890-series-i-volume-xxiv-iii-serial-38-vicksburg-part-iii.html); NPS Civil War Series sec. 5 (https://npshistory.com/publications/civil_war_series/24/sec5.htm).
**Verdict:** agrees with 18 May in the city. The Big Black alternative is unsupported.
**Draft change needed:** close open decision 8 on 18 May at Vicksburg, and drop the 17 May note in V3.

**L3. Union positions on 18–19 May; was a route open?**
**Finding:**
- **XV Corps (Sherman).** It crossed the Big Black at Bridgeport by pontoon on the night of 17 May and the morning of the 18th. Sherman's report says the column reached the Benton road "by 9.30 a.m. of May 18". The corps then "commanded the Yazoo", with a battery at the forks. By 8 a.m. on 19 May it had closed the north side, its right on the Mississippi. The 4th Iowa Cavalry took the evacuated Haynes' Bluff battery by 4 p.m. on 19 May.
- **Confederate side.** Snyder's Mill had already been ordered abandoned. On 18 May the Confederates reported Union troops crossing the Yazoo at Brandon's Ferry.
- **XVII Corps (McPherson)** camped near the lines on the Jackson road on the night of 18 May.
- **XIII Corps (McClernand)** went by Mount Albans to the Baldwin's Ferry road.
- **Grant:** by the morning of 19 May the investment was "as complete as my limited number of troops would allow".
- **First assault:** 2 p.m. on 19 May, on the Graveyard road at the Stockade Redan.
**Source:** OR I/24/1 p. 755, Sherman's report (https://civilwar.com/official-record/991-vicksburg-part-i/183235-755-series-i-volume-xxiv-i-serial-36-vicksburg-part-i.html); Grant, *Memoirs* ch. 36 (https://americanliterature.com/author/ulysses-s-grant/book/personal-memoirs-of-us-grant/chapter-xxxvi); NPS, First Assault (https://www.nps.gov/vick/learn/historyculture/firstassault.htm).
**Verdict:** **disagrees** with a march northeast on the night of 18–19 May. Sherman was astride the Benton/Yazoo City road from mid-morning on 18 May, before the council voted. My inference, not sourced: the south side (Hall's Ferry and Warrenton roads) was the thinnest part of the line.
**Draft change needed:** one of three. (a) March on the night of 17–18 May; this needs a council the record lacks. (b) Fight through Sherman's forks on 18–19 May. (c) Go south first, then northeast. Canon's "a Union advance closes the route" fits all three.

**L4. Strengths.**
**Finding:** Pemberton had about 30,000 inside the lines, and 29,495 surrendered on 4 July. Loring's division escaped to Johnston. NPS says Johnston gathered "some 31,000" around Jackson. Wikipedia gives about 32,000 by 1 June, gathering at Canton.
**Source:** Epperson chronology (https://www.jfepperson.org/vicks1.htm); NPS Civil War Series sec. 9 (https://npshistory.com/publications/civil_war_series/24/sec9.htm); Wikipedia, Vicksburg campaign (last resort).
**Verdict:** agrees with about 30,000 on each side.
**Draft change needed:** V3's 15,000 out plus 8,000 in makes 23,000. Either account for about 7,000 more (sick, the river batteries, losses), or raise the figures.

**L5. Davis's instructions.**
**Finding:** Davis telegraphed Pemberton on 7 May 1863 that holding "both Vicksburg and Port Hudson" was necessary to the trans-Mississippi connection. On 18 May he urged Johnston to unite with Pemberton.
**Source:** HistoryNet (https://historynet.com/unsupported-and-outmatched/); OR I/24/1 p. 190 (https://civilwar.com/official-record/991-vicksburg-part-i/182670-190-series-i-volume-xxiv-i-serial-36-vicksburg-part-i.html).
**Verdict:** agrees.
**Draft change needed:** none. The CR can cite the 7 May telegram as what canon's "Richmond authorizes" replaces.

## 1. New Orleans barrier, *Louisiana*, *Pensacola* (N1–N4)

**Finding:**
- **Barrier.** The log-and-chain raft gave way to drift from February. Its cables parted on 10 March (weak source), and the gap was filled with dismasted schooners on chains.
- **Night of 20 April.** *Itasca* and *Pinola* (with *Kineo*, per NHHC) went up. *Pinola*'s petard failed. *Itasca* rammed through the chain and opened a single-file gap.
- ***Pensacola***: second ship of Bailey's first division, behind *Cayuga*.
- **Losses.** One Union ship was lost, *Varuna*, sunk by rams just above the forts. Three third-division gunboats turned back.
- ***Louisiana*.** She was moored half a mile above Fort St. Philip on 21 April, unable to steam. Duncan asked for her to be moved downstream against the mortars. A naval council under Mitchell refused unanimously: the water was deep, the risk high, and her guns could not elevate enough. Earlier McIntosh had refused because she had no power. NHHC says some of her mountings limited her arcs of fire.
**Source:** NHHC, Forts Jackson and St. Philip (https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/wars-conflicts-and-operations/civil-war/cw-operations-and-engagements/1862-civil-war/forts-jackson-and-stphilip.html); NHHC, Farragut lessons learned (https://history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/heritage/usn-lessons-learned/farragut-new-orleans.html); SHSP vol. 2, Mitchell inquiry (https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Southern_Historical_Society_Papers/Volume_02/November/Letter_from_Commander_Mitchell_on_Defence_of_New_Orleans); NHHC, CSS *Louisiana* (https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/confederate_ships/louisiana.html); weak: https://historyweblog.com/2018/05/preliminary-operations-before-new-orleans.
**Verdict:** N1, N2 and N4 agree; the quarrel is real and usable. **N3 disagrees:** a ship was lost (*Varuna*).
**Draft change needed:** N3 should read "historically only *Varuna* was lost, to the rams above the forts". For the N2 scene, the documented hinge is gun elevation and mountings.

## 2. *Tennessee*; Mobile 1862 (M3, S3)

**Finding:**
- ***Tennessee*.** Commissioned at Selma on 16 February 1864. Lifted over the Dog River bar on "camels" and anchored in the lower bay on 18 May 1864. Her tiller chains were exposed on deck and shot away. Hits jammed her sliding port shutters at bow and stern. Her primers "snapped several times". Speed about 5 knots.
- **Mobile 1862.** Benjamin's order of 18 February 1862 told Bragg to withdraw from Pensacola and Mobile and abandon the Gulf coast except New Orleans, but to leave "an effective garrison" in Mobile's forts. Mobile's 1862 garrison strength: not found.
**Source:** NHHC, CSS *Tennessee* (https://history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/confederate_ships/tennessee.html); *Navy in the Civil War* III.8, after Mahan (https://usnlp.org/OurNavy/section_III_chapter_VIII.html); OR I/6 p. 828 (https://civilwar.com/official-record/574-fort-pulaski-new-orleans/156053-828-series-i-volume-vi-serial-6-fort-pulaski-new-orleans.html).
**Verdict:** M3 agrees, but the battle literature says **primers**, not fuzes. S3 partly agrees: Mobile's forts were to be kept.
**Draft change needed:** M3: "primers (Boatner: fuzes)". S3: "stripped to its forts" rather than "abandoned".

## 3. The Matamoros road and the Rio Grande station (T4, Part IV)

**Finding:**
- **The Cotton Road** ran from the Alleyton railhead by wagon via Goliad, San Patricio and the King Ranch to Brownsville. After November 1863 it went to Laredo and Eagle Pass, about 300 miles upriver.
- **Louisiana link.** The military road from Niblett's Bluff to Alexandria was about 130 miles. The total from Matamoros to the Mississippi: not found.
- **Off the mouth.** US warships seized *Labuan* off Bagdad (February 1862), and *Phaeton* faced down USS *Montgomery* (April 1862).
**Source:** Clio, Alleyton (https://theclio.com/entry/24911); Texas State Library 1864 (https://www.tsl.texas.gov/exhibits/civilwar/1864_2.html); Clio, military road (https://theclio.com/entry/21331); weak: https://brettschulte.net/CWBlog/2014/09/15/bagdad-back-door-to-the-confederacy.
**Verdict:** the route agrees; the 700-mile total is not found. **T4 partly disagrees:** there was no declared blockade (see 6), only cruisers off the mouth.
**Draft change needed:** in T4, replace "blockade station" with "cruising station".

## 4. Ship Island; squadron refit (S1, S2, CT1)

**Finding:** The Confederates evacuated Ship Island about 17 September 1861 as USS *Massachusetts* arrived, and Union control began then. Phelps landed about 2,500 men on 3 December 1861. Butler's force peaked at more than 15,000 in April 1862. *Hartford* went to Pensacola "for repairs" in August 1862.
**Source:** NPS Gulf Islands (https://www.nps.gov/guis/learn/historyculture/uss-massachusetts.htm); Mississippi History Now (https://mshistorynow.mdah.ms.gov/issue/union-soldiers-on-ship-island-during-the-civil-war); ACW High Command (https://americancivilwarhighcommand.com/chronology-day-by-day/chronology-1861/1861-december-3rd); Wikipedia, USS *Hartford* (last resort).
**Verdict:** S1 and CT1 agree. S2 partly agrees: the burned yard repaired ships from August 1862.
**Draft change needed:** confine S2's "cannot repair" to May–June 1862.

## 5. Burnside's recall; Phelps (S4, S5)

**Finding:** While preparing to move on Goldsboro, Burnside was ordered to Virginia to cover McClellan's withdrawal. He left about 6 July 1862 with about 7,000 men, the core of the IX Corps. That was before Halleck took office on 11 July. The exact order date and landing place were not found. Phelps commanded at Ship Island from December 1861.
**Source:** Wikipedia, Burnside's North Carolina Expedition (last resort).
**Verdict:** partly agrees.
**Draft change needed:** in S4, the War Department ordered Burnside north during the Seven Days, and Halleck inherited the decision.

## 6. The *Peterhoff* (T4)

**Finding:** USS *Vanderbilt* seized her near St. Thomas on 25 February 1863. In the 1866 ruling, the Rio Grande's mouth was not blockaded without "an express declaration", and neutral trade with Matamoros was free. Overland carriage into Texas broke no blockade. Military goods were contraband even when consigned to Matamoros and were condemned with the owner's other cargo. The ship was restored on payment of costs.
**Source:** 72 U.S. 28 (1866) (https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/72/28).
**Verdict:** agrees. Continuous voyage was applied to contraband, not to the blockade.
**Draft change needed:** "seized 1863; Supreme Court 1866".

## 7. Grant on Mobile; Welles and Farragut; Wilmington (M4, M-a)

**Finding:**
- **Grant on Mobile.** He proposed Mobile after Vicksburg and renewed it in July and about 1 August 1863. Halleck refused because Banks was going to Texas. On 9 August Lincoln wrote that Mobile was "tempting" but that Texas came first. In winter 1863–64 Grant again proposed a Banks move on Mobile.
- **Welles and Farragut.** On 5 September 1864 Welles named Farragut to the North Atlantic Squadron for Wilmington. Farragut had reported failing health on 27 August, and on 22 September he asked for leave again. Porter got the command.
- **Wilmington.** The XXIII Corps moved from Clifton, Tennessee, to Fort Fisher in 17 days. Schofield marched from Wilmington to join Sherman at Goldsboro (23 March 1865).
**Source:** Grant, *Memoirs* ch. 39 (https://www.historyofwar.org/sources/acw/grant/chapter39c.html) and ch. 46 (https://www.wtj.com/archives/grant/grant46b.htm); Lincoln to Grant, 9 Aug 1863 (https://civilwarnotebook.blogspot.com/2013/08/president-abraham-lincoln-to-major.html); History Central, Sept 1864 (https://historycentral.com/navy/cwnavalhistory/September1864.html); https://runningtheblockade.blogspot.com/2020/01/january-21-1865-schofields-xxiii-corps.html.
**Verdict:** agrees. The historical reason for the refusal was health.
**Draft change needed:** in M4, note that the historical refusal was on health. Replace "Sherman's supply" with "Schofield's junction with Sherman".

## 8. Jackson; dispersal; Port Hudson (V4, V5, open decision 9)

**Finding:**
- **Jackson expedition, 5–25 July 1863.** Sherman led about 40,000. Johnston left Jackson on the night of 16 July.
- **Dispersal.** The IX Corps went to Kentucky. The XIII Corps went to Banks on 7 August. A division of 5,000 went to Missouri. Troops freed by the siege joined Steele at Helena for Little Rock.
- **XV Corps.** It went by steamer to Memphis and was moving by 3 October. A start on 22 September: not found.
- **Port Hudson.** About 7,500 (ABT), or "no more than seven thousand" (64 Parishes), at the Red River junction with a rail line from Clinton. Banks landed above it at Bayou Sara on 22 May 1863 (weak source). Morganza and Tunica: not found.
**Source:** Wikipedia, Jackson expedition (last resort); Grant ch. 39 (above); Encyclopedia of Arkansas (https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Little-Rock-Campaign-517); NPS Handbook 25 (https://www.npshistory.com/handbooks/historical/25/hh25i.htm); ABT (https://www.battlefields.org/learn/civil-war/battles/port-hudson); 64 Parishes (https://64parishes.org/?p=60376); weak: https://pth.thehardyparty.com/battle_desc.htm.
**Verdict:** V4 and V5 agree. Part III partly agrees: a corps went to Kentucky, divisions to Arkansas and Missouri.
**Draft change needed:** Part III wording as above. Bayou Sara is a documented landing for open decision 9.

## 9. Armour; vice admiral (F1–F3, M4)

**Finding:**
- ***New Ironsides*.** Launched 10 May 1862, commissioned 21 August 1862. Off Charleston from January 1863, under repair in June–August 1864, then at both Fort Fisher attacks.
- **Monitors.** Nine Passaic-class were commissioned between November 1862 and April 1863. Five Canonicus-class were commissioned between April and September 1864, *Tecumseh* lost. That is 14 by December 1864, not counting one-offs.
- ***Galena* at Drewry's Bluff.** 28 hits and 18 penetrations (one count). DANFS gives 12 killed and 15 wounded.
- **Vice admiral.** Congress passed the bill on 22 December, Lincoln signed it on 23 December 1864, and the date of rank is 21 December.
**Source:** DANFS, *New Ironsides* (https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/n/new-ironsides.html); Navypedia, Passaic (https://navypedia.org/ships/usa/us_mon_passaic.htm); Wikipedia, Canonicus class (last resort); DANFS, *Galena* (https://history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/g/galena-i.html); https://www.historyofwar.org/articles/battles_drewrys_bluff_1862.html; https://americancivilwarhighcommand.com/chronology-day-by-day/chronology-1864/1864-december-23rd/.
**Verdict:** *New Ironsides* agrees; the Part V table's "1861" is only right as the order year. The monitor count partly agrees. The rank date partly agrees.
**Draft change needed:** M4: "created 23 December, rank from the 21st". Part V: "about fourteen" (or count the one-offs).

## 10. Distances (Part VI, Part II)

**Finding:**
- Norfolk to Key West 969 nm, plus Key West to Pensacola 510 nm, gives about 1,480 nm.
- Pensacola to Port Isabel (Brazos Santiago) 632 nm; to Brownsville 643 nm.
**Source:** NOAA, *Distances Between United States Ports*, Tables 12 and 16 (https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/publications/docs/distances.pdf). The extraction was partly garbled, so verify.
**Verdict:** partly. The draft's figures are slightly low, and "600 miles" holds only in nautical miles.
**Draft change needed:** "about 1,450–1,500 nm" and "about 630 nm".
