## Load-bearing

### L1 · Snake Creek Gap (task 8; A2)

- **F** Thomas's cavalry found the unguarded gap in February 1864; Thomas proposed sending the Army of the Cumberland through it to Resaca. Sherman adopted the route but gave it to McPherson. Cox says Thomas "very earnestly" urged that the other two armies hold Dalton while his went through; Sherman "preferred his own view". Letter text and date not found. **S** NPS Civil War Series, *The Campaign for Atlanta*, sec. 2 (npshistory.com/publications/civil_war_series/7/sec2.htm); Cox, *Atlanta*, quoted in CivilWarTalk, "Decision at Snake Creek Gap" (civilwartalk.com/goto/post?id=914783). **V** agrees. **Δ** none; cite Cox/NPS instead of "Hist.; to verify".
- **F** Dates: McPherson passed the gap 8 May, came out mid-morning 9 May, reached the hills before Resaca mid-afternoon, skirmished and fell back to the gap's mouth without cutting the railroad. Johnston knew by about 10 a.m. on 9 May and that night railed three of Hood's divisions toward Resaca, most of which went back. Johnston left Dalton the night of 12 May; Resaca was fought 13/14–15 May; Johnston crossed the Oostanaula the night of the 15th. **S** NPS sec. 2; Stephen Davis, ABT "Johnston is Turned" (battlefields.org/learn/articles/johnston-turned); Wikipedia "Battle of Resaca". **V** agrees.
- **F** Gap and road: gorge about 4 miles (Wikipedia); Resaca 5–8 miles beyond its mouth on "a characteristic Georgia wagon-track" (Cox). McPherson's ~23,000–25,000 took from 8 May to the morning of the 9th; three more corps passed on 13–14 May. No figure found for 60,000. **S** Wikipedia "Battle of Resaca"; Cox; Georgia marker 064-8 (hmdb.org/m.asp?m=13913). **V** partly. **Δ** keep "two days" as MODEL, not Hist.
- **F** Resaca's defenders on 9 May: ~4,000 per Castel; >6,000 with Reynolds's and Vaughan's brigades per a forum critic. Cox thought Johnston could have moved his main body there on the night of the 9th. No historian's judgement found on a whole-Cumberland trap; only forum opinion. **S** Castel, *Decision in the West*, p. 138 (via civilwartalk.com/goto/post?id=912971); Cox as above. Cleaves, Einolf, McMurry: **not found**. **V** not found (judgement).
- **F — key interaction with A1/A5.** Polk's Army of Mississippi was part of what held Resaca. Cantey's division of 5,300 came from Mobile on 7 May. Loring's division of 5,145 reached Rome on 10 May and Resaca on 10–12 May, and Polk arrived with it on the 11th. Polk sent nearly all his infantry on Johnston's request of 4 May. **S** Wikipedia "Battle of Resaca" (reinforcement table); NPS sec. 2. **V** **disagrees with the draft's internal logic.** If Polk "never" joins (A1), Resaca in mid-May lacks Loring and probably Cantey, and the gap move is more dangerous than A2 allows. **Δ** Either let Polk's troops come to Resaca in May and go back to Alabama in June (and say so), or show why Johnston still escapes without them (rail shift of Hood's corps, as on 9–10 May).

### L2 · Polk and the Blue Mountain line (task 9; A1, A5, A6)

- **F** Polk's reinforcements of May 1864: Cantey's division 5,300 (7 May); Loring's 5,145 (10–12 May); a French detachment of 550 (12 May); Jackson's cavalry 4,477 (17 May); French's division 4,174 (19 May). That totals about 19,650. **S** Wikipedia "Battle of Resaca". **V** agrees with the draft's ~20,000.
- **F** Johnston's army: 53,800 present at Dalton, about 68,800 with reinforcements (ABT). The 30 April return gives about 52,900 of all arms (Wikipedia). Union: about 99,000 effectives. **S** ABT "The Atlanta Campaign" (battlefields.org/learn/articles/atlanta-campaign); Wikipedia "Battle of Resaca". **V** partly. **Δ** A1: "60,000 … alone, excluding Polk" is high. Without Polk it is about 53,000–56,000. Lower it or cite it as H&J's, and recompute A7.
- **F** The Alabama and Tennessee Rivers Railroad (Selma–Blue Mountain) carried trains to Blue Mountain from spring 1862. The link to Rome was never finished. In May 1864 an engineer reported partial grading at both ends, about 30 miles still ungraded, no track laid and no iron obtained. The line ran "regularly" until late March 1865. **S** csa-railroads.com, "The Construction of the Blue Mountain Railroad"; company superintendent's report, 14 April 1866 (csa-railroads.com/Essays/Orignial%20Docs/AR/AR,_A_and_TR_4-1-66_S.htm). **V** agrees: the line ran to Blue Mountain and worked in 1864. **Δ** A6 should say that Blue Mountain is a railhead with no rail to Rome. An army there threatens Thomas only by a long march (inference), which supports "beyond his wagon radius" but weakens "come in behind him".
- **F** Rousseau's raid, 10–22 July 1864, about 2,700 men: at Talladega on the 15th it burned the depot and two cars of the A&TR. The line was suspended only a few days. The main damage was some 30 miles of the Montgomery & West Point. **S** Encyclopedia of Alabama, "Rousseau's Raid"; 1866 A&TR report. **V** n/a (draft silent). **Δ** none required. (Rousseau's was Sherman's raid; its absence fits R5.)
- **F** Polk was killed on Pine Mountain on 14 June 1864 by Union artillery. **S** ABT "The Death of Bishop Polk". **V** agrees.
- **F** H&J's flank-position argument: **not found** online (book only).

### L3 · Thomas: record, seniority, Nashville (task 8; C7, A9; Part III)

- **F** Seniority (dates of rank):
  - Thomas: BG volunteers 17 Aug 1861; MG volunteers 25 Apr 1862; BG Regular Army 27 Oct 1863; MG Regular Army after Nashville.
  - McPherson: MG volunteers 8 Oct 1862; BG Regular Army 1 Aug 1863 (appointed 4 Aug).
  - Hooker: MG volunteers 5 May 1862; BG Regular Army 20 Sep 1862.
  - Schofield: MG volunteers 29 Nov 1862, not confirmed until May 1863.
  - Howard: MG volunteers "spring 1863" (exact date not found).

  **S** HistoryCentral (Thomas); American Civil War High Command chronology, 27 Oct 1863 and 1 Aug 1863; ABT biographies of McPherson, Schofield and Howard; thelatinlibrary.com civilwarnotes, Hooker. **V** **partly.** Thomas is senior as a major general of volunteers, but by ten days only over Hooker. In Regular Army grade, Hooker (Sep 1862) and McPherson (Aug 1863) both rank Thomas (Oct 1863). **Δ** C7 and the Part III table: say "senior major general of volunteers". Hooker is the awkward name: he was senior in regular rank and nearly level in volunteer rank.
- **F** Nashville. Grant pressed Thomas by telegram on 2, 5, 6, 8 and 11 December. On 9 December he asked Halleck for an order putting Schofield in command, then suspended it after Halleck reported the ice storm. On 13 December he sent Logan to take over if Thomas had not attacked by the time he arrived. Grant got as far as Washington on 15 December. Thomas attacked on 15–16 December, and Logan stopped at Louisville. Grant's memoirs call the delay "unaccountable" and Thomas "deliberate and so slow, though effective in defence". **S** ABT "Battle of Nashville: Enemies Front and Rear" (battlefields.org/learn/articles/battle-nashville-enemies-front-and-rear); Grant, *Personal Memoirs*, ch. 60 (historyofwar.org/sources/acw/grant/chapter60b.html). **V** agrees with A9's analogue. **Δ** open decision 13 can cite both steps (Schofield order suspended; Logan sent).
- **F** Thomas and raids: after Nashville Thomas sent Wilson's cavalry corps (~13,500) on the Selma raid of March–April 1865. At Chattanooga Grant records Thomas saying he "could not move a single piece of artillery". **S** Wikipedia "Wilson's Raid"; Encyclopedia of Alabama, "Battle of Selma"; Grant, *Memoirs*, ch. 45 (historyofwar.org/sources/acw/grant/chapter45.html). **V** partly: Thomas did order a deep cavalry raid when he held the cards. **Δ** Part III/R5 should say "Thomas does not cut loose with infantry". Cavalry raids are in character.

### L4 · Halleck (task 7; H1–H3, §9 text)

- **F** Halleck sailed to California with the Army on the *Lexington* round Cape Horn, served in the military government as secretary of state, and helped write the 1849 constitution. He resigned from the Army in August 1854. **S** thelatinlibrary.com civilwarnotes, "Henry Wager Halleck"; California State Military Museum (militarymuseum.org/Halleck.html). **V** n/a (the draft departs from this knowingly). **Δ** H1 "about 1854" can rest on the real resignation date, August 1854.
- **F** The firm: Billings and Peachy founded it in 1849 and Halleck joined soon after. It acted for claimants to Mexican land grants and had more than half of the land-claim cases after the Land Act of 1851. Halleck was director (or director-general) of the New Almaden quicksilver company. The Almaden litigation itself: **not found**. **S** Wikipedia "Halleck, Peachy & Billings"; Military Museum. **V** agrees with H1.
- **F** Railway: "Atlantic and Pacific Railroad" (1855), San Francisco–San José. **S** latinlibrary; Military Museum. **V** agrees in substance. **Δ** the name is Atlantic & Pacific, not "Pacific & Atlantic".
- **F** Militia: major general of California militia, 1860–61, commissioned by the governor. **S** latinlibrary; Military Museum. **V** agrees with H2's historical premise.
- **F** Lincoln to Cameron, 17 Aug 1861: appointed "on Gen. Scott's recommendation". Halleck was commissioned MG of the regular army on 19 Aug 1861 **while still in California**. He was in Washington by about 7 November (John Hay), was ordered to Missouri on 9/11 November and arrived in St. Louis on 19 November. Exact sailing date and ship: **not found**. **S** Abraham Lincoln's Classroom, "Abraham Lincoln and Henry W. Halleck" (citing Marszalek); ACW High Command chronology, 9 Nov 1861; jfepperson.org/Hitchcock.htm (McClellan's order of 11 Nov). **V** **disagrees.** Historically he left after August and arrived about November. **Δ** H3 and the §9 text: label the May 1861 sailing a PROPOSED divergence, not history. "By August a major general" still works (historically conferred in absentia).
- **F** General in chief: Lincoln's order is dated 11 July 1862; Halleck assumed command on 23 July. **S** Military Museum; Wikipedia (IPFS mirror) "Henry Halleck". **V** partly. **Δ** "from 11 July (appointed; took office 23 July)".
- **F** *Elements of Military Art and Science*, 1846, from his Lowell Institute lectures. **S** latinlibrary. **V** agrees.

## Research tasks (draft numbering)

**1 · Shiloh order of battle.**
- **F** The 11th and 13th Iowa were in Hare's 1st Brigade of McClernand's 1st Division. Their losses were 194 and 162. Their strengths were **not found**.
- **F** The 15th Iowa (760 engaged) and 16th Iowa (785 present) were ordered to Prentiss's 6th Division but sent to McClernand's right on 6 April. Losses were 206 and 132.
- **F** Lew Wallace: about 5,800 (Wikipedia). Nelson's strength: **not found**.
- **S** OR I, 10, pt. 1 casualty table (civilwar.com OR page 100); SUVCW, "Controversies over the 15th and 16th Iowa monuments" (suvcw.org PDF); NPS Shiloh plaque I; historyofwar.org "Armies at Shiloh".
- **V** agrees on divisions. ~3,000 for four regiments is plausible.
- **Δ** none. Wallace "about 7,000" in Part II may be high; Wikipedia gives ~5,800 effective.

**2 · Buell's arrivals.**
- **F** Nelson reached Savannah on 5 April. On 6 April he left at 1:30 p.m. and reached the river at 5 p.m. Ammen's brigade was across by 7:30 p.m. and the division by 9 p.m. Crittenden was across by 1 a.m. on 7 April, with McCook overnight. Wood came too late to do much. Thomas's division was not engaged. About 15,000 of Buell's men were in line by dawn on the 7th, with 17,918 engaged in all.
- **S** jfepperson.org/shiloh.htm; NPS Civil War Series, *Shiloh*, secs. 3 and 12 (npshistory.com/publications/civil_war_series/22/); Wikipedia "Battle of Shiloh" (IPFS mirror).
- **V** agrees.
- **Δ** S7: historical fresh men on 7 April were about 21,000–25,000 (Buell 15,000–18,000 plus Wallace ~5,800). "About 25,000" is the top of the range.

**3 · March delay, 3–4 April.**
- **F** Hardee's corps did not start until 3 p.m. on 3 April, slowed by bad roads, poor guides and green troops. Heavy rain on the night of 4 April left Hardee and Bragg about twelve hours behind, and Breckinridge started only on the afternoon of the 4th. On 5 April a jam of Polk's wagons at Michie's held Bragg until about 4 p.m. Sources differ on the planned day: Wikipedia has an attack for 4 April delayed 48 hours; the NPS summary has dawn on 5 April.
- **S** NPS *Shiloh*, sec. 3; Wikipedia.
- **V** partly.
- **Δ** S1/Part II: the night rain of 4 April is weather, not a "staff and road contingency". The draft must also have the columns march through it (or the rain fall later). The blocking that is documented is Polk's wagons in front of Bragg on the 5th.

**4 · McArthur; 5th Division colonels.**
- **F** McArthur was appointed brigadier on 22 March 1862, ranking from 21 March; Lauman and W. H. L. Wallace had the same date. McArthur commanded the 1st Brigade of C. F. Smith's division at Donelson. At Shiloh he had the 2nd ("Highland") Brigade of W. H. L. Wallace's division and was wounded. At Corinth McKean superseded him in division command, so he led a brigade there. He commanded the 6th Division of the XVII Corps at Vicksburg and the 1st Division of the XVI Corps at Nashville (Compton's Hill).
- **F** Sherman's brigades: McDowell (40th Illinois, 6th Iowa, 46th Ohio), Stuart (55th Illinois, 54th Ohio, 71st Ohio), Hildebrand (53rd, 57th, 77th Ohio), Buckland (48th, 70th, 72nd Ohio). Lauman had the 3rd Brigade of Hurlbut's division. The colonels' seniority: **not found**.
- **S** ACW High Command chronology, 21 Mar 1862; Wikipedia "John McArthur (general)"; historyofwar.org; ABT "Battle of Nashville".
- **V** agrees, except Corinth.
- **Δ** Part II: "Vicksburg and Nashville", not Corinth, for division-level record.

**5 · June 1862.**
- **F** Grant calls his post under Halleck "a nominal command and yet no command". He had leave to go. Sherman came, "urged me so strongly" to stay, and Grant stayed. Sherman's memoirs date Grant's note that he would stay to 6 June. McPherson was Grant's chief engineer from February 1862; at Shiloh he warned that the forward camps could not be defended and had a horse killed under him. McPherson is not mentioned in the June episode.
- **S** Grant, *Memoirs*, ch. 27 (historyofwar.org); Sherman, *Memoirs*, excerpt (civilwarnotebook.blogspot.com, Oct 2013); NPS "James B. McPherson"; ABT McPherson biography.
- **V** agrees.
- **Δ** none (C2 is an honest reassignment).

**6 · Steele, Blair, Logan.**
- **F** Steele graduated from West Point in 1843, 30th of 39; Grant was 21st. At Chickasaw Bayou Steele held Sherman's left with a division. He was given the Arkansas command in July 1863 and took Little Rock on 10 September 1863.
- **F** The XV Corps was formed on 18 December 1862 under Sherman, who rose to the Department/Army of the Tennessee about 24–27 October 1863. Blair commanded the corps at Chattanooga, and Logan took it over on 11 December 1863. Blair took the XVII Corps only on **4 May 1864**.
- **S** civilwarintheeast.com, "Class of 1843"; ABT "December Disaster"; Fox, *Regimental Losses*, XV Corps (wargame.ch mirror); Encyclopedia of Arkansas, "Frederick Steele"; ACW High Command, 11 Dec 1863; Wikipedia "XVII Corps".
- **V** partly.
- **Δ** C4: historically Blair took the XV Corps in late October 1863, not August. C6: Blair to the XVII "in December" is not where history put him (May 1864). Mark both as PROPOSED.

**7 · Halleck.** See L4.

**8 · Thomas.** See L1 and L3.

**9 · Blue Mountain; Polk.** See L2.

**10 · A. J. Smith, Tupelo, Brice's.**
- **F** About 10,000 of Sherman's Army of the Tennessee (elements of the XVI and XVII Corps) left Vicksburg on 10 March 1864 on a 30-day loan to Banks. After Red River, Smith marched from La Grange on 5 July to protect Sherman's supply line and fought Tupelo on 14–15 July. At Brice's Crossroads on 10 June 1864, Sturgis's ~8,000 were routed by Forrest's ~3,000.
- **S** civilwarmonths.com, "The Red River Campaign Begins" (blog; weak source); ABT, A. J. Smith biography; Wikipedia "Smith's Expedition to Tupelo"; historyofwar.org "Brice's Crossroads".
- **V** agrees.
- **Δ** R4 note: historically Sturgis and Smith already kept Forrest off Sherman's railroad in June–July, so the "plus" is mainly earlier availability.

**11 · *Vanderbilt*; Galena.**
- **F** Stanton accepted *Vanderbilt* as Cornelius Vanderbilt's gift on 20 March 1862, armed "especially for the destruction of the Merrimack". She reached the Roads about 23 March. *Arago* and *Illinois* were also fitted as rams. Hulks ready to sink: **not found**.
- **F** Drewry's Bluff, 15 May 1862: Rodgers commanded, with Galena, Monitor, Naugatuck, Aroostook and Port Royal. Galena was hit about 45 times; her losses were 12 killed and 15 wounded (DANFS) or 14 dead and 10 wounded (Wikipedia). Her armour was stripped in 1863, and she fought at Mobile Bay in 1864.
- **S** Chuck Veit, "Mutiny aboard the Arago and the Illinois" (navyandmarine.org/ondeck/1862mutiny.htm); NHHC DANFS "Galena I"; Wikipedia "Battle of Drewry's Bluff".
- **V** partly (rams yes, hulks not found).
- **Δ** E3: keep the hulks OPEN or drop them. E4: the "other ships" are Naugatuck, Aroostook and Port Royal.

**12 · XIX Corps.**
- **F** The 1st Division of the XIX Corps had just reached Fort Monroe from New Orleans. It was kept aboard ship and sent to Washington, landing partly on the night of 11–12 July. In Sheridan's Army of the Shenandoah (August 1864) the XIX Corps was Emory's, with the divisions of Dwight and Grover, from the Department of the Gulf.
- **S** ACW High Command chronology, 11 Jul 1864; civilwarintheeast.com, Army of the Shenandoah XIX Corps.
- **V** agrees with the draft's flags.
- **Δ** none.

## Other to-verify items

- **S3 (15th and 16th Iowa on the morning of the battle).** The 15th landed about daylight on 6 April. The 16th's arrival is disputed (4 or 5 April). Both drew ammunition on the morning of the 6th. **S** SUVCW PDF. **V** partly. **Δ** "the 16th by the 5th, the 15th on the morning of the battle".
- **S5.** Prentiss surrendered about 5:30 p.m. with about 2,300 men. Johnston died about 2:45 p.m. (Epperson) against 2:30 (Boatner). **V** agrees.
- **S6.** Lew Wallace arrived about 6:30–7:15 p.m. **V** agrees.
- **S2 (Beauregard's wish to withdraw; Johnston's "a million").** Not checked.
- **Part II (W. H. L. Wallace taking C. F. Smith's division).** The order of battle shows Wallace in command. Smith's injury was not checked. **V** partly.
- **Part II (Sherman "held until mid-morning").** His division took the first assault and fell back behind Shiloh Church; the timing was not confirmed. **V** partly.
- **C1.** Anderson handed over to Sherman on 8 October 1861 on Scott's order. Buell's takeover in mid-November is from search listings only. **S** Rebellion Record blog, 8 Oct 1861. **V** agrees.
- **C3.** Arkansas Post was fought 9–11 January 1863 by McClernand's Army of the Mississippi (~32,000), which was Sherman's Chickasaw force. **S** Encyclopedia of Arkansas. **V** agrees.
- **C5 (Jackson, July 1863).** Not checked.
- **C6.** The Military Division of the Mississippi was created on 16 October 1863, and Grant assumed command on the 18th. McPherson took the Army of the Tennessee on 26 March 1864. At Tunnel Hill Sherman attacked with the XV Corps under Blair (Morgan L. Smith, John E. Smith, Ewing) plus Jeff C. Davis's division, and gained little. Thomas's four divisions carried the centre. Knoxville: Granger (IV Corps, ~20,000) failed to start, so Grant sent Sherman to take command. Longstreet left on 4 December and Sherman arrived on the 6th. **S** ACW High Command, 16 Oct 1863; ABT "Chattanooga" (battlefields.org/node/5771); NPS Handbook 25 (npshistory.com/handbooks/historical/25/hh25k.htm); Grant, *Memoirs*, ch. 45. **V** **disagrees on Knoxville.** **Δ** C6: "Granger relieves Knoxville" is wrong as history. - **C7 (Halleck backs Thomas).** Not found.
- **A3.** Sherman left the railroad for Dallas–New Hope Church (25–28 May). **S** ABT "Atlanta Campaign". **V** agrees.
- **A8.** On 16 July Davis asked for Johnston's "plan of operations". Johnston answered that he was outnumbered two to one, would watch for a chance to fight, and would hold Atlanta "for a day or two" with militia. Bragg visited on 13–15 July and Hood wrote a critical memorandum. Cooper's telegram of 17 July relieved Johnston for having "failed to arrest the advance of the enemy" and for expressing no confidence he could repel it. Hood took command on 18 July. **S** NPS *Campaign for Atlanta*, sec. 6 (npshistory.com/publications/civil_war_series/7/sec6.htm); Ohio Civil War Central, Cooper's message of 17 July 1864. **V** agrees. **Δ** A8's written plan must answer exactly this 16 July request.
- **Part V (Hood's July losses).** ABT estimates Confederate losses of about 2,500 at Peachtree Creek, 5,500 at Atlanta and 3,000 at Ezra Church, about 11,000 in all, against Union losses of about 6,100. **S** ABT battle fact pages. **V** partly. **Δ** H&J's 15,000 must include other actions; cite it as H&J's.
- **A9 (railroads cut).** Sherman sent most of his infantry toward Jonesboro from 25 August and cut the last line on 31 August. Atlanta was evacuated on 1 September. **S** ABT. **V** agrees.
- **E1–E2.** Virginia drew 22 ft and made about 5 knots (NHHC), against the draft's 4 knots (H&J). Minnesota was refloated on 10 March. Norfolk was abandoned on 9 May and Virginia destroyed on 11 May. **S** NHHC "Hampton Roads"; Wikipedia "Battle of Hampton Roads". **V** agrees (the speed difference is minor).
- **O2 (Hooker).** He resigned on 21 February 1853 and farmed near Sonoma until about 1858, then superintended military roads in Oregon. He was a **colonel of California militia 1859–61**. He watched Bull Run as a civilian and was made brigadier from 17 May 1861. **S** latinlibrary "Hooker"; Military Museum "Hooker". **V** partly. **Δ** O2's "on Halleck's terms" needs Hooker to refuse militia rank too, which also bears on Self-review question 5.
- **O3.** A. S. Johnston arrived in San Francisco on 14 January 1861, resigned on 10 April and handed over to Sumner on 25 April (Military Museum, "Civil War Defenses of SF"). Hancock was quartermaster of the Southern District from May 1859 to August 1861 (UCLA newsroom story). McPherson was building Alcatraz's fortifications when war broke out (NPS); 1857 as his start year was **not found**. **V** agrees.
