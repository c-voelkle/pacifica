## Load-bearing

### L1 · Snake Creek Gap (task 8; A2)

- **F** Thomas proposed the move.
  - His cavalry found the unguarded gap in February 1864, and he proposed taking the Army of the Cumberland through it to Resaca.
  - Sherman kept the route but gave it to McPherson.
  - Cox says Thomas "very earnestly" urged that the other two armies hold Dalton while his went through, and that Sherman "preferred his own view".
  - The text and date of a letter from Thomas were not found.

  **S** NPS Civil War Series, *The Campaign for Atlanta*, sec. 2 (npshistory.com/publications/civil_war_series/7/sec2.htm); J. D. Cox, *Atlanta* (1882), quoted at civilwartalk.com/goto/post?id=914783. **V** agrees. **Δ** cite Cox and NPS in place of "Hist.; to verify".
- **F** Dates.
  - McPherson went through the gap on 8 May and came out mid-morning on the 9th.
  - He skirmished before Resaca and fell back to the gap's mouth without cutting the railroad.
  - Johnston knew of him by about 10 a.m. on the 9th. That night he sent three of Hood's divisions toward Resaca by rail, and most of them came back.
  - Johnston left Dalton on the night of 12 May. Resaca was fought on 14–15 May (skirmishing from the 13th). He crossed the Oostanaula on the night of the 15th.

  **S** NPS sec. 2; Stephen Davis, ABT "Johnston is Turned" (battlefields.org/learn/articles/johnston-turned); Wikipedia "Battle of Resaca". **V** agrees.
- **F** The gap and its road.
  - The gorge is about 4 miles long.
  - Resaca lies 5–8 miles beyond its mouth on "a characteristic Georgia wagon-track" (Cox).
  - McPherson's 23,000–25,000 men took from 8 May to the morning of the 9th to pass. Three more corps went through on 13–14 May.
  - No figure was found for passing 60,000 men.

  **S** Wikipedia "Battle of Resaca"; Cox; Georgia marker 064-8 (hmdb.org/m.asp?m=13913). **V** partly. **Δ** "two days on one road" is MODEL, not Hist.
- **F** Judgements on the counterfactual.
  - Castel puts Resaca's defenders on 9 May at about 4,000. A forum critic counts more than 6,000 once Reynolds's and Vaughan's brigades arrived.
  - Cox thought Johnston could have moved his main body to Resaca on the night of the 9th.
  - No historian's judgement was found on whether the whole Army of the Cumberland would have trapped Johnston. The only discussion found was forum opinion.

  **S** Castel, *Decision in the West*, p. 138 (as quoted at civilwartalk.com/goto/post?id=912971). Cleaves, Einolf, McMurry: **not found**. **V** not found.
- **F — the key interaction with A1 and A5.** Polk's Army of Mississippi helped hold Resaca.
  - Cantey's division (5,300) came from Mobile on 7 May.
  - Loring's division (5,145) reached Rome on 10 May and Resaca on 10–12 May, with Polk on the 11th.

  **S** Wikipedia "Battle of Resaca" (reinforcement table); NPS sec. 2. **V** **the draft contradicts itself.** If Polk "never" joins Johnston (A1), Resaca has no Loring and probably no Cantey, and Thomas's move is more dangerous than A2 admits. **Δ** Either bring Polk's divisions to Resaca in May and send them back to Alabama in June, or show how Johnston escapes without them (by railing Hood's corps, as on 9–10 May).

### L2 · Polk and the Blue Mountain line (task 9; A1, A5, A6)

- **F** Polk's reinforcements in May 1864 came to about 19,650 men:

  | Unit | Men | Arrived |
  |---|---|---|
  | Cantey's division | 5,300 | 7 May |
  | Loring's division | 5,145 | 10–12 May |
  | French's detachment | 550 | 12 May |
  | Jackson's cavalry | 4,477 | 17 May |
  | French's division | 4,174 | 19 May |

  **S** Wikipedia "Battle of Resaca". **V** agrees with the draft's ~20,000.
- **F** Johnston had 53,800 present at Dalton and about 68,800 with reinforcements (ABT). The Union had about 99,000 effectives. **S** ABT "The Atlanta Campaign" (battlefields.org/learn/articles/atlanta-campaign). **V** partly. **Δ** A1's "60,000 … excluding Polk" is too high; without Polk the figure is about 54,000. Lower it or attribute it to H&J, and recompute A7's 45,000.
- **F** The Alabama and Tennessee Rivers Railroad.
  - Trains ran from Selma to Blue Mountain from spring 1862.
  - The link to Rome was never finished. In May 1864 about 30 miles were still ungraded, and no track or iron was in hand.
  - The road ran "regularly" until late March 1865.

  **S** csa-railroads.com, "The Construction of the Blue Mountain Railroad"; superintendent's report of 14 April 1866 (csa-railroads.com/Essays/Orignial%20Docs/AR/AR,_A_and_TR_4-1-66_S.htm). **V** agrees. **Δ** A6 should say that Blue Mountain is a dead-end railhead with no rail to Rome. That supports "beyond his wagon radius", but an army there can reach Thomas's rear only by a long march (my inference).
- **F** Rousseau's raid (10–22 July 1864, about 2,700 men) burned the A&TR depot and two cars at Talladega on the 15th. The line stopped for only a few days. The heavy damage was to the Montgomery and West Point line. **S** Encyclopedia of Alabama, "Rousseau's Raid"; 1866 A&TR report. **V** the draft is silent. **Δ** none (Rousseau's was Sherman's raid, so leaving it out fits R5).
- **F** Polk was killed by Union artillery on Pine Mountain on 14 June 1864. **S** ABT, "The Death of Bishop Polk". **V** agrees.
- **F** H&J's flank-position argument: **not found** online.

### L3 · Thomas: seniority, Nashville and raids (task 8; C7, A9)

- **F** Dates of rank:

  | General | Major general of volunteers | Brigadier, Regular Army |
  |---|---|---|
  | Thomas | 25 Apr 1862 | 27 Oct 1863 |
  | Hooker | 5 May 1862 | 20 Sep 1862 |
  | McPherson | 8 Oct 1862 | 1 Aug 1863 |
  | Schofield | 29 Nov 1862 (confirmed May 1863) | — |
  | Howard | "spring 1863" (exact date not found) | — |

  **S** HistoryCentral (Thomas); American Civil War High Command chronology, 27 Oct and 1 Aug 1863; ABT biographies; thelatinlibrary.com civilwarnotes (Hooker). **V** **partly.** Thomas is the senior major general of volunteers, but only by ten days over Hooker. In Regular Army rank both Hooker and McPherson outrank him. **Δ** C7 and Part III should say "senior major general of volunteers". Hooker is the awkward case.
- **F** Nashville, December 1864.
  - Grant pressed Thomas by telegraph on the 2nd, 5th, 6th, 8th and 11th.
  - On 9 December he asked for an order putting Schofield in command, then suspended it when he heard of the ice storm.
  - On 13 December he sent Logan to take over if Thomas had still not attacked.
  - Grant himself got only as far as Washington. Thomas attacked on 15 December, and Logan stopped at Louisville.
  - Grant's memoirs call the delay "unaccountable" and Thomas "deliberate and so slow, though effective in defence".

  **S** ABT, "Battle of Nashville: Enemies Front and Rear"; Grant, *Personal Memoirs*, ch. 60 (historyofwar.org/sources/acw/grant/chapter60b.html). **V** agrees. **Δ** open decision 13 can use both steps: the suspended Schofield order and the dispatch of Logan.
- **F** Thomas and raids. Thomas himself sent Wilson's cavalry corps (about 13,500 men) on the Selma raid of March–April 1865. **S** Wikipedia "Wilson's Raid"; Encyclopedia of Alabama, "Battle of Selma". **V** partly. **Δ** R5 and Part III should say "will not cut loose with infantry". A cavalry raid ordered by Thomas is in character.

### L4 · Halleck (task 7; H1–H3, the §9 text)

- **F** Halleck came to California with the Army round Cape Horn on the *Lexington*, was secretary of state in the military government and resigned from the Army in August 1854. **S** thelatinlibrary.com civilwarnotes, "Halleck"; California State Military Museum (militarymuseum.org/Halleck.html). **V** the draft departs from this knowingly. **Δ** H1's "about 1854" can rest on the real resignation date.
- **F** His firm, Halleck, Peachy & Billings (from 1849), acted for claimants to Mexican land grants and handled more than half of the claims after the Land Act of 1851. He was a director of the New Almaden quicksilver company. The Almaden lawsuit itself was **not found**. **S** Wikipedia "Halleck, Peachy & Billings"; Military Museum. **V** agrees.
- **F** He was president of the **Atlantic and Pacific** Railroad (San Francisco–San José, 1855). **S** latinlibrary; Military Museum. **Δ** correct the name, which the draft gives as "Pacific & Atlantic".
- **F** He was a major general of the California militia in 1860–61. **S** latinlibrary. **V** agrees with H2's premise.
- **F** Departure from California.
  - Lincoln appointed him "on Gen. Scott's recommendation" (letter of 17 August 1861).
  - He was commissioned major general of the Regular Army on 19 August 1861, **while still in California**.
  - He was in Washington by about 7 November (John Hay), and was ordered to Missouri on 9–11 November.
  - The exact sailing date and ship were **not found**.

  **S** Abraham Lincoln's Classroom, "Abraham Lincoln and Henry W. Halleck" (citing Marszalek); jfepperson.org/Hitchcock.htm. **V** **disagrees.** Historically he sailed after August and arrived in November. **Δ** H3 and the §9 text should label the May 1861 sailing as a PROPOSED divergence.
- **F** General in chief: Lincoln's order is dated 11 July 1862, and Halleck took office on 23 July. **S** Military Museum. **V** partly. **Δ** add "took office 23 July".
- **F** *Elements of Military Art and Science* was published in 1846, from his Lowell lectures. **S** latinlibrary. **V** agrees.

## Research tasks (draft numbering)

**1 · Shiloh order of battle.**
- **F** The 11th and 13th Iowa were in Hare's brigade of McClernand's division; they lost 194 and 162 men. Their strengths were not found.
- **F** The 15th Iowa (760) and 16th Iowa (785) were assigned to Prentiss but sent to McClernand's right; they lost 206 and 132.
- **F** Lew Wallace's division numbered about 5,800 (Wikipedia).
- **S** OR I, 10, pt. 1, casualty return (civilwar.com, OR p. 100); SUVCW, "Controversies over the 15th and 16th Iowa Monuments"; historyofwar.org, "Armies at Shiloh".
- **V** agrees; about 3,000 men for the four regiments is plausible.
- **Δ** Part II's "about 7,000" for Wallace may be high.

**2 · Buell's arrivals.**
- **F** Nelson's division:
  - It reached Savannah on 5 April.
  - On the 6th it marched at 1:30 p.m. and reached the river at 5 p.m.
  - Ammen's brigade was across by 7:30 p.m., the whole division by 9 p.m.
- **F** Crittenden was across by 1 a.m. on the 7th, and McCook came over during the night.
- **F** Wood arrived too late to do much. Thomas's division did not fight.
- **F** About 15,000 of Buell's men were in line at dawn; 17,918 were engaged in all.
- **S** jfepperson.org/shiloh.htm; NPS Civil War Series, *Shiloh*, secs. 3 and 12.
- **V** agrees.
- **Δ** S7: the historical fresh troops on 7 April were about 21,000–25,000, so 25,000 is the top of the range.

**3 · The march delay, 3–4 April.**
- **F** Hardee's corps did not start until 3 p.m. on 3 April, slowed by bad roads, poor guides and green troops.
- **F** Heavy rain on the night of 4 April left the army about 12 hours behind.
- **F** On 5 April a jam of Polk's wagons at Michie's held Bragg until about 4 p.m.
- **S** NPS *Shiloh*, sec. 3.
- **V** partly.
- **Δ** S1: the rain is weather, not a "staff and road" contingency, so the draft must either march through it or move it. The corps blocking that is documented happened on the 5th, not the 4th.

**4 · McArthur and the 5th Division.**
- **F** McArthur was appointed brigadier on 22 March 1862, ranking from 21 March; Lauman and W. H. L. Wallace hold the same date.
- **F** Donelson: he commanded the 1st Brigade of C. F. Smith's division.
- **F** Shiloh: he led the 2nd Brigade of W. H. L. Wallace's division and was wounded.
- **F** At Corinth he was superseded in division command. He led divisions at Vicksburg (6th Division, XVII Corps) and Nashville (1st Division, XVI Corps).
- **F** Sherman's brigade commanders were McDowell, Stuart, Hildebrand and Buckland, as the draft has them. The colonels' seniority was not found.
- **S** ACW High Command, 21 March 1862; Wikipedia "John McArthur (general)"; historyofwar.org.
- **V** agrees, except at Corinth.
- **Δ** Part II: cite "Vicksburg and Nashville" for his division record, not Corinth.

**5 · June 1862.**
- **F** Grant called his post under Halleck "a nominal command and yet no command".
- **F** He had leave to go. Sherman "urged me so strongly" to stay that he stayed, and Sherman dates Grant's reply to 6 June.
- **F** McPherson was Grant's chief engineer from February 1862. At Shiloh he warned that the camps could not be defended.
- **S** Grant, *Memoirs*, ch. 27; Sherman, *Memoirs*, ch. 11 (excerpt at civilwarnotebook.blogspot.com); NPS, "James B. McPherson".
- **V** agrees.
- **Δ** none.

**6 · Steele, Blair and Logan.**
- **F** Steele graduated from West Point in 1843, 30th of 39; Grant was 21st.
- **F** At Chickasaw Bayou he led a division on Sherman's left.
- **F** He went to Arkansas in July 1863 and took Little Rock on 10 September 1863.
- **F** XV Corps commanders:
  - Sherman led it from 18 December 1862 until late October 1863.
  - Blair led it at Chattanooga.
  - Logan took it over on 11 December 1863.
- **F** Blair took the XVII Corps on **4 May 1864**.
- **S** civilwarintheeast.com, "Class of 1843"; ABT, "December Disaster"; Fox, *Regimental Losses*, XV Corps; Encyclopedia of Arkansas, "Frederick Steele"; ACW High Command, 11 December 1863; Wikipedia "XVII Corps".
- **V** partly.
- **Δ** C4: historically Blair took the XV Corps in late October, not August. C6: Blair's move to the XVII Corps "in December" is not historical. Mark both as PROPOSED.

**7–9.** See L4, L1 and L3, and L2.

**10 · A. J. Smith, Tupelo and Brice's Crossroads.**
- **F** Red River: about 10,000 men of the XVI and XVII Corps left Vicksburg on 10 March 1864 on a 30-day loan to Banks.
- **F** Tupelo: Smith marched on 5 July to cover Sherman's line and fought Forrest there on 14–15 July.
- **F** Brice's Crossroads: on 10 June 1864, Sturgis's 8,000 were routed by Forrest's 3,000.
- **S** civilwarmonths.com (blog; a weak source); ABT, "Andrew J. Smith"; Wikipedia "Smith's Expedition to Tupelo"; historyofwar.org.
- **V** agrees.
- **Δ** R4: historically Forrest was already kept off Sherman's railroad, so the gain is mainly that Smith is available earlier.

**11 · The *Vanderbilt* and Galena.**
- **F** Stanton accepted the *Vanderbilt* as a gift on 20 March 1862, armed "especially for the destruction of the Merrimack". She was in the Roads by about 23 March, and the *Arago* and *Illinois* were also fitted as rams.
- **F** Hulks ready to be sunk in the channels: **not found**.
- **F** Drewry's Bluff: Rodgers went up with Galena, Monitor, Naugatuck, Aroostook and Port Royal. Galena was hit about 45 times, with 12 killed and 15 wounded (DANFS) or 14 dead and 10 wounded (Wikipedia).
- **S** Chuck Veit, "Mutiny aboard the Arago and the Illinois" (navyandmarine.org/ondeck/1862mutiny.htm); NHHC DANFS, "Galena I"; Wikipedia "Battle of Drewry's Bluff".
- **V** partly.
- **Δ** E3: leave the hulks OPEN or drop them. E4: the wooden gunboats are Aroostook and Port Royal, and Naugatuck was an ironclad.

**12 · The XIX Corps.**
- **F** The 1st Division reached Fort Monroe from New Orleans, was kept aboard and was diverted to Washington, where it landed on 11–12 July 1864.
- **F** In Sheridan's army the XIX Corps under Emory had the divisions of Dwight and Grover, from the Department of the Gulf.
- **S** ACW High Command, 11 July 1864; civilwarintheeast.com, "Army of the Shenandoah".
- **V** agrees with the draft's flags.
- **Δ** none.

**13 · California officers.** See O2 and O3 below.

## Other to-verify items

- **S3.** The 15th Iowa landed about daylight on 6 April. The 16th's arrival is disputed (4 or 5 April). Both drew ammunition that morning. **S** SUVCW. **V** partly.
- **S5.** Prentiss surrendered at about 5:30 p.m. with about 2,300 men. A. S. Johnston died at about 2:45 p.m. (Epperson), against Boatner's 2:30. **V** agrees.
- **S6.** Lew Wallace arrived between 6:30 and 7:15 p.m. **V** agrees.
- **Part II, Sherman "held until mid-morning".** His division took the first assault and fell back behind Shiloh Church; the time was not confirmed. **V** partly.
- **C1.** Anderson handed Kentucky to Sherman on 8 October 1861. Buell took over in mid-November (search results only). **S** Rebellion Record blog, 8 October 1861. **V** agrees.
- **C3.** McClernand's Army of the Mississippi (about 32,000, Sherman's Chickasaw force) took Arkansas Post on 9–11 January 1863. **S** Encyclopedia of Arkansas. **V** agrees.
- **C6.** Grant's Military Division of the Mississippi was created on 16 October 1863, and he took command on the 18th. McPherson's historical date for the Army of the Tennessee is 26 March 1864. At Tunnel Hill Sherman attacked with the XV Corps under Blair plus Jeff C. Davis's division and gained little, while Thomas's four divisions carried the centre. **Knoxville:** Granger's IV Corps (about 20,000) failed to start, so Grant sent Sherman, who arrived on 6 December after Longstreet had left on the 4th. **S** ABT, "Chattanooga" (battlefields.org/node/5771); NPS Handbook 25; Grant, *Memoirs*, ch. 45. **V** **disagrees on Knoxville.** **Δ** "Granger relieves Knoxville" is wrong as history. Give the relief to McPherson, or mark it as PROPOSED.
- **C2, C5, C7, S2.** Not checked or not found (Halleck backing Thomas; Jackson in July 1863; Beauregard's wish to withdraw).
- **A3.** Sherman left the railroad for Dallas and New Hope Church (25–28 May). **S** ABT. **V** agrees.
- **A8.** On 16 July Davis asked for Johnston's "plan of operations".
  - Johnston replied that he was outnumbered two to one and would watch for a chance to fight.
  - He also said he would hold Atlanta "for a day or two" with militia.
  - Bragg's visit (13–15 July) and a memorandum from Hood came first.
  - Cooper's telegram of 17 July relieved him for having "failed to arrest the advance of the enemy".

  **S** NPS *Campaign for Atlanta*, sec. 6; Ohio Civil War Central, Cooper's message of 17 July 1864. **V** agrees. **Δ** A8's written plan is the answer to this request of 16 July.
- **Part V, Hood's July losses.** ABT estimates Confederate losses at about 11,000 (2,500 + 5,500 + 3,000) and Union losses at about 6,100. **V** partly. **Δ** attribute "15,000" to H&J.
- **A9.** The infantry moved on Jonesboro from 25 August and cut the last line on the 31st; Atlanta was evacuated on 1 September. **S** ABT. **V** agrees.
- **E1–E2.** Virginia drew 22 feet and made about 5 knots according to NHHC (H&J: 4). Minnesota was refloated on 10 March. Norfolk fell on 9 May, and Virginia was destroyed on 11 May. **S** NHHC, "Hampton Roads". **V** agrees.
- **O2, Hooker.** He resigned on 21 February 1853 and farmed in Sonoma until about 1858, then built Oregon military roads. He was a **colonel of California militia in 1859–61**, watched Bull Run as a civilian, and became a brigadier ranking from 17 May 1861. **S** latinlibrary, "Hooker"; Military Museum, "Hooker". **V** partly. **Δ** "on Halleck's terms" means Hooker too refuses militia rank. That bears on Self-review question 5.
- **O3.** A. S. Johnston arrived on 14 January 1861, resigned on 10 April and handed over to Sumner on 25 April (Military Museum, "Civil War Defenses of SF"). Hancock was quartermaster at Los Angeles from May 1859 to August 1861 (UCLA story). McPherson was at work on Alcatraz in 1861 (NPS), but his 1857 start was not found. **V** agrees.
