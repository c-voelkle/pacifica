# Fresh-context review — CR-063 (the Gulf without New Orleans)

Inputs: the six supplied files plus web checks (Grant, *Memoirs* ch. 36; OR I/24/1 p. 755; NPS Big Black; USS *Hartford*). CTs judged as proposals. The CR is careful and well sourced; its two hinges, the Vicksburg exit and the February attack, need work before adoption.

## Blockers

**1. Farragut's death contradicts APPROVED canon, and no tension declares it (Part VI step 7, open decision 12).** CR-015 F4 (APPROVED) says Logan's mines killed him. The bible says the same: his mine field "kills Farragut". In the draft, *Hartford* crosses the field without a strike and a shell from the *Tennessee* kills him. That also weakens F4's other approved line, that the order to ignore the mines is "the order that sank his fleet". In the draft the lead ironclad sinks before the order, and the order costs only one burnt sloop. **Fix:** the strongest version is for *Hartford* to strike one of the new row's charges as she leads the column across under his order. Farragut is killed by the explosion or the falling rigging, and the *Tennessee* then takes the leaderless ships one at a time. Alternatively add a CT amending F4.

**2. The 17 May exit is not physically credible as written (V1, V3, Part III steps 3–5, storyline).**
- (a) The beaten divisions are not "already in marching order". At the Big Black that morning Bowen's division and Vaughn's brigade (of Smith's division) were routed: 1,751 men and 18 guns were taken, many men threw away their rifles, and the survivors reached the works "badly shaken" in the afternoon (NPS). Stevenson's division had lost heavily the day before at Champion Hill. They are the worst-organised men to march at dusk; Forney's and Smith's are fresh, with trains in the city.
- (b) "Nothing is yet north of the city" is false for the council's afternoon. Sherman was at Bridgeport by about noon on 17 May, with Blair's division and the pontoon train, and he captured the Confederate picket there (Grant ch. 36; OR p. 755). The council would know a corps was bridging the Big Black within a few miles of the Benton road.
- (c) The timing does not close. A column of about 16,000 men plus trains is roughly 10–12 miles long, and its tail should pass the forks some hours after midnight. Yet step 4 says the column must clear the roads before dawn, while step 5 has its trains still at the forks at 9:30 a.m.
- **Fix:** swap the roles. Forney's and Smith's divisions, rested and with their trains (less Vaughn), march at dusk. Stevenson's and Bowen's men, who historically fought well behind parapets, man the works; Bowen's division was the siege reserve. Have the council weigh Sherman at Bridgeport and gamble that his crossing will not be complete before first light. Then explain the tail's delay with a concrete cause, such as exhausted artillery horses or a broken bridge on the Benton road, and catch it at first light, not at 9:30. Update V3, the steps, the storyline and open decision 8.

**3. The strengths do not add up, and they disagree with the CR-064 interface (V3, step 5, step 9, Repair pass).**
- (a) Step 5 sends the rear-guard brigade back into the lines. Then about 14,000 cannot both reach Johnston and leave about 14,000 inside: 16,000 − 2,000 lost − a returning brigade of about 1,500 gives about 12,500 out and about 15,500 in.
- (b) The surrender of about 12,000 from 14,000 implies about 2,000 lost in the assaults and the siege. Say so (MODEL).
- (c) The Repair-pass record still says 15,000 march out and 8,000 stay, "the number CR-064 takes". V3 says CR-064 takes 14,000 escapees. One of them is wrong, and CR-064 depends on it.
- **Fix:** recompute after Blocker 2 (research task 12), state one pair of figures, align CR-064, strike the Repair-pass line.

**4. The vice admiral's promotion has no cause in this world (M4; storyline "is made the Navy's first vice admiral").** The Act of 21 December 1864 created the grade to reward Mobile Bay. In this world Farragut has been repulsed at New Orleans and has won nothing, and a reader who knows the war will stop at this sentence. *Hartford* is a related problem. She historically reached New York on 13 December and was decommissioned for repairs about a week later. The draft has Farragut go north and then fight in *Hartford* in February. **Fix:** drop the promotion; a vice admiral's commission after his death can serve Grant's martyr-making (F4). Send Farragut north by mail steamer and leave *Hartford* refitting at Pensacola, or move his flag to another ship. Correct M4/research task 9: the act was approved 21 December.

**5. The storyline contradicts itself (§11, the Butler insert).** "There is no Union field army in the Gulf again until 1870" conflicts with Terry's 8,000 on Dauphin Island in February 1865, which is canon ("re-embarks the Dauphin Island troops"). **Fix:** "no Union army comes to the Gulf for two and a half years", or "none that stays".

## Should fix

1. **The draught argument is backwards (step 1, "What a reader … should see").** *New Ironsides* drew about 15 ft 8 in. *Hartford* drew about 17 ft and crossed the bar in August 1864. So draught is not the "first difficulty" and the causal sentence fails. **Fix:** rest it on handling (*New Ironsides* was long and unhandy) and the narrower gap.

2. **The sinking and the losses (steps 5 and 8).** A large broadside ironclad has far more reserve buoyancy than a low-freeboard monitor: *New Ironsides* survived the *David*'s spar torpedo in October 1863. One keg torpedo will not sink her "in a few minutes". Her crew was also about 400–450, so "most of her crew" alone exceeds the total of "about 400" losses. **Fix:** give Logan's row larger boiler-iron charges, which is the kind of cargo the corridor carries, or have her strike two of them. Let her settle over twenty minutes under the fort's guns. Raise the losses (MODEL, perhaps 700–900), or make the second group smaller ships with smaller crews and say so in Part V.

3. **Eight days between the landing and the attack (steps 2–4).** In August the gap was two days. A week of investment warns Mobile, gives Logan time to add to the field, and gives Farragut's boats nights to sweep it, as Union boats did before the August attack. **Fix:** give a cause (northers, tide, colliers), and a reason for waiting on the Hampton Roads conference; as written it is the scene's convenience.

4. **Authority over the new torpedo row (step 0).** "Logan's men have laid a second row" leaves out the Confederate authority. Canon and the bible insist that Confederate officers decide and execute. **Fix:** the district's engineer (von Sheliha historically; to check) orders it, Logan's men lay it.

5. **Can about 11,000 effectives hold the works to early July (V4, step 9)?** It is credible for the assaults of 19 and 22 May, given the narrow fronts and interior lines. Seven weeks is harder: deserters will tell Grant the garrison is halved, and Johnston has about 45,000 at Grant's back. **Fix:** add mechanisms. The escapees lost their trains, reserve artillery and ammunition on the 18th, so Johnston cannot attack until they are refitted. Johnston, who wanted the city given up, will not spend that army to rescue a garrison he advised against leaving. Grant's general assault is set for the mine of 25 June. Or move the surrender to late June, which is still "Summer 1863" (CT8).

6. **Davis's reaction is missing (V2).** Pemberton shuts himself in with half the army against Davis's "not to be shut up". One sentence on Davis's answer makes the rear garrison read as character with a price.

7. **The Peterhoff logic is inverted (T4, open decision 7).** *The Peterhoff* means the Navy *cannot* stop overland trade from Matamoros at sea. A landing at Brownsville is the only way to cut it, which is why Banks went. **Fix:** rest "no landing" on the lack of men in the autumn of Chickamauga and on the trade moving upriver to Laredo and Eagle Pass, which happened historically (Irby).

8. **"Unsounded bay" (S2 and the storyline).** The Coast Survey had charted Mobile Bay before the war. **Fix:** "a bay whose new defences and obstructions nobody has reconnoitred".

9. **"Davis's order to give up Mobile lapses" (storyline).** S3 itself shows that the order was Benjamin's and that it kept a garrison at Mobile. It also does not say where the troops that "fill the city" come from in May–June, with Beauregard holding Corinth. Canon's Pensacola evacuation of 9 May was the execution of that same order. **Fix:** "Benjamin's order stripping the coast is not carried through at Mobile once New Orleans holds", name the troops' source (for example regiments Lovell can spare after the repulse), and add one clause on why Pensacola is still abandoned.

10. **Corridor distance (Part IV, CT5).** With New Orleans Confederate the natural route is Niblett's Bluff–Brashear City–Opelousas railroad to Algiers, shorter than by Alexandria, with rail on parts of the Texas leg. **Fix:** drop "more than seven hundred miles by wagon" from the ledger-quoted sentence or label it MODEL; the forage ratio computes to three to five times.

13. **Lincoln's foreknowledge (§11 text, Part VI).** In December 1864 no armistice exists. **Fix:** he wants the bay "before a successor who may negotiate takes office".

## Consider

2. **Fuzes and primers are different faults (M3, CT11).** Primers fire the gun; shell fuzes make the shell burst. The battle literature has both: guns misfiring, and shells striking Union ships without exploding. Make them two items, both carried across from Matamoros.

3. **Terry's force** historically included Paine's USCT division; Black troops landing in Alabama, and their fate if captured, is a real stake.
