# Fresh-context review — CR-064 (the Union's war without Sherman)

Inputs: the CR-064 draft, AGENTS.md, canon §§8–13, CR-015 A and F, and the bible. Web checks: NPS *Campaign for Atlanta* sec. 2; Wikipedia on the River Line and Rousseau's raid; SUVCW on the Iowa regiments; California State Military Museum on Halleck. No protected intent is touched.

## Blockers

**1. C7, the Part III table and the storyline "after Vicksburg": Thomas's "seniority" claim is wrong, and the CR's thesis rests on it.** C7 says seniority among volunteers settles army-group command. History says otherwise:
- Sherman's volunteer major-generalcy (1 May 1862) was junior to Thomas's (25 April 1862), and Grant still gave Sherman the Military Division in March 1864.
- Sherman's regular commission (4 July 1863) outranked Thomas's (27 October 1863).

By Grant's own precedent he would pick McPherson: his protégé, already an army commander, and senior in the regular army (C7 admits this). So "his institutions work as designed" describes an institution that did not exist.

**Fix:** keep Thomas, with a real cause and a cost for Grant. Lincoln and Stanton want the hero of Chickamauga and Missionary Ridge, Halleck backs them and Grant is overruled. Or Grant decides that the Army of the Cumberland, three-fifths of the force, will not serve under a 35-year-old engineer. Rewrite option (b)'s "Against" column to match. The stall is unaffected, since H&J do not count McPherson among the raiders.

**2. A2, Part V steps 2–3 and open decision 7: Snake Creek Gap fails on the 10th–11th, not on the 9th.** The 9th is plausible: a lead division declining to attack about 4,000 men in works repeats McPherson's choice. But Thomas's plan was to *hold* the railroad, which can be cut anywhere along the 16 miles north of Resaca without touching Cantey. By the draft's own timing, two corps (about 40,000) are out of the gap on the 10th, and Johnston marches south across their front that night. Nothing explains why Thomas is not astride the railroad near Tilton on the 11th instead of attacking on the 12th–13th. A reader who knows the campaign will see this.

**Fix:** use the precedent the record offers. On 10–12 May McPherson entrenched at the gap's mouth expecting Hood, and Johnston slipped past. Give Thomas the same reading: Hood's divisions probe the mouth on the 10th, Thomas's lead corps digs in, and he will not commit until his trains and second corps are up. Johnston marches that night behind Wheeler.

Also reconcile the figures and wording:
- Cantey's strength: 5,300 (A2), about 4,000 ("What the record changed"), "perhaps 5,000" (step 3).
- The force that declines to attack: "half a corps" (A2) against "one division" (step 2).
- Drop the claim that Thomas's "whole career" argues against taking the chance. Mill Springs and Nashville argue the other way.

**3. A5, A6, A8 and Part V step 5: the June detachment fails on motive, supply and authority.**
- *Motive.* Johnston spent 1864 asking for men and asking *other* commands (Forrest, S. D. Lee) to strike Sherman's rail. Sending away a quarter of his infantry while outnumbered two to one goes against the record that CT-JOHNSTON-JE rests on.
- *Supply.* Atlanta did feed Johnston with Polk, and then Hood with the militia, all summer; forage was the strain. "Cannot feed nearly 70,000" is not credible as stated.
- *Authority and geography.* Polk "carries the letter" and Davis approves "a plan Polk brings in person", while Polk marches west to Blue Mountain, away from Richmond. Johnston also cannot put troops into Lee's department without Davis's order.

**Fix:** make the flank Richmond's demand, which Johnston then makes his own. A. J. Smith on the Memphis–Corinth flank (R4) alarms Selma and Mobile. Davis, advised by Bragg, orders a force west. Johnston protests, then writes it into his plan. Keep Loring and French with Johnston, and build the flank from Cantey, the Vicksburg escapees and Lee's cavalry, about 10,000–12,000 (MODEL). Davis's approval should come before the troops move, and the letter should go by courier.

**4. A6, A9 and steps 6 and 9: the deterrent is asserted, not shown.** A railhead four days from Rome threatens points 80–100 miles behind the front. Sherman's rear garrisons historically already numbered about the "25,000" of A6, and in October 1864 he ignored Hood's whole army on his line. As written, the stall rests on temperament alone, which contradicts the draft's "three things a reader can count".

**Fix:** let Polk strike once (July–August): cut the line at the Etowah, at Kingston, or at Allatoona as French tried on 5 October, and burn a depot. After that, Thomas's caution is learned rather than innate. Also address Rousseau's raid (10–22 July 1864). Thomas's own command wrecked Talladega, on Polk's Selma–Blue Mountain line, and about 30 miles of the Montgomery and West Point. Here it is likely, and it bears on both A5 and A10.

## Should fix

**5. "Atlanta's two railroads" (A9, A10, steps 5 and 11, the storyline).** Atlanta had three lines into the Confederacy. The Georgia Railroad to Augusta was cut only in July 1864, by McPherson's men at Decatur. Thomas's upstream bridgeheads (step 8) put it a short swing *east*, away from Polk, so the flank does not deter that move. Either have Thomas cut it and then say "two", or explain why he does not.

**6. Step 8 is wrong on the River Line.** Johnston's River Line (the Shoupades) stood on the *north* bank. It was begun on 19 June and abandoned on 9 July. Have Johnston hold it first, then the south bank.

**7. Confederate arithmetic (A1, A7, steps 3, 5 and 6).**
- 53,800 plus 19,650 is 73,450, not 68,800. The 68,800 omits Jackson's cavalry, so say so.
- Step 6's "about 55,000 with the cavalry" after Polk leaves needs about 7,000 more from somewhere (militia?).
- A7's 45,000 implies very light losses, and Wheeler disappears from it.

Add one strength table and make A1, A7 and the steps quote it. Also settle whether the escapees *enlarge* Polk's army (summary, storyline "filled with") or *replace* paroled men (A1). A5's historical 15,000 implies they replace them.

**8. Stale sections contradict the body.** The 8 November repair pass gives 60,000, about 20,000 on the flank and V3's 15,000/8,000. Self-review question 2 gives "about 20,000". Mark both superseded or update them.

**9. Kennesaw or Allatoona in July?** A4 puts Kennesaw in August, while step 7, A8 and the storyline put Johnston on the Kennesaw line on 16 July. Pick one.

**10. The "would never do to conduct one of your campaigns" quotation (C7, Part III, storyline "he has written").** "Your" is Sherman, so in this timeline the letter has no addressee. Check H&J's date; if it is after March 1864, Grant cannot have "written" it by then. Give Grant the judgement in his own words to Halleck or Rawlins, unquoted.

**11. A9 and open decision 13: the suspended replacement should be McPherson.** Schofield was picked in December 1864 because he was on the spot at Nashville. Here McPherson is alive (C8), senior to Schofield and Grant's man. Naming him also sharpens blocker 1.

**12. H3: an early Halleck has a ripple.** Halleck in the East by June would be commissioned at once and would be the obvious alternative to Frémont for the Western Department (July 1861). Explain why the 19 August date and the November posting stand. One option is to use the summer: Halleck plans the Sound expedition as the Union's Pacific expert.

**13. H2, Part I and self-review question 5: the line is citizenship.** Canon §8 has Sherman in "militia affairs" in 1856, before naturalizing. Make Halleck's refusal a personal choice and naturalization the line. O2's Hooker should follow the same logic.

**14. Shiloh: "hours" or "nothing" against Nelson?** The summary, S6's heading and CT1 say "hours"; S6's body and Part II say "nothing". Choose "nothing", which fits the argument.

**15. Storyline text compares with history (AGENTS forbids this).**
- "The man who would have led Grant's river corps…"
- "regiments that would have stood in Grant's lines"
- "as it would have been with any Union ironclad"
- "never lent to a Red River campaign"
- "a Meridian nobody raided"

Rewrite each as an event: "In Monterey, Sherman drills reserves"; "Four Iowa regiments are on the Panama road." The 1864 paragraph is also a list of mechanisms. Give it one viewpoint beat, such as Thomas at the gap's mouth on the 10th.

**16. O3 is incomplete.** It omits Grant's Fort Humboldt posting and resignation (1853–54), the crisis behind his whole career, and the Californian years of Steele and Crook. List them, with no effect claimed.

## Consider

**17.** E2: McClellan advanced up the Peninsula by land, and the Yorktown–Gloucester batteries closed the York to the navy. "Goes up the York" misstates both.

**18.** H1: I believe the 1851 San Francisco–San José company was the *Pacific and Atlantic* Railroad; please verify. "Argued half the land titles before the Republic's courts" also sits oddly with CR-033's administrative patents "within months". Make it conveyancing and patent work instead.

**19.** R4: Forrest's spring raid and Fort Pillow (12 April) fall in the window when Smith "holds the flank". Say whether they happen. A purely defensive Smith may leave Forrest freer to raid Thomas's line than Sturgis's offensive did, which would help the stall.

**20.** C2: McPherson cannot use Sherman's argument, which drew on Sherman's own disgrace and recovery in 1861. Give him his own; as Halleck's former staff officer he has a distinct one available. Also, the §9 Halleck paragraph narrates May but sits after the July sentence, so move it earlier.

**21.** S3: the 15th Iowa landed only at dawn on 6 April, and the 16th may still have been aboard ship. Against a 5 April attack their diversion changes little. The interface's line that they must be gone "before 6 April" overstates their role.

**22.** A8: Davis's distrust of Johnston predates 1864, and Hood wrote privately to Davis and Bragg all spring. Keep Bragg's July visit to Atlanta. Here he finds the written plan being followed, so the pressure exists and fails.

## What holds

- Shiloh: the earlier execution, the dusk halt and Beauregard's withdrawal on the night of the 6th all fit canon's "costly operational respite".
- Hampton Roads and Drewry's Bluff are precise and keep canon's rule.
- Halleck's going east is well motivated, apart from items 12–13.
- The CT1–CT4 recommendations are sound. "Atlanta untaken" is better than "held".
