# Fresh-context review 1: CR-059 and CR-062 (13 November 2026)

Reviewer: a separate agent context given only AGENTS.md, the two drafts, the canon extract of §§8–13 and named rows of the neighbouring drafts. It did not see the session records or CR-068-record.md. Points marked "(my knowledge)" are the reviewer's own. Line numbers are those of commit 91739ce.

## Verdict
Not ready to integrate. The decisions of 12 November were written into the rows but not carried through the dependent rows, tables and storyline text. The biggest obstacle is that several decided items still stand beside their pre-decision wording: W11, the unconditional Oregon article, open decision 49, Logan's motive and Deseret's status. One editing pass fixes most of it; no author decision needs reopening.

## Blockers

1. **W11 withdrawn, but its laws are still voided (CR-059).**
   - W11 (l.127): "The 1862 marriage ban and the $5 tax are not passed under the compact in this timeline."
   - X4 (l.179): "voids Oregon's 1862 marriage ban on accession"; X5 (l.180): "the 1862 poll tax falls for confederal citizens".
   - The same laws appear in the Part VII table (l.500–501: "Voids the 1862 ban", "the poll tax falls") and the offers table (l.443: "Marriage ban void; Chinese ban and poll tax void").
   - Fix: reword X4/X5 and both tables as a floor that bars such laws in future. Keep only the 1857 constitution's Chinese property and mining ban as something actually struck.

2. **The Oregon article is still "conditional" in three places (CR-062), against CT7 and CR-059 CT14 as decided.**
   - O1 (l.72): "The article carries no condition of its own."
   - Summary (l.25): "Its Oregon article is conditional on Oregon's vote."
   - O4 (l.75): "the Senate will not consent to a cession whose other branch is a disarmed Union territory"; Part III (l.195): "a conditional cession is a weak text to send: Republicans would vote on a Union Oregon without a naval station."
   - Under the reconciliation the no-branch sits only in the executive protocol, so the Senate never votes on it. McClellan's first reason for holding the treaty is gone.
   - CR-059 has the same residue: §12 text (l.602) "It transfers Oregon only if Oregon votes for it"; ledger (l.721) "the conditional Oregon article".
   - Fix: restate the reason as "the article points to a vote not yet held". Change l.25, O4, Part III, and "article" to "protocol" in the CR-059 ledger line.

3. **Open decision 49 ("all three candidates are staged") contradicts Part VI and the §12 text (CR-059).**
   - Decision 49(a) (l.834): "an August report after Roseburg shows the margin closing."
   - Part VI (l.424): "his estimate is not shown to be wrong until the returns commission counts Jackson County"; §12 (l.602): "Elias expects a comfortable majority."
   - None of the three scenes is in "The campaign's scenes" (l.483–492), and 49(b) still routes through "Hawaii's session", which CT15(b) replaced with a message.
   - Fix: add the three scenes, rewrite the Part VI sentence, and move (b) to San Francisco.

4. **Logan's decided motive is not on the page and contradicts the test (CR-062).**
   - A5 (l.44): "he refuses, because the Gulf's merchants have made him a better offer."
   - Part I (l.163): "he refuses while the armistice holds… The page shows a promise kept by a man Pacifica no longer pays."
   - Storyline (l.285): "he turns it down, and the consul reports that too", with no motive.
   - A refusal for money is not the "promise tested once" of principle 5. Fix: rewrite Part I and the §12 sentence to the decided motive, and say what the consul's report now proves.

5. **Deseret's status: CT11 is decided "amend", but the rows keep canon (CR-059).**
   - W10 (l.126): "Deseret is already a member state (canon §10). If CR-B makes it an associate first…"; route table (l.359): "a Pacifican member state from 1862–63."
   - CT11 (l.674) and CR-060 D5/D12: association December 1862, member on the exchange of 1867, accession vote December 1866.
   - Fix: apply the knock-on wording CT11 already lists, and drop "if CR-060 CT1 is adopted" at l.451.

## Should fix

1. **Thayer residue (CR-059).** l.59 "made on the floor by Hughes, Thayer and Stephens" and l.316 "(Hughes, Thayer, Stephens, Colfax)" against H5 "Thayer is not in the scene"; l.57 "turned against Thayer". Fix: remove him from all three.

3. **June 1866 is overloaded in the §12 text (CR-059, l.602).** "In June 1866 Idaho's voters approve…, the Confederal chambers and both founding states entrench Oregon's, and the Treasury publishes the customs houses."
   - I11 dates the map to July and V10 the entrenchment to "summer".

   - Fix: reorder, and date the entrenchment after her visit.
4. **Sumner's third ground (CR-062 l.299, l.461).** "the Union's own Enabling Act never removed it" reads against CR-059 E2 ("a convention without slavery or exclusion") and V7, where his principle kills the House amendment. Fix: keep the decision but say what is meant, for example that the Act's offer stood five years and Oregon never took it.
5. **Hawaii's entry power is granted twice.** CR-059 X1 and CT4 (decided): the 1856 Articles leave residence to members, "Hawaii demanded it in 1855–56". CR-068 CT8: Victoria wins "Hawaii's control of entry at its own ports" as a new condition in 1864. Fix: say what 1864 adds, for example entry against the Protector's emergency and customs powers.

## Literary effect
1. **Lincoln's letter costs nothing (CR-059 V11, l.164).** Elias concedes in print that no one can dismiss him, in the paper the swing voters read, and "the MODEL margin is unchanged". Let it be the cause of decision 49(a)'s closing August report.
2. **X2 has no opponent.** Part VII calls the limit "New, on the common-market principle", which is Elias's own. Victoria "wins" against nobody. Name who resists, such as Oregon's agents or California's delegation, and what she trades.
3. **Bush's replacement leaves Marion without a Democratic "no".** The decisive bloc is Salem's Douglas Democrats, but the only voice against is a Republican ex-President in a Portland Republican paper. Give the 40 per cent who vote no a Democrat of their own.
4. **Elias's "price" against the third reason (Part VI).** If he "cannot refuse in 1865 a vote he has offered in print for two years", Washington knows it, and he has nothing to sell for the no-branch. Show why McClellan pays anyway.
5. **Walla Walla, July 1862 (I3).** The convention has no Union signatory, and Wright, canon's "competent opposition", vanishes. Say whether he signs and marches.
6. **Sumner.** A year after his principle kills Oregon's clause in committee, he votes to cede Oregon to a state that keeps it. The speech should concede that cost in his own voice.
