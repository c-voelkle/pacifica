# Fresh-context review 3: CR-063, CR-064 and CR-065 (13 November 2026)

Reviewer: a separate agent context given only AGENTS.md, the three drafts, the canon extract of §§8–13 and named rows of the neighbouring drafts. It did not see the session records or CR-068-record.md. Points marked "(my knowledge)" are the reviewer's own. Line numbers are those of commit 91739ce.

## Verdict

Not ready to integrate as a set. The biggest obstacle is that CR-063 still has Sherman commanding the Union corps at Vicksburg in May 1863, in its rows, its step-by-step and its §11 storyline text. CR-064 and the character bible both say he is Pacifican and has no Union role there. After that, CR-065's 12 November additions sit on top of older text that still assumes a silent Pacifica and an open Mobile date.

## Blockers

1. **Sherman at Vicksburg (CR-063 lines 30, 77, 79, 161–164; §11 text line 283).**
   - CR-063: "Sherman bridging the river at Bridgeport"; "Sherman's leading division comes onto it"; "Sherman, warned in the night by his pickets".
   - CR-064 C3–C4: Steele "leads the XV Corps through Vicksburg". Character bible line 503: "He has no Union field role at Shiloh, Chickasaw Bayou, Jackson or Vicksburg".
   - Fix: write "Steele's XV Corps" (Blair's division leading) in every alternate-timeline sentence. Keep "Sherman" only where the OR report is cited as the historical source.

2. **The column's commander at Vicksburg (CR-063 line 162 against line 465).**
   - Part III step 3: "Forney commands the column"; Stevenson's division "man the works".
   - Open decision 8, decided "as recommended": "Stevenson commands the column".
   - A decided item names a general whose division stays behind. Fix: correct decision 8 to Forney, or have the author confirm.
   - The §11 text's closing "[OPEN] The date of the surrender, the column's commander and the Port Hudson column's landing place" (line 285) lists three things decisions 8 and 9 settled (about 4 July; Bayou Sara). V4 "date OPEN" and M6 "Date in February OPEN" are stale the same way.

3. **CR-065 still argues from a silent Pacifica and an absent West (12 November passages against older text).**
   - Line 40: "Pacifica's restraint in the autumn → no outrage for Lincoln to campaign on". Line 177: "the West stays off the ballot. That suits Elias". Line 181: "without a Pacifican hand (P1)". Line 211: "Pacifica's part (P1–P2). Elias does nothing in the campaign."
   - Against these: P3 (the State Department publishes Pacifican correspondence with Richmond in September), E3's new sentence (War Democrats campaign on "Monterey's printed heads"), and CR-068 G6 reservation (b), in print since 1863: "If on the fourth of March 1865 the Union has sworn a President who will not, the Republic will take the last American station on the Sound."
   - P1's own rationale ("An exposed Pacifican hand would give Lincoln his best argument") is contradicted by P3's outcome ("The Democrats use it for their own case") with no reason given. CR-068 G8 has the reason: "the Union fights two enemies and can finish neither".
   - Fix: rewrite the four passages; add CR-068's reason and Seward's motive ("to draw an answer from Monterey before the vote") to P3; widen open decision 20 from the recognition promise to the Sound ultimatum, which is the more usable Republican text.

2. **Lincoln and the West (CR-065 L2, L7, line 226 against CR-068 G8, G9).**
   - CR-065: Lincoln "offers no line in the West… would not if he could".
   - CR-068 G8 has his public offer of spring 1864 ("Leave the Columbia and the Sound, and the Union will waive its war claims"). G9 puts a sentence on the West in the December message ("no power to cede it and will not treat for it").
   - CR-065 mentions neither. Add the message sentence to L2 and limit "no line" to the winter.

3. **The dissolution campaign (CR-065 §12 text line 270, M4).**
   - CR-065: "not to change governments in the middle of a war".
   - CR-068 G8: he dissolves after McClellan's win is known and "campaigns on a war that is ending on published terms".
   - The sentence order in line 270 also has him win the Assembly before he dissolves it. Reword and reorder.

4. **"Pacifican credit works in London" (CR-063 line 278; CT5 decision line 341).** CR-068 CT26 has London repaid in 1859 and the 1862 London tranche "tried first and dropped". The decision says reword; it is not reworded. Suggest: "Pacifica's credit to Richmond, lent on the terms printed in Texas, buys in Europe".

5. **Butler has no ending.** CR-063 S6: "His fate after the election belongs to CR-065". CR-065 has no Butler row. S2 still lists him as a radical candidate, though CR-063 removes New Orleans and the "Beast" fame that made him one. Add a row; reconsider S2.

6. **The West in Lincoln's last winter (CR-064 A9, step 12; CR-065 L4–L5).** Grant "drafts an order giving the western command to McPherson and suspends it" because Lincoln "will not relieve the general whose army is in contact" before the vote. After 8 November that reason is gone, yet the last push is only Petersburg and Mobile. State why a hundred thousand men stay still from November to March.

7. **Vicksburg strengths.**
   - The figures agree everywhere they appear in CR-063 and CR-064: 15,000 out, 12,500 reach Johnston, 16,500 hold, 15,000 surrender.
   - The "two sets" the author deferred (line 465) are not both on the page. State the second set or drop the deferral.
   - Forney's and M. L. Smith's divisions less Vaughn were nearer 9,000–10,000, and Pemberton had almost no cavalry inside the lines (my knowledge; research task 12 should settle it).
   - Historically all 29,495 were paroled and most returned (my knowledge). If the 15,000 are paroled here too, the escape nets the Confederacy nothing. Say whether Grant paroles them.

8. **Mobile steps 7–8 (CR-063 lines 252–253).** The *Tennessee* "takes them one at a time: first *Hartford*, sinking by the bow", then *Hartford* is towed out past Fort Morgan and "beached at Pensacola", some forty miles off. Choose one.

9. **Logan at Vicksburg (CR-063 line 164).** "Logan's workshop goes with the trains", and the trains are captured at the forks. Canon has him preserve "portable tools, knowledge and some components". Say where Logan and Marcus are at 9:30 on the 18th.

10. **CR-065 E6:** Lincoln keeps "New England except New Hampshire", but McClellan carries Connecticut in the same row.

11. **CR-065 §12 text** drops canon's "Lincoln pursues his last winter operations". The "four ways" of Part V become three; Petersburg and Mobile vanish from §12.

12. **CR-065 line 220:** Mobile would "close Pacifica's channel at its hinge". CR-063's corridor runs Matamoros–Texas–below Port Hudson, not through Mobile. CR-063 gives Lincoln a different motive (the Alabama flank). Align them.

## Literary effect

1. **P3 has no content (CR-065).** Pacifica's dealings with Richmond are already public: the terms were printed in Texas, Elias went there in 1863, Logan is at Mobile. Say what the captured letters reveal that print did not. CR-063 T4's seizures are the natural source.
2. **Logan is offstage at his own climax (CR-063 step 10).** He "learns from the Confederate newspapers". Put him at Fort Morgan or on the shore, or give a reason he is away.
3. **Hampton Roads lacks Richmond's motive (CR-065 L6).** Why send commissioners to a lame duck a month before McClellan? CR-068's clause that the Matamoros credit "falls due if Richmond treats for reunion" bears directly on Stephens and is unused.
4. **Halleck's departure costs Elias nothing on the page (CR-064 §9 text).** "Elias does not look for one" is a non-decision. Let Marcus or Sherman urge detaining him and Elias refuse.
5. **McClellan only waits (CR-065 L1, M1).** Give him one refusal of his own in the winter, for instance on the amendment or on Mobile.
6. **Pemberton's council (CR-063 step 3).** The generals vote and Pemberton stays. The scene needs the man who argues against splitting the army, and what Marcus says when asked.
