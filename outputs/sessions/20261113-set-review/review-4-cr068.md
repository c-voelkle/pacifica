# Fresh-context review 4: CR-068 and its companion files (13 November 2026)

Reviewer: a separate agent context given only AGENTS.md, CR-068.md, CR-068-lincoln-career.md, CR-068-two-houses.md, CR-068-samples.md, the canon extract of §§8–13 and named rows of the neighbouring drafts. It did not see the session records or CR-068-record.md. Points marked "(my knowledge)" are the reviewer's own. Line numbers are those of commit 91739ce (revision 25).

## Verdict

CR-068 is not ready to integrate with the set. The new price of 12 November 1864 is carried through in wording, but its consequences are not: the draft never says what Elias holds between the Protectorship's end and the Enabling Law, and it leaves canon's 1865 bargain and Lincoln's 1866 letter as if the price had never been printed. The interregnum is the main obstacle. The neighbour drafts match on most rows; the exceptions are listed below.

## Blockers

1. **No authority stated for 1865 (CR-068.md G8 item 6, l.328; TL6 table, l.462; §12 block, l.619).**
   - CR-068: "no renewal of the Protectorship after the war".
   - Canon §12: "In 1862, 1863 and 1864 both member legislatures renew"; the term's working end is late May (§9).
   - The armistices run "March–June 1865" (CR-062 A1), and CR-062 l.179 has Elias "in December 1865 … securing the Enabling Law".
   - So from late May 1865 he has no Article XIV powers (joint command, customs, the decrees the occupation rests on) for six months or more. Canon §12 implies he is still Chancellor ("the chancellorship lapses"), but CR-068 never mentions that office.
   - "After the war" is also undefined: armistice, the treaty of December 1865, or the Senate's consent in 1867.
   - Fix: define the trigger in G8, CT17 and the §12 block. Then either state that he governs as Chancellor under ordinary law until the amendment (a real cost, see Literary 1), or allow one last renewal in May 1865 and amend canon and C-1861-PROTECTOR.

2. **Canon's 1865 bargain is duplicated and no tension is declared (CT17, l.754; §12 block, l.619).**
   - CR-068: "with Hawaii's crown, the Kuhina Nui and its local institutions as protected provisions", accepted in November 1864.
   - Canon §12: "Lot supports the bargain in return for assurances protecting Hawaiian kingship, the Kuhina Nui, local institutions and agreed revenues. Victoria negotiates these protections".
   - After integration Hawaii is promised the same three things twice, Lot's "in return for" is already owed, and "agreed revenues" appears only in 1865. Lot, king since November 1863, is absent from G8.
   - CT17 says only that "Canon §12's sentence stands" (the dissolution). The status line claims the Enabling Law "redeems" the pledge, but no storyline text is offered for the Enabling Law paragraphs or for C-1865-HI-GUARANTEES' quoted sentence.
   - Fix: add to CT17 (or a new CT) a replacement sentence for the 1865 paragraph. Suggested content: the form and the three protections redeem the Council's resolution; what Lot and Victoria still bargain for in 1865 is the revenues, the reach of cabinet legislation and the life term.

3. **Lincoln's Oregon letter ignores a printed fact (G10, l.346–347; PP12; CR-059 V11 and l.602).**
   - CR-068: "Then the Protector could be dismissed every twelvemonth. Who dismisses the Providentor?"
   - The end of the twelvemonth was Hawaii's own condition, stated in a resolution G8 calls "printed" (item 1; the test in provision 2). McClellan's government read that resolution (CR-065 M1), so Lincoln knows it in 1866.
   - As written, any Pacifican paper answers him in one line, yet PP12b never uses it ("voted by both legislatures after the peace").
   - Fix: Lincoln names it and turns it. Hawaii asked for an end and a lawful settlement and named no office; the Protector filled the blank with his own life. PP12b then concedes or answers that. Carry the change to CR-059 V11 and its §12 text.

## Should fix

4. **G8 item 6, "The safeguards" (l.309), contradicts itself.**
   - "The merchants … lose their reason to vote on his powers", then: "The majority brings in the revocation bill in the spring".
   - A right the merchants already hold cannot change their behaviour, and a ratification vote in 1866 does not give them Lincoln's offer in 1864.
   - "what the Articles already give them" is also unsupported. Canon §8 gives that route only for "constitutional change"; CR-062 O6 says "Exact procedure OPEN". The §12 block makes it canon.
   - Fix: drop the claim that they lose their reason, and say it peels off some merchants but not the majority. Declare the ratification rule as new, or settle it in CR-062 O6.

5. **X12 (l.554) keeps the old pledge.** "For Elias, governing the war without his Assembly's vote on the peace": nothing in revision 25 closes that. Reword, for example "the yearly office: the Protectorship ends with the war".

6. **"Fourth condition" conflicts with CR-060 C6 (CR-068 CT8, l.691–692; G8 item 6; B13; X12).**
   - CR-068 CT8: "three conditions: no annexation, Carson's guarantees kept, and Washoe to decide its own future"; entry control is "a fourth".
   - CR-060 C6, decided 12 November: the Washoe condition she "does not get". Entry control is the third granted of four asked.
   - Fix: update CT8's "Current" and the four places.

7. **Standing instructions cannot know of the vote (G8 item 6, l.328; W1 "instructed by his wife"; A11 l.135).**
   - The vote is called on the 9th for the 12th, and Honolulu is about twelve days away (CT24). Yet the price is fitted to that day: "A Protector confirmed by her delegation's votes owes her".
   - The author's decision that the delegation acts on standing instructions stands. Fix: say when the instruction was given and that it is general (for example, after the third renewal: at the next occasion the Protector needs Hawaii's votes), and that it is the Hawaiian government's instruction, with Lot. Canon §8: "no confederal article assigns the Kuhina Nui an exclusive personal countersignature".

8. **The loan's outcome (G11, l.355) is not in CR-062.**
   - CR-068: "The loan passes smaller and shorter than offered (CR-062 M3 is to follow)".
   - CR-062 l.435: "Whether the Senate follows his advice on the loan goes with M3, which waits for modelling."
   - Fix: mark the CR-068 sentence OPEN/MODEL.

9. **Stale cross-references after the 12 November write-in.**
   - G8 provision 2 (l.320): "CR-065 M1 gives McClellan's inaugural no sentence on the West". M1 now has it.
   - CT27 (l.839) and CT6 (l.685) still say CR-065 and CR-059 are "to be reworded"/"to be cut"; both are done.
   - CR-065 Part VI still reads "**OPEN:** the dissolution's date; recommended after the news", although open decision 14 settles it.
   - CR-065 Part III: "without a Pacifican hand". CR-068 G8: "Every part of it has a Pacifican hand". Reconcile the wording.

10. **TL6 (l.450), "each price is Hawaii's".** CR-059 W4's price for the 1862 renewal is an Oregon relief boat, and CR-060 C6's conditions concern Washoe. The table omits 1862. Add it or narrow the claim.

## Literary effect

1. **The November price costs Elias nothing (G8 item 6; TL6).** The old price constrained him. The new one hands him the door to the life office, and he accepts it in a sentence. Let it bite: the months of 1865 without emergency powers (Blocker 1), or a term she names that he must overcome in 1865.
2. **The judge is absent from the heaviest beat (B15).** TL6 says the heavy beats are decided where "her voice or her party's counts", but an unnamed delegation head speaks. Name him and give him latitude and a line of his own. Canon does not appear to fix her at Honolulu that month, so she could also be put at Monterey.
3. **The Assembly majority waits so the vote can happen (G8 item 6, item 9).** It holds its bill eight months and then announces its timetable. The blind vote does not touch the bill, so the jeopardy is entirely self-made. Give the majority a reason of its own to wait, or one attempt to force the vote that fails.
4. **Lincoln's opening in G8 is weak for the man described.** The offer needs a Senate he may not have, and the Council sees that at once (reason 1). The September publication does nothing. Give the offer one thing he can deliver by executive act, for example suspending search in Hawaiian waters, so that refusing it costs something.
5. **G10 and G11 leave nothing behind (TL2).** The chain has no row for either; the letter "holds voters already counted". Tie PP12b's concession forward, for example Lincoln's 1871 brief quoting it. Blocker 3's fix supplies the link.
6. **Honolulu 1863 (B9): the reversal is under-motivated.** A legislature moved by the Proclamation renews after hearing "no condition about slavery". "Eyes: Victoria's", yet she does nothing and takes no price. Give her one act in the chamber.
