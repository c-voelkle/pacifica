It did not see the session records, the earlier reports or CR-068-record.md. Points marked "(my knowledge)" are the reviewer's own. ## Verdict

Neither draft is ready to integrate. CR-068's new round G9a is written into the row and the §12 text but not into the rest of the draft or its neighbours, and its motive logic fails for Hawaii and for Washington. CR-065 is close: what remains is a few stale conditionals and the placement of the dissolution text.

## Blockers

1. **Elias's calendar: three passages still bring him home in March.**
   - L243 (G4): "Elias has been home from Texas since March".
   - L626 (§11 storyline text): "He is back at Monterey in March."
   - CR-068-samples.md L95 (A8b): "He had come home from Texas in March".
   - Against these: CT24 L828–829 ("Late February to late April | The road home"), B9 L523 ("a week home from Texas") and CR-061 C12 ("home by late April").
   - Fix: "late April" in all three; the storyline block is the one that would enter canon.

2. **The blind vote's premise conflicts with the telegraph elsewhere.**
   - L322: the result comes "by the Union's wire to Denver, a rider over the mountains to Salt Lake and Deseret's wire west", reaching San Francisco on the 17th.
   - CR-060 D4a: "The Union's commercial traffic goes on passing through Salt Lake under the Compact, and Pacifica's office there reads it."
   - L837 (CT24): Gettysburg and Vicksburg come "over the wire here" at Salt Lake.
   - If a Union wire reaches Salt Lake, Monterey has the result on 9–10 November and B15 collapses.
   - Fix: one dated fact in CR-060 D4a for when the wire east of Salt Lake is cut or closed, and make L263 and L837 match it.

3. **G9a's Port Townsend scene: wrong commander, and Marcus's place is unsettled.**
   - L349: "a shot is fired between Casey's pickets and Naglee's … and Naglee asks for orders. Marcus Vance offers the short way".
   - CR-067 L80: the Columbia is under "Marcus again from mid-1864", and G8 L320 stages "Vance's force" on the Sound that winter.
   - Either Marcus is on the Columbia (then Naglee asks him, and he cannot make an offer at Monterey) or he is at the Staff (then CR-067's table is wrong).
   - The offer is also nearly empty: under CR-067 A1 the Chancellor already "directs the confederal forces for the Council". Say what he offers, namely orders without the Council's vote.
   - Undeclared tension with canon §12 and CT-MARCUS-I: Marcus "can accept durable executive authority if lawfully established".
   - Conflict with O6 (L453, APPROVED): "No tempter… There is no scene in which he is offered more and refuses it." L349 is headed "The temptation".
   - Fix: decide Marcus's post in June 1865, amend CR-067 B7, and amend O6 or cut the offer.

4. **"Why Hawaii agrees" contradicts the regime G9a has just set up.**
   - L352: "The alternative to the founder is the Assembly's majority… the merchants who tried to revoke his powers in 1864."
   - That majority lost the dissolution election (L332: "wins a more favourable Assembly"; canon §12).
   - The real alternative is the one in force from June: a Chancellor elected by "the equal member delegations" (canon §8), with "the Council vot[ing] each measure" (L638). That gives Hawaii nine of eighteen votes, its best constitution yet.
   - Hawaii's ratification is required, so Lot and Victoria hold a veto. L353 says they "lose the third" without saying to what.
   - Fix: give the lever. The cleanest is that Elias refuses any office with a term, and they must weigh parity without him against guarantees with him.

5. **Washington signs before the guarantor exists.**
   - L350 makes Washington's commissioners the first to ask "who will be bound in five years?".
   - CR-062 T1: the treaty is signed at "Washington, December 1865", by Vallejo "and William T. Sherman".
   - L640: Hawaii ratifies the Enabling Law "in December" at Honolulu. That news cannot reach Washington before the signing, so the chief asker signs with a Chancellor's commissioners.
   - Sherman must leave Monterey by about early November, yet he "argues for the term" in the bargain.
   - Fix: move the signing or the ratification, or drop Washington from the askers; date Sherman's argument.

6. **G9a is not carried through CR-068.**
   - Counts: L47 and L153 still say thirteen rounds, "Elias opens six". The Part G heading, the score table (L384–398), the weights, "Where Elias could have lost" and the stratagems table have no G9a.
   - L67 (AG5): "Power asked for before it is used, and then never given back."
   - L427 (W1): "offered himself for dismissal in 1861 and again that November, and never after".
   - L430 (W4): "the Founder's own office was never again put to a vote". But L361 says it was "voted by both legislatures".
   - L164 (MD4): "He will not give up the power itself", scored without 1865.
   - L895 (Commitments ledger): "Four existing entries change" omits C-1861-PROTECTOR and C-1865-HI-GUARANTEES, which CT29 changes. The latter's quoted sentence is deleted by the §12 text, so the ledger check will fail.

7. **The account of the nations still states something false in-world.**
   - L239: "no volunteer company ever paid from the treasury".
   - CR-067 B6: "paid by the Treasury: the Washoe volunteers of 1860".
   - Canon §8: Hays's volunteers "defeat Paiute forces".
   - The Chronicler is "never [adversarial] in fact", and Lincoln "never answered" this paper; here he could have.
   - Fix: narrow to canon §5's refusal of 1851, and let the account or the Chronicler own Pyramid Lake.

## Should fix

1. **"After the war" does not yield the lapse.** L328 calls ending the office in wartime a "strict" reading of "no renewal… after the war", and CT17 L775 says "therefore". L346 says the price "falls due", yet a bridging renewal "would probably [be] grant[ed]" and G10 says "as he had promised". Choose one: the resolution says "the renewal of 1864 is the last", or the lapse is his free act.
2. **Who moves the life term?** G9a never says. L328 has "the life term is his move"; L361 has "asked of him". O1 and L444 ("the life office… still his") need him to bring it over his name.
3. **"Why for life" has no answer to death.** The office dies with him (C-1865-PROVIDENTOR), so a life term also gives enemies a date and guarantees nothing "in five years". Sherman has only "argues for the term and loses".
4. **G10 against G11.** L360 has Lincoln use the guarantor maxim in June 1866. L369 still has Elias surprised in 1867 that he "met his own maxim".
5. **"Providentor" in Lincoln's mouth.** L123: he "never writes 'Providentor'". L360, CR-059 V11 and CR-059's §12 text have him write "who dismisses a Providentor?".
6. **Two storyline blocks tell the Oregon letter.** CR-059 §12 and L644 differ in content; keep one.
7. **The maxim changes sense.** Canon §4 concerns a third-party guarantor of another state; G9a means a surety for the Republic's own promises. L360 says he "calls himself the guarantor… by a maxim", which he does not.
8. **The December 1862 order is not in CR-061.** L239 and CT15 cite "its order of December 1862" from K4 and N2. K4 is a compact clause and N2 an agreement of May 1863. CR-061 needs a dated order.
9. **A8b overclaims.** Samples L91 has peons "whom its own law was setting free", against the narrowed account. L626's "undertaking" also overstates "declared purpose".
10. **G5 and PP5.** "Elias opens, spring 1863" with terms over his authority, but he is on the road or at sea from November to 10 June, bar about a week. Date it or give it to the government.
11. **The dissolution is told three times in §12.** Canon's paragraph, CR-065 L270 and CR-068 L634 each tell it; the cause (offer, blind vote) comes last, and all precede the paragraph where Lincoln loses. Merge into one block after the McClellan paragraph.
12. **Armistice dating.** L270, W1 and A9 say "within three months"; L332 says "in the spring"; G9a says mid-June. CR-062 A6 puts the Amity treaty after Pacifica's armistice, yet canon dates it "spring 1865". Say whether Protector or Chancellor signs it.
13. **What holds the ground from 1 to 15 June.** L347: "by the compacts… and by the armistice lines". No lines exist yet, and Washoe was taken by arms. Canon §12's wartime decrees for procurement and credit are "spent" with no replacement named.
14. **Hawaii's December ratification** needs an extraordinary session; CT24 assumes a spring sitting.

## Literary effect

1. **G9a has no opponent.** Lincoln only receives it. Either make it an interlude or let his reading (MD4) predict a bridging renewal and break.
2. **The risk is asserted, not shown.** The Assembly is friendly and Hawaii "would probably grant" a renewal. Show one measure the Council refuses him that summer.
3. **Victoria prices and then yields.** Give her the parity argument of Blocker 4, and make her December vote a trade she names.
4. **Sherman needs his sentence.** Give him the argument from the founder's death, unanswered; it seeds 1887.
5. **The Port Townsend shot resolves itself** once the armistice news arrives, and Sherman travels a week for a day's work. Put the temptation where delay costs something.
6. **G10 improves by conceding** that he did not cling. Reserve the maxim for 1867, and let Oregon turn on "who can lay it down for him?".
