**B1-1. BLOCKER, author. What the extended controls do on Pacifican soil is not said, and the texts pull two ways.**
- The rule since 1863: refused "at the crossings"; whoever reaches the soil is free and "is not given up" ([175]; CR-061 S4). At a land line "refused" means turned back before the soil.
- 1871: the closed line is "extended to the railway and the ports" (CR-061:555; CR-059 S2; CR-068:378, :796) or "to the railway and the Gulf ports" (CR-061:556; CR-068:382; candidate [364], second mention). The man is detained by "a port officer" at San Francisco "under the controls… extended to the ports" (candidate [364], first mention; CR-068:378).
- A railway station or a port inside the Republic is on its soil. Either (a) the controls only work at points of departure (Texas stations and Gulf ports, through the customs co-administration of [352]), in which case the San Francisco officer has no ground, anyone who lands is free without a court, and the ransom article's trigger "reaches a court" and the cabinet's aim "so that few reach a court" mean nothing more than "reaches the soil"; or (b) the controls also work at arrival, in which case a person found without papers at San Francisco is held to be put back on the ship, which is a return from the soil, and the opening sentence of [364] ("returns no one who has reached Pacifican soil", stated for 1863–1889) is false from the winter of 1871–72 for everyone who does not get a writ.
- CR-068:381 in fact says (b): the ministers' override is "so that the new controls can send back a man found without papers". That is a leftover of the returning policy in a row that elsewhere says no one is ever returned.
- The candidate avoids the phrase and so leaves three things unexplained: on what ground the officer detains (only "under the controls"); what the override would have done; and what "few reach a court" means for those who reach the soil and not a court.
- The consul's ground: none is stated, and none exists after the refusal at the treaty table. CR-068:378 says he "claims him all the same". That is acceptable (a demand without a title), but the storyline should say so in five words, or the reader assumes a clause.
- Why a court at all: the honest answer in the drafts is "because he is held, and because the ministers mean to use the new controls to send him back". The candidate should say the first half.

  Decision needed: (a) or (b). If (b), the opening sentence of [364] needs "landed" or "admitted" in place of "reached Pacifican soil", and Lincoln's argument (the article of 1863 as evidence that no one who reached the soil was given up) becomes the case against the 1871 controls themselves, which is a stronger round. If (a), the port officer's act is an excess, the writ is easy, and the override has no legal object. Then fix "ports"/"Gulf ports" in candidate [364], CR-061:556 and CR-068:382 to one wording.
