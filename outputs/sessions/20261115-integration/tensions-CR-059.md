### CT3 — The interior concentration (§10)
**Current:** Union troops keep an "interior concentration, giving later reinforcements somewhere to arrive."
**Adapted:** it is Fort Walla Walla, held by the regulars Wright carried upriver. It ends at the July 1862 convention, after the failed recovery attempt. The historical record (OR Series I, Vol. 50; session note `research-official-records-vol50.md`) shows why. Historically every regular was ordered east in October 1861; here the order reaches only the cut-off posts east of the Cascades (I2, 13 November 2026). The interior was supplied up the Columbia, and its valley could not feed the post in the winter of 1861–62. Its later garrisons came from California, Oregon and San Francisco recruiting. What coin the coast's paymasters had came through San Francisco, and by 1863 they were short of it. Here none of these is available to the Union. The historical order of 8 July 1862 shows the federal supply organization working, but by the river and from California.
**If later interior operations need it:** move the convention to 1863.
**Recommendation:** adapt. Fact changes: none.
**Decision (author, 12 November 2026):** adapt.

### CT4 — The 1856 Articles' residual powers (§8; C-1856-ARTICLES)

**Recommendation:** amend. Fact changes: none.
**Decision (author, 12 November 2026):** amend.

### CT8 — New members' representation (§10 "the new members' representation remain [OPEN]")
**Amended:** Oregon, Idaho and Washington each get a Council delegation and Assembly seats by population. Constitutional ratification stays with the founding states, and each charter can be amended only with its own state's consent.
**Knock-on:** the 1887 Constitution of the Realm ends the member states (canon §21), and canon names only the founding states as its ratifiers. Either each charter's protected provision allows its supersession by a constitution the member states ratify, or Oregon, Idaho and Washington ratify in 1887 as well. Deseret raises the same question (CT11).
**Recommendation:** amend; the weighting is open decision 18; the 1887 knock-on goes to the CR that revisits 1887. Fact changes: none.
**Decision (author, 12 November 2026):** amend, as recommended: a Council delegation for each new member, equal with the others, and Assembly seats by population; each charter amendable only with its own state's consent. The 1887 knock-on goes to a later CR. (CR-068 CT17 fixes the Council of 1864 at eighteen, nine from each founding state.)

### CT14 — The conditional Oregon article (§12 "Pacifica keeps the Northwest"; CR-015 A6; C-1865-ARMISTICE; C-1867-WESTERN-LINE)
**Current:** the Western line is agreed in the 1865 peace and ratified in 1867, and "Pacifica keeps the Northwest". The ledger lists the cession of the Northwest as an unconditional US obligation from 1867. CR-015 A6 calls the Union's full exit from the coast "the indispensable term": otherwise the Union keeps a Pacific naval base, Russia sells Alaska to Washington, and a Union squadron sits on the flank.
**Adapted (recommended):** the condition lives only in McClellan's executive armistice convention of 1865 (C-1865-ARMISTICE, whose terms beyond the ceasefire canon leaves unstated). It is spent in October 1866. The treaty the Senate ratifies in 1867 records Oregon's choice and cedes the Northwest without condition, so §12, the 1867 ledger entry and A6 stay true as written. The no-branch (V1) is a counterfactual with real stakes. It is written to keep A6's purpose even if it were taken: Washington Territory and Puget Sound go unconditionally (V17), and a Union Oregon would hold no naval station, fortification or garrison. Canon text to change: one sentence in the 1865 peace paragraph of §12, "The peace transfers Idaho outright. It transfers Oregon only if Oregon votes for it" (already in this draft's §12 text). The ledger's C-1865-ARMISTICE gets the conditional article; C-1867-WESTERN-LINE gets only the record of Oregon's choice.
**Amended:** keep canon's unconditional cession in 1865. Oregon's 1866 vote then decides only how Oregon joins Pacifica (member state with a charter, or a territory of the Confederation), not whether. This is simpler, but the Union-or-Pacifica question, the exclusion hinge and the Senate's "Oregon left by its own choice" (Part VI) all go, and Pacifica takes a white settler state by treaty without its consent, against the consent principle of 1856 and the Protector's ban on annexation by decree.
**Decision (author, 7 November 2026): adapt,** with Elias's expectation of about 55 per cent and the narrowing by his own floor and Native article written on the page (Part VI, "Why Elias takes the risk"). Fact changes: none (the cession's date and outcome are unchanged).
**Two homes for the condition (set review, 8 November 2026; author to confirm).** The drafts placed it in two places: the treaty's article (V1 and Part VI as first written: "cession only if a majority of votes cast approve accession") and the armistice convention alone (this tension). CR-062 (O1, O3, O5, Part III) reconciles them: the treaty's Oregon article is worded as a cession "as Oregon's voters choose under the protocol of [date]" and carries no condition of its own; the armistice protocol signed the same day, a supplement to the 1865 armistice convention and in force at once, carries the machinery (threshold, electorate, polls, observers, returns commission) and the no-branch; the protocol of certification records the returns and strikes the no-branch before the Senate votes. **Recommended:** adopt CR-062's reconciliation. It keeps the author's choice of 7 November (the condition lives in the armistice instruments and is spent in October 1866) and lets the Senate consent to an article that never states a condition. V1 and Part VI are now written to it, marked "if the author confirms CR-062's O1". Fact changes: none.
**Decision (author, 12 November 2026):** CR-062's reconciliation is confirmed (the two homes): the treaty article states no condition, the armistice protocol carries the machinery and the no-branch, and the certification strikes the no-branch before the Senate votes. The marks "if the author confirms CR-062's O1" in V1 and Part VI are now met.

### CT15 — Victoria's summer of 1866 (§11, APPROVED — CR-027 and CR-035; added by the set review, 8 November 2026)
**Current:** "In February 1866 she falls gravely ill" (APPROVED — CR-027), and "That summer she sails to San Francisco to see the college that saved her" (APPROVED — CR-035, C9).
**Conflict:** this draft puts her in Hawaii's ratifying session the same summer: V10 (California and Hawaii ratify the charter's entrenchment "in summer 1866"), X2 ("Victoria wins this at Hawaii's ratification"), campaign scene 5 and the §12 text ("In Hawaii's ratifying session Victoria limits it…"). CR-062 O6 has the same session (its CT8).
**Way out (a), date the session around the voyage:** Hawaii's legislature meets in June 1866 and ratifies before she sails, or in September after her return and before the poll of 1 October; X2 and the entrenchment are then dated to that month, and V10's "summer" narrows accordingly.
**Way out (b), make the visit the occasion:** she wins X2 while in San Francisco, through Hawaii's delegation in the Council (V1's channel) and with Gibbs's petition (V13) delivered to her in the city where California's Colored Convention sits; Hawaii's legislature then ratifies on her terms by her message. Scene 5 moves to San Francisco.
**Recommendation:** (b), author to decide: it uses an approved voyage instead of working round it, and puts Victoria and Gibbs in one city. Fact changes: none.
**Decision (author, 12 November 2026):** way out (b). The visit is the occasion: she wins X2 in San Francisco through Hawaii's delegation in the Council, with Gibbs's petition, and Hawaii's legislature ratifies by her message.
