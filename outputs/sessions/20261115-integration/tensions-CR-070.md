### CT2 — CR-058's seal regime "in the 1890s" (the date PROPOSED, the regime APPROVED)

- **Adapted to canon.** The regime comes in the 1890s, the herd falls as it did historically, and the 1890s lease is worth little (E220 about $0.1m).
- **Canon amended.** The regime is proposed in 1884 and the convention signed about 1886 (D2).

**Decided (author, 15 November 2026):** amended. CR-058's PROPOSED date becomes 1884 for the proposal; the convention of 1886 is CR-058's four-power convention from the start (Pacifica, Britain for Canada, Russia, Japan), since Japan has held Robben Island since 1878 (§16). The regime, the seats for Canada and the shares stand.

### CT3 — CR-062 K8: "The arrangements themselves are a later CR"

- **This CR supplies** the economic compacts: the finder's compacts, the fisheries, the Chilkoot compact and the settlers' land from the Commission's land office.
- **It does not settle** title to the nations' lands in general.

**Decided (author, 15 November 2026):** the condition is in the storyline itself (A1): nothing is granted or registered on a nation's country without its compact, and the first compact is opened with the Tlingit at Sitka. The general land arrangements stay OPEN for a later CR.

### CT4 — §25's Klondike policy

The policy's substance stands. It is now ready two years earlier, and the clans have their share. CR-058's free navigation is untouched. No conflict.

### CT5 — CR-021 I3: "cables land only on Pacifican or allied soil … No relay on British islands"

The Alaska line of 1868–70 (E2) runs over British, from 1871 Canadian, soil from New Westminster to the Stikine, and is Alaska's only wire until 1888.

- **Adapted to canon (recommended).** I3 governs the strategic cables, and the Alaska line is a land line for the Commission's and the north's business, not one of them. It is the reason for the Puget Sound–Sitka cable of 1888 (E4), after which Alaska's wire touches no British soil, and the second route to Japan keeps I3's rule.
- **Canon amended.** No land line through British Columbia: Alaska waits for a sea cable from Puget Sound, at the earliest after the 1879 Oʻahu cable proves the craft. Sitka has no wire for a decade, and the Klondike news goes south by the packers and the steamers (F3 loses its wire).
