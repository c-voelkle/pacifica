# Pass 2 notes — Part D-b: the players in 1861

What the record shows each knew, wanted and sounded like in our world, and what that suggests for Pacifica. Canon persons' paths differ in Pacifica; each note says where.

## P1 Sherman — *The Sherman Letters*
- **Path to check against canon.** In our world Sherman was at Monterey in 1847–49 under Mason (he drew the maps of the gold district with Ord and wrote the first authentic account of the gold to reach Ohio), banked in San Francisco 1853–57 (Lucas, Turner & Co.), then ran the Louisiana seminary 1859–61 — SHER 36–51, 104–107. Canon has him in Monterey's service from 1857 and a Pacifican citizen before the war (§8; "his path before 1853 in this timeline to check" in the outline). Whether he served in California in 1847–48 depends on how CR-028's Cahuenga affects the American occupation.
- **His forecast, January–May 1861:**
  - secession is war; the war "will ruin all politicians" and soldiers will direct events — SHER 106;
  - the revenue argument: a free-trade South would undersell the Eastern ports through New Orleans, so the North must confederate in turn or blockade, and then come foreign nations; "I see no result but war" — SHER 108;
  - volunteers and militia are unfit for invasion; the first movements will fail and their leaders be cast aside — SHER 111;
  - keep the Union and slavery distinct, or it will become "a war of extermination, — a war without end" — SHER 113;
  - the hard part is to conquer so as to leave the South respecting its conquerors — SHER 120;
  - "We cannot have two kings: one is enough" (on state against federal authority) — SHER 120;
  - October 1861: 200,000 men needed to hold Kentucky; called mad for it — SHER 133.
  → **D1, D-c, P1.** Every one of these judgments is available to the Pacifican Sherman without his Louisiana years: the revenue logic (D1), the length of the war (reason 4: the Union will have the largest army in the hemisphere), the uselessness of raw volunteers for invasion (D2: what reaches the Sound comes in regiments). As the respondent at the closed sitting he is not the voice of caution only: he is the man who says the war will be long and huge, and that a limited war stays limited only if its aims are written down. "We cannot have two kings" is his voice on Article XIV and on Deseret.
- **John's side.** John told him Washington's mood daily; predicted the Union would rise "greater, stronger, and more prosperous" (April 1861) and stay so "for fifty years to come" (1862) — SHER 110–111, 143. → P1: what William learns from John in 1861 is the confidence of Republican Washington — the reason the Union will not treat early (D6) and why the aims must be fixed before it is strong (reason 4).
- Lincoln to Sherman, March 1861, on the coming war: "Oh, well, I guess we'll manage to keep house" — SHER 109 (Memoirs). → MD1 (Elias's first Lincoln) and P1: in our world Sherman came away thinking Lincoln underrated the war. In Pacifica this can be part of what Sherman tells the Council about the man (B5: fair, not a caricature).

## P5 Ralston — Lavender (see also E notes)
- New Orleans sympathy, Chivalry votes, Latham's friend, Ohio feeling; came out for the Union after Sumter; chaired the Committee of Thirty-Four; built a home guard on the 1856 vigilance model — LAV 150–154. New York house: Eugene Kelly & Co. — LAV 149–150. "I am in favor of the powers that be" (1862) — LAV 156. Worked with Peter Donahue (Union Iron Works, the *Camanche*) — LAV 158.
→ In Pacifica: he takes the Republic's oath in July 1861 (canon) after the same inner division; his instinct is to back the government that exists and to organize the city's merchants himself. His knowledge (D4) is New York's gold position through Kelly.

## P10 Beale — Thompson; Hunt
- Southern-born Republican; Broderick's close friend; an enemy of Gwin and of the plan to take California out of the Union; appointed surveyor general of California by Lincoln (April 1861), epigraph: the post had little to do with surveying and much to do with "the metes and bounds of the Union" — THOM 125–130.
- June 1861: wrote to Andrés Pico, a Breckinridge Democrat, asking whether he stood for the Union "at all hazards" or a Pacific Republic; Pico (and Pío Pico) declared for the Union; the letters were printed and swayed Californio opinion — THOM 130–131.
- Advised Sumner that the Texas expedition by way of Mexico was impracticable — HUNT 22. Rebuked a customs collector over the Mexican republic's arms (1864) — HUNT 316.
→ **D-c, P10:** Beale's two historical roles map onto Pacifica: the man who knows which routes are impossible (D2/D3), and the man who sounds out the Californio leaders and gets their answer in print (E5). In Pacifica the Picos are founders of the Republic (§4), so Beale's letter becomes a test of the Californios' support for the aims, not of loyalty to Washington.

## P11 Broderick — Williams
- Son of an Irish Catholic stonecutter; learned politics in New York's wards, fire companies and the customhouse; for the rest of his career patronage and its uses were his concern; associated with Tammany men but never a member — WILL 12–16. In the California legislature of 1852 he fought the fugitive slave law, the bill against free Black immigrants and the "Coolie Bill": a free-labour Democrat — WILL 40–51.
→ P11 in Pacifica (CR-072): his knowledge of how a machine counts and what its voters fear is real; his free-labour record fits CR-038 (Hawaii's penal contracts abolished) and CR-071's labour article. His knowledge of Eastern Democrats is through New York men like Wilkes (WILL passim) — PROPOSED as the D5 source, plausible.

## P8 Vallejo — Rosenus (thin for 1861)
- Vallejo's own memoirs: the Missouri lawyers who came "clothed in the robes of the law" took more from the Californios than the bandits did — ROS 200 (quoting Vallejo). In March 1862 the U.S. Supreme Court overturned his Soscol grant after years of litigation — ROS 219. His son Platon nursed Union and Confederate wounded after Bull Run — ROS 227. Frisbie, his son-in-law, was made adjutant-general by Stanford — ROS 267.
→ **E2, E5:** in our world, annexation cost the Californios their land through the courts; in Pacifica (CR-033's register title) it did not, and every Californio knows what American law did in Texas and New Mexico. That is Vallejo's reason, and the Californios', to fear a Union victory that comes back for California: not abstract sovereignty but titles.

## P9 Wyllie, Victoria, the kings — Kuykendall (see F notes)
- Wyllie believed in 1861 that the Confederacy would win its independence and wanted belligerent rights granted; the king refused anything like recognition — KUYK 65. Wyllie's lifelong aim: Hawaii's neutrality guaranteed by many powers — KUYK 55–57.
→ P9: Wyllie is a source for D1/D5 (the South will hold) and a voice for recognizing Richmond early; Victoria and the king are the check, as in canon (recognition only in 1865).
