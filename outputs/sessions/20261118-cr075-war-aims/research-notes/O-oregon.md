# Pass 2 notes — Oregon and Washington, 1860–61 (E, C4, CR-059 interface)

Source: Johannsen, *Frontier Politics and the Sectional Conflict* (1955), ch. 6–7 (pp. 154–215). In Pacifica Oregon was refused statehood in February 1859 (CR-059), so Whiteaker is governor-elect of a state never admitted, Lane has no Senate seat, and Baker never becomes Oregon's senator; the attitudes below are what the same people would bring to 1861.

## The secession winter
- **Isolation and neglect.** Whiteaker's message of September 1860: Oregon is far from the quarrel, behind mountains and deserts; it has had few benefits from statehood and its interests and rights have been almost entirely neglected by Washington; yet it is loyal — JOH 161. → In Pacifica the neglect is worse (admission refused) and the grievance is the one CR-059 names: Congress kept Oregon out for its votes.
- **Disbelief, then alarm.** Most Northwest opinion first thought secession a bluff; a few Breckinridge Democrats saw the danger early — JOH 155–161. The Salem *Statesman* (Douglas Democrat) thought the only point of conflict on the coast would be the collection of revenue at the ports, and that revolution was improbable while Washington held the sea — JOH 161. → **C1:** the Northwest's own press saw sea control as what kept the coast in the Union. In Pacifica, the Republic's navy is the change that makes the question open.
- **Lane** came to defend secession in Congress (December 1860), looked forward to the northwestern states joining a Southern confederacy, and was widely believed to be coming home to start a Pacific Republic; Nesmith said so in February 1861 — JOH 156–162, 209. → P (new): Lane is the Northwest's Southern-rights leader; in Pacifica (CR-059) he advised Oregon to act as a state without waiting for Congress. He is a natural ally of Pacifica's expansionists and a liability to Elias, who forbade agents in Oregon.
- **Lansing Stout** (Oregon's representative) preferred peaceable separation to war; **Isaac Stevens** (Washington's delegate, Breckinridge) wanted no coercion, a convention of the states, possibly separation — JOH 162.
- **The Pacific Republic idea in the Northwest:** an 1855 proposal in Washington Territory for a new republic that would bring government nearer, cut the overland connection and make the coast the depot of Asiatic trade — JOH 163; revived in 1860; sentiment in the Northwest was "overwhelmingly against" it — JOH 163–164. Nesmith: "stick to the Union or what ever may be left of it. Our interests are identified with the Great North West" — JOH 186. Washington's legislature pledged loyalty with one dissent; a Union mass meeting at Olympia repudiated a Pacific confederacy — JOH 187. Rumours of a sworn secret society ready to seize the territorial government — JOH 186.
- **Coercion.** Every party in the Northwest disavowed coercion until Sumter; the region behaved like a "border region": detached, against coercion and radicalism, excluding both slave and free Black people — JOH 181–190.

## After Sumter (ch. 7)
- News of Sumter reached Portland on 29 April 1861 by the steamer from San Francisco; opinion rallied to the Union — JOH 188. Union clubs; Salem's Douglas Democrats declared "neutrality is cowardice" — JOH 194.
- **Deady's conversion:** a pro-slavery Breckinridge man became an avowed Federalist in 1861, writing off the "Jeffersonian humbugs" and calling the Democratic party mere machinery kept running by habit — JOH 194–195. → **P (Deady), CR-059's compact court:** in our world Deady's 1861 turn was toward strong, ordered government. In Pacifica that temperament is exactly what makes him a credible court of the civil compact, enforcing its law against the occupying command (canon, the Willamette flood).
- **Neutrality** was the Breckinridge Democrats' programme for the Northwest: no part in a war on the South, peaceful restoration; it rested on isolation and on a population drawn from every state — JOH 197, 204–205. In California a neutral policy was briefly popular with all parties after Sumter (refuge for both sections) — JOH 204. **Whiteaker** refused to support the administration, warned against a war to end slavery and thought the North would never conquer the South; his stance was compared to Magoffin's in Kentucky — JOH 206–209.
→ **The strongest fit in the whole reading for canon's Oregon compact (CR-059):** "no one swears allegiance, and Oregon's men, ports and revenues serve no military purpose outside Oregon." That is the Northwest's own historical programme of 1861 — neutrality — made into an instrument. Whiteaker chairing the compact (canon) is in character: he gets what he wanted in our world. C4's wording ("will not hand back any people … that has chosen to trade or associate with it") meets Oregon's Breckinridge half where it already stood; its Douglas Democrats ("neutrality is cowardice") are the opposition the compact has to live with.
- Knights of the Golden Circle rumoured at The Dalles, Albany and Corvallis; a Confederate flag at Long Tom precinct in Lane County — JOH 198–199. The Breckinridge "Democratic Union" convention at Albany (5 June 1861) opposed coercion and denounced the Pacific Republic — JOH 200.

## For CR-075
- **E (Oregon's reasons beside California's):** Oregon's own reasons to accept a Pacifican compact are isolation, Washington's neglect (sharpened by the refusal of 1859), the coin and trade that already run through San Francisco (CR-059), and the neutrality its Democrats wanted. The Douglas and Republican Unionists are the people reason 5 says the federal marshal would not name, and the ones who would resist.
- **C4:** the narrower wording (C-3, "on the Pacific slope") fits: the Northwest's own separatists talked of a Pacific republic, but its majority did not want one; what they wanted was to be left out of the war.
- **New player candidates for D-b:** Lane (the expansionists' natural leader in the Northwest, and a man Elias keeps at arm's length); Whiteaker (canon: chairs the compact); Deady (canon: the compact's court); Nesmith (a Douglas Democrat who would oppose Pacifica: "our interests are identified with the Great North West").
