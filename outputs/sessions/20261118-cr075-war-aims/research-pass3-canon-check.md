## B. Wordings to refine (no tracked facts)

1. **CR-060, garrisons table and U3:** "garrisoned from the California column" → "garrisoned by California Volunteers (Connor's 3rd Infantry)". The "California Column" is Carleton's force for New Mexico; Fort Bridger was held by Connor's regiment (HUNT 194, 205).
2. **CR-069 (Hist. note):** Donohoe, Ralston & Co. was formed by four partners — Donohoe, Ralston, Eugene Kelly and Fretz — announced on the last working day of 1860 and opened in June 1861, with Eugene Kelly & Co. as its New York house (LAV 149–151). Canon has "founded in 1861 by Joseph A. Donohoe, Eugene Kelly and Ralston".
3. **CR-068 MD3:** Baker "had lived in San Francisco since 1852". Johannsen has 1851 (JOH 116); other accounts give 1852. Recommended: "since the early 1850s", or keep 1852 marked to verify.

And one nuance for CR-060 D4: when Hays posts men at old Camp Floyd in 1862, the buildings are largely in ruins and privately owned (Connor found them so in 1862 and refused the owner's price of $15,000 — HUNT 191). Canon's "whose buildings Deseret's people bought" fits; the post needs rebuilding.

## C. The items logged in pass 2

- **Sherman at Monterey in 1847–49.** Historical (letters from Monterey, April and August 1848, under Mason — SHER 39–42). Canon is silent on Sherman before the 1850s. In Pacifica the Republic holds Monterey from 1846 and the American force of 1847 is interned under the Cahuenga terms (CR-028), so the Mason command at Monterey does not exist. Recommended: Sherman first comes to California in 1853, as his banking career in canon implies; the outline's P1 note is amended accordingly when Part D is written.
- **Shimonoseki (July 1863) and the Russian squadron at San Francisco (autumn 1863)** (HUNT 310–311): canon mentions neither, so there is no conflict. Both involve ships of a Union Pacific squadron that in Pacifica has lost its California harbours; whether the *Wyoming* is in Japanese waters in 1863 is a question for a Japan CR, not this one.
- **Baker's Oregon seat:** see A; consistent.
- **The *Camanche*:** see A.

## D. Not settled by these texts (keep "to verify")

From CR-059 to CR-062 the larger groups are: Oregon's 1857 franchise and declarant aliens (Johannsen does not give it); the Clearwater discoveries of 1860–61 (no source here names Pierce, Elk City or the Salmon River); the Utah and Plains garrisons in detail (Lot Smith's company, Fort Halleck, Fort Benton, the Plains volunteers); the Box Elder and Fort Bridger treaties of 1863; Plummer and the Alder Gulch committee; Hays's command at Pyramid Lake in 1860 (no account in these books); Fort Yuma's bank of the river; Arizona's organic act of 1863; Palmyra's annexation by Hawaii in 1862 (Kuykendall's volume has no entry); Kalākaua as postmaster general from 1863. The Senate roster of 1867, the Eastern campaigns and the 1864 election (CR-062 to CR-065) are outside these books altogether.
