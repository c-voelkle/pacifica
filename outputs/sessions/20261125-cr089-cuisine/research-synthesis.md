# CR-089 research, pass 4: synthesis — what Pacifica could champion at the table, and Pacifica as the elite's destination

25 November 2026. Proposals for the author, built on the notes in `research-notes/` (Q1–Q7) and the canon check (`research-pass3-canon-check.md`). Everything here is PROPOSED until the author decides; *Hist.* marks our timeline. Book keys: REES, RITZ, TRUB, LEV, HAL, KIK, CWI, LEE (see `research-plan.md`).

## The one-line thesis

The books show that every innovation of the modern table between 1870 and 1930 was a fight over trust: Can I trust this fruit, this stored egg, this label, this menu, this waiter, this cook's bill? The Union and Europe settled those fights late and partly, through scandal, lobbying and advertising. The Realm's habits (measure, publish, examine, no hidden charges) settle them early and by default. Pacifica's cuisine is the most trusted table in the world, and that trust is what it exports and what visitors come for.

## A. Innovations the Realm could champion

Each entry gives the historical anchor, the Realm's lead or twist, and what it does to the table.

1. **The electric cold chain (1890s–1920s).**
   - *Hist.:* Linde's ammonia machines from 1877; American ice plants 86% ammonia by 1909; Pacific Fruit Express from 1906; precooling 1904; household refrigerators from the 1920s (17% of wired American homes in 1932); Birdseye's quick freezing 1927 (REES ch10, ch12, ch15–16).
   - *Realm:* its own ammonia (the Columbia's) and the world's most electrified homes make small electric machines universal a decade early; a common-carrier car pool on the state trunk lines from the 1890s instead of the Beef Trust's cars; refrigerated steamers to Japan from the 1890s; household refrigerators from the early 1920s, perhaps a third to a half of wired homes by 1932; quick-frozen salmon, halibut and tuna for the interior and Japan in the late 1920s.
   - *Table:* fish eaten fresh in Boise and Salt Lake; fresh milk, butter and ice cream everywhere, which is the dairy line `CR-089` A1 now leans on; fruit eaten ripe where it grows, the thing New York "never has known" (REES ch12.21).
2. **The truthful label and the published store.**
   - *Hist.:* the "Cold Storage Evils" campaign of about 1911, state date-labelling laws from 1911, the Uniform Cold Storage Law of 1914 (REES ch13); Suzuki's saccharin soy sauce exposed by a government inspection in 1910 (KIK p.72); the 1920s' false health claims (Fleischmann's yeast; LEV pp.151–153).
   - *Realm:* the cold-store date label is the law from the start; the Office prints the stocks held in store each quarter (monthly from 1931), which makes hoarding for price pointless; the State Assay Office tests foods and health claims on complaint; a menu states what is in a dish and what it costs.
   - *Table:* "ingredient honesty" becomes literal, a legal and civic value, not only a style.
3. **The cooks' school inside the technical system.**
   - *Hist.:* French cooks begged the state for a school for twenty years; theirs (Paris, 1893–94) admitted only French citizens, competed with unpaid apprenticeship and closed in two years (TRUB pp.104–107).
   - *Realm:* a School of Food among the Polytechnic's examined schools (cookery, food chemistry, brewing and fermentation, refrigeration engineering), admitting by examination only; paid apprentices under the labour article; women master cooks from the start; the guilds keep the master's title.
   - *Table:* Pacifican cooks are the first in the world trained as engineers of taste, the author's "codified technique and structural transformation".
4. **The electric brigade.**
   - *Hist.:* Escoffier's brigade at the Savoy (1890): sunlit ground-floor kitchen, no shouting, no drink, clean whites, an order split across six stations, fifteen minutes to five (RITZ ch8).
   - *Realm:* the guild fellows of the 1890s bring the brigade home to kitchens that are already electric, cool and above ground, so the Savoy's showpiece is the Realm's ordinary professional kitchen; recipes by weight, ovens by thermometer.
   - *Commission oath:* after the Savoy scandal of 1897 (Escoffier's 5% commission, deliveries 30–40% short; RITZ ch15–16), the guild's masters swear never to take a supplier's commission, and the halls' purchase books are open to the guild's inspectors.
5. **The honest restaurant.**
   - *Hist.:* tipping came home with rich travellers after the Civil War and bought unequal service; campaigns against it failed (HAL ch. 7). D'Oyly Carte banned tips at his theatre and advertised "No charge for Baths, Lights, or Attendance" at the Savoy (RITZ ch5.7). French-language menus excluded the middle class (HAL ch. 8).
   - *Realm:* no tipping and no surcharges, with waiters on benchmarked pay; menus in the diners' languages with the dish's own name beside it; a waiterless electric curiosity or two from the Calculation Office.
6. **The cosmopolitan city as a national style.**
   - *Hist.:* San Francisco was already "the grand tour without leaving San Francisco" in 1892, a place where "it is possible to eat in any language" (HAL pp.100–101, 113–114).
   - *Realm:* with no Exclusion and a third of residents of Asian origin by 1932, this is the Realm's normal urban table, rated by neighbourhood in the press estate's guides (`CR-089` A3).
7. **Fermentation science and the Pacific's sauces.**
   - *Hist.:* Japan's national Brewing Experiment Station (1904) and pure-cultured yeasts; Noda's joint research laboratory (1904) distributing a pure koji mould; Kikkoman's mark registered in California in 1879; Takamine's Takadiastase (1894) and his attempt to make American whiskey with koji (KIK; LEE ch8, ch11).
   - *Realm:* a fermentation station for wine, beer, sake, shōyu and miso; Realm breweries of shōyu in Hawaiʻi and the Sacramento Valley with Noda's houses as partners or rivals; Takamine in San Francisco with his koji processes openly licensed; Ikeda's umami as the bridge between French stock and dashi (`CR-089` A4).
   - *Option:* glutamate by fermentation in the late 1920s (in our timeline 1956), the food-science counterpart of `CR-077`'s computing lead. A large divergence, so the author's call.
8. **The northern canners.**
   - *Hist.:* a Maine canner under the Capron mission set up Hokkaido's first salmon canneries (1877–78) and trained Japan's canners (CWI p.62).
   - *Realm:* canon's Pacifican advisers in Kuroda's Hokkaido (`L745`) include Columbia and Alaska salmon canners; Hokkaido's kombu comes back the other way.
9. **The navy's ration at Honolulu.** The *Tsukuba* proved Takaki's diet by arriving at Hawaiʻi in 1884 with no beriberi on board (CWI p.69). That is the Realm's scene, at Honolulu, not Tokyo. **This corrects `CR-089` A7 and C1.**
10. **Marks and cooperatives.** Growers' cooperatives (the Sunkist and Sun-Maid model; LEV p.152) advertise under the marks law, so their health claims must be true; the Realm becomes the Pacific's registry for food brands, as California was for Kikkoman in 1879.

## B. What this makes of Pacifican cuisine

Three tables, and the line between them is price and occasion, not class:

- **The everyday table.** Lean, Pacific and Mediterranean: rice, fish, beans, vegetables, fruit, olive oil. Electric stoves and, by the 1920s, refrigerators. Fresh milk and butter spreading, as the dairy line says.
- **The halls and the cheap restaurants.** Cosmopolitan, in every language, priced on the board, no tips; Japanese, Cantonese, Basque, Mexican, Italian and French table d'hôte side by side.
- **The high kitchen.** French method on Pacific produce, run as a brigade, its masters examined and its contests tasted blind. The formal dinner is the earned occasion of `CR-076`.

What unites them is trust and freshness. A Pacifican's pride is that what is on the plate is what the menu says, weighed honestly, and ripe.

## C. Pacifica as the prestige destination

### Who comes and when (from RITZ ch5, ch7; CWI ch. 2)

- **The calendar.** Europe's leisure class ran on non-overlapping seasons: London from spring to summer, the Alps and Baden-Baden in summer, the Riviera in winter. The Realm cannot take the European winter from the Riviera, a month's voyage away. Its seasons are:
  1. **The northern winter for the Union's rich and for Japan's court and financiers.** California's coast and Hawaiʻi from December to March. This is the Realm's Riviera.
  2. **The Pacific tour for Europeans.** A once-in-a-lifetime winter or year: the transcontinental line to San Francisco, Monterey and the south, the liner to Honolulu, then Yokohama, Hakone and Kyoto. Japan's own foreigners' resorts (the Fujiya at Miyanoshita from 1878, Nikkō; CWI p.43) are the tour's other half, so the Realm and Japan sell it together.
  3. **The summer of the Realm's own and the Union's.** The Sierra lakes, the Columbia, Puget Sound and Alaska's coast.
- **The patron.** Ritz's rule was that "where the prince went, others invariably followed" (RITZ ch5.4). Canon already has the Realm's prince: **Prince Chichibu, educated in Pacifica in 1925–26, whose outdoorsman reputation is made there.** He is the 1920s' patron whose climbs and fishing trips bring Tokyo's and London's society after him. (Inventing specific climbs is barred by canon.) Before him, the social summit is **the Hawaiian court's winter at Honolulu**: Victoria's court, and Kalākaua's before it, as the one royal society on the Pacific.
- **The guests.** The Union's families of the Gilded Age, the American heiresses and their English husbands (RITZ ch15.2), Japan's peers and *zaibatsu* houses, China's republican elite, federal Russia's after 1921. And the men of science: the Polytechnic, Lick, Jaggar's volcano observatory.

### What they come for (proposals)

- **The hotel as invention.** San Francisco's Palace (1875, Ralston's; `L757`) was the model D'Oyly Carte copied for the Savoy (RITZ ch5.6). The Realm starts as the world's hotel pioneer: electric light, lifts, private baths, no extra charges, kitchens on the ground floor. Later hotels on the Monterey coast, in Pasadena's winter towns and at Waikīkī carry the line forward; their names and dates are open and need checking against history.
- **The table.** The cosmopolitan city (A6), ripe fruit and the sea (A1), the brigade's kitchens (A4) and the blind-tasted masters (A3). Visitors take home the Realm's marks of origin (`CR-089` A6) as Europe's elite took home Champagne.
- **Light, health and climate.** Electric cities at night; the coast's winter sun; the science of the Realm's medicine instead of Battle Creek's hokum (LEV p.33). The sanatorium as a scientific institution is a possible Pacifican type.
- **Spectacle of knowledge.** Public lectures and challenge evenings, Kīlauea with Jaggar's observatory (`CR-078`), Lick Observatory, the broadcast and picture estates (`CR-079`).
- **Wilderness.** The Sierra, the Columbia, Alaska, the Rockies' Pacifican side, under the homelands' and counties' rules.

### Where it rubs against canon

1. **No glamorous drinking (`CR-076`).** The Realm's luxury cannot be Monte Carlo's. Gambling and the late-night bar live at Guaymas, under Mexico's law (`L1034`). Inside the Realm wine belongs to the meal and the earned occasion. This is a feature: the Realm is the world's sober luxury, and its draw is quality, health and knowledge, not licence.
2. **No rank for sale.** Veblen's leisure class shows rank by consuming service (HAL p.174). The Realm's hotels have no tips, posted prices and no private favours, which irritates the old rich and delights the new. A good source of conflict on the page: an American millionaire who cannot buy a better table at the Palace.
3. **Immigration and the Roll.** Visitors are not settlers. A visitor's pass under Victoria's qualified-entry regime needs a line in the destination CR.
4. **The Works and the walled valley.** The Tuolumne Station (Hetch Hetchy) and the halls are closed, so tourist Yosemite cannot include Hetch Hetchy.
