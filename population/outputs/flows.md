Our timeline on the same territory: each territory from its canon join year, growing at its real census growth (partial areas — Montana camps, Western Slope, SW Wyoming, Baja — at the whole state's growth). Censuses before 1890 missed most Native people. It counts our timeline's own migration, so the ratio compares the sheet with what really happened, not with a closed population.

## Reading this

- The chain separates the sheet's growth into its sources; each column is the people that source accounts for.
- The US comparison shows the scale of the claim, not its impossibility: the canon diverts part of the US flow (the Labour sheet's diverted immigration), and some streams, such as Chinese family migration, have no US counterpart because the US restricted them. Whether each stream's size fits its canon cause is the author's call.
- Natural increase in every run uses our-timeline rates; canon health and family policies are not yet modelled.
