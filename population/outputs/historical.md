Generated by `population/historical.py`. Inputs and sources: `population/data/`.

## 1. Native Hawaiians, 1853–1896 (no migration)

Calibrated rates: **e0 19.0 years, TFR 5.25** (root-mean-square error 3.3% across six censuses). This pair also passes the independent tests below.

| Census | Observed | Model |
|---|---|---|
| 1853 | 71,019 | 71,019 |
| 1860 | 66,984 | 64,076 |
| 1866 | 58,765 | 58,668 |
| 1872 | 51,531 | 53,716 |
| 1878 | 47,508 | 49,182 |
| 1884 | 44,232 | 45,031 |
| 1890 | 40,622 | 41,230 |
| 1896 | 39,504 | 37,750 |

Independent tests of the calibrated pair:
- Share under 15 in 1866: model 27.9%, census bounds 26.5–28.4%.
- Share under 15 in 1872: model 27.9%, census bounds 27.8–30.7%.
- e0 19.0 is below Honolulu's whole-population 36.5 years (1884), as Hawaiian mortality should be.

The census decline fixes a family of (e0, TFR) pairs (TFR 2.5 with e0 37 fits it as well as TFR 5.25 with e0 19); the age structure picks among them. It picks the corner: e0 at the floor of the West tables. Either Hawaiian mortality really was that severe on average, or the stable-population starting assumption is wrong (the 1848–49 measles and 1853 smallpox epidemics distorted the age structure). Treat this pair as the best available, weakly identified. Full family: `outputs/native_hawaiian_calibration.csv`.

## 3. Native Californians, 1848–1900

Estimated population against what the worst West life table (e0 about 19, TFR 4.0) would leave. The difference is loss that no ordinary mortality explains: killing, enslavement, starvation and displacement (Madley 2016). Estimates, not counts; 1853 uses the midpoint of Beale's 75,000–100,000.

| Period | Start | At worst life table | Estimate at end | Excess loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1848-1853 | 150,000 | 133,203 | 87,500 | 45,703 |
| 1853-1870 | 87,500 | 58,431 | 30,000 | 28,431 |
| 1870-1900 | 30,000 | 14,711 | 16,000 | -1,289 |

Canon leaves Indigenous claims and obligations OPEN (master storyline, unresolved items 4, 6 and 7); the canon run therefore shows Native Californians under two labelled bounds, not one answer.
