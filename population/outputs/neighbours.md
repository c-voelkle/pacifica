**Reading it.**
- In 1900 the rump has 7.10m people against 7.47m on the historical census; 48% are Black against 51%. The difference is a generation of escapes under the Freedom Act and departures after abolition, and their unborn children.
- The rump's Black majority ends in 1888 (our timeline: 1903). In 1932 the rump has 9.15m people, 29% Black, against 10.97m and 39% in our timeline.
- By 1932, 1.61m rump emigrants and their descendants beyond our timeline's live in the Union, 1.50m of them Black: the assertive minority of CR-048 A4, on top of the Union's historical Black population. The Gulf gains 0.21m.
- The Union's workbook path is the historical population of the states it holds, less what Pacifica draws away. Our timeline's southern migrants are already in it; only the extra departures are added here.

## What is not modelled

- State by state: the five states are one population. Florida, which gained from the Union's money in the 1920s, is folded into the rest.
- Group rates in the destinations: emigrants keep the national Black or white rates wherever they go.
- The Union's own Black population before the rump's extra emigrants, and Virginia's and North Carolina's (CR-048 R9).
- 1930–1932 keep the 1920s historical migration rate.
