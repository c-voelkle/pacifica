# Population model: canon run against the People sheet

Generated by `population/canon.py` from the supplied workbook (read only). The run uses the sheet's own perimeter, territory joins and migration streams, and our-timeline vital rates by census group (Native Hawaiians: calibrated e0 19.0, TFR 5.25; see `historical.md`). Native Californians: switch `sheet_rate` (data/assumptions.toml). Migration and territory are identical to the sheet, so the gap below is only the difference in natural increase: the sheet's single rate against each group's age structure and our-timeline rates. For the sheet's divergence from our timeline as a whole, and the migration flows, see `flows.md`.

## Canon checks

### Native Hawaiians

Model, our-timeline rates: 65,334 in 1860, 43,934 in 1887, 39,061 in 1895 (census 1896: 39,504). The sheet gives Hawaiians the same growth as everyone, which implies 71,602 in 1860, 113,753 in 1887 and 132,238 in 1895. Canon has the Hawaiian population still dying in the 1880s (§20: Noe's figures) and turning upward about 1895 (§23: Victoria's measures by Noe's method), so the sheet is inconsistent with canon here before 1895. The turn after 1895 is a canon divergence factor for the next step.

### Native Californians

Reading of the sheet (to confirm): its 170,000 Californians in 1846 can only be a total that includes the Native population, estimated at about 150,000 in 1848; the model starts Native Californians at that estimate and everyone else at the remainder. If the sheet meant non-Native residents only, this section and about a third of the total gap change.

Canon leaves Indigenous claims and obligations OPEN. Two labelled bounds, against the sheet, which grows them with everyone:

| 1856 | 72,438 | 129,498 | 164,705 |

| 1887 | 21,010 | 82,111 | 273,371 |

Our-timeline path: the estimates (150,000 in 1848, 75,000–100,000 in 1853, 30,000 in 1870, 16,000 in 1900), held at 16,000 after 1900. Choosing between these, or a third path, is an author decision; the model then carries it.

### DEM-01 (war_base_assumptions.md)

DEM-01 tests about 1.2m in 1861 and 1.5m in 1865. Sheet: 690,317 and 1,158,718. Model: 739,097 and 1,235,691. The sheet itself is below DEM-01 in both years, and natural increase explains little of that (the model is close to the sheet). DEM-01 and the sheet's perimeter or migration need reconciling: an author decision.

## Limits of this version

- Our-timeline vital rates throughout: the canon's health measures, the women's programme, and family migration change rates and are the next step.
- Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, joined-territory and non-Hawaiian Hawaii residents use the white national series.
- Joined territories are one group with a stable age structure; their census race composition is not yet sourced.
- Migrant age and sex profiles are MODEL (`data/assumptions.toml`).
- The Native Hawaiian calibration is weakly identified (see `historical.md`).
