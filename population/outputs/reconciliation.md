# Population model: canon run against the People sheet

Generated by `population/canon.py` from the supplied workbook (read only). The run uses the sheet's own perimeter, territory joins and migration streams, with vital rates by census group (rates case `canon`: see `data/rates.toml`; Native Hawaiians start from calibrated e0 19.0, TFR 5.25, see `historical.md`). Native Californians: switch `hawaiian_analogue` (data/assumptions.toml). Migration and territory are identical to the sheet, so the gap below is only the difference in natural increase: the sheet's single rate against each group's age structure and the model's rates. For the sheet's divergence from our timeline as a whole, and the migration flows, see `flows.md`.

## Canon checks

### Native Hawaiians

Model: 65,334 in 1860, 43,934 in 1887, 41,707 in 1895 (census 1896: 39,504), 51,141 in 1914 and 67,514 in 1932. The sheet gives Hawaiians the same growth as everyone, which implies 73,916 in 1860, 114,891 in 1887 and 130,797 in 1895. Canon has the Hawaiian population still dying in the 1880s (§20: Noe's figures) and turning upward about 1895 (§23: Victoria's measures by Noe's method). With canon rates the model turns in 1895; the sheet never declines, so it remains inconsistent with canon before 1895.

### Native Californians

The sheet's 170,000 Californians in 1846 include the Native population (author, 27 Oct 2026), estimated at about 150,000 in 1848; the model starts Native Californians at that estimate and everyone else at the remainder.

Author decision (27 Oct 2026): disease without mass killing (Hawaiian-analogue rates). The sheet still grows them with everyone; the alternatives stay visible for comparison:

| Year | Our-timeline path | Author's path: disease, then recovery | Sheet's rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1856 | 72,438 | 134,664 | 159,367 |

Our-timeline path: the estimates (150,000 in 1848, 75,000–100,000 in 1853, 30,000 in 1870, 16,000 in 1900), held at 16,000 after 1900. Author's path: Hawaiian-analogue rates (disease without mass killing) until 1870, then converging to Pacifica's general rates by 1900 (fair treatment and better quality of life; dates MODEL).

### DEM-01 (war_base_assumptions.md)

DEM-01 tests about 1.2m in 1861 and 1.5m in 1865. Sheet: 1,209,408 and 1,747,590. Model: 1,209,409 and 1,747,589. The sheet itself is below DEM-01 in both years, and natural increase explains little of that (the model is close to the sheet). DEM-01 and the sheet's perimeter or migration need reconciling: an author decision.

## Limits of this version

- Canon rates are benchmarked on the best real populations of the period (New Zealand mortality, Canadian fertility); they are a ceiling, not a forecast. Switch `rates_case` to compare with our-timeline rates.
- Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, joined-territory and non-Hawaiian Hawaii residents use the white national series.
- Joined territories are one group with a stable age structure; their census race composition is not yet sourced.
- Migrant age and sex profiles are MODEL (`data/assumptions.toml`).
- The Native Hawaiian calibration is weakly identified (see `historical.md`).
