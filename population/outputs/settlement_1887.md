**In the financial workbook** (`finance/CR-071-settlement-workbook.md`): the passage and the offices ($8.7m net here) are already inside Budget T (People T x assist_cost, about $109m in 1867-87), so only the irrigation loans and the land's timing are added. The irrigation loss is $2.8m. **The land is a change of timing, not new acres:** the workbook's `Members' land` sheet (L15) sells our timeline's census farm acreage as it grew, most of it after 1887; the programme sells about this many acres earlier. In the workbook it is an earlier path for L15's acreage, at L15's price, not a second land line. The irrigation loans ($11.2m lent in 1872-1887) are a balance-sheet item repaid over about twenty years.

**The railways** (I-53) cost the Treasury no cash: their grants are 10.3m acres of Land Fund land along 1,675 miles less about 60 inside homelands, which give a right of way by compact and no land (Raton line (1878-79) 250, Thirty-fifth parallel (1880-83) 575, Salt Lake-Snake-Columbia (1881-84) 850). Land that would not sell without the line.

**Not costed here** (private, or already in the workbook): the smelters, coal and timber that anchor the mining and forest counties (private capital, some on confederal loans); the Union's plains settlers diverted to the Palouse and the Sound (they pay their own way; the offices above recruit them); California's own Land Fund (CR-033).
